# Continuity Offers — Deep Dive

> "*You can shear a sheep for a lifetime, but you can only skin it once.*" — John, an early Hormozi mentor (p. 142)

> "*The only thing better than getting someone to buy once, is getting them to buy again.*" (p. 175)

Continuity is the endgame of the Money Model — but it's also the offer most often used wrong. Hormozi's rule: **make it last, never first.**

## The Sequencing Rule (p. 175)

"*I use Continuity Offers differently. I make them **last**. I start with profitable Attraction Offers. Then make my Upsell and Downsell Offers. **Then,** I offer Continuity. And if they accept, I upsell a bulk amount of time or product at a discount. Then, they automatically enter continuity after they've used up their bulk purchase.*"

**Why never first:** "*Continuity Offers are tough to advertise profitably. Nobody wants to make a recurring commitment to something they haven't tried.*" (p. 147)

## The Economic Math (p. 142–143)

| Model | Conversion | Customers (per 100 leads) | 30-day cash | Lifetime |
|-------|-----------|--------------------------|-------------|----------|
| One-time $1,000 | 10% | 10 | **$10,000** | $0 over time |
| $50/mo continuity (held 20 months) | 40% | **40** | $2,000 | **$40,000 over time** |

Per-customer LTV identical at $1,000. But **4× the customer pool** to upsell. "*$10,000 now and $0 over time → $2,000 now and $40,000 over time.*"

## Three Sub-types

| Type | Mechanism | When |
|------|-----------|------|
| **Continuity Bonus** | Awesome bonus *if* you sign up today | When you have something high-value to give away |
| **Continuity Discount** | Free time now or later *if* you commit | When customers will commit for a known term |
| **Waived Fee** | Pay a setup fee *or* commit and we waive it | When you need stronger lock-in (slow-payoff services) |

## 1. Continuity Bonus Offers (p. 145–155)

**Tagline:** "*If you like this, you're gonna love what I have next…*"

**Definition:** "Give the customer an awesome thing *if* they sign up today. Typically, the bonus has more value than the first continuity payment." (p. 146)

### Worked case study: gym pivot (Fall 2019, p. 145–146)

| | Old: 6-week challenges | New: free challenges as bonus into membership |
|---|------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Sign up to challenge | 34/100 | 15/100 |
| Convert to membership | 17 (50% of challenge sign-ups) | **40 (straight into continuity)** |
| Take 6-month prepay upsell | — | **8 of those 40** |

Result: **tripled membership sales** + still got upfront cash + stacked prepaid memberships.

### Worked examples (p. 147–149)

| Industry | One-Time Bonus | Monthly bonus | Anchor |
|----------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Pet food | Every dog toy we've made (**$800**) | New toy each month | $59/mo |
| Coaching VIP | $1,000 short-term accelerator | First-line event access, longer support hours | $100/mo |
| Newsletter | Past 40 issues (**$15,880**) | Physical copy + early digital access | $399/mo OR pay today + lock $299/mo for life |

### 11 Important Rules (p. 149–154)

1. **Focus on the bonus, not the membership.** "Get this free valuable thing" beats "Join my membership."
2. **Bonuses work like upsells.** Three flavors: more of the same · complementary · upgrade ("free gold when you buy bronze, limited availability").
3. **Keep bonuses related to your core offer.** Unrelated → wrong customers.
4. **Make bonuses things you already have and do.** "If you value it, they will too."
5. **Physical bonuses on digital products. Digital bonuses on physical products.** Lowers CAC relative to bonus cost.
6. **Use realistic bonus pricing.** Inflated values destroy trust.
7. **Bonus customers with titles.** Silver / gold / diamond / double diamond at 3, 6, 12 months.
8. **Free bonuses ↔ discounts are interchangeable.** Test both framings.
9. **Anchor the bonus first.** Sell the bonus value before the membership price.
10. **More bonuses = more sign-ups.**
11. **Make bonuses **only** available if they join.** Forces continuity.

### Standalone vs Continuity Take Rate (p. 152)

| Standalone price relative to monthly continuity | % choosing continuity |
|------------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| 1.33× (e.g., $399 standalone / $199/mo) | **50%** |
| 1.66× (e.g., $499 / $199/mo) | 60% |
| 2× (e.g., $599 / $199/mo) | 70% |
| 2.33× (e.g., $699 / $199/mo) | 80% |
| 2.66× (e.g., $799 / $199/mo) | **90%** |

> "*The smaller the standalone price compared to the continuity price, the more people buy the standalone. The larger the standalone price, the more people choose continuity.*" (p. 153)

### Advanced moves (p. 153)

- **More up-front cash:** Make bonus-only offer 1.33–2.66× more expensive than first month of continuity+bonus → "*half will buy it*" even at 33% premium.
- **Bulk Prepaid Discount:** "Buy 5 months get 1 free." Only **1 of every 8 customers** has to take it to raise 30-day profits by **50%**.
- **Trade Bonuses For Commitment:** Only allow customers to get the bonus if they commit 3-6-12+ months. "*Nets fewer sales, but more committed customers. This is the trade you make.*"

### Verbatim copy patterns

> "*Become a VIP member today and you'll get it all as a free gift for joining. Or, you can just buy it for $XXX — which would you prefer?*" (p. 151)

> "*On top of that…when you become a member you'll get…amazing thing 1, amazing thing 2, amazing thing 3.*" Mention individual dollar values to anchor. (p. 151)

## 2. Continuity Discount Offers (p. 156–165)

**Tagline:** "*If you sign up today, you get X time free.*"

**Definition:** "Give products or services away for free if the customer commits to buying more *over time*. Easy sale anyone can close." (p. 157)

### Origin: Spring 2018 trash hauler (p. 157)

> "*I'd do their trash for a whole year free if they contracted me to do the next five years paid… They all made me their trash man before I knew it.*"

After year 1, "*the cash came floodin'. I ate **real fat** then. And after a few years…I sold the whole shebang for a pretty penny.*"

### Four Application Methods (p. 158–160)

| Method | How | When |
|--------|-----|------|
| **Up Front** | Free time first, term starts after | Industries with successful contract enforcement (cell, storage, RE, anything with collateral). **Avoid for high-churn.** |
| **At The End** | Earn free time at term completion if all payments made on time | When you want to reward retention behavior |
| **Spread Over Time** | $600 discount ÷ 12 months = $50/mo discount | Safest default; tell customer "lifetime lower rate if all payments on time" |
| **After 1–2 Payments** | Pay activation/first/last; bonus value kicks in after | Bake into "first and last month" or "activation fee" framing |

### Math example: spread (p. 159)

3 months free × $200/mo = $600 discount ÷ 12 months = **$50/mo discount each month**.

### Worked example: rice company (p. 162)

Three pricing options:
1. One-time price
2. **5% off subscription**
3. **15% off if you stay 5 straight months** ("*just beyond where most people canceled*")

> Lifetime-discount-at-churn-point math: "*If everyone churns at month 4, tell everyone they get a lifetime discount after month four.*" (p. 162)

### "Highest Value Per Word Note In This Book" (p. 160)

> "*There are 12 months in a year, but the year has **13 four-week cycles**. **That's an 8.3% difference.** If I offer my thing at '$100 every four weeks' (versus $100 a month), the same number of people buy. But, I make 8.3% more annually. To put this into perspective, if your business has 20% margins, this skyrockets annual profit by **41%**.*"

### 3% Processing Fee Add-on (p. 161)

> "*plus a 3% processing fee.*"
>
> "*Do you want to save the processing fee? Awesome. Give us a second form of payment in case anything happens to the first one.*"
>
> "*We only have the processing fee because it costs us man hours to get new payment information every month. So if you save us time, we pass the savings onto you.*"

On a 10% margin business, +3% = **+30% to profit**.

### Get two forms of payment + prefer ACH (p. 161–162)

ACH bypasses card networks → fewer declines + lower fees.

### Gift Cards as Discount (p. 162)

Use gift cards instead of straight discount. Recipient may forget to redeem ("*you just got a full-priced sign-up. Nice!*"). Doubles as referral lead-magnet.

## Cancellation Policy Framework (p. 162–164)

> "*Small businesses don't get rich by making stuff hard for their customers.*"

### Hormozi's preferred policy

> "*Just make the cancellation fee equal to the discount they agreed to get.*"

If they got $600 in discounts → they pay $600 to cancel. **Equitable, not punitive.**

### Five rules

1. **You need to have a cancellation policy figured out ahead of time.** (p. 162)
2. **Make sure customers know how to cancel.** "*If customers have nowhere to complain inside your business, they will definitely complain outside.*" (p. 163)
3. **If a customer wants to cancel, ask to do an exit interview.** Let them vent; resolve solvable issues; rollover-upsell. (p. 164)
4. **Use cancellation fees to the customer's advantage.** "*I'll waive your cancellation fee if you come in and tell me what I could do better.*" Hormozi saves a third of customers via exit interview. (p. 164)
5. **Light cancellation terms = more sign-ups + more leavers. Harsh terms = fewer sign-ups + fewer leavers.** Hormozi's compromise: cancel by paying back the discount, then revert to month-to-month. (p. 165)

## 3. Waived Fee Offer (p. 167–172)

**Tagline:** "*You can sign up month-to-month with a setup fee, or I'll waive it if you commit to a year.*"

**Definition:** "Ask the customer to pay a startup fee as part of joining a month-to-month program (typically 3–5× monthly rate). Offer to discount the *entire* fee *if* they commit longer term. If they cancel inside the term, they pay the fee." (p. 169)

### Two psychological mechanisms (p. 170)

- **Fees get them to start.** "People want to avoid fees → more sign up to continuity."
- **Fees get them to stick.** "By sticking, they avoid the fee. People quit for millions of reasons. But by incurring an additional fee *in order to* cancel, their original reason for quitting immediately shrinks. **If the cost to quit exceeds the cost to stay, they will probably stay.**"

> Bottom line: "*Customers will stay longer if leaving costs more than staying.*" (p. 169)

### Worked example (p. 169–170)

Commitment length: 12 months. Monthly rate: $1,000. Fee: $5,000.

| Option | Setup | Monthly | Cancel anytime? |
|--------|-------|---------|-----------------|
| **A — Month-to-month** | $5,000 | $1,000 | Yes |
| **B — 12-month commit** | $0 (waived) | $1,000 | Pay $5,000 to cancel early |

### Six rules (p. 170–172)

1. **Justify the fee.** "*It costs us money to get you started. If you only wanna test us out, you cover those costs. If you commit longer, I'll cover them.*"
2. **&gt;5% want to cancel early → fix the product.** "*Pricing can't (and shouldn't) overcome a terrible product. You want to nudge them, not handcuff people.*"
3. **More up-front cash, smaller fee.** Make the fee 1.5–3× monthly rate.
4. **Drop the fee after the customer fulfills the commitment.** Equitable.
5. **Prefer commitments of one year+** (especially slow-payoff: SEO, investing, weight loss). "*Keeps people committed when they get emotional.*"
6. **Cancellation Fees For A…Cause?** "*What cause do you absolutely hate? Great. **If you cancel early, I will be donating your setup fee to them.*** Gives them two reasons to stay." (p. 171)

### Specs (p. 171)

- Fee = 3–5× monthly rate
- Commitment ≥ 1 year
- Larger fee → more commit; smaller fee → more up-front cash
- Fee officially goes away when commitment is met

## Decision Tree

| Goal | Use |
|------|-----|
| Reward customers for joining (high-value bonus available) | **Continuity Bonus** |
| Reward customers for staying (committal-reward) | **Continuity Discount** |
| Need stronger lock-in for slow-payoff service | **Waived Fee** |
| Need more up-front cash | Continuity Bonus + bulk prepay upsell |
| Need to maximize membership conversion (vs. standalone) | Price standalone 2.33–2.66× monthly continuity → 80–90% take continuity |
| Need to mask billing cadence increase | 4-week billing instead of monthly → +8.3% revenue, +41% profit at 20% margins |

## How Continuity Caps the Money Model (p. 175)

"*In the next section we will create our $100M Money Model by combining all four offer types: Attraction Offers, Upsell Offers, Downsell Offers, and Continuity Offers. Let's put a bow on it.*"

Bonus + Discount = rewards-based. Waived Fee = punishment-based. "*There will always be times when a more traditional contract makes sense. In those situations, I like Waived Fee Offers.*"
