# Attraction Offers — Deep Dive

> "Attraction Offers generate leads *and* convert them into customers. They turn advertising into money by offering something free or at a discount." — Hormozi (p. 35)

The five most powerful Attraction Offers Hormozi uses across his portfolio, in the order he says to test them. Every claim is page-cited from $100M Money Models (Acquisition.com, 2025).

## The Free / Discount Continuum (p. 35)

"Any time I say 'free' you can also use 'discount' or '$1.' Any time I use 'discount' you can also use 'free' or '$1' — they exist on a continuum because they all discount a product to some level." Pick where to land:

- **Free** → most leads, lowest show rate
- **$1** → almost as many leads, higher commitment
- **Discounted** → fewer leads but **higher show-up rate** (use this for doctors, lawyers, dentists where no-shows are expensive, p. 64)
- **Full price** → fewest leads, highest commitment

## Universal Ground Rules (p. 32–33)

1. **If A Customer Asks For Their Money Back — Give It Back.** Avoid the headache. Fix the goof.
2. **Hard Selling Is For Weak Products.** Don't convince someone against their will.
3. **Obey The Law.** Advertising laws around "free" tighten constantly. Lawyer up.
4. **Be Transparent.** Don't lie. Bad reputations stick for life.
5. **Any Offer Can Be Used On Its Own, At Any Time, In Any Order.**

## 1. Win Your Money Back (p. 38–48)

**Tagline:** "*If you do x within y time within z rules, you can get it free.*"

**Definition:** "You set a goal for the customer and tell them how to reach it. If they reach it, then they qualify to get their money back or get it back as store credit." (p. 43)

### Three goal structures

| Structure | What customer bets on | Best for |
|-----------|----------------------|----------|
| **Results** | Their ability to hit the outcome | Fitness, weight loss, revenue goals |
| **Actions** | Their ability to follow directions | Course completion, attendance, posting |
| **Both** | Following directions to get the result | Coaching, certification programs |

### Recipe — three criteria for the goal

1. **Easy to track.** Phones already track steps; word processors already track word count. Bonus points if customers already do the action.
2. **Gets customers results.** Realistic conditions; mirror what your best customers already do.
3. **Advertises the business.** Bake in posting, tagging, referring, or testimonial as a qualifying action.

### How to apply store credit (p. 45–46)

"Avoid giving them three free months *up front*. Spread it." Math example exactly as printed: $200/mo product × $600 winnings credit ÷ 12 months = $50/mo discount → customer pays $150/mo for a year.

### Worked examples

- **Free 28-Day Blueprint (B2C):** Deposit $X. Get it back if you attend all consulting calls, post weekly progress, journal daily, attend feedback + transformation sessions. (p. 43)
- **5 Customers In 5 Days Free Challenge (B2B):** Send 100 messages/day, report stats, attend daily training, post homework, attend day-5 call. (p. 40)
- **Put 1,000,000 Miles On Your Car, Get A Free Car (Physical product):** Buy → drive → press release → credit purchase price toward next car. *(Actual offer)* (p. 40)

### "Make Everyone A Winner" pitch (p. 46)

> "*I know you're trying to hit this short-term goal, but what's your long-term goal?…okay great. You get that it's not about this program but about your long-term results. Tell ya what — I'll credit this program toward the next one whether you hit the short-term goal or not. How's that sound?*"

### "Let The Losers Win" pitch (p. 47)

> "*Don't worry about it. You started. That's the biggest victory of all. To show you we're in this for the long haul, we'll credit your entire deposit toward staying with us long term. That way you get your money back and we can still hit your goal.*"

### Guardrails

- "Only Offer 'Win Your Money Back' If You Feel Ok With Giving Money Back" (p. 41)
- Refund rate must be **&lt;5%** — otherwise fix the product first (p. 46)
- "Don't Take Blood Money. If a customer asks for a refund — entitled or not — *I give it to them*." (p. 42)
- Track record claim: "This offer has generated over $1 billion in sales industry-wide" (p. 41)

## 2. Giveaways (p. 50–58)

**Tagline:** "*Many will enter…many will win.*"

**Definition:** "Advertise a chance to win a big prize in exchange for contact information. Then, after picking a winner, offer everyone else the big prize at a discounted price." (p. 52)

### Six-step recipe

1. **Pick a Grand Prize** — make it the thing you want everyone to buy. Assign a monetary value as the price anchor.
2. **Pick your promotional offer** — the discounted version everyone-else gets. "The bigger the value you assign your Grand Prize, the better!"
3. **Ask for contact information + eligibility criteria** ("Do you own a vet clinic?")
4. **Pick qualifying actions** — attending a call, making a post, joining a group
5. **Set a deadline** — 3–7 days from start of promotion. Whisper-tease-shout countdown
6. **Announce winner publicly + contact everyone else privately** — "the participation trophy"

### The 10–30% rule (p. 54)

"Make your core offer discount equal to 10%–30% of your gross margins." Example: Grand Prize $5,000 value with $2,000 retail. Promotional offer $1,800 (10% off retail). Pitch: $5,000 value for $1,800 = "*64% difference in cost-to-value*."

### Worked examples (p. 54)

| Industry | Grand Prize | Promotional Offer |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Dentist | Free invisible braces ($6,000) | $2,000 gift card for braces |
| Pet products | Free year of organic dog food ($1,000) | $300 gift card *only useable with 1-year subscription* |
| Services | Free 1-year package ($5,000) | $2,000 voucher toward 1-year service agreement |
| Consulting | Free 16-Week Turnaround ($12,000) | $6,000 partial scholarship |

### Refer-to-win mechanic (Skool case study, p. 56)

"If a person you refer gets in the top 10, YOU GET TO COME TOO. Aka if they win, you win." Plus 40% lifetime commission for any Skool referral.

### Guardrails

- Sweepstakes are heavily regulated — "*Jail time, no bueno*" (p. 50). Consult counsel.
- "Somebody actually has to win the Grand Prize" — make qualifications clear (p. 55)
- "If your giveaway doesn't work, your Grand Prize wasn't grand enough." Tickets-only Grand Prize flopped; replaced with $50,000 industry-supplier bundle + 1 year of core product → crushed (p. 55)

## 3. Decoy Offer (p. 60–66)

**Tagline:** "*Which one do you think will get you the best results?*"

**Definition:** "Advertise something free or discounted. When leads ask to learn more, *also* present a more valuable premium offer." (p. 62)

### Two-step recipe

1. **Advertise a lesser, smaller, simpler version of your premium offer as a decoy.**
2. **When leads engage, offer both options** — emphasize the premium one.

### Origin: 5-Day $5 VIP Tanning Pass (p. 60–61)

Five-day pass advertised. Customer comes in expecting to get tan; can't get tan enough in 5 days. "Turkey talk": "*Let's just credit your VIP pass toward your first month. Why buy so many $25 day passes when members get unlimited access for just $19.99?*"

Hormozi's gym version (p. 61): Free option = 1 session/week. Premium = $399 unlimited + 1:1 coaching + guarantee. **80% take the $399.**

### Worked examples (p. 63)

| Industry | Decoy | Premium |
|----------|-------|---------|
| Lemonade stand | Water + powdered lemon + corn syrup | Organic, vegan, gluten-free, imported Italian lemons, cold-processed, shipped + sparkling rosewater flavor |
| Float tank | One float/month + DIY stress exercises | 2x/week × 6 weeks + 1:1 + journal + sleep routine + guarantee |
| Gym | Skool group workouts + general nutrition + recordings + no support, no guarantee | Unlimited workouts + personalized nutrition + 1:1 accountability + results guaranteed |

### Permission close (p. 65)

> "*Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?*"

If "results" → skip to premium. If "free stuff" → present decoy then immediately contrast with premium. Then ask: "*Which do you think will get you to your goal faster?*"

### Assumed close (p. 65)

> "*This is what everyone does. This is just a formality. Let me get your ID and credit card so you can get your value.*"

### Guardrails / pitfalls

- Make the contrast huge — basic decoy, awesome premium (p. 64)
- Discount Offers have **higher show-up rates** than Free Offers — use Discount for high-no-show-cost industries (p. 64)
- "Expect to make money fast. If you're not, make the contrast bigger" (p. 66)

## 4. Buy X Get Y Free (p. 67–72)

**Tagline:** "*Buy one puppy, get two puppies free!*"

**Definition:** "When customers buy something, they get other stuff free. The more free stuff and the higher its value, the better it works." (p. 68)

### Origin: Boot Factory (Nashville 2020, p. 67–68)

Storefront: "**BUY 1 PAIR GET TWO PAIR FREE**." Final price $600 for the trio. Realization: "*They charged three times the price for a single pair of boots because they came with two more pairs.*" Bachelorettes filing in for matching boots — *brilliant*.

### The math (p. 68–69)

"I could sell three t-shirts for $10 each = $30 *or* sell one t-shirt for $30 and give two away free. **Same price, more free stuff.**"

With a 33% discount: "Three shirts for $6.67 each = $20 *or* sell one for $20 + give two free. **More free stuff again.**"

### Three-version pricing pattern (p. 70)

Same total revenue ($1,800), maximum free-stuff framing wins:

- Good: "*Buy 12 Months Get 6 Months Free*" — $1,800
- Better: "*Buy 9 Months Get 9 Months Free*" — $1,800
- Best: "*Buy 6 Months Get 12 Months Free*" — $1,800

### Important rules (p. 70–72)

- **Raise prices first.** Permanently raise to accommodate the discount. Don't lie.
- **More free than paid wins.** "Buy 1 Get 2" beats "Buy 2 Get 1."
- **Free things can be different.** Buy a $10 shirt + $20 of free socks = "great deal" anchor.
- **More cheap free things &gt; fewer expensive free things.** "Buy 1 shirt, get 3 socks free" can beat "Buy 1 shirt, get 1 shirt free."
- **Free beats percentage off.** Test "buy one shirt for $20 get two free" against "$10 shirts for $6.67 each (33% off)."
- **Don't sell what you can't deliver.** Selling stuff you can't deliver "breaks the law and *ruins* your reputation."
- **Existing-customer fast cash:** "Buy Ten Get Two Free" to existing customers — limit to 10% of base.

## 5. Pay Less Now or Pay More Later (p. 74–78)

**Tagline:** "*Time is money. — Benjamin Franklin*"

**Definition:** "Give people a choice to pay full-price later OR pay a discounted price now. Removes *all* risk from the customer — they pay later *and* only if they like it." (p. 75)

### The split

- **Pay Later:** $0 today, billed $X tomorrow if they don't qualify for the conditional satisfaction guarantee. *Get card on file.*
- **Pay Now:** 20–50% discount + bonuses (instead of guarantee).

### Origin: Reading-speed workshop (p. 74)

> "*Double your reading speed in 3 hours, or it's free.*"
>
> Pay Later: "*$0 now, billed $297 tomorrow. If your reading speed doesn't double, just email us before then and we'll cancel the charge. But you must attend in order to be eligible.*"
>
> Pay Now: "*Pay $97 right now, and as a free bonus, get the recordings.*"

Hormozi took Pay Later, attended, doubled, paid $297.

### Recipe checklist

1. **Promise a clear yes/no result.** Measurable, deliverable in your time frame.
2. **Conditional satisfaction guarantee** — they only qualify to cancel if they did the work. Aligns guarantee criteria with what gets them value.
3. **Pay Now option** — 20–50% off + bonuses.
4. **Have an upsell ready** — present after delivery.

### Worked examples (p. 75–76)

| Industry | Pay Later | Pay Now | Upsell |
|----------|-----------|---------|--------|
| Real estate workshop | $0 → $500 after 3-day workshop | $299 + recordings + 1:1 expert call + printed materials | $30,000 6-month deal close program |
| Local landscaping | $0 lawn cut + hedges → $599 after | $369 + lawn treatment | $199/mo lawncare |
| Fashion DTC | $0 → $149 in 14 days | $97 + accessory | Monthly clothing subscription |

### Optimization rules (p. 76–77)

- Too many take Pay Later → discount Pay Now more or add bigger bonuses
- Too many take Pay Now → do the opposite
- &gt;10% cancel Pay Later → you over-promised, guarantee criteria too low, or price too high
- For event/workshop businesses: tease the next offer at the start ("*everyone wants to know when my next intensive starts — I'll get to it at the end*")

### Decision rule (p. 147)

Use **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later** for *physical products* or *one-time services*. Use **Trial With Penalty** (downsell) for *recurring services* where the customer must do work to get results.

## Choosing between the five

| If you have… | Use |
|--------------|-----|
| Customers who must work to get results, refund rate &lt;5% | Win Your Money Back |
| Capacity to call all qualified leads in 7 days, "grand" Grand Prize | Giveaways |
| Cheap leads / multi-industry flexibility | Decoy Offer |
| Multiple units or long-duration deliverables | Buy X Get Y Free |
| A measurable yes/no result deliverable in &lt;1 month | Pay Less Now or Pay More Later |
| High no-show cost (doctors/lawyers/dentists) | Any of above, but as **discount** not free (p. 64) |
