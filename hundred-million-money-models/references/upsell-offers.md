# Upsell Offers — Deep Dive

> "Upsells just mean *whatever we offer next*." — Hormozi (p. 78)
>
> "**Warning**: This section is brutally effective and must be used ethically." (p. 82)

The four most powerful upsells Hormozi uses, with the McDonald's economics that justify them and the Magnetic Middle pricing tweak that compounds them.

## Why Upsells Make or Break the Money Model

McDonald's unit economics, exact figures (p. 80–81):

| Stage | Pitch | Profit/sale | Lift |
|-------|-------|-------------|------|
| Burger only | (none) | $0.25 | 1× |
| + Fries | "*Do you want fries with that?*" | $0.25 + $0.75 = $1.00 | 4× |
| + Drink (meal) | "*Do you want to make it a meal?*" | $2.00 | **8×** |
| Supersize | "*Do you want to supersize for just a buck more?*" | $3.00 | **11.6×** |

> "*The thing you sell the most isn't always the thing you make the most profit on.*" (p. 81)

## The Single Most Important Principle (p. 80)

"When an offer solves a problem, another appears. You *upsell* the solution to the problem your offer reveals. So, every offer opens the door to an upsell…even upsells!"

Corollary on timing: "*The second worst thing that happens is they say no. The worst thing is if they would've said yes but you never asked.*" (p. 92)

## When Upsells Fail (p. 81)

- Offering something they don't want (too different / wrong problem)
- Offering at the wrong time (before they've experienced the problem)
- Offering the wrong way (they don't believe you)

## Three Upsell Categories (p. 81)

- **More** — same thing in larger quantity
- **Better** — higher quality version
- **New / complementary** — different thing

## 1. The Classic Upsell (p. 83–94)

**Tagline:** "*You Can't Have X Without Y!*"

**Definition:** "Solves the customer's *next problem* the moment they become aware of it." (p. 85)

### Origin: Fur Coat / Earmuff Storage (p. 83–85)

Free earmuffs advertised with coat storage. When customer comes to retrieve muffs and store coats: "*Great. And we'll store those as well for $30. You don't want to store anything else do you?*" Pay for storage of the *free* earmuffs by saying no.

### "Say No To Say Yes" psychology (p. 88)

People are trained to say "no" to "*you don't want anything else, do you?*" That "no" is actually a "yes" to *everything you just offered*. The framing translates to: "*You don't want anything [besides what I just offered] do you?*"

### Worked examples (p. 86)

| Industry | First purchase | Upsell pattern |
|----------|---------------|----------------|
| Local service | Car wash | "*You're not gonna wanna do the wash without sealant.*" |
| Physical product | Bicycle | "*You can't have a bike without a helmet!*" + lights + puncture-resistant tires |
| Digital product | Exercise course | "*You can't out-exercise a bad diet…*" → nutrition course |

### Important rules (p. 87–91)

1. **Actually do it.** "You barely have a business — you have a front end."
2. **Offer more profitable upsells first.**
3. **Get them to "say no to say yes."**
4. **Surprise and delight.** Save four bonuses; add one at a time. If they say yes early, give them all four anyway.
5. **Hyper buying cycle.** Sell more when they're buying more — weddings, new hobbies, new babies, new home.
6. **Use free bonuses to create problems your upsells solve.** Earmuffs → upsell paid storage of *the free thing*.
7. **Faster access = higher value perception.** Bonus points: put the upsell in their hands *before* they've said yes. "It's way harder to give something back than say no."
8. **Name your bundles.** "Fastest results bundle" / "Transformation package" / "Minimum package."
9. **Integrate upsells into other offers.** Meal plans suggested supplements; sales training suggested software.
10. **BAMFAM (Book A Meeting From A Meeting).** Sharran's quote: "*A customer should know the next time they see you — and why — before they leave.*"
11. **Upsell as many times as it makes sense.** "Don't be shy."

### Magnetic Middle pricing (p. 92–93)

How to push customers up a tier without changing the top option's price:

**To get more LARGE buyers:**
- Small $5
- Medium $8 *(rather than rational $7)*
- Large $9

→ "*People who normally take medium will now probably take the large.*"

**To get more MEDIUM buyers:**
- Small $6 *(rather than rational $5)*
- Medium $7
- Large $9

→ "*Most people who would normally get a small, will get a medium.*"

**Bottom line:** Lots of customers buying small? Push to mediums. Lots buying medium? Push to large. Lots buying large? **Raise all prices.**

### Upsell guarantees / warranties / insurance (p. 93)

Art studio replaced damaged portraits free. Started asking for **+10%** for the same protection. "Now, 30% of customers buy stuff the art studio used to give away for free. *Pure profit, baby.*" Range: 5–50% premium for a guarantee.

## 2. Menu Upsell (p. 95–106)

**Tagline:** "*You don't need that…you need this.*"

**Definition:** "Tell customers which options they don't need. Then, tell them what they do need, their preferences, *and* how to get value from it." (p. 99)

### Four-step recipe

1. **Unsell** what they don't need
2. **Prescribe** what they do need + how to use it
3. **A/B Upsell** — offer a choice ("chocolate or vanilla?")
4. **Card On File** — "*Wanna just use the card on file?*"

### Why each step works (p. 99–100)

- **Unselling** removes the "should I buy?" frame; replaces it with "what should I cross out?"
- **Prescribing** removes the option of *not* buying — you talk as if they already have it.
- **A/B Upsell** changes the question from "if" to "which" — both choices result in a sale.
- **Card On File** lowers the "hidden cost" of buying.

### Origin stories

- **Dec 2013, supplement consults (p. 95–96):** 19 consults, 0 sales. On consult #20, Hormozi forgot his script: "*You've got a protein shake — chocolate or vanilla?*" → "Chocolate." → "Great, I'll take one." Then "*kiwi or strawberry pre-workout?*" → sold. Then "*Just use the card on file?*" → "Yep." Sold the next 20 in a row.
- **Aug 2014 (p. 97):** Customer asking endless questions; Hormozi *wrote down* personalized step-by-step instructions on the order form. "*Hot diggity dawg. She just bought all those products…and I didn't even ask her anything. I told her.*" Lesson: **prescription beats vague suggestion.**
- **Nov 2016 (p. 97–98):** Out of stock; sent customer to a competitor at $20 less. Built so much goodwill that crossing out other irrelevant items ("you don't need testosterone") let him upsell what was left.

### Worked examples (p. 101–102)

**Massage therapist:**
- Unsell: "*We have lymphatic massage, but you're not pregnant or just out of surgery, right? Cross that out.*"
- Prescribe: "*Since your shoulder hurts we'll heat you up first, hit your trigger points, then dynamic stretches.*"
- A/B: "*Before work or on the way home?*"
- Card on file: "*Wanna just use the card on file?*"

**Dog food retailer:**
- Unsell: "*You don't need this small bag or this puppy stuff — you've got a big dog! No vitamins either, the food has them.*"
- Prescribe: "*Give your dog one of these joint chews per meal. Every 90 days, give them a heartworm wafer. Bring him back next month.*"
- A/B: "*Beef or chicken flavor?*"
- Card on file: "*Wanna just use the card on file?*"

### Make anything A/B-sellable (p. 103)

Quantity (one bottle or two?) · start dates (tomorrow or Monday?) · payment (cash or card?) · flavors · time slots · media (read or listen?) · delivery speed · sizes · colors · materials · personnel · communication channel.

### Add a nudge to the A/B (p. 103)

"*This is my favorite*" / "*X is usually a safe bet*" / "*A lot of people love this*" / "*Tuesday's sessions are smaller if you like that.*"

### The "Economist" Decoy Pricing Play (p. 104)

Original pricing:
- A — Digital $59/year
- B — Digital + Print $125/year

Print sales plummeted (everyone took A).

Fixed with decoy:
- A — Digital $59/year
- **B — Print only $125/year** *(the decoy)*
- C — Digital + Print $125/year *(the winner)*

Result: customers now took C. **Bottom line:** Three options at A / B / C-Both. Price C the same as B. Customer choice becomes obvious; you sell both.

### Pro tip (p. 103)

"*Card on file for first purchases — what card do you wanna use?*"

## 3. Anchor Upsell (p. 107–113)

**Tagline:** "*The only thing worse than making a $1,000 offer to a person with a $100 budget…is making a $100 offer to someone with a $1,000 budget.*"

**Definition:** "Offer premium stuff first. If the customer gasps, offer a cheaper but acceptable alternative." (p. 109)

### Why it works (p. 109)

A premium version 5–10× the price first makes the main offer look like "*a much better deal*." Two bonuses: anchored customers spend more than they planned to, and **some customers still buy the super-expensive thing**.

### Origin: Suit Shop (2016, p. 107–109)

Hormozi budgeted $500. Owner put two suits on the rack.
- Suit 1: **$16,000.** Hormozi: "I felt horrified."
- Owner: "*Do you care about the designer much?*" "Not at all."
- Whirled around with second suit:
- Suit 2: **$2,200.** "Sigh of relief."
- Bought the $2,200 suit + socks + handkerchief + shirt = **+$300**.
- "I spent five times more than I had budgeted and felt OK about it."

### Five-step recipe (p. 110)

1. **Present the Anchor** — really expensive thing
2. **Get "The Gasp"** — expect customer to freak out about cost
3. **Come to the rescue** — "*Do you care about [premium feature]?*"
4. **Present main offer** — relief and "better deal" perception
5. **Ask how they wanna pay** — "*Which card do you prefer?*"

### Worked examples (p. 110–111)

| Industry | Premium Anchor | Main Offer |
|----------|----------------|-----------|
| Lawn care | Cell-phone access + fancy mulch + natural pest + bi-weekly = **$1,000/wk** | Team number + generic mulch + normal pest + bi-weekly = **$200/wk** |
| Painting | Super protective packaging + 20yr insurance + gift wrap = **$1,000** | Normal packaging + 1yr insurance + sticker = **$200** |
| Newsletter | All past issues + new + 24hrs early = **$199/mo** | New issues only + on time = **$19/mo** |

### Important rules (p. 111–113)

- **Don't treat the anchor as fake.** "If you treat it like a fake, so will the customer."
- **Build a premium offer you actually want to deliver.** Friend tweaked his anchor → "*tripled his profits.*"
- **The bigger the gasp, the more they bought.**
- **No gasp?** They find premium reasonable. Ask if they want to use the card on file. (Don't gasp yourself when they say yes.)
- **Tweak only secondary features for the main offer.** Primary features stay the same. "Offering the primary features for a fifth of the price makes the main offer a *great deal.*"

## 4. Rollover Upsell (p. 117–123)

**Tagline:** "*Wanna just roll it forward?*"

**Definition:** "Credits some or all of a customer's previous purchases toward your next offer." (p. 118)

> "This is the upsell I use most frequently. It has elegant urgency built in + goodwill." (p. 123)

### Origin: Win Your Money Back evolution (June 2014, p. 117–118)

$600 fitness program with money-back-on-goal. Winners applied $600 → 3 months membership → churned before first out-of-pocket payment. Friend Justin's twist: **"I just *roll over* their winnings into a year-long membership"** — $50/month off for 12 months. Customers "*start paying immediately*" and "*still get their money back…it just takes a year.*"

### Three-question framework (p. 118)

- **Who** to upsell
- **What** to upsell
- **How** to roll over the credit

### Who (p. 118)

1. Re-engage customers who left months ago
2. Rescue *upset* customers as a refund alternative
3. Rescue *other people's* upset customers (scrape negative reviews)
4. Upsell regular customers

### What (p. 119)

More of what they got · something better · something new. **Roll their credit over to something more expensive.**

### How (p. 119)

Apply credit up front · or spread over time.

### Worked examples (p. 119–120)

| Industry | Who | What | How |
|----------|-----|------|-----|
| Chiropractor | 6-mo lapsed | Pain-free plan | Up-front $500 credit |
| Dentist | Upset customer | Whitening package | Front-load $200 credit |
| Software | Competitor's upset customer | Service agreement | Roll over remaining contract value |
| Membership | Current customer | 12-month membership | Spread $600 over 12 months = $50/mo |

### "Save Your Own Upset Customer" script (p. 119)

> "*Hi Mrs. Banks, I wanted to give you your money back — do you have a minute? Great. How's your back pain? Sorry to hear that. As a thank you, I want to give you $500 of your money back as credit toward staying pain-free for good. Is that of any interest? Great…let's get you in.*"

### "Steal a Competitor's Upset Customer" script (p. 119)

> "*Hi John, I saw your negative review on their product and it really upset me. To make it up to you, I'll credit whatever payments you have left with them to switch to ours. This way, you don't lose a thing and you start getting the benefits now. Fair enough?*"

### Lapsed-customer winback (p. 121)

Hormozi recorded **200 personalized videos** offering past customers $4,000 of credit to return. **20% take rate. ~$1.9M extra annual revenue from one day of recording.** "Worth it."

### The 4× pricing rule (p. 122)

"Price your next offer *at least* 4× higher than the credit. This makes a 25% discount." Even applying the entire first-purchase credit, you discount only ~25%.

### "Famous" Gift Card Play (p. 121–122)

Advertise gift cards at 90%+ off. Example: $200 gift cards for $20. Limit 2 per customer; only useable on *other people*. Set the gift-card value at 20% of your next offer's price (so the next offer is at least $1,000). "*People pay you to refer their friends.*"

### Important rules (p. 120–122)

- Apply credit *before* refunding
- Make Rollover Offers **one-time only** for urgency
- "Bigger discounts make less profit per sale, but get more sales"
- Just make sure you make a profit after applying the credit

## Decision Rules

| When the customer is… | Use |
|----------------------|-----|
| Just bought, has next-problem now | **Classic Upsell** |
| Confused / has many options to navigate | **Menu Upsell** |
| Has wide budget variance, you don't know yet | **Anchor Upsell** |
| Lapsed, upset, competitor's, or current with new need | **Rollover Upsell** |
| Has lots of small-tier customers | **Magnetic Middle** to push them up |
| Has lots of large-tier customers | **Raise prices** |
