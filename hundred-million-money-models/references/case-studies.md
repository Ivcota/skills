# Case Studies — Worked Money Models

Eight cited case studies from $100M Money Models, with the exact numbers Hormozi published. Use these as reference patterns when adapting the framework to a new industry.

## 1. The Storage Unit Money Model (p. 11–14)

**Industry:** Self-storage
**Mentor:** Owner of 27-unit chain across the street from Hormozi's first gym
**Attraction Offer:** "Free month of storage" (loss-leader)

**The reveal — what "free month" actually costs the customer (p. 12–13):**

| Item | Price | Logic |
|------|-------|-------|
| Heavy-duty lock | $47 | "*Yeah, and not one of those flimsy locks kids use on their lockers.*" |
| Boxes / tape / labels / heavy-duty markers | varies | "*If you have **stuff** to store, you're gonna need **boxes** to store it in!*" |
| Optional moving services | affiliate (kickback) | Local moving partner; or rent dollies/hand trucks/straps "for a fee" |
| $100,000 insurance upgrade | $10/mo | Standard $500 free insurance baked in; upgrade for "the special locks *only I offer*" |
| Size upgrade | varies | "*Everyone has way more stuff than they think. They **always** rent too small. We **always** offer one size up.*" |

**Total realized first-month revenue:** **$127** ("*free*" month).

**The lesson:** Free attraction offer → built-in upsell sequence (lock → boxes → insurance → size) → continuity (monthly storage). Hormozi's takeaway: "*When you figure out how to make money in one business, you see ways to make money in any business.*"

## 2. Hormozi's Gym Money Model (Pre-Launch, p. 15–16)

**Industry:** Brick-and-mortar fitness
**Stage at meeting:** 6 gyms, $1M/mo

**Front-end metrics:**

- 20 leads/day @ **$5/lead** (cost per lead)
- 50% show rate
- 50% close rate of show-ups
- Result: **25% of leads → paying customer**
- $600 program AOV
- +$80 supplement profit per customer
- = **$680/customer** before opening doors

**The breakthrough math:** "*$1 in → $34 out in 48 hours.*" (p. 16)

**The back-end (continuity rollover, à la Justin's twist, p. 117):**
- After 6-week program, offer membership: $600 credit applied if they commit to 12 months
- ⅔ of program graduates convert to membership
- Outcome per launch: **full gym + $20,000/mo recurring** for $3,000 of upfront marketing

**Result:** Gym Launch sold 66% of the company at **$46.2M cash**. Hormozi crossed $100M net worth at age 31 (p. 17).

## 3. Boot Factory — Buy 1 Get 2 Free (Nashville 2020, p. 67–68)

**Industry:** Cowboy boot retail
**Customer:** Mostly tourists (one-shot)

**Storefront price ladder:**
- Anchored: 3 pairs at $200 each = $600 retail
- Sale 1: marked down
- "Final offer": **$600 for the trio** as "Buy 1 Pair Get Two Pair Free"

**The trick:** Customer pays $600 either way. The "free 2 pairs" framing **converts more buyers** because "more people know the value of *free* than the value of one shirt."

**Why it works for tourists:** One-shot purchase. "*If customers only buy once, make them buy big.*" Bachelorettes filed in to get matching boots.

## 4. The Suit Shop Anchor (2016, p. 107–109)

**Industry:** Custom suits
**Customer:** Hormozi (budget: $500)

**Sequence:**
1. Owner pulls Suit 1: tag = **$16,000**. Hormozi: "*I felt horrified.*" (The Gasp.)
2. Owner: "*Do you care about the designer much?*" → "Not at all."
3. Owner whirls around with Suit 2: tag = **$2,200**. ("*Sigh of relief.*")
4. Hormozi buys Suit 2 + socks + handkerchief + shirt = **+$300**.
5. Total: **$2,500** (5× original budget, "*felt OK about it*").

**Anchor ratio:** $16,000 / $2,200 ≈ **7.3×** — squarely inside Hormozi's prescribed 5–10× range (p. 113).

## 5. The Reading-Speed Workshop — Pay Less Now or Pay More Later (June 2016, p. 74)

**Industry:** Digital education
**Headline:** "*Double your reading speed in 3 hours, or it's free.*"

**Two-option pricing:**

| Option | Price | What you get | Risk |
|--------|-------|--------------|------|
| **Pay Later** | $0 today, billed **$297** next day | Workshop only | Cancel charge if reading speed didn't double + you attended |
| **Pay Now** | **$97** today | Workshop + recordings (not for sale anywhere else) | None — no guarantee |

**Hormozi's choice:** Pay Later. Attended. Speed doubled. Paid $297.

**Lesson:** Pay Later got the card on file with $0 commitment. The conditional guarantee (you must attend) made the cancellation rate manageable. Workshop became the upsell platform.

## 6. Justin's Rollover Upsell (June 2014, p. 117–118)

**Industry:** Fitness coaching
**Problem:** Win-Your-Money-Back program ($600) → customers won → applied $600 toward 3-month membership → churned before paying out of pocket. Net: "Buy 6 weeks, get 3 months free."

**Justin's twist:**
- Roll over $600 winnings as **$50/mo discount × 12 months**
- Customer pays **immediately** at the discounted rate
- **Still gets the money back — over a year**

**Result:** "*Boom. This was it. The missing link in my Money Model.*" Became Hormozi's most-used upsell type.

## 7. Hormozi's 200-Video Lapsed-Customer Winback (p. 121)

**Industry:** Coaching / education
**Audience:** Past customers, 6+ months since last purchase
**Action:** Hormozi recorded **200 personalized videos**, each offering **$4,000 of credit** to return.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Videos recorded | 200 |
| Take rate | **20%** (40 customers) |
| Annual revenue lift | **~$1,900,000** |
| Time invested | 1 day of recording |

**Lesson:** "*Worth it.*" Lapsed customers are the easiest Rollover Upsell target.

## 8. The Trash Hauler — Continuity Discount (Spring 2018, p. 157)

**Industry:** Residential trash collection (B2B sales)
**Offer:** "*I'd do their trash for a whole year free if they contracted me to do the next five years paid.*"

**Mechanics:**
- Year 1: Free service
- Years 2–5: Paid contracts ("the cash came floodin'")
- "*I ate real fat then.*"

**Exit:** "*I sold the whole shebang for a pretty penny.*"

**Why it worked:** Trash service is sticky (low-rebid behavior). Up-front discount applied works **only** when the industry has reliable contract enforcement (Hormozi's cautionary note: avoid up-front discounts in high-churn industries — p. 158).

## 9. Gym → Membership Pivot via Continuity Bonus (Fall 2019, p. 145–146)

**Industry:** Gym
**Problem:** Selling 6-week challenges as the front-end → 17/100 converted to members (a 50% take rate from the 34/100 who joined the challenge).

**Pivot:** Make the 6-week challenge a *bonus* for joining the membership directly.

| Stage | Old | New |
|-------|-----|-----|
| Sign up to challenge | 34/100 | 15/100 |
| **Convert to membership** | 17 (50% of 34) | **40 straight to continuity** |
| Take 6-month prepay upsell | — | **8 of 40** |

**Result:** **Tripled membership sales.** Still got challenge cash. Stacked prepaid memberships on top.

**Pitch:** "*Wanna save even more money?*" → 6-month prepay.

## 10. The HR Software — Trial With Penalty (Leila's experience, p. 137–138)

**Industry:** B2B SaaS (HR onboarding)
**Setup:** $500 onboarding + $99/mo subscription

**Trial With Penalty offer:**
- Skip the $500 onboarding fee **if** the customer:
  1. Attends 3 × 60-min Zoom onboarding calls
  2. Activates employer profile
  3. Sets up employees by end of call 3
- Otherwise: pay the $500 fee

**Result:** Leila completed all three calls (avoidance of $500 fee + sunk-cost commitment). After investing the time, she "became locked in, kept paying" the $99/mo. Trial → continuity conversion was nearly automatic.

**Lesson:** Trial With Penalty works *only* when the customer must do work to get results. The work itself becomes the moat.
