## Definitions

### Lead
- definition: A person you can contact.
  source: "$100M Leads, Leads Alone Aren't Enough, p. 42"
  quote: "a **lead** is a *person you can contact*. That's all. … *If you can contact them, they are leads.*"

### Engaged Lead
- definition: A lead that has shown interest in the stuff you sell.
  source: "$100M Leads, Leads Alone Aren't Enough, p. 42"
  quote: "We want **engaged leads:** *people who \*show\* interest in the stuff you sell.*"
- examples Hormozi gives (p. 43): gives contact info on a website; follows you on social media (and you can contact them); replies to your email campaign.

### Advertising
- definition: The process of making known.
  source: "$100M Leads, Section I prologue, p. 8"
  quote: "**Advertising**, *the process of making known*, lets strangers know about the stuff you sell."

### Offer (Core Offer)
- definition: What you promise to give in exchange for something of value (typically money).
  source: "$100M Leads, Engage Your Leads, p. 49"
  quote: "**Offers** are what you promise to give in exchange for something of value. Often, a business promises to give its product or service in exchange for money. This is a *core offer*."

### Lead Magnet
- definition: A complete solution to a narrow problem; typically free or low-cost; reveals a larger problem the core offer solves.
  source: "$100M Leads, Engage Your Leads, p. 49"
  quote: "A **lead magnet** is a complete solution to a narrow problem. It's typically a lower-cost or free offer to see who is interested in your stuff. And, once solved, it reveals another problem *solved by your core offer*."

### Call to Action (CTA)
- definition: Tells the audience what to do next; good CTAs include both what to do AND a reason to do it right now.
  source: "$100M Leads, Step 7 (Engage Your Leads), p. 71"
  quote: "A **Call To Action (CTA)** *tells the audience what to do next.* … Good CTAs have two things: 1) what to do and 2) reasons to do it *right now*."

### Warm vs. Cold Audiences (referenced for completeness; introduces Section III)
- Warm: "people who gave you permission to contact them" (p. 78).
- Cold: "people who have not given you permission to contact them" (p. 78).

## Core Offer vs. Lead Magnet (the Engagement Equation)

Hormozi frames two paths to engaging a lead — advertise the core offer directly, or advertise a lead magnet first.

- claim: Try advertising the core offer first; it's the direct path to money.
  source: "$100M Leads, Engage Your Leads, p. 49"
  quote: "If you advertise your core offer, then you go straight for the sale–the direct path to money. … Try this way first."

- claim: Lead magnets shine when the core offer is expensive or requires trust-building.
  source: "$100M Leads, Engage Your Leads, p. 49"
  quote: "Sometimes, though, people want to know more about your offer before they buy. This is common for businesses that sell more expensive stuff."

- claim: A person who pays with time is more likely to pay with money later.
  source: "$100M Leads, Engage Your Leads, p. 50"
  quote: "*A person who pays with their time now is more likely to pay with their money later.*"

- claim: Even free lead magnets have a cost — the lead's time.
  source: "$100M Leads, Engage Your Leads (Pro Tip), p. 50"
  quote: "if they think your lead magnet is worth their time, they'll think your core offer is worth their money."

- claim: This skill ties to the Value Equation from $100M Offers via the rule: "Grand Slam Offers work for free stuff as much or better than they do for paid stuff."
  source: "$100M Leads, Seven Steps preamble, p. 51"
  quote: "Grand Slam Offers work for free stuff as much or better than they do for paid stuff. So make your lead magnet so insanely good people will feel stupid saying no."

(This is the explicit bridge to $100M Offers — Hormozi imports the Grand Slam Offer / Value Equation framework into lead-magnet design rather than restating it.)

## Lead Magnet Framework — Seven Steps

Verbatim list:
- source: "$100M Leads, Seven Steps To Creating an Effective Lead Magnet, p. 51"
- Step 1: Figure out the problem you want to solve and who to solve it for
- Step 2: Figure out how to solve it
- Step 3: Figure out how to deliver it
- Step 4: Test what to name it
- Step 5: Make it easy to consume
- Step 6: Make it darn good
- Step 7: Make it easy for them to tell you they want more

(Note: Hormozi restates the same list at the end of the chapter, p. 76, with slightly tweaked wording — Step 4 becomes "Make the name interesting and clear"; Step 6 "Make sure it's darn good"; Step 7 "Tell them what to do next, why it's a good idea, do it clearly, and do it often.")

### Step 1 — Figure out the problem (Problem-Solution Cycle)

- Concept: Pick a *narrow* problem whose solution naturally surfaces a *next* problem your core offer solves. Hormozi calls this the "Problem-Solution cycle."
- Quote (p. 52): "Every problem has a solution. Every solution reveals more problems."
- Decision rule: "*if we can solve that new problem with our core offer, we've got a winner.*" (p. 53)
- Example given (homeowners selling, p. 53): broad goal = sell home; narrow lead-magnet problems = home valuation, value-increase guidance, listing photos, cleaning, landscaping, fixes, moving, staging.
- Action Step (p. 53): "Pick the narrowly defined problem you want to solve. Then, make sure your core offer can solve the next problem that comes up."

### Step 2 — Figure out how to solve it (Three Lead-Magnet Types)

- source: "$100M Leads, Step 2, pp. 53–55"
- Hormozi names exactly three types:

1. **Reveal Their Problem** ("diagnosis"). Best when "problems get worse the longer you wait."
   - Examples (p. 54): website speed test that quantifies dollars lost; posture analysis vs. a pain-free baseline; termite inspection that reveals damage cost vs. prevention cost.
2. **Samples and Trials.** "You give full but brief access to your core offer."
   - Examples (p. 55): faster-server demo; free posture adjustment; "fun-sized" consumables ("It's how Costco sells more food than other stores–they give out samples!").
3. **One Step Of A Multi-Step Process.** Used when the core offer is complex.
   - Examples (p. 56): the book itself (one step toward $1M+ profit); free first coat of garage-door sealant out of three required coats; free finance calculators/templates that reveal the labor problem solved by financial services.

- Pro Tip (p. 55–56): "Be A Drug Dealer." Quote: "like a drug dealer, you'd wanna give the strongest 'hit' *first*."
- Author note — Dressing Rooms (p. 57): the dressing room is "all three types of lead magnet in one" — trial + reveal-problem + step-1-of-multi-step (outfit). Hormozi's recommendation: "if you can, try and get a lead magnet that does all three."

### Step 3 — Figure out how to deliver it (Four Delivery Mechanisms)

- source: "$100M Leads, Step 3, pp. 58–59"
- Four mechanisms, each labeled with a verb:
  1. **Software**: "*You give them a tool.*" (Hormozi's gym example: spreadsheet/dashboard that benchmarks gym KPIs vs. industry averages and ranks the gym.)
  2. **Information**: "*You teach them something.*" (Mini-course on writing an ad.)
  3. **Services**: "*You do work for free.*" (Run a gym owner's ads free for 30 days.)
  4. **Physical Products**: "*You give them something they can hold in their hands.*" (Hormozi sells a book, *Gym Launch Secrets*.)
- Combinatorial point (p. 59): "With three different types of lead magnets and four ways to deliver them, that's up to twelve lead magnets that solve a single narrow problem."
- Action Step (p. 59): "As a thought exercise, think of a lead magnet and then a version of it for each delivery method. You always can, I promise. Then, pick how to deliver *your* lead magnet."

### Step 4 — Test what to name it

- source: "$100M Leads, Step 4, pp. 59–66"
- Headline first (Ogilvy citation, p. 59): Hormozi paraphrases Ogilvy that the headline is ~80% of the ad spend.
- Three elements to test, in order: **headline, image(s), subheadline.** "If you only test one thing, test that [headline]." (p. 60)
- Worked example: he tested four book-title rounds via Instagram polls (p. 60–62):
  - Round I: $100M Advertising 69% vs. $100M Promotion 31% → "Advertising" wins.
  - Round II: $100M Advertising 42% vs. $100M Leads 58% → "Leads" wins.
  - Round III: $100M Marketing 46% vs. $100M Leads 54% → "Leads" wins.
- Image test: real cover vs. cartoon cover.
- Subheadline tests: four rounds; winning subheadline = "How to get strangers to want to buy your stuff." (Hormozi's lesson, p. 64: "The only difference is two little words: 'how to.' … Small changes can make big differences.")
- Subheadline rationale (p. 64): he didn't title it "buy your stuff" because that crosses into sales — "The point of this book is to get strangers to show interest, not to buy (yet). A raised hand is where this book ends."
- Action Step (p. 64): "Test. If people engage in droves, you've got a winner."
- Cheap-test tactic (p. 64): if you have any following, run polls; if not, post on every platform and ask people to reply '1' or '2'.

### Step 5 — Make it easy to consume

- source: "$100M Leads, Step 5, pp. 65–66"
- Claim: "You can see 2x, 3x, and even 4x+ increases in take rates *and* consumption simply by making it easier to consume." (p. 65)
- Per-mechanism rules:
  - Software: phone + computer + multiple formats.
  - Information: video, text, audio, images — offer all (p. 65).
  - Services: more times of day, more days of the week, more channels (video / phone / in person).
  - Physical products: simple ordering, fast delivery, simple instructions; Apple as exemplar.
- Hormozi's $100M Offers data point (p. 66): near-perfect ¼/¼/¼/¼ split between ebook, physical book, audiobook, and video — making it available in multiple formats yields "2-3-4x the amount of leads for the same work."

### Step 6 — Make it darn good

- source: "$100M Leads, Step 6, pp. 66–67"
- Subtitle: "Give Away The Secrets, Sell The Implementation" (p. 66).
- Quote (p. 66): "you can never provide too much value. But, you *can* provide too little. So you want your lead magnet to provide so much value people feel obligated to pay you. The goal is to provide more value than the cost of your core offer *before they've bought it*."
- Mechanism (p. 67): "people buy stuff based on how much value they think they'll get *after* they buy it. And the easiest way to get them to think they'll get tons of value after they buy is… drum roll please… to provide them with value *before* they buy."
- Anti-pattern (p. 66–67): "sucky fluff" → people who would have bought from you tell others not to.
- Action Step (p. 67): "99% of people aren't gonna buy, but they will create (or destroy) your reputation based on the value of your free stuff."

### Step 7 — Make it easy for them to tell you they want more (CTA)

- source: "$100M Leads, Step 7, pp. 71–75"
- A good CTA = (1) what to do + (2) reasons to do it *right now*.
- "Don't be clever, be clear." (p. 73)
- Hormozi's three favorite reasons-to-act-now:

#### a) Scarcity
- Definition (p. 71): "Scarcity *is when there is a limited amount of something.*"
- Best practice: "ethical scarcity" — be transparent about the real constraint. "If you can't handle more than five new customers per week, *say so*." (p. 71)
- Copy patterns (p. 71):
  - "The most convenient class times fill up fast. Call now to get the one you want."
  - "I can only handle five people per week, so if you want it solved soon, do xyz…"
  - "We only printed one batch of shirts and will never reprint this design, get one so you don't regret missing out forever…"

#### b) Urgency
- Definition (p. 72): "**Urgency** *is when people act faster because they have a short amount of time.*"
- Three flavors: hard deadline, hard deadline + bonus, bonus that decays over time.
- Copy patterns (p. 72):
  - "Our July 4th promotion ends Monday at midnight, so if you want it, take action now."
  - "Our Black Friday promotion ends at midnight. There are only four hours left. Get it while the gettin's good."
  - "Through Friday, I'll also throw in a free hat to anyone who buys more than three books."
- Pro tip — Hormozi's most-used urgency tactic (p. 72): time-limit *bonuses*, not products/prices. Rotate bonuses weekly. "It's an *easy* way to make CTAs more effective *without capping sales*."

#### c) Fraternity Party Planner — "Make Up A Reason"
- Source: "$100M Leads, Step 7, p. 73"
- Citation: Harvard study (Langer's "because" experiment) — any reason beats no reason; reasons that make sense beat random reasons; but random reasons still beat nothing.
- Pattern: "[directive] **because** [reason]"
- Examples (p. 73):
  - "Because…moms know best."
  - "Because…your country needs you."
  - "Because…it's my birthday, and I want you to celebrate with me."

#### Worked Math (p. 74)
- Hormozi's lead-magnet-vs-direct-call CAC math:
  - Direct: $1,000 to get a call × 1/3 close rate = $3,000 CAC against $10,000 profit.
  - With a lead magnet: extra engagement drops cost-per-call from $1,000 to $75 + $25 magnet delivery = $100 per call. 1/10 closes → $1,000 CAC. "We just cut our cost to get a customer by 3x. … *we triple our business*."

## Section I Story / "How I Got Here" (case study + ethos)

The book opens with a narrative arc rather than a framework; it is positioned as social proof for the rest of the book. Cited beats:

- The 2017 disaster: $150,000 in refunds from two gym launches; one gym owner stood on a chair and told members to refund and go home; another owner offered customers half price if they refunded and paid him directly. (p. 14–15)
- A fraudulent partner emptied the company bank account during a launch, sent funds offshore, filed bankruptcy. "*when money meets experience... the money gets the experience, and the experience gets the money*." (p. 19)
- Recovery via "selling the secrets": Hormozi licensed the Gym Launch advertising system to gym owners for $6,000+ each. "*In a single day, I collected $60,000 selling something with zero cost to fulfill.*" (p. 27)
- 30-day result: $215,000 profit. Average Gym Launch client added "an extra $30,000 in cash in their first 30 days." (p. 28)
- Track record framing (Section I prologue, p. 11):
  - Acquisition.com portfolio: "$250,000,000+ in annual revenue" generating "20,000+ leads per day selling offers from $1 to $1,000,000+."
  - Personal lifetime ROAS: 36:1.
  - Net worth at 32: $100M+.

These are the credibility levers Hormozi uses; in our skill they belong as evidence in `case-studies` references, not as method.

## Section II Story (lead magnet origin — the Webinar → Case Study pivot)

A second-personal narrative, used as the case study for "lead magnets beat untested products."

- Setup (p. 35): Hormozi paid $25,000 to be in a mastermind. His mentor told him: "Do a webinar every week until you make a million dollars. Until then, don't ask me about anything else."
- Failure (p. 38): After 8 Sundays of building, he spent $450 on ads, got 80 leads, 0 sales. Nobody watched the webinar.
- Pivot (p. 39): saw an ad — "Free Case Study on How I Spent $1 and Made $123,000 in a Weekend." Recorded a 13-minute screen-share walkthrough of one gym's actual ad account, with new headline:
  - copy pattern: "FREE Case Study: How we added [N] members and [$revenue] in revenue to a [niche/location]"
  - actual headline (p. 39): "FREE Case Study: How we added 213 members and $112,000 in revenue to a small gym in San Diego."
- Result (p. 39–40): calendar booked solid overnight.
- Lesson (p. 40): "*They didn't want my webinar. But they did want my case study.* … you have to *give people something they want.*"
- Author note — webinars still work (p. 40): "Spend your time testing the offer rather than perfecting an untested product."

## Mistakes / Anti-Patterns

- **Skipping straight to "make money."** "Everyone tries to skip to the 'make money' part. It doesn't work." (p. 6)
- **Perfecting an untested product.** Webinar lesson (p. 40): "Spend your time testing the offer rather than perfecting an untested product."
- **Sucky fluff.** Step 6 (p. 66–67): If you give away weak free content, prospects who would have bought tell others not to. "It's a vicious cycle you don't want to ride."
- **Diluting lead magnet value to "save" the paid product.** Pro Tip "Be A Drug Dealer" (p. 55): "we don't 'dilute' the value of our lead magnets or give away sucky fluff."
- **Trusting partners with "poor financials."** "When money meets experience..." (p. 19) — the partner had a prior fraud indictment Hormozi already knew about.
- **One-format publishing.** $100M Offers data (p. 66): single-format would have lost 3–4x of the leads.
- **Negation in CTAs.** "Not 'don't delay' but instead 'call now.'" (p. 73)
- **Burying scarcity.** (p. 71): "Don't keep it a secret - advertise it. … If you can't handle more than five new customers per week, say so."
- **Cleverness over clarity in CTAs.** "Don't be clever, be clear." (p. 73)
- **Treating leads as a single thing.** Conflating "leads" with "engaged leads" — Section II's whole reason for existing (p. 41–43).

## Ethical Boundaries

- claim: Scarcity must be "ethical" — backed by an actual constraint.
  source: "$100M Leads, Step 7a, p. 71"

  source: "$100M Leads, Pro Tip Step 2, p. 55"
  quote: "PS - Whatever you do, make sure it's legal."

  source: "$100M Leads, Step 6 Action Step, p. 67"
  quote: "99% of people aren't gonna buy, but they will create (or destroy) your reputation based on the value of your free stuff."

  source: "$100M Leads, Step 4, p. 64"
  quote: "The point of this book is to get strangers to show interest, not to buy (yet). A raised hand is where this book ends."

## Section II Conclusion (Hormozi's own four-point summary)

A good lead magnet does four things (verbatim, p. 75–76):
1. Engages ideal customers when they see it.
2. Gets more people to engage than your core offer alone.
3. Is valuable enough that they consume it.
4. Makes the right people more likely to buy.
