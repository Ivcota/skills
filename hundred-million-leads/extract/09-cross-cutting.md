## Scoring Model

**There IS an explicit diagnostic / level model in the source.** It is the **"Seven Levels of Advertisers"** in the chapter "The Roadmap — Putting it All Together" (Section V).

- **Name:** Seven Levels of Advertisers (also called "the advertising totem pole")
- **Scale:** Levels 1 through 7, where Level 1 = "Your friends know about the stuff you sell" and Level 6 = "You hire killers" (battle-hardened executives running departments). Level 7 is intentionally left unwritten — Hormozi says he'll "come back and edit this chapter once I cross a billion."
- **Citation:** "$100M Leads, 'The Roadmap — Putting it All Together', pp. ~369–375"
- **Quote (purpose of the model):** "Acquisition.com uses this roadmap to scale our portfolio companies from a few million a year, all the way to $100,000,000+. These levels will help you identify where you are on the advertising totem pole so you know what to do to get to the next level."

**What each level means** (with primary action verbatim from text):

| Level | Headline | Primary Action (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Your friends know about the stuff you sell." | Warm outreach. |
| 2 | "You consistently let everyone you know about the stuff you sell." | "Do as much warm outreach and post as much content as you can consistently." |
| 3 | "You get employees to help you do more advertising." | "You hire people to advertise profitably on your behalf." |
| 4 | "Your product is good enough to get consistent referrals." | "Focus on your product until you get consistent referrals then go back to scaling your advertising with a bigger team." |
| 5 | "You advertise in more places in more ways with more people." | "Advertise profitably using at least two methods on multiple platforms." |
| 6 | "You hire killers." | "Get battle-hardened executives and department heads to take over new advertising activities and channels." |
| 7 | (unwritten) | "I'll come back and edit this chapter once I cross a billion." |

**Note on use as a diagnostic:** there is **no 1–10 numeric rating per asset** (e.g., no "rate your lead magnet 1-10"). The diagnostic is positional: identify your level on the totem pole, then execute the level's "Primary Action." A complementary diagnostic is the **"More Better New"** logic (Section III conclusion) — find the constraint, test until freed, do more until constrained again — quoted in 'A Decade in a Page', p. ~377, 4b: "Finding the constraint in our advertising. Then testing until it frees the constraint. Then doing more until it gets constrained again."

## Consolidated Mistakes

Hormozi does **not** present a single titled "list of mistakes" chapter. However, two passages in the back matter consolidate the recurring failure modes; per-section extracts should still own the granular mistakes.

**Consolidated failure modes Hormozi explicitly calls out (back matter):**

1. **Underestimating volume / stopping too soon.**
   source: "Open To Goal, p. ~363"
   quote: "Most people dramatically underestimate the volume it takes to make advertising work. They're not doing half as much or a third as much as is required. In fact, they're doing dramatically less. I was doing 1/1500th the level of effort required to make a flyer campaign work."

2. **Treating advertising as a one-shot rather than as inputs/outputs.**
   source: "Open To Goal, p. ~363"
   quote: "Most people do not get that advertising is an inputs and outputs game. To them, outputs appear out of their control. Their low effort inputs get them their low and unreliable output of engaged leads."

3. **Letting the product slip while scaling ads (Level 4 trap).**
   source: "Roadmap, Level 4, p. ~371"
   quote: "Focus on your product until you get consistent referrals then go back to scaling your advertising with a bigger team. **This is where most people mess up. They let their product slip and never recover.**"

4. **Hiring potential instead of experience to run a Core Four channel.**
   source: "Roadmap, Level 6 'Pro Tip', p. ~373"
   quote: "Pick experience. They should know more than you do. If you're not learning from them in the interview, you've got the wrong person."

5. **Quitting before the green roll (the "Many Sided Die" parable).**
   source: "A Decade in a Page → Many Sided Die, pp. ~382–384"
   quote: "If you quit, you lose. … You cannot lose if you do not quit."

6. **Reading instead of advertising.**
   source: "Conclusion of 'Open To Goal' chapter, p. ~366"
   quote: "Reading doesn't get people interested in the stuff you sell… advertising does. If you're not telling anyone about the stuff you sell, then you aren't getting anyone interested in the stuff you sell. Period."

7. **Writing a 100-page plan instead of a 1-page plan.**
   source: "Conclusion of 'Open To Goal' chapter, p. ~366"
   quote: "Many skip planning, or worse, they write a one hundred page plan that never gets used. So skip the atrocious waste of time that is writing pages of baloney. Harness the power of laying out your action steps on a single page."

For per-channel mistakes (Warm Outreach, Cold Outreach, Content, Paid Ads, Lead Magnets, Lead Getters), see the per-section extracts — those mistakes are distributed across chapters and are not consolidated into one back-matter list.
