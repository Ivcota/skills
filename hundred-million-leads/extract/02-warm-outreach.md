# Warm Outreach Extraction

Source: $100M Leads by Alex Hormozi (2023), Section III #1 — "Warm Outreach: How To Reach Out To People You Know." Chapter spans roughly PDF pages 78-110 (Post Free Content begins ~p. 110). Page numbers below refer to PDF page numbers in the file `100M Leads by Alex Hormozi.pdf`.

---

## Definition

- claim: "Warm reach outs" = one-to-one private contact with people who already know you (your warm audience).
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 86"
  quote: "Warm reach outs are when you make one-to-one contact with your warm audience - aka - the people who know you. It's the cheapest and easiest way to find people interested in the stuff you sell. It's super effective–and most businesses don't do it."

- claim: Warm reach outs take the form of calls, texts, emails, DMs, voicemails — and you advertise either a lead magnet or your core offer.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 86"
  quote: "Warm reach outs usually come in the form of calls, texts, emails, direct messages, voicemails, etc. And like we learned in Section II, you advertise one of two things. You let them know about your lead magnet (something free and valuable), or you let them know about your core offer (the main thing you sell)."

- claim: Warm Outreach is the "1-to-1 to a Warm Audience" cell of the Core Four.
  source: "$100M Leads, Section III intro, p. 76"
  quote: "1-to-1 to a Warm Audience = Warm Outreach"

## Mechanism / Why It Works

- claim: It is reliable because every message is personal — sacrificing volume for certainty.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 86"
  quote: "When you start doing warm reach outs, you don't get many engaged leads for your time. You do everything on your own and make each message personal. But, for that reason, it is reliable. As certain as the sun rises and sets, it works."

- claim: Everyone has a warm audience whether they realize it or not.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 86"
  quote: "Also, you do have a warm audience, even if you don't know it. Everybody knows somebody. So your personal contacts are the easiest place to start."

- claim: The system scales — same mechanics whether you have 100 contacts or a million.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 86"
  quote: "Reaching out to your warm audience works whether you have 100 contacts or 1,000,000. So as your business grows, you will use automation and employees to make it more efficient. The systems start small, with you, but they scale all the way up."

## When To Use

- claim: Warm reach outs are the best way to get your first five clients for any new product or service. Advanced operators use them for re-engagement and new product lines.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 87"
  quote: "Warm reach outs are a fantastic way to get your 'First Five Clients' For any new product or service. Advanced Folks: Think re-engagement and new product lines."

- claim: When you're starting out, warm outreach should be the majority of your time — at least four hours/day, first thing in the morning.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 109"
  quote: "When you're starting out, getting new customers should take the majority of your time. Think four hours per day, minimum. It should be the first thing you do when you get up. And you shouldn't stop until you achieve your goal."

- claim: Hormozi himself started with warm outreach when launching his fitness business in 2013, even though he didn't know the term.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 84"
  quote: "It's how you get leads. So even though I had no idea I was doing warm reach outs, one of the core four, it's how I got my first leads. I still get leads this way (just with bigger numbers)."

## Process / Steps — "How To Do Warm Reach Outs in 10 Steps"

Master list (verbatim).
- source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 87"
- quote: "Step 1: Get your list / Step 2: Pick a platform / Step 3: Personalize your message / Step 4: Reach out / Step 5: Warm them up / Step 6: Invite their friends / Step 7: Make them the easiest offer in the world / Step 8: Start at the top / Step 9: Start Charging / Step 10: Keep Your List Warm"

### Step 1: Get Your List ("Everyone Has A List")
- What: Compile contacts you already have from every channel — your phone, every email account ever used, and every social platform.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 88"
  quote: "Grab your phone. Inside you have contacts. Each contact has subscribed to communication from you. They have given you the means and permission to contact them. / Pull up all the email accounts you've used over the years. Pull your contacts and address list from each. Bingo! Look at all them leads. / Now, go to all your social media profiles. See your followers, subscribers, friends, connections, or whatever kids call 'em these days...eureka – you got more leads!"
- How: Add up the totals across all platforms; for many people this is 1,000+ contacts.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 88"
  quote: "Add up all your contacts from all the platforms. Seriously, figure out the number. Between your phone, email, social media, and other platforms you will have more than enough contacts to get started. For many of you, this will be your first 1000 leads."

### Step 2: Pick A Platform
- What: Pick the single platform where you have the most contacts and start there.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 89"
  quote: "Pick the platform you have the most contacts on. Phone, email, social media, mail, carrier pigeon, etc. It doesn't matter. Just pick the one with the most contacts. You'll hit 'em all eventually anyways."

### Step 3: Personalize Your Greeting
- What: Open with something specific you know about the contact; if you don't know much, do light research first.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 89"
  quote: "Use something you know about the contact as your actual reason to reach out. If you don't have much personal info, you can check their social media profiles etc. to learn a bit about them first."
- How: Frame it as social check-in, not pitch.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 89"
  quote: "Don't be a weirdo. Pay your social dues. Remember, you haven't asked for anything. You're just checking in and providing value. So…relax."
- Example given:
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 89"
  quote: "Ex: Saw you just had a baby! Congrats! How is the baby doing? How are you?"

### Step 4: Reach Out — 100 People Per Day
- What: Send personalized messages to 100 contacts every day, following up up to three times.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 89"
  quote: "Now, reach out to 100 of them per day with your personalized messages. You'll call, text, email, message, send a postcard, etc."
- How: Cadence is once per day for three days (or once per week for physical mail), or until they reply.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 90"
  quote: "And you will reach out to them up to three times. Once per day for three days* or until they respond. Whichever comes first. / *Once per week with physical mail."
- Insight ("Rip Off The Bandaid"): the first reach out is the hardest; subsequent reach outs take seconds.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 90"
  quote: "The first reach out is always the hardest and takes the longest. Your second reach out will take minutes. Your third, seconds. Be okay with sucking. It's new. This is how we learn. When thinking about starting new things, I remember this Chinese proverb - 'Everything must be hard before it can be easy'"

### Step 5: Warm Them Up — the A-C-A Framework
- What: When they reply, use Acknowledge → Compliment → Ask.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 90"
  quote: "Reply using the A-C-A framework: / Acknowledge what they said. Restate it in your own words. This shows active listening. / Compliment them on whatever they tell you. Tie it to a positive character trait if you can. / Ask another question. Lead the conversation in whatever direction you want. In this case, to a topic closer to your offer."
- Acknowledge example:
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 90"
  quote: "Ex: Two kids. And you're an accountant…"
- Compliment example:
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 91"
  quote: "Ex: …Wow! Supermom! So hardworking! Managing a full-time career and two kids..."
- Ask examples by niche:
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 91"
  quote: "Therapy/Life Coaching: …Do you get time for yourself? / Fitness/Weight Loss: ...Do you have time to get workouts in? / Cleaning Services: ...Do you have anyone who helps you keep the house tidy?"
- Why it works:
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 91"
  quote: "People love talking about themselves. So let them. They also love to be complimented, so do that too. And if people feel good when talking to you, they'll like and trust you more. You want people to like and trust you more."
- Email/voicemail variant: do it all at once with a 2–3 sentence personalized opener, then transition straight to the offer.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 92"
  quote: "On email you will have a personalized opener to show that you actually took time to research them in some way. Think 2-3 sentences. Then, you will transition directly to your offer or lead magnet which we talk about next. You sort of 'do it all at once' with email or voicemails."

### Step 6: Make Them An Offer (Invite Their Friends)
- What: After ~3-4 exchanges (or 3-4 minutes in person), make an offer that asks for a referral, not a purchase.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 92"
  quote: "Get through a 'normal' amount of conversation. Think 3-4 exchanges if on the phone or messaging and 3-4 minutes if in person. Then, you'll make them an offer to see if they're interested."
- How: Frame the offer using the Value Equation (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood / Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice).
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 92-93"
  quote: "When I make an offer from scratch, I refer to the value equation. […] 1) Dream Outcome […] 2) Perceived Likelihood of Achievement […] 3) Time Delay […] 4) Effort and Sacrifice […] The goal is to maximize the first two and minimize the second two."
- Insight: don't ask them to buy — ask if they know anyone who would.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 95"
  quote: "There's an important feature here. We're not asking them to buy anything. We're asking if they know anyone. And of the people who say yes, most say they are interested. This entire thing is engineered to boost their perceived likelihood of achievement."
- Why ask-for-referral works in 3 ways:
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 95"
  quote: "Some people will show interest in your stuff. Some will refer you to those who might. Some will do both. In all three outcomes, you win. And you win without pushing anything on anyone."

### Step 7: Make It Easy To Say Yes — Make It Free
- What: Use FREE as the easiest offer enhancer when launching.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 97"
  quote: "After people show interest, make your offer easy to say yes to. I like to start with the easiest offer enhancer in the world - FREE"
- How: Stay honest; set the trade in plain terms.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 97"
  quote: "And don't try to look advanced if you're not. People aren't dumb. Just be honest and keep it simple"
- Insight ("Stack 'Yeses' To Build Early Momentum"): low-pressure free yeses build the business.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 99"
  quote: "Early on, I felt terrified asking for money. So, if you recall from the story above, I told people I would work with them for free as long as they donated to a charity of their choice. I still got them invested in their results, but asking them for a feelgood tax write-off felt like a much safer way to do it. By the way, this was the first thing I ever sold. Looking back, I wanted easy low pressure YESES under my belt. And those early yeses built my first business."
- Why "first five free":
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 99"
  quote: "My recommendation - whenever you launch a new product or service - make the first five free. The exact number matters less than knowing why you benefit from it."
- Four reasons to give it free up front:
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 99"
  quote: "1. You get the reps in and become comfortable with making offers to people. […] 2. You probably suck (for now). People are far more forgiving when you haven't charged anything. 3. Because you probably suck, you need to learn how to suck less. You suck less by doing more. […] 4. If people get value, especially for free, they're far more likely to: a. Leave positive reviews and testimonials. b. Give you feedback. c. Send their friends and family."
- Three additional ways free customers make money:
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 100"
  quote: "1) They convert into paying customers. 2) They send you paying customers via referrals. 3) Their testimonials bring in paying customers."

### Step 8: Start Back At The Top
- What: After exhausting one platform, move to the platform with the next-most contacts; then the next; cycle indefinitely.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 104"
  quote: "After reaching out to all the leads on one platform, switch to the platform you have the second most leads on. After you reach out to those leads, go to the platform you have the third most leads on and so forth."
- How: A 1,000-contact list = ~10 days of work; a month including follow-ups should produce ≥5 free yeses.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 104-105"
  quote: "Let's say you follow this to the tee […] If between all platforms, you have 1000 leads, that gives you ten solid days of work. A month of work including follow-ups. By this point, I promise, five or more people will have accepted your free offer."

### Step 9: Start Charging
- What: When customers begin referring, that's the litmus test — start charging.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 105"
  quote: "This is your litmus test to know when you're 'good enough' to charge. Once people start referring, start charging. When that happens, swap out '… free…' in the script above to '80% off for the next five'. Then '60% off for the next five.' Then '40% off for the next five,' and so forth."
- Pricing rule: raise 20% every five customers; price hikes also create real urgency.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 105"
  quote: "The 'I increase my prices every five' rule also adds urgency because prices actually go up. […] Feel free to keep raising it by 20% every five until you find your sweet spot."
- Pro Tip ("Prepay + Guarantee"): only offer guarantees to people who pay up front.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 106"
  quote: "You can offer a guarantee only to people who pay up front. Reason why: People who invest up front are more committed. And as a result, we're able to guarantee their outcomes. So if you'd like our guarantee, you can prepay our service."
- Dr. Kashey wording variant:
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 106"
  quote: "After the person agrees to buy, you say 'would you rather pay less today or get all your money back?' Paying less today = payment plan, so less money down. Get all your money back = prepay and get a guarantee that you get the result you want."

### Step 10: Keep Your List Warm
- What: Give regular value via email/social to keep the list primed; periodically probe with a one-line ask.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 106"
  quote: "Give regular value to your list through email, social media, etc. to keep it warm. A warm list stays primed for your warm reach outs in the future."
- How: Use Dean Jackson's "9-word email" template.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 106-107"
  quote: "Once you've given value for a while, or see who wants value, probe your list with Dean Jackson's timeless '9-word email' template': / Are you still looking to [4 word desire]? / No images. No frills. No links. Just a question. Nothing else. This message is money for getting leads to engage."
- Why a warm list compounds:
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 108"
  quote: "The main point is that a warm list is a huge asset because it's a consistent and growing source of engaged leads. If you treat them well, your audience will feed you forever."

## Cases / Examples

- case: Hormozi's first business (May 2013, Impetus Group LLC → "The Free Training Project") — fitness coaching launched purely with warm outreach to phone/text/Facebook contacts.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 81"
  quote: "But I had a problem - I didn't know anything about advertising or sales. But I did know I needed clients. So, I just asked around where I could. I called, texted, and sent Facebook messages to a bunch of people I knew."

- case outcome: 6 yeses out of all the people he contacted (2 high-school friends, 1 college friend, 3 referrals).
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 82"
  quote: "Only six people said yes. Six. Two high school friends. One college friend. And three people they referred."

- case outcome: After 12 weeks, those free-for-charity clients agreed to start paying him; referrals brought 5–6 more paying clients; the business cleared ~$4,000/mo and replaced his job income.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 84"
  quote: "after twelve weeks of the 'pay a charity period,' I asked them to pay me instead. […] Once they got results, I asked them to send their friends over. To my surprise, I got another five or six clients from their referrals. I asked the referrals to pay me directly. Again, none of them minded. That little business made about $4000 per month and replaced the income from my first job."

- case (illustration in Pro Tip): Advertising agency plays a recording of a real sales call to demonstrate the outcome live for prospects.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 96"
  quote: "Ex: Advertising agency plays a recording of a call that a gym owner has to make to a lead on the sales call. 'Could you handle making a call like that to a lead if we get them for you?' It demonstrates the outcome of the advertising services - people don't want 'leads' they want customers. They just don't know a better way to ask for them."

- case (analogy): Warren Buffett offered to work for Benjamin Graham for free; Graham initially refused ("you're overpriced") because the hidden cost was his training time — used to underline that early customers train you for free.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 102"
  quote: "Before Warren Buffet became the greatest investor of our time, he offered to work for his hero, Ben Graham, for free. Wanna know Graham's response? 'You're overpriced.' Graham knew what was up. The most expensive thing about hiring Buffett wasn't a paycheck, but the time to train him. […] PS - Buffet still managed to get Graham to take his free offer. The rest is history."

## Mistakes

- mistake: Underestimating that you have a list at all.
  why fails: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 88" — quote: "'I don't have any leads.' Psh. Just found some."
  fix: Compile contacts from phone + every email account + every social platform.
  citation: $100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 88

- mistake: Trying to look "advanced" / overselling early when you have nothing to back it up.
  why fails: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 97" — quote: "And don't try to look advanced if you're not. People aren't dumb. Just be honest and keep it simple"
  fix: Make the first five free, openly stating it's because you want feedback/testimonials.
  citation: $100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 97

- mistake: Being a "weirdo" — opening with the pitch instead of paying social dues.
  why fails: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 89" — quote: "Don't be a weirdo. Pay your social dues. Remember, you haven't asked for anything. You're just checking in and providing value."
  fix: Personalize the opener using something specific about them; start with the ACA framework when they reply.
  citation: $100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 89

- mistake: Asking them to buy instead of asking if they know anyone.
  why fails: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 95" — quote: "We're not asking them to buy anything. We're asking if they know anyone. […] Since you didn't ask them to buy anything, you don't come off as pushy."
  fix: Use the "Do you know anybody who…" referral template.
  citation: $100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 94-95

- mistake: Expecting comfort/perfection on the first reach out.
  why fails: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 90" — quote: "Be okay with sucking. It's new. This is how we learn."
  fix: Rip off the bandaid; expect to suck; reps make subsequent reach outs take seconds.
  citation: $100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 90

- mistake: Refusing to work for free because someone says you're "undervaluing yourself."
  why fails: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 103" — quote: "If anyone tells you not to 'undervalue yourself' by giving your services away for free in the beginning, tell them to hush. Sure, you're a special snowflake. But the stuff you sell isn't valuable yet. You've barely started. The objective right now is to learn not to earn."
  fix: Treat early reps as paid education; learn first, earn second.
  citation: $100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 103

- mistake: Treating "no" as the end of the conversation instead of intel.
  why fails: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 101" — quote: "Their answers give you a chance to solve their problem. […] even if they don't buy from you, they'll give you the ammo to get the next person to."
  fix: Ask "What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?" to surface hidden costs.
  citation: $100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 101

- mistake: Stalling on price increases after you start charging.
  why fails: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 105" — quote: "The 'I increase my prices every five' rule also adds urgency because prices actually go up."
  fix: Raise prices 20% every five customers as soon as referrals start.
  citation: $100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 105

- mistake: Letting the warm list go cold between asks.
  why fails: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 106-108" — quote: "Give regular value to your list through email, social media, etc. to keep it warm. A warm list stays primed for your warm reach outs in the future."
  fix: Provide regular value; probe periodically with the 9-word email.
  citation: $100M Leads, Warm Outreach, pp. 106-108

## Ethical Boundaries

- rule: Don't lie or exaggerate when constructing the offer.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 94"
  quote: "We gotta get as close to that as we can without lying or exaggerating."

- rule: Don't be pushy — design the ask so you win without pushing anything on anyone.
  source: "$100M Leads, Warm Outreach, p. 95"
  quote: "Since you didn't ask them to buy anything, you don't come off as pushy. […] In all three outcomes, you win. And you win without pushing anything on anyone."
