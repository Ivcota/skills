# Lead Magnets — The Seven-Step Framework

Expansion of [SKILL.md §1 "Engage Your Leads — Lead Magnets"](../SKILL.md). Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads* (Acquisition.com, 2023), Section II ("Get Understanding"), pp. 41–76. Page numbers below are PDF pages from the source.

The book ends at the raised hand — *"a raised hand is where this book ends"* (p. 64). Everything in this reference is about producing one of those raised hands as cheaply as possible.

---

## 1. Definitions

Five terms carry the chapter. Hormozi defines each in one line; everything downstream is mechanics.

**Lead** — *"a person you can contact. That's all. … If you can contact them, they are leads"* (p. 42). No qualification, no interest, no buying intent. Just a contactable human.

**Engaged Lead** — *"people who *show* interest in the stuff you sell"* (p. 42). The actual output of advertising. Hormozi's three example signals (p. 43): they gave you contact info on a website, they followed you on social and you can DM them, they replied to your email campaign. Raw leads are not engaged leads, and the conflation is the entire reason Section II exists.

**Offer (Core Offer)** — *"what you promise to give in exchange for something of value. Often, a business promises to give its product or service in exchange for money. This is a *core offer*"* (p. 49).

**Lead Magnet** — *"a complete solution to a narrow problem. It's typically a lower-cost or free offer to see who is interested in your stuff. And, once solved, it reveals another problem *solved by your core offer*"* (p. 49). Three load-bearing words: *complete*, *narrow*, *reveals*.

**Call to Action (CTA)** — *"tells the audience what to do next. … Good CTAs have two things: 1) what to do and 2) reasons to do it *right now*"* (p. 71).

## 2. Core Offer vs. Lead Magnet — When to Use Which

Hormozi gives a two-path frame. Don't default to a lead magnet.

**Default rule (p. 49):** *"If you advertise your core offer, then you go straight for the sale — the direct path to money. … Try this way first."*

**When to lead with a magnet (p. 49):** *"Sometimes, though, people want to know more about your offer before they buy. This is common for businesses that sell more expensive stuff."* High-trust, high-price, high-consideration purchases need a magnet. A $19 ebook does not.

**The principle behind the rule (p. 50):** *"A person who pays with their time now is more likely to pay with their money later."* Even a free magnet has a price — the lead's time. *"If they think your lead magnet is worth their time, they'll think your core offer is worth their money"* (p. 50).

**The CAC math from p. 74.** This is the only quantitative argument Hormozi makes for adding a magnet:

| Path | Cost per call | Close rate | CAC | At $10k profit/customer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct (no magnet)** | $1,000 | 1 in 3 | $3,000 | 3.3:1 |
| **With lead magnet** | $75 ad + $25 magnet delivery = $100 | 1 in 10 | $1,000 | 10:1 |

*"We just cut our cost to get a customer by 3x. … We triple our business"* (p. 74). Note the close rate *drops* — magnet leads are colder per unit. The win comes from getting 10x the calls at 1/10 the cost.

**Bridge to $100M Offers (p. 51):** *"Grand Slam Offers work for free stuff as much or better than they do for paid stuff. So make your lead magnet so insanely good people will feel stupid saying no."* Score the magnet on the Value Equation just like a paid offer.

## 3. The Seven Steps

Hormozi lists the seven on p. 51 and restates them at the end of the chapter on p. 76 with slightly tweaked wording. Use the p. 76 wording as the cleaner spec.

### Step 1 — Figure out the problem (Problem-Solution Cycle)

The trap is picking a problem whose solution doesn't lead anywhere you can sell. Hormozi's framing (p. 52): *"Every problem has a solution. Every solution reveals more problems."* That cycle is the whole job.

**The decision rule (p. 53):** *"if we can solve that new problem with our core offer, we've got a winner."*

**Worked example — homeowner selling a house (p. 53).** The broad goal is "sell my home." The narrow lead-magnet problems Hormozi brainstorms: home valuation, value-increase guidance, listing photos, cleaning, landscaping, repairs, moving, staging. Any of these, solved, reveals the next problem — and "sell my home" (the agent's core offer) is the final box.

**Action Step (p. 53):** *"Pick the narrowly defined problem you want to solve. Then, make sure your core offer can solve the next problem that comes up."*

If you can't draw an arrow from the magnet's exit problem to the core offer's entry problem, pick a different magnet.

### Step 2 — Figure out how to solve it (Three Magnet Types)

Hormozi names exactly three (pp. 53–56). They are not interchangeable; pick by problem geometry.

**Type 1 — Reveal Their Problem (diagnosis).** Best when *"problems get worse the longer you wait"* (p. 54). The magnet quantifies a hidden cost the prospect didn't see.

- Website-speed test that quantifies dollars lost (p. 54)
- Posture analysis vs. a pain-free baseline
- Termite inspection that reveals damage cost vs. prevention cost

**Type 2 — Samples and Trials.** *"You give full but brief access to your core offer"* (p. 55).

- Faster-server demo
- Free posture adjustment
- Costco "fun-sized" consumables — *"It's how Costco sells more food than other stores — they give out samples!"* (p. 55)

**Type 3 — One Step Of A Multi-Step Process.** Used when the core offer is complex enough that you can't sample it without context (p. 56).

- *$100M Leads* itself (one step toward $1M+ profit)
- Free first coat of garage-door sealant out of three required coats
- Free finance calculators / templates that reveal the labor problem solved by the financial-services core offer

**Pro Tip — "Be A Drug Dealer" (p. 55–56):** *"like a drug dealer, you'd wanna give the strongest 'hit' *first*."* Don't dilute. The drug-dealer parenthetical: *"PS — Whatever you do, make sure it's legal."*

**Author note — Dressing Rooms (p. 57):** the dressing room is *"all three types of lead magnet in one"* — trial + reveal-problem + step-1-of-multi-step (the outfit). Hormozi's recommendation: *"if you can, try and get a lead magnet that does all three."*

### Step 3 — Figure out how to deliver it (Four Delivery Mechanisms)

Three magnet types × four delivery mechanisms = up to twelve magnets per problem (p. 59).

| Mechanism | Verb (Hormozi's) | Gym-owner example (p. 58–59) |
|---|---|---|
| **Software** | *"You give them a tool."* | Spreadsheet/dashboard benchmarking gym KPIs vs. industry averages and ranking the gym |
| **Information** | *"You teach them something."* | Mini-course on writing a gym ad |
| **Services** | *"You do work for free."* | Run a gym owner's ads free for 30 days |
| **Physical Product** | *"You give them something they can hold in their hands."* | A book — *Gym Launch Secrets* |

**Action Step (p. 59):** *"As a thought exercise, think of a lead magnet and then a version of it for each delivery method. You always can, I promise. Then, pick how to deliver *your* lead magnet."*

The combinatorial point matters because most operators get stuck at "we tried a PDF, it didn't work." A PDF is one of twelve options.

### Step 4 — Test what to name it

Headline first. Hormozi paraphrases Ogilvy on p. 59 — the headline is roughly 80% of the ad's effect.

**Three things to test, in order (p. 60):** **headline → image(s) → subheadline.** *"If you only test one thing, test that [headline]."*

**The book-title A/B test (pp. 60–62)** — Hormozi tested his own book's title with Instagram polls:

| Round | Option A | Option B | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | $100M **Advertising** (69%) | $100M Promotion (31%) | Advertising |
| II | $100M Advertising (42%) | $100M **Leads** (58%) | Leads |
| III | $100M Marketing (46%) | $100M **Leads** (54%) | Leads |

Then one image test (real cover vs. cartoon cover) and four subheadline rounds. Winning subheadline: *"How to get strangers to want to buy your stuff."*

**The "how to" lesson (p. 64):** *"The only difference is two little words: 'how to.' … Small changes can make big differences."*

**Why he didn't title it "buy your stuff" (p. 64):** *"The point of this book is to get strangers to show interest, not to buy (yet). A raised hand is where this book ends."* The magnet's job is not to close.

**Action Step (p. 64):** *"Test. If people engage in droves, you've got a winner."*

**Cheap-test tactic (p. 64):** if you have any following, run polls. If not, post on every platform and ask people to reply "1" or "2".

### Step 5 — Make it easy to consume

*"You can see 2x, 3x, and even 4x+ increases in take rates *and* consumption simply by making it easier to consume"* (p. 65).

Per-mechanism rules (p. 65):

- **Software** — phone + computer + multiple formats
- **Information** — video, text, audio, images; *"offer all"*
- **Services** — more times of day, more days of the week, more channels (video / phone / in person)
- **Physical products** — simple ordering, fast delivery, simple instructions; Apple as exemplar

**The $100M Offers data point (p. 66).** Near-perfect ¼/¼/¼/¼ split between ebook, physical book, audiobook, and video. Single-format publishing would have lost 3–4x of the leads for the same work.

### Step 6 — Make it darn good

Subtitle: *"Give Away The Secrets, Sell The Implementation"* (p. 66).

**The principle (p. 66):** *"you can never provide too much value. But, you *can* provide too little. So you want your lead magnet to provide so much value people feel obligated to pay you. The goal is to provide more value than the cost of your core offer *before they've bought it*."*

**The mechanism (p. 67):** *"people buy stuff based on how much value they think they'll get *after* they buy it. And the easiest way to get them to think they'll get tons of value after they buy is… drum roll please… to provide them with value *before* they buy."*

**Anti-pattern — "sucky fluff" (p. 66–67):** weak free content destroys reputation. People who would have bought tell others not to. *"It's a vicious cycle you don't want to ride."*

**Action Step (p. 67):** *"99% of people aren't gonna buy, but they will create (or destroy) your reputation based on the value of your free stuff."*

The implementation-not-secrets framing answers the perpetual objection: *"if I give it away, why would they pay?"* Hormozi's answer: because executing it themselves is harder than paying you to do it. The secret is the easy part.

### Step 7 — CTA: what to do + reason to do it now

A CTA = (1) what to do + (2) reasons to do it *right now* (p. 71). The "right now" is where most CTAs fail.

**Construction rule (p. 73):** *"Don't be clever, be clear."* And: *"Not 'don't delay' but instead 'call now.'"* Positive imperatives, not negations.

Hormozi's three favorite reasons-to-act-now:

#### 7a. Scarcity

*"Scarcity *is when there is a limited amount of something*"* (p. 71).

**Best practice — ethical scarcity (p. 71):** *"the best strategy I know for scarcity is — *reality*. … This gives you *ethical* scarcity. If you can't handle more than five new customers per week, *say so*."* Don't keep the constraint a secret — advertise it.

Copy patterns (p. 71):
- *"The most convenient class times fill up fast. Call now to get the one you want."*
- *"I can only handle five people per week, so if you want it solved soon, do xyz…"*
- *"We only printed one batch of shirts and will never reprint this design, get one so you don't regret missing out forever…"*

#### 7b. Urgency

*"Urgency *is when people act faster because they have a short amount of time*"* (p. 72).

Three flavors: hard deadline, hard deadline + bonus, bonus that decays over time.

Copy patterns (p. 72):
- *"Our July 4th promotion ends Monday at midnight, so if you want it, take action now."*
- *"Our Black Friday promotion ends at midnight. There are only four hours left. Get it while the gettin's good."*
- *"Through Friday, I'll also throw in a free hat to anyone who buys more than three books."*

**Hormozi's most-used urgency tactic (p. 72):** time-limit *bonuses*, not products or prices. Rotate bonuses weekly. *"It's an *easy* way to make CTAs more effective *without capping sales*."* Capping the product caps revenue; capping a bonus doesn't.

#### 7c. "Make Up A Reason" (Fraternity Party Planner)

Hormozi cites the Harvard "because" experiment (Langer): any reason beats no reason; reasons that make sense beat random reasons; but random reasons still beat nothing.

Pattern: *"[directive] **because** [reason]."*

Examples (p. 73):
- *"Because…moms know best."*
- *"Because…your country needs you."*
- *"Because…it's my birthday, and I want you to celebrate with me."*

## 4. Worked Example — Webinar → Case Study Pivot (pp. 38–40)

The book's own origin story for lead-magnet design.

**Setup (p. 35):** Hormozi paid $25,000 to be in a mastermind. His mentor's directive: *"Do a webinar every week until you make a million dollars. Until then, don't ask me about anything else."*

**Failure (p. 38):** After eight Sundays of building, $450 in ads, 80 leads, **0 sales**. Nobody watched the webinar.

**Pivot (p. 39):** Hormozi saw a competitor's ad — *"Free Case Study on How I Spent $1 and Made $123,000 in a Weekend."* He recorded a 13-minute screen-share walkthrough of one gym's actual ad account. New headline:

> *"FREE Case Study: How we added 213 members and $112,000 in revenue to a small gym in San Diego."*

**Result (p. 39–40):** calendar booked solid overnight.

**Lesson (p. 40):** *"They didn't want my webinar. But they did want my case study. … you have to *give people something they want*."*

**Author note — webinars still work (p. 40):** *"Spend your time testing the offer rather than perfecting an untested product."* The webinar wasn't broken; the magnet wrapper was.

**Reusable template (p. 39):**

> *"FREE Case Study: How we added [Quantified Outcome] in [revenue/results] to a [Specific Avatar in Specific Place]."*

## 5. What a Good Lead Magnet Does — Hormozi's Four-Point Summary (pp. 75–76)

A good lead magnet does four things, verbatim:

1. **Engages ideal customers when they see it.**
2. **Gets more people to engage than your core offer alone.**
3. **Is valuable enough that they consume it.**
4. **Makes the right people more likely to buy.**

Use this as the audit rubric. If a magnet fails any of the four, fix that step before testing more variants. (1) is Step 4 — naming/headline. (2) is Steps 2–3 — type and delivery. (3) is Steps 5–6 — consumability and quality. (4) is Step 1 — Problem-Solution Cycle.

## 6. Common Mistakes

Drawn directly from the source extract, pp. 6–73:

1. **Skipping straight to "make money"** (p. 6) — *"Everyone tries to skip to the 'make money' part. It doesn't work."*
2. **Perfecting an untested product** (p. 40) — *"Spend your time testing the offer rather than perfecting an untested product."*
3. **Sucky fluff** (p. 66–67) — weak free content destroys reputation. *"It's a vicious cycle you don't want to ride."*
4. **Diluting the magnet to "save" the paid product** (p. 55) — *"we don't 'dilute' the value of our lead magnets or give away sucky fluff."* Strongest hit first.
5. **One-format publishing** (p. 66) — single-format would have cost Hormozi 3–4x of *$100M Offers'* leads.
6. **Negation in CTAs** (p. 73) — *"Not 'don't delay' but instead 'call now.'"*
7. **Burying scarcity** (p. 71) — *"Don't keep it a secret — advertise it. … If you can't handle more than five new customers per week, say so."*
8. **Cleverness over clarity in CTAs** (p. 73) — *"Don't be clever, be clear."*
9. **Conflating leads with engaged leads** (pp. 41–43) — Section II's whole reason for existing.

## 7. Ethical Guardrails

Hormozi explicitly delimits the techniques in this chapter to real constraints and real value.

- **Scarcity must be backed by an actual constraint** (p. 71). *"This gives you *ethical* scarcity. If you can't handle more than five new customers per week, say so."* Manufactured scarcity is dishonest scarcity.
- **The magnet must actually deliver** (p. 67). *"99% of people aren't gonna buy, but they will create (or destroy) your reputation based on the value of your free stuff."*
- **The book stops at the raised hand** (p. 64). *"The point of this book is to get strangers to show interest, not to buy (yet)."* A magnet that crosses the line into pressure selling violates the design intent.
- **Be A Drug Dealer — but legally** (p. 55). The strongest-hit-first heuristic comes with the parenthetical *"PS — Whatever you do, make sure it's legal."*
