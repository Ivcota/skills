# Warm Outreach — The 10-Step Process

> Reference deep-dive for Section 2 of the parent skill. Source: *$100M Leads* by Alex Hormozi (Acquisition.com, 2023), Section III #1, "Warm Outreach: How To Reach Out To People You Know" (pp. 78–110). All page references are to the PDF edition.

## What Warm Outreach Is

Warm outreach is **one-to-one private contact with people who already know you** — your warm audience. It is the "1-to-1 to a Warm Audience" cell of the Core Four (p. 76). The medium is whatever you have: calls, texts, emails, DMs, voicemails, postcards (p. 86). What you advertise is one of two things: a **lead magnet** (something free and valuable) or your **core offer** (the main thing you sell).

Hormozi's claim for it: *"It's the cheapest and easiest way to find people interested in the stuff you sell. It's super effective — and most businesses don't do it"* (p. 86). Each message is personal, so volume is sacrificed for certainty. *"As certain as the sun rises and sets, it works"* (p. 86).

## When To Use It

Two clean use cases (p. 87):

1. **First-five-clients launch.** *"Warm reach outs are a fantastic way to get your 'First Five Clients' for any new product or service."* This is where every new operator should start. Hormozi himself launched his fitness business this way in 2013, before he had a name for it (p. 84).
2. **Advanced operators: re-engagement and new product lines.** When you already have a list, warm outreach is the cheapest way to wake it up (Step 10) or to seed a new product into an existing audience.

When you are starting out, this should be the **majority of your time** — *"Think four hours per day, minimum. It should be the first thing you do when you get up. And you shouldn't stop until you achieve your goal"* (p. 109).

## The 10 Steps

### Step 1 — Get Your List ("Everyone Has A List")

You already have a list; you just haven't compiled it. Pull contacts from three sources: your phone, every email account you have ever used, and every social platform (followers, friends, connections, subscribers). Add them all up. *"Between your phone, email, social media, and other platforms you will have more than enough contacts to get started. For many of you, this will be your first 1000 leads"* (p. 88). The reflex *"I don't have any leads"* gets dispatched here — *"Psh. Just found some"* (p. 88).

### Step 2 — Pick A Platform

Pick the **single platform where you have the most contacts** and start there (p. 89). Phone, email, social, mail, carrier pigeon — *"It doesn't matter. Just pick the one with the most contacts. You'll hit 'em all eventually anyways."* This is a focus mechanic, not a strategic preference.

### Step 3 — Personalize Your Greeting

Open with something specific you know about the contact. If you don't have personal context, do light research on their profile first. The frame is **social check-in, not pitch**: *"Don't be a weirdo. Pay your social dues. Remember, you haven't asked for anything. You're just checking in and providing value. So… relax"* (p. 89). The opener is reason-to-reach-out, not a sales hook. Example: *"Saw you just had a baby! Congrats! How is the baby doing? How are you?"* (p. 89).

### Step 4 — Reach Out (100 Per Day)

Send personalized messages to **100 people per day** (p. 89). Channels: call, text, email, DM, postcard. Follow up **up to three times**: once per day for three days, or once per week for physical mail, *"or until they respond. Whichever comes first"* (p. 90). The first reach out is the hardest and slowest; by the third it takes seconds. *"Be okay with sucking. It's new. This is how we learn"* (p. 90). Hormozi's "Rip Off The Bandaid" insight: the discomfort is front-loaded; volume cures it.

### Step 5 — Warm Them Up (the A-C-A Framework)

When a contact replies on a real-time channel (phone, text, DM), use **Acknowledge → Compliment → Ask** (pp. 90–91):

- **Acknowledge** what they said. Restate it in your own words. This signals active listening. *"Two kids. And you're an accountant…"*
- **Compliment** them on something they revealed. Tie it to a positive character trait. *"…Wow! Supermom! So hardworking! Managing a full-time career and two kids…"*
- **Ask** a question that lifts the conversation toward your offer. By niche (p. 91): therapy/life coaching → *"Do you get time for yourself?"*; fitness → *"Do you have time to get workouts in?"*; cleaning services → *"Do you have anyone who helps you keep the house tidy?"*

Why ACA works: *"People love talking about themselves. So let them. They also love to be complimented, so do that too. And if people feel good when talking to you, they'll like and trust you more"* (p. 91).

For email and voicemail, you compress ACA into a single message: a 2–3 sentence personalized opener that proves you researched them, then a direct transition into the offer. *"You sort of 'do it all at once'"* (p. 92).

### Step 6 — Make Them An Offer (the "Do You Know Anybody" Pivot)

After **3–4 exchanges (or 3–4 minutes in person)**, make an offer (p. 92). The signature mental shift: **don't ask them to buy — ask if they know anyone who would.** *"We're not asking them to buy anything. We're asking if they know anyone. And of the people who say yes, most say they are interested. This entire thing is engineered to boost their perceived likelihood of achievement"* (p. 95).

Why the referral framing wins on every branch (p. 95):
1. Some people show interest themselves.
2. Some refer you to people who might.
3. Some do both.
4. *"In all three outcomes, you win. And you win without pushing anything on anyone."*

Build the offer body with the **Value Equation** (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood ÷ Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice) — maximize the first two, minimize the second two (pp. 92–93).

### Step 7 — Make It Free (First Five Free)

Once a contact shows interest, *"make your offer easy to say yes to. I like to start with the easiest offer enhancer in the world — FREE"* (p. 97). The recommendation: **make the first five customers free** (p. 99). The exact number matters less than the four reasons it works (p. 99):

1. **You get reps.** You become comfortable making offers.
2. **You probably suck (for now).** People are far more forgiving when you haven't charged.
3. **Because you suck, you need to learn how to suck less.** You learn by doing more.
4. **Free customers compound.** They leave reviews, give feedback, and refer.

Three additional ways free customers make money (p. 100): they convert into paying customers, they refer paying customers, and their testimonials sell future buyers.

Stay honest: *"Don't try to look advanced if you're not. People aren't dumb. Just be honest and keep it simple"* (p. 97). And don't accept *"don't undervalue yourself"* lectures: *"The objective right now is to learn not to earn"* (p. 103).

### Step 8 — Start Back At The Top (Rotate Platforms)

After exhausting one platform, switch to the platform with the **second-most contacts**, then the third, and so on (p. 104). A 1,000-contact list = roughly **10 days of solid work**, or about a month including follow-ups. *"By this point, I promise, five or more people will have accepted your free offer"* (pp. 104–105).

### Step 9 — Start Charging (20% Every Five)

The litmus test for "good enough to charge" is **referrals**. *"Once people start referring, start charging"* (p. 105). Swap the script's *"…free…"* for *"80% off for the next five,"* then *"60% off for the next five,"* then *"40% off,"* and so on. **Raise prices 20% every five customers** until you find the sweet spot. The price-laddering also creates **real urgency** because prices actually go up (p. 105).

**Pro Tip — Prepay-with-Guarantee.** Offer the guarantee *only* to people who pay up front. *"People who invest up front are more committed. And as a result, we're able to guarantee their outcomes. So if you'd like our guarantee, you can prepay our service"* (p. 106). Dr. Kashey's wording variant after they agree to buy: *"Would you rather pay less today or get all your money back?"* — pay less today = payment plan; get money back = prepay + guaranteed result (p. 106).

### Step 10 — Keep Your List Warm

Give regular value via email and social to keep the list primed for future warm reach outs. Periodically probe with **Dean Jackson's 9-word email** (pp. 106–107): *"Are you still looking to [4-word desire]?"* — no images, no frills, no links, just the question. Examples: *"…buy your dream home?"* / *"…tone up your arms?"* / *"…open an online store?"* / *"…start a YouTube channel?"* The asset compounds: *"a warm list is a huge asset because it's a consistent and growing source of engaged leads. If you treat them well, your audience will feed you forever"* (p. 108).

## Volume Math

The benchmark conversion sequence (p. 109):

- **1 in 5** of 100 reach outs reply → ~20 replies
- **1 in 5** of those replies takes the free offer → ~4 free customers
- **1 in 4** of those free customers later converts to paid → **~1 paying customer per 100 reach outs (~1%)**

The income math (p. 109): 500 reach outs/week × ~1% conversion × $400 product = 5 customers/week × $400 = $2,000/wk × 52 = **$104,000/yr from warm outreach alone**. *"This process alone can take you to $100,000+ per year with nothing else. Wild, I know."*

## The Daily Checklist (p. 108)

| Field | Default |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Yourself |
| **What** | First five free |
| **Where** | Phone / Email / Physical Mail / SMS / etc. |
| **To Whom** | Your contacts |
| **When** | First four hours of your day |
| **Why** | You want to get customers or intros |
| **How** | Personalized message using ACA |
| **How Much** | 100 attempts per day |
| **How Many** | Follow up two more times after the first |
| **How Long** | Until you get customers |

## Representative Scripts

**Personalized opener (Step 3, p. 89):**
> *"Saw you just had a baby! Congrats! How is the baby doing? How are you?"*

**Master "Do you know anybody" referral ask (Step 6, p. 94):**
> *"By the way, do you know anybody who is [struggle] looking to [dream outcome] in [time]? I'm taking on five case studies for free, because that's all I can handle."*

**Honest first-five-free pitch (Step 7, p. 97):**
> *"Since I'm only taking on five people, I can give you all the attention you need to get brag-worthy results. And I'll give it all for free so long as you promise to: 1) Use it 2) Give me feedback 3) Leave a killer review if you think it deserves one."*

**Handle a "no" (p. 101):**
> *"What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?"*

**Hormozi's original 2013 fitness reach out (p. 81) — the one that started the business:**
> *"Hey, do you know anyone who's trying to get into shape? I'm training people for free for twelve weeks. On top of that, I'll make them a custom nutrition plan and grocery list. All they have to do is donate to a charity of their choice and let me use their testimonial."*

## Common Mistakes

1. **Believing you don't have a list.** *"'I don't have any leads.' Psh. Just found some"* (p. 88). Compile contacts from phone + every email account + every social platform; most people have 1,000+ contacts they didn't know they had.
2. **Trying to look "advanced" when you aren't.** *"People aren't dumb. Just be honest and keep it simple"* (p. 97). Make the first five free, openly stating it's because you want feedback and testimonials.
3. **Being a weirdo (opening with the pitch).** *"Don't be a weirdo. Pay your social dues. Remember, you haven't asked for anything"* (p. 89). Personalize the opener; let ACA do the warm-up work.
4. **Asking them to buy instead of asking who they know.** *"Since you didn't ask them to buy anything, you don't come off as pushy"* (p. 95). Use the *"Do you know anybody who…"* pivot.
5. **Expecting comfort on the first reach out.** *"Be okay with sucking. It's new. This is how we learn"* (p. 90). Reps make subsequent reach outs take seconds.
6. **Treating "no" as the end of the conversation.** *"Even if they don't buy from you, they'll give you the ammo to get the next person to"* (p. 101). Probe with *"What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?"*
7. **Stalling on price increases.** Once referrals start, raise 20% every five customers. The ladder also creates real urgency *because prices actually go up* (p. 105).
8. **Letting the warm list go cold between asks.** Give regular value; probe periodically with the 9-word email (pp. 106–108).

## Ethical Guardrails

- **Don't lie or exaggerate when constructing the offer.** *"We gotta get as close to that as we can without lying or exaggerating"* (p. 94).
- **Don't be pushy.** Design the ask so you win without pushing anything on anyone. The referral pivot is the structural protection (p. 95).
- **Be honest about your stage.** *"Don't try to look advanced if you're not"* (p. 97). The "first five free" framing is itself an honesty mechanism.
- **Set reasonable expectations up front.** When offering free work, state the trade plainly: use it, give feedback, leave a review (p. 97).
- **Don't push the legal envelope.** *"There are lots of people who pride themselves on pushing the legal envelope. Don't be that person. It always comes back to bite you. Do it the right way and you will feed yourself for life"* (p. 110).
- **Respect the consent boundary.** Your contacts subscribed to communication from you — *"They have given you the means and permission to contact them"* (p. 88). That is the line. Don't extend the list by harvesting strangers; that's cold outreach territory.
