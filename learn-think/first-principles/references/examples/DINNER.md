# Golden Example: Choosing Dinner Before a Movie

This example works through every named step. Each **Gate Not Yet** is a plausible mistake; each **Gate Pass** demonstrates the required standard.

The Inquiry requires two cycles:

1. The first produces an investigative Next Move.
2. New Evidence updates the Working Model.
3. The second produces the restaurant decision.

# Phase One: Ground

## Step 1: Bound the Inquiry

### Input

Four friends are standing near a movie theater at 5:45 PM and need to choose somewhere to eat.

### Gate Not Yet

> “Where should we eat?”

### Why it does not pass

The question does not establish:

- Who must be served
- What an acceptable outcome requires
- How much time or money is available
- What is at stake
- When the decision must be made

Almost any restaurant could be proposed because the Inquiry has no meaningful boundary.

### Work performed

The group makes the relevant conditions explicit:

- **Objective:** Select and secure a restaurant where all four friends can eat before the movie.
- **Scope:** Begin with three nearby restaurants.
- **Affected parties:** All four diners, including Maya, who has a severe peanut allergy.
- **Time horizon:** It is 5:45 PM; they must return to the theater by 7:20 PM for a 7:30 movie.
- **Budget:** No more than $40 per person.
- **Stakes:**
  - Allergy safety: high
  - Missing the movie: moderate
  - Choosing less-preferred food: low
- **Cost of delay:** Every minute reduces dining time and could remove reservation availability.

### Output

> **Which nearby restaurant should the four friends select and secure now so everyone can eat safely for no more than $40 per person and return to the theater by 7:20 PM?**

### Gate Pass

Yes. It is clear what outcome the Inquiry must enable and what boundaries apply.

### What happens next

Map the current Working Model.

## Step 2: Map the Working Model

### Input

The bounded Inquiry and three nearby restaurants:

- Siam Garden
- Oak Table
- Pasta Corner

### Gate Not Yet

> “Siam Garden is probably best. It has the highest rating and is closest.”

### Why it does not pass

This statement:

- Treats a rating as a conclusion
- Silently assumes the restaurant is safe
- Silently assumes the wait is short
- Ignores the budget and deadline
- Confuses a food preference with a Constraint
- Leaves material Unknowns invisible

### Work performed

The group separates the contents of the Working Model.

### Claims and their basis

| Claim | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---:|
| Siam Garden is five minutes from the theater | Map data | High |
| Oak Table is ten minutes from the theater | Map data | High |
| Pasta Corner is two minutes from the theater | Map data | High |
| Siam Garden costs approximately $30 per person | Menu and estimated tax/tip | Medium |
| Oak Table costs approximately $36 per person | Menu and estimated tax/tip | Medium |
| Pasta Corner costs approximately $24 per person | Menu and estimated tax/tip | Medium |
| Oak Table shows a 6:00 PM reservation | Booking platform | High |
| The group needs about 50 minutes to order and eat | Previous group experience | Medium |
| Siam Garden can probably handle the allergy | One online review | Low |

### Assumptions

- The available Oak Table reservation will remain available.
- The restaurants’ current waits are reasonably short.
- A 50-minute meal estimate is adequate.
- An online review accurately represents Siam Garden’s current allergy practices.
- Being close to the theater makes Pasta Corner safe on time.

### Constraints

- Maya’s allergy-safety threshold must be met.
- Total cost must not exceed $40 per person.
- Everyone must return to the theater by 7:20 PM.
- All four friends must be able to eat together.

For this Inquiry, Maya’s safety threshold requires a restaurant manager to confirm:

- No peanut ingredients in her meal
- Separate preparation surfaces and utensils
- Staff awareness of the allergy

This does not eliminate Risk, but it defines the minimum acceptable boundary.

### Criteria

Among restaurants that satisfy every Constraint, the group prefers:

1. More enjoyable food
2. More schedule buffer
3. Lower cost
4. Greater convenience

### Unknowns

- Current wait at Siam Garden
- Siam Garden’s actual allergy protocol
- Oak Table’s actual allergy protocol
- Current wait at Pasta Corner
- Pasta Corner’s allergy protocol
- Whether the 6:00 Oak Table reservation will remain available

### Relevant relationships

- Distance, waiting, and meal duration consume the same limited time.
- A longer wait reduces schedule buffer.
- Allergy safety cannot be inferred from distance, rating, or cuisine preference.
- A lower price cannot compensate for violating a safety or time Constraint.

### Output

An initial Working Model that explicitly separates Claims, Evidence, Assumptions, Constraints, Criteria, and Unknowns.

### Gate Pass

Yes. The potentially material elements are represented and correctly classified.

### What happens next

Challenge the material Claims and establish the First Principles.

## Step 3: Establish the First Principles

### Input

The initial Working Model.

### Gate Not Yet

> “Siam Garden is the best restaurant because everyone prefers Thai food.”

### Why it does not pass

“Thai food is preferred” is a Criterion, not a First Principle.

“Siam Garden is best” is a conclusion. It depends on untested Assumptions about safety and waiting.

Neither statement is foundational or sufficiently reduced.

### Work performed

The group asks which Claims must serve as the foundation for relevant reasoning.

### First Principle 1: Time is finite and additive

There are 95 minutes between 5:45 PM and 7:20 PM.

For a restaurant to be viable:

> **Travel to restaurant + waiting + ordering and eating + return travel must not exceed the available time.**

The same minute cannot be allocated to both waiting and eating.

### First Principle 2: Severe peanut exposure can cause unacceptable harm

Maya has a medically established severe peanut allergy. A restaurant that cannot meet her minimum safety threshold is not an acceptable option.

Food preference or convenience cannot compensate for unacceptable medical exposure.

### First Principle 3: An acceptable move must satisfy every Constraint

The Inquiry is not fulfilled unless:

- All four friends can participate
- Maya’s safety threshold is met
- The cost remains within budget
- The group returns by the deadline

Criteria compare acceptable options. They cannot make an unacceptable option acceptable.

### Establishing the Reasoning Floor

The group does not need to investigate:

- The molecular mechanics of allergic reactions
- The physics of time
- The economic origins of menu pricing
- A complete theory of food preference

Going deeper would not create a Material Change in this Inquiry.

### Output

A Working Model grounded in three scope-bound First Principles:

1. Available time is finite and consumed by every part of the dining process.
2. Severe allergen exposure creates unacceptable harm.
3. Every Constraint must be satisfied before Criteria can compare options.

### Gate Pass

Yes. Each First Principle is:

- **Defensible:** Supported logically or by strong Evidence
- **Foundational:** Relevant conclusions depend on it
- **Sufficiently reduced:** Going deeper would not materially improve the decision

### What happens next

Reason forward from the First Principles.

# Phase Two: Build

## Step 4: Derive from First Principles

### Input

The grounded Working Model.

### Gate Not Yet

> “The normal way to choose is to pick the restaurant with the highest rating.”

### Why it does not pass

This imports a conventional answer. It is not derived from the Working Model.

Ratings may inform a Criterion, but they do not establish allergy safety, schedule viability, or affordability.

### Work performed

The group derives relevant Claims from the First Principles.

### Time derivation

At 5:45 PM, 95 minutes remain.

Using a 50-minute meal estimate:

| Restaurant | Travel there | Travel back | Maximum viable wait |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Siam Garden | 5 min | 5 min | 35 min |
| Oak Table | 10 min | 10 min | 25 min |
| Pasta Corner | 2 min | 2 min | 41 min |

For example:

> Siam Garden is viable on time only if its wait is no longer than  
> `95 − 5 − 50 − 5 = 35 minutes`.

### Safety derivation

A restaurant cannot be considered viable until its allergy protocol meets Maya’s safety threshold.

An online review is insufficient because it may be outdated, incomplete, or based on a different allergy.

### Constraint derivation

A restaurant that fails safety, budget, or time must be rejected before food preference is considered.

### Information derivation

The allergy protocols and wait times are both:

- Unknown
- Material
- Potentially inexpensive to investigate

Therefore, gathering information may be more justified than immediately selecting a restaurant.

### Output

The following derived Claims:

1. Siam Garden requires a wait of 35 minutes or less.
2. Oak Table requires a wait of 25 minutes or less.
3. Pasta Corner requires a wait of 41 minutes or less.
4. No restaurant is currently established as meeting the allergy Constraint.
5. Current wait and allergy information could materially change which restaurants are viable.
6. A short investigation could improve the Working Model before meaningful time is lost.

### Gate Pass

Yes. Each derived Claim is traceable to the First Principles and Working Model.

### What happens next

Turn the derived Claims into possible Next Moves.

## Step 5: Construct Possible Next Moves

### Input

The derived Claims.

### Gate Not Yet

> “Let’s go to Siam Garden.”

### Why it does not pass

This produces only the group’s preferred answer. It does not consider:

- Other restaurant choices
- The material Unknowns
- An investigative move
- The possibility that the preferred restaurant violates a Constraint

### Work performed

The group constructs the immediate moves available to it:

1. Choose Siam Garden now.
2. Reserve Oak Table now.
3. Walk into Pasta Corner now.
4. Call the three restaurants in parallel to check:
   - Current wait or seating availability
   - Peanut ingredients
   - Preparation surfaces and utensils
   - Staff ability to follow the requested protocol
5. Search for additional restaurants.

Each is something the group could actually do next.

### Output

A set of possible Next Moves containing direct choices, an investigation, and expanded search.

### Gate Pass

Yes. Each possible Next Move addresses the Inquiry or a material information gap preventing its resolution.

### What happens next

Evaluate the possible Next Moves.

## Step 6: Evaluate Possible Next Moves

### Input

The five possible Next Moves.

### Gate Not Yet

The group assigns preference scores:

| Restaurant | Preference score |
|---|---:|
| Siam Garden | 9 |
| Oak Table | 7 |
| Pasta Corner | 6 |

It then selects Siam Garden.

### Why it does not pass

The evaluation allows a Criterion to override Constraints.

A high food-preference score cannot compensate for an unknown allergy protocol or an excessive wait. The score creates false precision while leaving the most consequential Risks unresolved.

### Work performed

The group evaluates Constraints before Criteria.

| Possible Next Move | Constraint assessment | Assumptions and Unknowns | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choose Siam Garden | Safety and time not established | Assumes safe handling and short wait | Not currently viable |
| Reserve Oak Table | Time and budget appear viable; safety not established | Assumes allergy protocol is adequate | Not yet justified |
| Walk into Pasta Corner | Budget appears viable; safety and time not established | Assumes short wait and safe handling | Not currently viable |
| Call all three | Consumes about five minutes but violates no Constraint | Restaurant statements may still be imperfect | Viable and informative |
| Search for more options | Consumes time without first testing existing options | Assumes better choices exist | Possible, but lower information value |

Calling the restaurants has:

- Low cost
- Low delay
- High potential to change the viable set
- No dependency on a hidden material Assumption

### Output

A defensible comparison showing that calling the restaurants is the strongest immediate move.

### Gate Pass

Yes. Constraint violations, Criteria, Assumptions, Unknowns, and Risks are explicit.

### What happens next

Determine whether the group has Sufficient Clarity.

# Phase Three: Move

## Step 7: Establish Sufficient Clarity

### Input

The evaluated possible Next Moves.

### Gate Not Yet

> “We are fairly sure Siam Garden will be fine. Let’s stop overthinking.”

### Why it does not pass

“Fairly sure” does not address materiality.

The unresolved safety Claim has severe consequences. The wait-time Unknown could also make the restaurant nonviable. Both can be investigated at little cost.

### Work performed

The group tests the remaining Unknowns for Material Change.

| Unknown | Could it change the restaurant choice? | Consequence | Cost to investigate |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Allergy protocol | Yes | Potential medical emergency | One phone call |
| Current wait | Yes | Could miss the movie | One phone call |
| Exact food preference | Possibly | Less-enjoyable dinner | No investigation needed |
| Exact final bill | Possibly | Small budget overrun | Check menu during call |

The group does **not** have Sufficient Clarity to select the final restaurant.

It **does** have Sufficient Clarity for the immediate Next Move: call the restaurants.

### Output

- **Material information gap:** Current safety and wait information
- **Justified Next Move:** Call the three restaurants
- **Information requested:** Safety protocol, wait, reservation availability, and expected price

### Gate Pass

Yes. There is enough clarity to justify the immediate Next Move.

### What happens next

Make the calls.

## Step 8: Make the Next Move

### Input

The justified Next Move: call the restaurants.

### Gate Not Yet

> “Someone should call them.”

The group continues discussing the restaurants without anyone making a call.

### Why it does not pass

A recommendation is not an executed Next Move. No new Evidence has been created, and the material Unknowns remain unresolved.

### Work performed

Three friends call the restaurants in parallel while the fourth keeps the Oak Table booking page open.

At 5:50 PM, they obtain:

### Siam Garden

- Current wait: approximately 25 minutes
- Uses peanuts in multiple dishes
- Shared preparation surfaces
- Manager cannot meet Maya’s requested safety protocol
- Expected cost: approximately $30 per person

### Oak Table

- 6:00 PM reservation is still available
- Manager confirms no peanut ingredients in Maya’s meal
- Separate preparation surface and utensils are available
- Expected dining time: approximately 50 minutes
- Expected cost: approximately $36 per person

Maya considers this protocol acceptable, while recognizing that restaurant execution still carries residual Risk.

### Pasta Corner

- Current wait: approximately 55 minutes
- Allergy protocol could meet Maya’s threshold
- Expected cost: approximately $24 per person

### Output

The calls have been completed and produced specific sourced information.

### Gate Pass

Yes. The investigative Next Move was actually made.

### What happens next

Treat the restaurant responses as Evidence and update the Working Model.

## Step 9: Update from Evidence

### Input

The information obtained from the three restaurants.

### Gate Not Yet

> “Siam Garden still sounds better. Maybe its manager was just being overly cautious.”

### Why it does not pass

This preserves the preferred conclusion by dismissing contradictory Evidence.

The Working Model must change when new Evidence changes confidence in a material Claim.

### Work performed

The group updates each option.

| Restaurant | Updated safety status | Updated time status | Budget status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Siam Garden | Fails safety Constraint | Can likely return by 7:20 | Passes |
| Oak Table | Meets stated safety threshold | Can likely return by 7:20 | Passes |
| Pasta Corner | Meets stated safety threshold | Cannot return by 7:20 | Passes |

The Working Model now contains stronger Evidence and fewer Unknowns.

The First Principles have not changed. The Inquiry has not changed. Therefore, the approach does not need to restart at Step 1.

The new Evidence changes the derived Claims and possible Next Moves, so the approach returns to **Step 4: Derive from First Principles**.

### Output

An updated Working Model showing:

- Siam Garden is unsafe for this Inquiry.
- Pasta Corner cannot meet the deadline.
- Oak Table appears to satisfy every Constraint.
- Oak Table still carries ordinary execution Risk.

### Gate Pass

Yes. The Working Model has been revised, and the correct return point has been identified.

### What happens next

Begin the second cycle at Step 4.

# Second Cycle: Build from the Updated Working Model

## Step 4: Derive from First Principles Again

### Input

The updated Working Model at 5:50 PM.

### Work performed

The group applies the same First Principles to the new Evidence.

### Siam Garden

Estimated time:

> `5 travel + 25 wait + 50 meal + 5 return = 85 minutes`

Starting at 5:50, the group would return around 7:15.

It passes the time and budget Constraints but fails the safety Constraint.

### Oak Table

The group can walk ten minutes and arrive for the 6:00 reservation.

Estimated return time:

> `10 travel + 0 expected wait + 50 meal + 10 return = 70 minutes`

Starting at 5:50, the group would return around 7:00.

It appears to pass safety, time, and budget Constraints, with approximately 20 minutes of schedule buffer.

### Pasta Corner

Estimated time:

> `2 travel + 55 wait + 50 meal + 2 return = 109 minutes`

Starting at 5:50, the group would return around 7:39.

It passes safety and budget but fails the time Constraint.

### Output

The following derived Claims:

1. Siam Garden is not viable because it fails a safety Constraint.
2. Pasta Corner is not viable because it fails the deadline Constraint.
3. Oak Table is the only known restaurant that currently satisfies every Constraint.
4. Continuing to search could improve food preference but could also consume the remaining buffer and lose the reservation.

### Gate Pass

Yes. Every derived Claim follows from the updated Working Model.

### What happens next

Construct the remaining possible Next Moves.

## Step 5: Construct Possible Next Moves Again

### Input

The updated derived Claims.

### Work performed

The group constructs two meaningful Next Moves:

1. Book Oak Table.
2. Continue searching for a restaurant that may score better on the Criteria.

Siam Garden and Pasta Corner are not included as viable choices because each violates a Constraint.

### Output

Two possible Next Moves derived from the updated Working Model.

### Gate Pass

Yes. Both moves address the Inquiry as it now stands.

### What happens next

Evaluate the two moves.

## Step 6: Evaluate Possible Next Moves Again

### Input

The two possible Next Moves.

### Work performed

| Possible Next Move | Constraints | Criteria | Assumptions, Unknowns, and Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Book Oak Table | All currently satisfied | Acceptable food, $36 cost, 20-minute buffer | Staff execution and actual service duration remain uncertain |
| Continue searching | No option yet established | Might find preferred food or lower price | Consumes time and may lose the Oak Table reservation |

Oak Table is not perfect, but perfection is not required. It satisfies every Constraint and performs acceptably against the Criteria.

Searching further primarily offers a possible improvement in preference. It introduces more schedule and availability Risk without addressing a material deficiency.

### Output

Oak Table is the preferred viable Next Move.

### Gate Pass

Yes. The trade-off between securing an acceptable option and searching for a potentially better one is explicit.

### What happens next

Test whether further inquiry is justified.

# Second Cycle: Move

## Step 7: Establish Sufficient Clarity Again

### Input

The evaluation favoring Oak Table.

### Work performed

The group examines the remaining Unknowns:

- The restaurant could take longer than estimated.
- Staff could execute the allergy protocol incorrectly.
- Another restaurant might provide more enjoyable food.

These Unknowns are real, but:

- Oak Table provides approximately 20 minutes of schedule buffer.
- The manager directly confirmed the requested safety protocol.
- Maya accepts the remaining Risk.
- Additional searching could cost the available reservation.
- Better food would not materially improve fulfillment of the Inquiry.

Further inquiry is unlikely to create enough Material Change to justify its cost or delay.

### Output

Sufficient Clarity to book Oak Table.

### Gate Pass

Yes. The Working Model supports a justified final selection with explicit residual Risk.

### What happens next

Book the restaurant.

## Step 8: Make the Next Move Again

### Input

The justified Next Move: book Oak Table.

### Work performed

The group:

1. Books the 6:00 PM reservation.
2. Adds Maya’s peanut allergy to the reservation.
3. Records the manager’s safety confirmation.
4. Receives a booking confirmation.
5. Begins the ten-minute walk to the restaurant.

### Output

Oak Table has been selected and secured.

The rationale is explicit:

- It meets the safety threshold.
- It remains within budget.
- It provides enough time to eat and return.
- It is preferable to continued searching given the cost of delay.

The residual Risks are also explicit:

- Actual service may take longer.
- Staff execution may differ from the stated protocol.

### Gate Pass

Yes. The Next Move was executed rather than merely recommended.

### What happens next

Use the booking confirmation as Evidence and determine whether the Inquiry is complete.

## Step 9: Update from Evidence Again

### Input

The booking confirmation and allergy note.

### Work performed

The Working Model is updated:

- Oak Table is no longer merely available; it is reserved.
- The allergy request is documented.
- Reservation uncertainty has been removed.
- The restaurant selection now exists rather than remaining a recommendation.

The group also identifies future Evidence that would reopen the Inquiry:

- The restaurant withdraws its allergy assurance.
- The table is not available on arrival.
- Service delays make the 7:20 deadline materially unlikely.

None of those conditions currently exists.

### Output

A completed Inquiry:

> **The four friends will eat at Oak Table. The restaurant has been reserved for 6:00 PM, is expected to cost $36 per person, has confirmed the required allergy protocol, and should return the group to the theater around 7:00 PM.**

### Gate Pass

Yes. The new Evidence supports completion, and no material information gap currently justifies another cycle.

# Final Outcome

The approach did not merely produce the name of a restaurant. It produced:

- A bounded Inquiry
- An explicit Working Model
- Defensible First Principles
- Traceable derived Claims
- Multiple possible Next Moves
- Constraint-first evaluation
- Sufficient Clarity for investigation
- New Evidence
- An updated Working Model
- Sufficient Clarity for the final decision
- An executed Next Move
- Explicit residual Risk and reopening conditions

The complete throughline is:

> **Bound → Map → Establish → Derive → Construct → Evaluate → Clarify → Make → Update → repeat where the Evidence requires it.**
