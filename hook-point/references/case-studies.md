# Case Studies

Reference catalog of every named case in *Hook Point*, organized by context. Each entry: brand, hook (verbatim where the source gives one), outcome (only what Kane states), and the pillar where Kane develops it. Verbatim quotes are in italics with citations.

## Direct-to-Consumer / Retail

### Tova Borgnine Perfume (Halbert, 1977)
- **Hook:** *"Tova Borgnine Swears Under Oath That Her New Perfume Does Not Contain an Illegal Sexual Stimulant."* Subhead: *"Wife of famous movie star agrees to give away 10,000 samples of her new fragrance just to prove it's safe to wear in public."*
- **Outcome:** 7,000+ at the LA launch. *"Borgnines went from grossing $20,000 a month to $800,000 a month"* (1977). *Time* magazine coverage. Purchase orders from major department-store chains.
- **Pillar 1 — Definition** (Ch. 1)

### Timberland Boots (Lupinacci)
- **Hook:** *"Your eyes are frozen. Your skin has turned black. You're technically dead. Let's talk boots."*
- **Outcome:** Lupinacci: people *"didn't even need to read the rest of the copy"*.
- **Pillar 1 — Definition** (Ch. 1)

### Pepsodent (Claude Hopkins, 1920s)
- **Hook:** *"FILM, a dangerous coating that robs teeth of their whiteness. Here's a way to remove it that quickly restores brilliance."* Tagline: *"Pepsodent Makes Teeth Far Brighter."* Jingle (1957): *"You wonder where the yellow went, when you brush with Pepsodent."*
- **Outcome:** Daily-brushing population went *"from 5 percent of the population to 65 percent within ten years."* Bestselling toothpaste for 30+ years.
- **Pillar 1 — Definition** (Ch. 1)

### Lucky Strike "Torches of Freedom" (Bernays, 1929) — *anti-pattern*
- **Hook:** Debutantes lit cigarettes at the New York Easter Day Parade as a "freedom" gesture. Bernays tipped off the press in advance.
- **Outcome:** *"That year alone, the percentage of female smokers rose by 7 percent."*
- **Kane's framing:** Hooks can change the world for worse. The Ian Malcolm filter applies.
- **Pillar 1 — Definition** (Ch. 1)

### Dollar Shave Club
- **Hook:** *"Our Blades Are F***ing Great."*
- **Outcome:** *"More than 26 million views… led to their acquisition by Unilever for more than $1 billion."*
- **Why it worked (Jeff King):** Used logic, emotion, and humor — the top three PCM vocabularies in North America.
- **Pillar 4 — Storytelling** (Ch. 4)

### TOMS Shoes
- **Hook:** *"One for one."*
- **Outcome:** $313M Bain investment; $600M valuation; 35M+ shoes donated. Hook decayed as competitors copied the "one for one" framing.
- **Pillar 2 — Creating / Pillar 10 — Iteration** (Ch. 2 / Ch. 10)

### Chatbooks (Nate Morley / Works Collective)
- **Hook evolution:** Feature hook → "$6 Instagram photo books" → Purpose hook → *"Hold On to What Matters."*
- **Pillar 10 — Iteration** (Ch. 10)

### CR7 Underwear (Cristiano Ronaldo, distributed by LMS Inc.)
- **Hook:** Ronaldo's 205M+ Instagram followers as the distribution engine.
- **Outcome:** Exclusive North American distribution for CR7.
- **Pillar 8 — Scale** (Ch. 8)

### Society / Dibs T-shirts (Zech Francis)
- **Hook:** Office *inside* the largest T-shirt print factory in North America → overnight sample turnaround for Buckle executives.
- **Outcome:** Dibs went *"from zero dollars in revenue to seven figures in sales in less than 45 days"* — fastest brand growth in Buckle's 50-year retail history.
- **Pillar 7 — Free Value** (Ch. 7)

### Morton Salt × OK Go
- **Hook:** First 4.2 seconds of the video are a complete moment; then revealed as a sped-up version of a 4-minute longer piece.
- **Lesson:** "Unsexy" brands can produce breakthrough content by pairing with the right creators.
- **Pillar 7 — Free Value** (Ch. 7)

### Away Suitcase (referenced)
- **Hook:** A suitcase that charges your phone — relatable element (suitcase) paired with a unique element (phone-charging).
- **Pillar 2 — Creating** (Ch. 2 — characteristics of a great Hook Point)

### IPSY (beauty subscription)
- **Hook for consumers:** Try the latest hair, makeup, skincare, fragrance products by mail each month.
- **Hook for brands:** Get products into the hands of high-intent consumers, many of whom convert.
- **Pillar 7 — Free Value** (Ch. 7)

## Software / Tech

### Netflix
- **Hooks (stacked over time):** *"DVDs to your door, no late fees"* → original content (*House of Cards*, *Orange Is the New Black*, *Stranger Things*) → binge-watching (releasing all episodes at once).
- **Outcome:** Blockbuster (once valued $8.4B) bankrupted 2010. Netflix reached $140B market cap.
- **Pillar 1 — Definition** (Ch. 1)

### Amazon (hook stack)
- **Hooks (in order):** World's largest bookstore → one-click checkout → Prime → Kindle → Sunday delivery → Echo.
- **Outcome:** Took share from Borders, Radio Shack, Payless, Toys-R-Us, Circuit City, Sears.
- **Pillar 1 — Definition** (Ch. 1)

### Tesla Cybertruck (Musk, 2019)
- **Hook:** Radically angular truck design unlike anything before.
- **Outcome:** *"250,000+ pre-orders immediately post-announcement."*
- **Pillar 2 — Creating** (Ch. 2)

### YouTube → Google
- **Hook:** Video embed code that worked inside MySpace profiles.
- **Outcome:** *"YouTube sold their company to Google for $1.65 billion in less than two years."*
- **Pillar 8 — Scale** (Ch. 8)

### Instagram
- **Hook:** Built off Facebook's traffic (same mechanism).
- **Pillar 8 — Scale** (Ch. 8)

### Google "Loretta" Super Bowl Ad
- **Hook:** A widower uses Google Assistant to find old photos of his late wife.
- **Why it worked:** *"Authentic reason to engage in a conversation about love, loss, memory, and nostalgia"* — not borrowed interest. Branded utility — the product is the mechanism.
- **Pillar 7 — Free Value** (Ch. 7)

### MTV Licensing (Kane's first deal)
- **Hook:** Frame MySpace peer-sharing as the first influencer-style ad channel.
- **Outcome:** Licensed to MTV; rolled into Vice, VH1, Comedy Central, Viacom, Rock Band.
- **Pillar 1 — Definition** (Ch. 1)

### Taylor Swift Website (Kane)
- **Hook variation by audience:**
  - Label / father: cost savings + bounce-rate fix (90% → lower)
  - Mother: trust + ease of use
  - Swift: creative control without code
- **Outcome:** Closed Taylor Swift as Kane's first major client. Site built in 6 hours.
- **Pillar 1 — Definition / Pillar 6 — Listening** (Ch. 1, Ch. 6)

### Paranormal Activity (Latham Arneson / Paramount)

### Rings — TV Store Prank (2017) — *anti-pattern*
- **Hook:** Customers shopping for TVs see the creepy girl from the movie crawl out of TVs.
- **Outcome:** ~10M views. Produced laughter, not fear. The film (horror genre) didn't lift at the box office.
- **Lesson:** Tone mismatch — viral ≠ effective.
- **Pillar 4 — Storytelling** (Ch. 4)

### Crank (Lakeshore, Kane's early influencer-style work)

### Lakeshore "Wrath of Con" Comic-Con Parties

- **Outcome:** Party went from ~50 attendees to one of the largest at Comic-Con.
- **Pillar 8 — Scale** (Ch. 8)

### Hot Ones (Sean Evans / Christopher Schonberger)

- **Outcome:** Scarlett Johansson, Shaq, Seth Meyers, John Mayer, Kevin Hart, Natalie Portman. Live version on *The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon* with Selena Gomez.

## B2B / Advertising

### Adobe / Hurricane Harvey Memory Restoration (Shareability)
- **Hook:** Students use Adobe to restore family photos destroyed in the hurricane.
- **Lesson:** Brand is the stage; victims, students, nonprofits are the heroes.
- **Pillar 4 — Storytelling** (Ch. 4)

### Adobe Experience Cloud / St. Jude (B2B, Shareability)
- **Hook:** How St. Jude uses Adobe's technology to be more effective.
- **Lesson:** Even in B2B, the customer is the hero.
- **Pillar 4 — Storytelling** (Ch. 4)

### Red Bull Stratos (Felix Baumgartner, 2012)
- **Hook:** Skydive from 24 miles up; break the sound barrier in freefall.
- **Outcome:** 340M+ YouTube views *before* the jump. 8M+ concurrent viewers — a YouTube record. Baumgartner reached 725 mph and broke 3 world records (Mach 1.24).
- **Investment:** Red Bull spent $60M+ to stage / film / promote.
- **Pillar 4 — Storytelling** (Ch. 4)

### Cricket Wireless / John Cena (Shareability)
- **Hook 1:** *"Unexpected John Cena Prank"* — fans audition for an ad, Cena bursts through a poster of himself.
- **Hook 2:** *"John Cena Reacts"* — fans surprise Cena with thanks for his "Never give up" motto.
- **Outcome:**
  - Hook 1: 235M+ views.
  - Hook 2: Most-shared ad of 2017. #3 YouTube trending. 175M+ views. 2.5M shares.
  - Total campaign: ~3M shares, 10M engagements.
- **Pillar 4 — Storytelling** (Ch. 4)

### Nike × Colin Kaepernick
- **Hook:** *"Believe in something. Even if it means sacrificing everything."*
- **Outcome:** Stock all-time high. *"+31% online sales that weekend."* 450K Twitter mentions in a day.
- **Why it worked:** Nike had earned the right via decades of "those who sacrifice" investment.
- **Pillar 5 — Authenticity** (Ch. 5)

### Nike "Dream Crazier"
- **Hook:** Reframe "crazy" — every female athlete called crazy was simply the first.
- **Lesson:** Female athletes are the hero. Nike is the stage.
- **Pillar 4 — Storytelling** (Ch. 4)

### Nike Zoom Vaporfly
- **Hook:** Olympics is considering banning the shoe. *"The five fastest men's marathons in history have all been run in the last 13 months, all by runners wearing Vaporflys."* Scientists estimate 4% efficiency gain.
- **Pillar 1 — Definition** (Ch. 1)

### Gillette "We Believe" — *anti-pattern*
- **Hook:** Toxic masculinity campaign.
- **Outcome:** Backlash. Gillette had no prior social-responsibility history → felt opportunistic.
- **Pillar 5 — Authenticity** (Ch. 5)

### Theranos / Fyre Festival — *anti-pattern*

- **Pillar 5 — Authenticity** (Ch. 5)

### Geico "Family: Unskippable" (Martin Agency, 2015)

- **Outcome:** Ad Age Campaign of the Year (the first time the category was awarded).

### Pizza Hut / Pepsi "Dangers of Selfie Sticks" (Shareability)

## Public Figures (PCM / political)

### Bill Clinton (1996 debate vs. Bush)

- **Pillar 4 — Storytelling** (Ch. 4)

### Hillary Clinton (2016) — *anti-pattern*

### Trump

- **Pillar 4 — Storytelling** (Ch. 4)
