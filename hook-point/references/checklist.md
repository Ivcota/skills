# Checklist — Drafting and Auditing a Hook Point

A working sheet for any copywriter at any artifact: LinkedIn post, X reply, cold email, video opener, landing page hero, ad. Bullets and tables. No prose. Steal it.

## Phase 0 — Decide what you're shipping

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What artifact am I writing? | (Post / video / email / page / ad) |
| Which platform? | (Constraint: length, format, behavior) |
| What's the one **gut reaction** I want at second 3? | (One sentence) |
| What's the **action** I want after consumption? | (Click / share / reply / sign up / buy / nothing) |

If the gut reaction and the action conflict (e.g., reaction = "laugh"; action = "buy our funeral plan"), stop here. Re-pick one.

## Phase 1 — Pre-write (listen before you draft)

- [ ] Spent ≥15 minutes with the audience in their own words (Reddit threads, comments, customer interviews, sales-call transcripts).
- [ ] Wrote down the **exact phrase** a real person used to describe the pain.
- [ ] Checked Google Trends / Google News for a trend wave I can ride.
- [ ] Checked Tubular Labs / YouTube / Facebook for high share-to-view-ratio content in the niche (>1% on Facebook = organic).
- [ ] Identified the **content behavior** in this niche, not just the topics (pacing, format, voice).
- [ ] Listed the audience's PCM mix — emotion / logic / humor minimum (North America default).

**Audience pain capture template:**

> The reader is currently __[doing/feeling/struggling with]__ because __[real cause]__. They've already tried __[X]__ and it didn't work because __[Y]__. They would pay attention if I could prove __[Z]__.

## Phase 2 — Drafting (volume, not perfection)

| Step | Output |
|---|---|
| Study what already works in this niche (5+ winning hooks) | A reference doc — verbatim hooks, not paraphrases |
| Study what doesn't work | 3+ underperformers and why |
| Generate **50–100 variations** | One list. No editing yet. |
| Rank against the reference set | Top 5 — your shortlist |

**Patterns to try while drafting** (cycle through these):

- One word, no setup ("Free Solo")
- Specific number ("96 million black balls"; "200,000 followers in 30 days")
- Subverted expectation ("WARNING!! Safety is Dangerous")
- "You know how…?" agreement-builder
- Proclamation lead ("Read This or Die")
- Question opener ("Why are there 96 million black balls on this lake?")
- The pairing — relatable + unique (Away suitcase that charges your phone)
- The reframe (Nike "Dream Crazier" — crazy as a badge)
- The category-of-one phrase (Sleep Doctor; Mother Monster)
- The shocking statistic + counter ("95% chance of dying from a curable disease")
- The reaction proxy (Paranormal Activity — show people scared, not the film)

## Phase 3 — Self-audit (against the 8 characteristics)

A great Hook Point is:

- [ ] **As few words as possible.** Can I cut one more word?
- [ ] **Subverts expectations.** Does it feel like the 50th headline in this niche?
- [ ] **Doesn't make people think.** Lands in one read?
- [ ] **Curious, not satisfied.** Do they want more, or is the hook the whole point?
- [ ] **Pairs relatable + unique.** What's the familiar handle? What's the new twist?
- [ ] **Specific.** Numbers, names, places — not "huge" or "many"?
- [ ] **One clear hook.** Could a stranger point to the hook within 3 seconds?
- [ ] **Authentic.** Backed by a story I can deliver — not clickbait?

## Phase 4 — The 4 hard tests

### 1. The 3-second test
Show the artifact to a stranger for 3 seconds, then hide it. Ask: *what is this about?*
- [ ] They got the topic. → Pass.
- [ ] They got the value. → Strong pass.
- [ ] They guessed wrong. → Fail. Rewrite.

### 2. The authenticity test
- [ ] Could I deliver everything the hook implies? (Theranos / Fyre filter.)
- [ ] Have I earned the right to this stance? (Gillette filter.)
- [ ] Would I be embarrassed if my best customer saw this? (Ian Malcolm filter.)

### 3. The tone-match test
- [ ] Does the emotion of the hook match the emotion of the product? (Rings filter.)
- [ ] If the audience reacts as I want, does that emotion route them *toward* purchase, or *away*?

### 4. The brand-as-stage test
- [ ] Who is the hero — the audience or my brand?
- [ ] Read the artifact aloud. Is the brand the noun in most sentences? (If yes → rewrite.)
- [ ] If I removed every mention of my brand, would the story still be valuable?

## Phase 5 — Testing (replace opinion with signal)

| Channel | What to A/B |
|---|---|
| Paid ads (Meta, X, LinkedIn) | Hook variants vs CTR / cost-per-click |
| Email | Subject line variants vs open rate |
| LinkedIn | Opening-line variants vs first-hour engagement |
| YouTube | Thumbnail + title variants — Photoshop options into a screenshot of your home feed |
| Friends / colleagues (5+ people) | Which variant did they *remember* an hour later? |
| Meme account / partner page | If a strong hook brings 5K–20K followers and a weak hook brings <200 — same audience, the hook is the variable |

Sample size: 50+ impressions per variant minimum. More if conversion is rare.

## Phase 6 — Listen to the signal

| Signal | Verdict |
|---|---|
| High views, low shares (<0.5%) | Paid lift, not hook strength. Keep testing. |
| High views, high shares (>1%) | Real hook. Scale. |
| High click-through, low conversion | Hook is wrong-tone (Rings). Realign. |
| Low click-through, high conversion (when clicked) | Hook is weak; offer is strong. Rewrite hook. |
| Loud comments + analytics flat | Don't over-weight comments. Trust the data. |
| Loud comments + analytics strong | Mine the comments for the *next* hook. |

## Phase 7 — Iteration triggers (when to refresh)

Refresh the hook when any of these fire:

- [ ] Click-through rate dropped >20% week-over-week.
- [ ] A competitor copied the framing publicly.
- [ ] The category-defining phrase ("one for one") got commoditized.
- [ ] Internal staff are bored of the hook (early sign of audience fatigue).
- [ ] Analytics flat for two cycles in a row.
- [ ] Major news, trend, or platform shift in your category.

**Have the next variant ready.** Build a rolling hook backlog. Don't be caught flat-footed.

## Phase 8 — The Moneyball move (anchor + rotate)

Pick the **core guiding belief** that does *not* move:
- Apple: *"Think Different."*
- FedEx: *"Absolutely, positively overnight."*
- Nike: *"Just Do It."*

Then rotate hooks around it. The brand stays. The hook changes. Schedule a quarterly review.

## Phase 9 — Scale once it's working

- [ ] Where does the audience for this hook already live? (Platform / account / blogger / podcast / publication.)
- [ ] Who is the **Super Connector** in this category?
- [ ] What can I give them — access, audience, expertise, results — that they want enough to introduce me?
- [ ] If I disappeared for a month, does the system keep working? (If no → depersonalize the hook.)
- [ ] Have I built both directions — online → offline AND offline → online?

## Phase 10 — The 30-second decision filter

Before shipping anything, answer these in 30 seconds:

| Question | Y / N |
|---|---|
| Does the first line capture attention in 3 seconds? | |
| Is the hook backed by a true, deliverable story? | |
| Is the hook *new* — not what competitors are using? | |
| Is the audience the hero, not the brand? | |
| Does the hook's tone match the product's tone? | |
| Have I earned the right to this stance? | |
| Did I generate 50–100 variants before picking? | |
| Am I measuring with A/B, not opinion? | |
| Do I have the next-generation hook ready? | |

**If any answer is "No": don't ship. Fix that row first.**

## Cheat sheet — the 1-paragraph pre-flight

> Audience: __________. Platform: __________. Gut reaction at second 3: __________. Hook: __________. Hero of the story: __________ (not the brand). Authenticity proof: __________. Tone match: __________ → product is __________. Variant count: __________. Test plan: __________. Refresh trigger: __________.

If you can fill all ten blanks specifically, you've earned the right to publish.
