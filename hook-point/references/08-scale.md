# 08 — Scale

A working hook is the front door. Scale is going where the traffic already lives so you don't have to build it from scratch. Ch. 8 is the playbook.

## The core principle

Ch. 8 — *"Don't start from scratch! Go where the traffic already exists."*

This isn't a metaphor. The case studies in Ch. 8 are all about finding an *existing audience* and getting your hook in front of it.

## YouTube → Google ($1.65B in under 2 years)

Ch. 8 — *"YouTube sold their company to Google for $1.65 billion in less than two years by harnessing Myspace's traffic. At the time, Myspace didn't have a video player built in to their platform. YouTube recognized this fact and was one of the first companies to create a video embed code that was compatible with Myspace."*

The mechanism: Myspace users embedded YouTube videos on their profiles → their friends saw the videos → clicked through to YouTube → uploaded their own videos → embedded those on Myspace. Closed loop. Myspace's traffic became YouTube's growth engine.

*"Instagram leveraged a similar model, building a user base quickly off of Facebook's traffic."*

**Translation to your work:** what's the equivalent of "MySpace has no video player" in your category? Where does an existing audience sit on a platform with a missing primitive you can supply?

## Lakeshore's "Wrath of Con" — building traffic by building access

Ch. 8 — Kane at Lakeshore Entertainment noticed film bloggers had traffic that traditional press didn't acknowledge yet. Lakeshore sponsored the bloggers' Comic-Con party, *"The Wrath of Con."* The party had ~50 attendees before Lakeshore stepped in.

For the indie film *Pathology*, Lakeshore gave the bloggers money to throw a bigger party and invited the stars (Milo Ventimiglia, Alyssa Milano). The party became *"one of the biggest parties at Comic-Con."*

The trade: bloggers got access to stars they wanted to write about → bloggers wrote about Lakeshore's films → moviegoers were exposed cheaply and at scale.

The pattern: identify under-respected traffic owners. Help them with what *you* uniquely have access to. They'll repay with reach.

## Super Connectors

Ch. 8 — *"To scale your business in the offline world, you need to look for what I call Super Connectors — people who are well connected to those whom you want to do business with."*

Operational rule: one well-respected person can make dozens of introductions. Kane (a self-described introvert): the MTV executive he did licensing deals with opened doors to Taylor Swift, *Vice* magazine, Snoop Dogg, Michael Strahan.

| Use of a Super Connector | Example (Ch. 8) |
|---|---|
| Open doors to celebrities | MTV exec → Swift's team |
| Source talent | Bill Gladstone (literary agent) → *For Dummies* writers |
| Generate referrals | Dr. Chris Renna → Peter Park's billionaire client pipeline |
| Provide credibility | Mayors and governors → Jim Kane's law firm clients |

The three-level pattern:

1. Find one Super Connector.
2. Deliver exceptional results for the clients they refer.
3. Those clients become testimonials → fuel for more referrals.

## The personalization trap — Peter Park

Ch. 8 — Park's referral sources cite *him*, not Platinum Fitness. *"When referrals hear Park's name, they don't necessarily want to work with a member of his team — they want to work with him personally — limiting the success and scalability of his company."*

Kane's fix: reposition the message. *"Now, when someone gives him a referral, he expresses the value of his entire team."* Park names specialists by area — back issues, knee issues, etc. — shifting the hook from *"Park is the best in the world"* to *"Park's team is the best in the world."*

**Diagnostic:** if your personal brand is the hook, write down what happens to revenue when you take a vacation, get sick, or scale beyond yourself. If the answer is "it dies," your hook is capping your ceiling.

## Cristiano Ronaldo / CR7 — celebrity = distribution

Ch. 8 — Alex Livian's LMS Inc. became the exclusive North American distributor for Ronaldo's CR7 underwear brand. *"Ronaldo currently has the largest Instagram following on the platform, with more than 205 million followers. This is more than Kim Kardashian, Taylor Swift, The Rock, and Ariana Grande have!"*

Livian's logic: Ronaldo had pre-established trust. Starting a brand from scratch would have required *building* that trust. The hook is the audience, not the product.

Three constraints Livian names on celebrity-as-distribution:

- **Choose carefully.** Negative news on the celebrity can poison your brand. (Cite a scandal-prone celebrity and your sales collapse with theirs.)
- **Authentic connection matters.** Shaq in a Buick ad fails ("the guy can't even fit in the car"). George Clooney for Casamigos works (the persona matches the product).
- **The celebrity's audience must be *your* audience.** Mismatch destroys the lift.

## The online → offline snowball (Kane's own ladder)

Ch. 8 — *"Do you see how each step both online and offline created a snowball effect to continue the growth of my brand?"*

Kane's actual sequence:

1. Built 1M followers in 30 days (online).
2. Used the story to secure a literary agent (offline).
3. Got a publishing deal — *One Million Followers* (offline product).
4. Used social platforms to sell the book (online).
5. Used the book + hook to land speaking engagements — IKEA, Mindvalley, Web Summit (offline).
6. Used speaking gigs to land podcast appearances (online).
7. Used podcast appearances to land TV — Fox Business, SiriusXM, KTLA, Yahoo! Finance (offline + online).

Each rung makes the next rung trivial. The story compounds.

## How to land your first podcast / speaking gig / TV appearance

Ch. 8 makes the operational steps explicit.

**Podcasts.**

- Hire a publicist (vet for short contracts).
- Use a company like *Interview Valet* (1K–40K download shows; start here).
- Get referrals from colleagues — strategic introductions are easiest and free.
- Once you have a large social following, podcast hosts will seek *you*.
- Take *every* podcast opportunity when starting out — even a 100-listener show is 20–30 minutes of practice.

**Speaking gigs.**

- Hire a speaking coach first (Kane did, before any gig).
- Tell every friend and colleague you're available.
- At your first event, meet other speakers in the green room — they're the referral engine.
- Add value before/after the event. Kane filmed a Web Summit recap that beat their top video (1.2M views vs ~1M); used that case study to book more gigs.

**TV.**

- Mostly via publicists.
- TV producers want guests with *audiences* because audience = viewership.

## Offline → online — Sleep Doctor case

Ch. 8 — Michael Breus has appeared on *The Oprah Winfrey Show*, *Today*, *Dr. Oz*. But after years of speaking, he's collected only a few thousand email addresses. Kane: *"In the online space, that number of email addresses can be acquired in just one or two days."*

The plan: take Breus's offline-tested Hook Points (e.g., *"What is the perfect time to have sex before going to bed?"*) and run them online. Reach hundreds of thousands; capture names and emails; convert to courses, sponsorships, book promos.

**The reverse direction matters as much as the forward direction.** Most brands skew to one side. The ladder works in both directions.

## "Go broad" beats "1,000 true fans" (Kane's contrarian take)

Ch. 8 explicitly disagrees with Kevin Kelly's *1000 True Fans*: *"I had to show up with ideas for reaching millions of people in the shortest possible amount of time."*

His argument: a large audience is itself a credibility hook. *"A large social following will cause people to pay attention to you and absorb your core messages."* Sophie Turner (Sansa Stark in *Game of Thrones*) admitted being chosen for a role over "a far better actress" because she had more social followers.

The 1M followers in 30 days wasn't the point. The point was *the leverage 1M followers gave Kane* — agents, publishers, speaking, podcasts, TV.

## What scales and what doesn't

| Doesn't scale | Scales |
|---|---|
| Personal brand only you can deliver (Park before repositioning) | Brand-as-team (Park after repositioning) |
| 100 hand-crafted DMs/day | One ad on a meme account with 19M followers |
| One channel | Online + offline + referrals + Super Connectors |
| Cold outreach with no Super Connector path | Cold outreach through a Super Connector's circle |

## Scale checklist

- [ ] Where does my audience *already* live? (Platform, account, blogger, podcast, magazine.)
- [ ] Who is the Super Connector in my space? (One name.)
- [ ] What can I give the Super Connector — access, audience, expertise, results — that they want?
- [ ] Have I built the offline → online flywheel? (Book / speech / podcast → social → email.)
- [ ] Have I built the online → offline flywheel? (Followers → speaking → TV → clients.)
- [ ] If I disappear for a month, does the system keep working?
- [ ] Is my hook personalized to me, or to a *category* I represent?
