# Profile

**Core concept:** Profile is the fourth P of the Key Person of Influence method — your ability to become known, liked and trusted in your industry so that you are demonstrably "web famous" inside your micro-niche. Harrington and Priestley compress the whole idea into one line: **"you are who Google says you are"** (S1, Ch 6, line 1877). Profile is not vanity reach; it is the visible evidence that a stranger who searches your name lands on page one and immediately concludes you are a KPI in the niche you claim.

**Why it works:** Almost every modern deal — sales, partnerships, media bookings, speaking gigs — starts with a Google search, and "nothing kills a deal faster than someone interesting who doesn't show up in a search" (S1, Ch 6, line 853/1877). Profile content also occupies the buyer's **~7-hour deliberation window** on substantial purchases (S1, Ch 6, line 1929), so the more of those hours your videos, podcasts, slides and posts fill, the more the buyer trusts you and trades up to higher-quality (higher-priced) options. And unlike traditional advertising, the channels needed to do this are essentially free — Harrington and Priestley call this the **rich media mogul uncle** opportunity (S1, Ch 6, line 1882).

**Key insights:**
- "You are who Google says you are." When a KPI Googles you, they want videos, blogs, photos, groups, mutual friends; if nothing comes up, the deal dies (S1, Ch 6, line 1877).
- The "rich media mogul uncle" frame — social platforms are the equivalent of a billionaire uncle offering to distribute your video, audio and articles worldwide for free; refusing is irrational (S1, Ch 6, line 1882).
- Don't water the concrete — traditional broadcast ads spray spend on a 90% uninterested audience; targeted profile content reaches only the 10% who care, at a fraction of the cost (S1, Ch 6, line 1902).
- The 7-hour deliberation window — buyers spend roughly 7 hours deliberating a substantial purchase; your job is to occupy as much of those 7 hours as possible (S1, Ch 6, line 1927).
- Profile success caps out at pitch quality — "social media is a microphone." A bad singer with a microphone just becomes a louder bad singer; amplifying a weak message damages the brand (S1, Ch 1, line 464; Ch 6, line 1969).
- We're at the start, not the end, of the social-media wave — the first decade was rail-line-building; the creativity boom in art, medicine, fashion and architecture is still ahead, and each micro-niche inside it will need a KPI (S1, Ch 6, line 1975).
- Profile must be coherent across channels — videos, blogs, slides, social presence and third-party media coverage on page one all reinforcing the same micro-niche claim (S1, Ch 6, line 1949).

## Sub-models

### Six objectives for building your profile

Harrington and Priestley insist profile is not "being famous" — it is a business asset built for six specific commercial outcomes (S1, Ch 6, line 1913):

1. **Generate leads and enquiries** — get strangers in your niche to raise their hand.
2. **Convert leads and enquiries into sales** — let prospects self-educate so they arrive ready to buy.
3. **Increase the amount people spend** — fill the 7-hour deliberation window so buyers trade up.
4. **Increase the frequency of purchasing** — stay top-of-mind through ongoing social touchpoints.
5. **Find ways to reduce costs** — replace broadcast ad spend ("watering the concrete") with targeted free channels.
6. **Improve your overall brand** — every published asset compounds reputation in the niche.

If a piece of profile content does not push at least one of these six outcomes, it is decoration, not profile.

### Six must-use sites

The 2015 book names six channels every KPI must populate to become "Google-able" (S1, Ch 6, line 1953):

| Site | What to publish | Why the book recommends it |
|------|-----------------|-----------------------------|
| **YouTube.com** | Video content — talks, explainers, demos, interviews | Largest video broadcasting site; video is the highest-trust profile asset |
| **Slideshare.net** | PowerPoint / Keynote decks from talks and webinars | Lets your slide decks rank in Google and circulate beyond the room you presented in |
| **Facebook.com** | Personal profile + business page; community + proof | Largest social network; combines social proof with a discoverable business presence |
| **LinkedIn.com** | Professional profile, articles, group activity | Largest directory of professionals — the default credibility check for B2B buyers |
| **Wordpress.com** | A blog hosting your long-form ideas | Owned-media home for blog posts and SEO-able articles |
| **Twitter** | Micro-blog updates; listen to customers and industry leaders | Real-time channel to hear your market and be heard by industry leaders |

> **Callout — the list is dated, the principle isn't.** This six-site list was published in 2015. Specific platforms shift (and will keep shifting — Slideshare and Twitter as named here are already different products today), but the underlying principle holds: cover the *channel-types* — long-form video, slide/deck sharing, social network with a business page, professional directory, owned blog, and a micro-blog/real-time channel. Translate to current equivalents in your niche, but do not reduce the spread. Do not "modernise" the named list inside this reference; the verbatim 2015 list is the source-of-truth citation (S1, Ch 6, line 1953).

### The 7-hour deliberation window

For any substantial purchase, the book reports that buyers spend roughly **7 hours deliberating** before they buy (S1, Ch 6, line 1927). Profile is how you occupy those 7 hours: podcasts they listen to on the commute, videos they watch in the evening, blog posts they read at lunch, slide decks they download, tweets they scroll. The more of those 7 hours you fill, the more they trust you — and the more they tend to trade up toward higher-quality (higher-priced) options inside your ecosystem. This is the mechanism behind Objective #3 ("increase the amount people spend") and Objective #4 ("increase the frequency of purchasing").

### The "rich media mogul uncle" frame

> "Imagine if a rich uncle of yours … said he had millions of dollars to invest in distributing your videos, podcasts, articles and ideas to anyone in the world who'd want to read or watch them. He didn't want anything in return for this, he was just rich and he loved you. … the reality is that someone is making these offers to you. There are free opportunities to get your videos, audio, articles, images, and ideas out to the world." (S1, Ch 6, line 1882)

The frame is a thought experiment: if a billionaire uncle offered to distribute your content worldwide for free, refusing would be irrational. YouTube, LinkedIn, Wordpress, etc. are that uncle. Most people refuse the offer — they don't publish — because they treat the distribution as "social media" rather than as the free global-distribution gift it actually is. The KPI move is to accept the offer and use it.

## Ethical boundary

> **"Whatever you put online is potentially there for life. Getting it wrong can leave you with content that keeps coming back to haunt you."** (S1, Ch 8, line 2323)

Three principles set the floor for ethical use of Profile:

1. **Permanence rule** — anything you publish can come back; treat every post as if a future major client will read it. Amateur efforts blow the gift of a captive audience and damage rather than build the brand (S1, Ch 8, line 2323).
2. **Amplification cap** — "social media is a microphone." If the pitch is weak, amplification doesn't help — it makes the weak message louder and "you might even be damaging your brand" (S1, Ch 6, line 1969). Sharpen the pitch *before* turning up the volume.
3. **Long-term reputation beats short-term hustle** — Harrington built a **thirty-year reputation as "fair and professional"** in an industry "known for speed, hustle and sometimes cut-throat behavior," and as a result was usually the first person called when a hot deal came along (S1, Ch 6, line 1877). Profile compounds; so does reputation damage.

## Common Profile mistakes

- **Mistake: Being invisible online / no Google profile.**
  - *Why it fails:* "If you're invisible, you won't complete bigger deals no matter how good your pitch." People who meet you will question your value if you don't come up in a Google search (S1, Ch 6, line 853 / line 1877).
  - *Fix:* Build a coherent profile across the six channel-types (YouTube, Slideshare, Facebook, LinkedIn, Wordpress, Twitter — or current equivalents). Make videos, blogs, photos and group memberships findable and on-message.
  - *Citation:* S1, Ch 6 "Becoming Google-able / overview", line 853 / 1877.

- **Mistake: Posting amplified bad content / off-message social media.**
  - *Why it fails:* Social media is a microphone — "a bad singer becomes a louder bad singer." Most people get busy with the technology and drift off message. Whatever you put online is potentially there for life and can come back to haunt you (S1, Ch 1, line 464; Ch 6, line 1970; Ch 8, line 2323).
  - *Fix:* Get the pitch right first. Make sure content represents you well; never amplify a message you haven't tested. Sharpen the singer before you turn up the microphone.
  - *Citation:* S1, Ch 6 "Becoming Google-able / The best is yet to come", line 1970.
