# Pre-send checklist

Run this before any high-stakes message, conversation, or call. Five minutes; saves weeks of cleanup.

## Stage 1 — name the situation (30 seconds)

Which is this?

- [ ] **Building rapport** (no ask yet) → emphasize Part 2
- [ ] **Making a request** (you need them to do something) → Part 1.3 + Part 3
- [ ] **Defending a position** (they're pushing back on you) → Part 3
- [ ] **Correcting someone** (they did something wrong) → Part 4
- [ ] **Receiving criticism** (they're correcting you) → 3.3 + 3.9

Different parts of the book apply to each. Don't mix scripts.

## Stage 2 — Part 1 hygiene check (the killers)

Read your draft. Does it:

- [ ] Criticize, condemn, or complain about the recipient or anyone they care about? → **rewrite**
- [ ] Lead with what *you* want? → **rewrite, lead with their interest**
- [ ] Contain flattery (generic praise that would work on anyone)? → **replace with specific appreciation or remove**

If any of these flagged, fix before going further. Parts 2–4 can't compensate for these.

## Stage 3 — Part 2 opener

Does the first 1–3 sentences include:

- [ ] Their name (correctly spelled)?
- [ ] Something specific you genuinely appreciate, are curious about, or noticed about them?
- [ ] Their topic, not yours?

Add what's missing.

## Stage 4 — pick your tactic

### If making a request (Part 1.3 + Part 3):
- [ ] What does *they* want — what problem are they trying to solve?
- [ ] How does my ask help with that?
- [ ] Have I led with their benefit, not mine?
- [ ] Am I asking, not telling? (3.7 — let them feel ownership)

### If defending a position (Part 3):
- [ ] Did I avoid "you're wrong" / "actually" / "no" as openers? (3.2)
- [ ] Did I acknowledge what's true in their view first? (3.9)
- [ ] If I'm wrong on any point, did I admit it before they made me? (3.3)
- [ ] Am I asking questions or making assertions? (3.6 — let them talk)

### If correcting someone (Part 4) — full ladder:
- [ ] Praised something specific and real first? (4.1)
- [ ] Avoided "but" — used "and" instead? (4.2)
- [ ] Mentioned my own analogous mistake? (4.3)
- [ ] Phrased it as a question, not an order? (4.4)
- [ ] Are we in private? (4.5 — never correct in public)
- [ ] Made the fix seem small and doable? (4.8)
- [ ] Framed it in terms of *their* benefit? (4.9)

## Stage 5 — read it back as them

Read the message *as if you were the recipient*.

- [ ] Do I feel respected?
- [ ] Do I feel free to disagree?
- [ ] Do I understand what's being asked of me?
- [ ] Would I do the thing willingly, or would I do it grumbling?

If any of those are no, soften.

## Stage 6 — Lincoln's pause

If the message contains anything you wrote in anger, frustration, or hurt:

- [ ] **Save it as a draft. Wait until tomorrow.** (Lincoln's letter to Meade was the right letter to write — and the right letter not to send.)

If after sleeping you still want to send it: send it. Carnegie's principle is not "never speak hard truths." It's "never speak them in a moment of pride or anger that you'll regret."

---

## The thirty-second version

If you only have time for one pass:

> 1. Their name, something specific you appreciate.
> 2. Their interest first, your ask second.
> 3. No "but," no "you're wrong," no "you should have."
> 4. End with a question, not a demand.

That's the book in four lines.
