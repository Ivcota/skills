# Epiphany Bridge

Build a finished Epiphany Bridge story the way Brunson teaches it: five phases, fourteen questions, mini-interview per phase, final story in two lengths (full + 30-second).

**Output is conversation-only.** No file is saved. The Final Story block at the end is the deliverable — user copy-pastes it.

## Pace and shape

- **Mini-interview per phase** — 2-4 questions per phase, not strict one-Q-at-a-time, not a single batched prompt.
- **Generation-heavy** — once the user gives raw facts, propose 2-3 concrete phrasings for each story beat. User picks / edits / rejects. Don't ask open questions and wait.
- **Each phase ends with an explicit exit** — a one-line beat carried forward into story assembly.
- **Backtracking allowed** — user can say "go back to Phase N" any time. Replay forward, don't restart.
- **Tag process moves, not story prose** *(book)* or *(workflow)*. Use tags on prompts, pushes, and explanations so the user trusts what's Brunson vs operationalization. Do not clutter final story prose with tags.
- **Voice-match.** Write beats in the user's natural register, not corporate copywriter voice. If they say "I was broke", don't render it as "I was facing significant financial headwinds."

**Load `PATTERNS.md` once at intake.** Reference it during every phase that requires generation — desire archetypes (win/retrieve/escape/stop), villain archetypes, internal-struggle prompts, guide types, epiphany phrasings, framework naming patterns, achievement-vs-transformation contrast, and the canonical potato gun exemplar threaded across all five phases.

## Intake (always, before Phase 1)

Ask both:

1. **Use case** — "Where will this story live? (podcast intro / webinar opener / sales video / ad / email / Facebook Live / keynote / one-on-one pitch). This shapes length and which sub-stories we tee up."
2. **The aha** — "In one sentence: what's the big epiphany this story leads to? (The 'new opportunity' you want listeners to believe in by the end.)"

Lock the epiphany before Phase 1. *(book — the whole story is reverse-engineered to deliver this aha.)* If the user can't name it, run a 2-3 question micro-probe ("What's the one shift you want them to make? What were they believing before? What do you need them to believe after?") and propose 2-3 candidate epiphanies. They pick one.

## Phase 1 — The Backstory *(book Phase #1)*

**Frame:** "We open at the moment before your aha — same place your listener is sitting right now. Three beats: where you were, what you wanted (external + internal), what you'd already tried that didn't work."

Mini-interview (3 questions):

1. **Setting + character moment** — "Where were you in life right before this all started? (Year, role, relationship, where you lived — one vivid scene.)"
2. **Desire — external** *(book — what you say out loud)* — "What did you want, on the surface? (Money, the body, the clients, the freedom.)" Pin it to one of the four archetypes *(book)*: **win** something / **retrieve** something lost / **escape** something / **stop** something. See `PATTERNS.md → Desire Archetypes`.
3. **Desire — internal** *(book — the vulnerable one)* — "Underneath that, what did you really want? Who were you trying to be, or stop being? What would the external thing have meant about you?" If the user gives a surface answer, gently push once: "And underneath that?" *(book — internal desire is where rapport gets built; don't let it stay shallow.)* See `PATTERNS.md → Internal Struggle Prompts`.

Then **old vehicle** *(book)* — "What had you already tried to get this, that didn't work?" Aim for 2-3 specific failed attempts (eBay, computer parts, MLM, diets, etc.). Vague is fine — what matters is the wall-hit moment.

**Generation:** Propose 2-3 candidate phrasings of the opening beat. Each should feel like the user could read it aloud without flinching.

**Exit beats:**
- One-line setting
- External desire (win/retrieve/escape/stop tagged)
- Internal desire
- 1-3 old vehicles that didn't work

**Completion criterion:** all exit beats are captured with concrete, user-approved wording before advancing.

---

## Phase 2 — The Journey / Conflict *(book Phase #2)*

**Frame:** "Something happens that turns 'I should try again' into 'I must try again' — and we meet the villain that's been keeping you stuck."

Mini-interview (3 questions):

1. **The call** *(book)* — "What pushed you to try one more time, despite everything that hadn't worked? (Event, conversation, hitting bottom, seeing someone else's result, a feeling.)"
2. **The villain** *(book — usually a false belief, an industry norm, or a system, not a literal person)* — "Who or what was actually fighting you? Name the enemy you want listeners to side against." See `PATTERNS.md → Villain Archetypes`. Push back on weak villains ("myself", "fear") and pull toward externalized ones ("the diet industry's calorie-counting lie", "Google changing the rules", "the guru tax").
3. **What if** *(book — stakes)* — "If this attempt didn't work either, what was the worst-case scenario? What would you have lost, become, or had to do? (Quit, get a job you hated, lose the relationship, stay stuck for 10 more years.)"

**Generation:** Draft the call moment + villain framing + stakes in 2-3 sentences. Two variations. User picks.

**Exit beats:**
- The call (one sentence — what triggered the attempt)
- Named villain
- Stakes ("if this fails, then ___")

**Completion criterion:** all exit beats are captured with concrete, user-approved wording before advancing.

---

## Phase 3 — New Opportunity *(book Phase #3)*

**Frame:** "This is the aha. Your only job here is to make the listener feel exactly what you felt the moment you got it."

Mini-interview (3 questions):

1. **The guide** *(book — can be a person, a book, a podcast, a sermon, a mentor, a chance comment, a divine impression)* — "Who or what handed you the missing piece?" If a person — name + 1-line context for who they are.
2. **The epiphany** *(book — the aha itself)* — "What did they show you / say to you? What was the shift?" Push for the **specific mechanism**, not the abstraction. Bad: "I realized funnels were the answer." Good: "He said it was like McDonald's — after the Big Mac, they ask if you want fries. He'd added an upsell page; 1 in 3 buyers took it; he could now outspend everyone on ads." See `PATTERNS.md → Epiphany Phrasing`.
3. **The new opportunity** *(book — the tangible thing that came out of the aha)* — "What did you go build, try, or create the moment you got it?" This is the **opportunity switch** — old vehicle (Phase 1) → new vehicle.

**Generation:** Stage the scene. Where were you when the guide delivered the aha? What were you doing? Propose 2 vivid renderings of the moment.

**Exit beats:**
- Guide (name + context)
- The epiphany in one short paragraph — written so a listener has the same aha you had
- The new vehicle

**Completion criterion:** all exit beats are captured with concrete, user-approved wording before advancing.

---

## Phase 4 — The Framework *(book Phase #4)*

**Frame:** "You proved it on yourself, then on others. Listeners need both — your guinea-pig results plus case studies — to believe the framework isn't a one-off."

Mini-interview (3 questions):

1. **Strategy / framework** *(book)* — "What system did you build out of the epiphany? Does it have a name or steps?" If not named, propose 2-3 names *(workflow — Brunson always names frameworks)*. See `PATTERNS.md → Framework Naming`. Keep step count to 3-7.
2. **Your results** *(book — the human guinea pig)* — "What happened when you applied it? Specific numbers, timeline, scope." Push for concrete metrics (leads, revenue, weight lost, time saved). Vague = unbelievable.
3. **Others' results** *(book — case studies)* — "Who else has used this, and what happened for them?" Get 2-4 named case studies if possible. *(book — pick which case study to share based on who you're telling the story to; capture multiple now, we'll tag by audience.)* See `PATTERNS.md → Case Study Tagging`.

**Generation:** Draft a tight framework reveal (3-5 sentences) + a results block. Two variations.

**Exit beats:**
- Named framework (with 3-7 steps if applicable)
- Your concrete results (numbers)
- 2-4 case studies, each tagged by audience type

**Completion criterion:** all exit beats are captured with concrete, user-approved wording before advancing.

---

## Phase 5 — Achievement and Transformation *(book Phase #5)*

**Frame:** "Two payoffs. Achievement = what you got (resolves external desire from Phase 1). Transformation = who you became (resolves internal desire from Phase 1)."

Mini-interview (2 questions):

1. **Achievement** *(book — external resolution)* — "Where are you now, externally? What did the new opportunity get you?" Mirror back to Phase 1's external desire — close the loop. (Phase 1: "I wanted to make money to support my wife." Phase 5: "My wife retired from her job.")
2. **Transformation** *(book — internal resolution, the more important one)* — "Who did you become through this? What identity died? What identity replaced it?" Mirror back to Phase 1's internal desire. *(book — this is the part most people skip and it's the part that makes the story stick.)* See `PATTERNS.md → Transformation Contrast`.

**Generation:** Draft the close in 3-4 sentences. The achievement line should land first, then the transformation line should land harder. Two variations.

**Exit beats:**
- Achievement (external close)
- Transformation (internal close)

**Completion criterion:** both loops close with concrete, user-approved wording before final assembly.

## Final Assembly — The Deliverable

Now stitch the exit beats into two finished cuts.

### Cut 1 — Full Epiphany Bridge

Render all 14 question answers as prose, in order, in the user's voice. Target length is set by the **use case** from intake:

- **Ad / email / Facebook Live opener:** 200-400 words
- **Podcast intro / sales video / speaking opener:** 500-900 words
- **Webinar Story segment / keynote:** 1000-1800 words
- **Long-form (interview, autobiography excerpt):** unbounded — fully expand every beat

Use this scaffold:

```
━━━ EPIPHANY BRIDGE — FULL ━━━

[PHASE 1 — Backstory]
[Setting + character] [External desire] [Internal desire] [Old vehicles]

[PHASE 2 — Journey/Conflict]
[The call] [Villain] [Stakes]

[PHASE 3 — New Opportunity]
[Guide] [Epiphany — same aha for the listener] [New vehicle]

[PHASE 4 — Framework]
[Named framework + how it works] [Your results — concrete] [Others' results — case studies]

[PHASE 5 — Achievement & Transformation]
[Achievement — closes external loop] [Transformation — closes internal loop]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Cut 2 — 30-Second Compression *(book — Brunson's own potato gun compression as template)*

One-line-per-phase version. Mandatory regardless of use case — every Epiphany Bridge ships with a short cut for when you don't have ten minutes.

```
━━━ EPIPHANY BRIDGE — 30 SECOND ━━━

Backstory:        [one line — desire + why it mattered]
Journey:          [one line — what you tried, what stopped it]
New Opportunity:  [one line — who gave you the aha + what it was]
Framework:        [one line — what you built]
Achievement:      [one line — external + internal payoff fused]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Audience-tagged sub-stories *(book — pick case study based on listener)*

If Phase 4 captured multiple case studies, list them grouped by audience type. The user picks which one to swap in when retelling to a specific listener:

```
SUB-STORIES (swap by audience)
• Speaking to [audience type A] → use case study: [name + 1-line result]
• Speaking to [audience type B] → use case study: [name + 1-line result]
```

Tell the user: "You now have one story you can stretch from 30 seconds to two hours. Use the Full cut for webinars / sales videos / keynotes; use the 30-Second cut for cold opens, ads, intros, and answering 'so what do you do?' Swap sub-stories by audience."

## Rules

- **One epiphany per story.** Don't bundle multiple ahas. If the user has more than one, run the skill again.
- **Internal desire is not optional.** *(book — this is where rapport happens.)* If the user gives only the external, push once. If they still won't, name it bluntly: "We can ship without it, but the story will feel like marketing instead of a memory."
- **Specific over vague.** Real numbers, real names, real settings. "I made some money" → cut. "I sold one DVD a day at $37" → keep.
- **Externalize the villain.** *(book)* Listeners can't side against "myself" or "fear." Pull toward false beliefs, broken systems, or industry lies.
- **Same aha for the listener.** *(book — Phase 3's job)* If the rendered epiphany wouldn't give a cold reader the same shift you had, rewrite it.
- **Close both loops in Phase 5.** External answer in Phase 5 must mirror external desire in Phase 1. Same for internal. If they don't pair up, the story leaks.
