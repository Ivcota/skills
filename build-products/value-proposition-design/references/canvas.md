# Canvas

**Core concept:** Use a customer profile for one segment's jobs, pains, and gains. Keep the value map separate: products and services, pain relievers, and gain creators. Treat drawn matches as hypotheses pending customer confirmation. [S3, the three subsections under “Design the offer customers would choose”]

**Why it works:** The map exposes assumptions about customer needs and value creation for discussion and testing. It complements the Business Model Canvas. [S4, “The Value Proposition Canvas” and introductory comparison]

**Key insights:**

- Jobs include functional, social, and emotional aims. Pains include costs, obstacles, and risks. Gains describe desired benefits. [S4, “Customer Jobs,” “Customer Pains,” “Customer Gains”]
- Rank pains by intensity and frequency; relate gains to customer success criteria. [S8, “Customer Pains,” “Customer Gains”]
- Map each reliever and creator to a specific customer item. Leave unmatched items visible. [S1, pp. 46–47]
- Distinguish problem-solution fit, supported needs and a proposed solution; product-market fit, demonstrated value and traction; business model fit, evidence of profitable scalability. [S1, pp. 48–49]

**Copy patterns:** Translate source prompts into questions, not untested marketing promises. Ask “What does [customer] find too costly?” [S4, “Customer Pains”]
