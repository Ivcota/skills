# Sweaty Startup Reference

Use these worksheets to turn the Sweaty Startup mental models into action. Keep answers concrete and evidence-based.

## 1. Idea filter

For each idea, answer:

| Question | Answer | Keep/Kill |
|---|---:|---|
| Does it require venture capital? |  | Kill if yes for beginner |
| Is it a brand-new business model? |  | Kill if yes for beginner |
| Is it a physical product/manufacturing play? |  | Kill if yes for beginner |
| Is it fun/sexy/high-status? |  | Be skeptical |
| Is there existing demand? |  | Keep if yes |
| Can it produce cash quickly? |  | Keep if yes |
| Can the work be delegated later? |  | Keep if yes |

## 2. Opportunity level selection

### Level 1: low capital, low skill, fast cash
Examples: cleaning, junk removal, lawn care, basic moving help, pressure washing, simple handyman work.

Best when user has:
- Little capital
- Little operating experience
- Needs revenue quickly
- Can personally do the work at first

### Level 2: some skill/capital, more niche
Examples: specialty cleaning, pest control, property services, niche B2B services, small equipment services.

Best when user has:
- $5k-$20k available or relevant tools
- Some domain skill
- Some runway
- Ability to sell a more specific customer

### Level 3: higher skill/capital/network
Examples: trades, specialty contracting, healthcare-adjacent services, logistics, self-storage, asset-heavy local services.

Best when user has:
- Meaningful capital or financing ability
- Industry knowledge or skilled hires
- Tolerance for operational complexity
- Larger upside requirement

## 3. Business quality scorecard

Score each 1-5. Higher is better.

| Factor | 1 | 3 | 5 | Score |

## 4. Ten-Minute Drill

Goal: learn whether competitors are hungry, fast, cheap, organized, or weak.

Steps:
1. Search Google Maps for the service in the target area.
2. Call 3-5 competitors.
3. Pose as a normal prospect with a plausible job.
4. Capture: answer speed, quote speed, availability, price, professionalism, follow-up.
5. Kill ideas where competitors are numerous, cheap, fast, and desperate.

### Call script

"Hi, I’m looking for help with [specific job]. Is that something you do?"

Then ask:
- "How soon could someone come out?"
- "Can you give a rough price range?"
- "How many people would handle it?"
- "How long would it take?"
- "What do I need to do to get on the schedule?"

After call, rate:

| Competitor | Answered? | Availability | Rough price | Speed | Quality signal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

## 5. Price / Speed / Quality audit

For each surviving idea, call or request quotes from 10 competitors.

### Price
Estimate revenue per man-hour:

`job price / (crew size × job hours)`

Example: $900 job / (3 people × 3 hours) = $100 per man-hour.

### Speed
Measure:
- Did they answer?
- How fast did they respond?
- How soon can they visit/quote/start?
- How quickly did they follow up?

### Quality
Look for:
- Clear communication
- Reviews and complaint patterns
- Professional quote process
- Before/after photos
- Guarantees
- Clean website and booking flow

### Wedge finder

Look for one simple way to win:
- Answer every call
- Same-day quotes
- Faster scheduling
- Cleaner communication
- Premium positioning
- Better online booking
- Better uniforms/trucks/presentation
- Transparent pricing
- Niche specialization

## 8. First-customer launch plan

Before buying equipment or building a brand, get demand signal.

1. Pick one narrow service and geography.
2. Write a simple offer: "We help [customer] get [outcome] by [method], faster/better than alternatives."
3. Create a basic landing page or Google Business Profile if needed.
4. Call/email/message potential customers.
5. Try to book paid work.
6. Rent/borrow equipment where possible.
7. Fulfill manually.
8. Document every step.
9. Improve the bottleneck.
10. Repeat until demand is obvious or idea is killed.

## 9. Sales checklist

Before call:
- Know the customer's likely pain
- Know your process
- Know risks and how you prevent them
- Know price/availability boundaries

During call:
- Ask specific questions
- Prove expertise with details, not bragging
- Set expectations clearly
- Offer useful advice
- Explain next step
- Follow up fast

After call:
- Send clear quote
- Confirm schedule
- Ask for review/referral after successful job

## 10. Hiring and delegation checklist

### First admin/ops hire

Consider when the founder is losing high-value time to:
- Scheduling
- Invoicing
- Follow-ups
- Collections
- Customer support
- Data entry
- Quote coordination

### Alignment interview question

"Imagine we are talking five years from now and you love your job here. What are you doing, how much responsibility do you have, how much are you making, and what does your team look like?"

Use answer to test fit with the actual path available.

### Delegation ladder

1. **My Job**: founder owns it, employee observes.
2. **Our Job**: employee does parts, founder reviews/coaches.
3. **Your Job**: employee owns results and decisions within clear boundaries.

Never dump high-stakes decisions without context, standards, and feedback loops.
