# Leadership Essentials

The appendix applies Essentialism to leadership. It compares Nonessentialist and Essentialist leadership across talent, strategy, empowerment, communication, accountability, and results. Source: `index_split_072.txt`.

## Use For

- Team strategy review.
- Hiring standards.
- Role clarity.
- Leadership communication.
- Accountability rhythms.
- Meeting and priority cleanup.

## Leadership Moves

| Area | Nonessentialist Pattern | Essentialist Pattern | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Talent | Hires frantically or impulsively. | Be ridiculously selective. | `index_split_072.txt` |
| Strategy | Everything becomes a priority. | Define an essential intent. | `index_split_072.txt` |
| Empowerment | Ambiguity over ownership. | Clarify each person's highest contribution. | `index_split_072.txt` |
| Communication | Talks in code or jargon. | Communicate the right things to the right people at the right time. | `index_split_072.txt` |
| Accountability | Checks in too much or checks out. | Check in gently to remove obstacles and enable small wins. | `index_split_072.txt` |

## Cases

### Jeff Weiner And FCS/FOCUS

Use fewer things done better as a leadership operating system. McKeown cites Weiner's focus on fewer priorities, communication, and decision quality/speed. Source: `index_split_072.txt`.

### PayPal And One Priority

Peter Thiel's PayPal example required employees to identify a single top initiative. Use this case when teams are splitting attention across many objectives. Source: `index_split_072.txt`.

### CEO Streamlining Direct Reports

The appendix describes a CEO who reduced ambiguity by streamlining to four direct reports with clear functional responsibility. Use this when "flexibility" is hiding unclear ownership. Source: `index_split_072.txt`.

## Leadership Diagnostic

1. Is hiring selective enough that a wrong hire is treated as costlier than an empty seat?
2. Can the team answer, "If we could only do one thing, what would it be?"
3. Does every person know their highest role and goal of contribution?
4. Is communication boringly clear and consistent?
5. Are check-ins removing obstacles and rewarding small wins?

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Result | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring to relieve pressure | Talent quality compounds downward. | Hold the bar. |
| Straddled strategy | Progress spreads thin. | Debate to essential intent. |
| Counterfeit agility | People look busy instead of owning outcomes. | Clarify responsibilities. |
| Code communication | Team guesses what matters. | Say the essential thing plainly. |
| Accountability swings | Disruption plus absence. | Gentle, frequent obstacle removal. |
