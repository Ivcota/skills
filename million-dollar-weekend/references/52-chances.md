# 52 Chances This Year — Deep Reference

Source: Noah Kagan & Tahl Raz, *Million Dollar Weekend* (Penguin Random House, 2024), Ch 9 "52 Chances This Year: Using Systems and Routines to Design the Business, and Life, You Want" + closing essay "Start Again." All quotes verbatim from the source PDF.

---

## 1. The 52-Week Framing

The chapter title *is* the frame: a year is not one shot at transformation, it is **52 weekly attempts**. Kagan restates it in the closing essay:

> "*Million Dollar Weekend* empowers you to create the life YOU want to live. And you get fifty-two chances to do it this year." ("Start Again", PDF p. 149)

The operating question that closes the book:

> "Achieving your dreams comes down to one question: How many times are you willing to get back up after falling down? Entrepreneurship is nothing more than the ability to come up with ideas and the courage to try them out." ("Start Again", PDF p. 149)

This frame is what makes the rest of Ch 9 actionable — Sunday (P)review, color-coded calendar, accountability buddy are all *weekly* cadences, not annual rituals.

## 2. Happiness-As-Target Shift (the AppSumo Reset)

In 2014 AppSumo was doing ~$4M/yr and Kagan took home ~$150K. Outwardly arrived. Inwardly:

> "I made it! And I felt like shit." (Ch 9, PDF p. 131)

He had built the "stereotypical CEO" calendar instead of one he wanted. The aggressive-hire example:

> "I was told by my $10,000 a day business coach to aggressively hire more people to get even more profits. So we went from four people to twenty people in six months." (Ch 9, PDF p. 132)

Plus: a $49 PDF he didn't believe in, reduced quirky branding, formal performance reviews. After India:

> "After actually growing a multimillion-dollar business, I was doing what I thought I should be doing versus what I really wanted." (Ch 9, PDF p. 132)

He undid it. From the flight home:

> "I committed to removing friends and colleagues who were toxic regardless of their 'rockstar-ness.' I wanted to keep our product ratings systems via tacos, not stars, as everyone else does. I arranged my day so I had no meetings before noon and spent Fridays drinking happy hour (all day) with great friends like Neville." (Ch 9, PDF p. 133)

The reframe — central to the whole chapter:

> "**The challenge of your business — and your life — is designing a system that optimizes for your overall happiness.**" (Ch 9, PDF p. 134)

> "**Entrepreneurship is your chance to build your work around your life, not be swallowed up by it.**" (Ch 9, PDF p. 134)

## 4. The 4-Step Calendaring System

Verbatim (Ch 9, PDF p. 138):

1. **Put everything in a Category.**
2. **Assign a Color Code to your categories.**
3. **Schedule with color** your key weekly priorities.
4. **Perform a weekly Sunday accountability (p)review.**

> "I'm not telling you *how* to spend your time — instead, I'm giving you the systems to make sure you're allocating time toward your goals." (Ch 9, PDF p. 138)

**Color code** (Ch 9, PDF p. 139): **Blue = Work / Green = Health / Purple = Personal / Yellow = Travel.**

What the color code is for:

> "What color coding does is help me look at my calendar and instantly see: Am I spending my biggest blocks of time toward my goals? One glance gives me an immediate snapshot of the alignment of my work and priorities — and the mix of each day." (Ch 9, PDF p. 139)

> "My stated priority is to build my YouTube audience to 500,000, so why is there so little blue?" (Ch 9, PDF p. 139)

**Pro tip — front-load:**

> "Front-load your priorities, meaning if your main goal is YouTube, focus on it earlier in the week to make sure you're getting done what matters most. I get tired as the week goes on, so I put my most important tasks on Monday and Tuesday." (Ch 9, PDF p. 140)

## 5. Sunday (P)review Template — 15 min, 4 questions

The four questions verbatim (Ch 9, PDF p. 140):

**1. How do I pick what to actually do each week?**
> "Every Sunday, I spend fifteen minutes looking over the past week and setting my tasks for the next one. This is your chance to revisit your yearly goals and choose activities each week to move you closer toward your goals."

**2. How do I know if these tasks are moving me in the right direction of my goal?**
> "During my Sunday review I also take my previous Sunday's goals and see how I did against them. This is my chance to evaluate if it moved the needle toward my yearly goal. It's not to judge or shame yourself, but to keep yourself accountable and continually improve."

**3. What if I want to be lazy, do I need to schedule it?**
> "WWND: What Would Neville Do? Hell no. There are days just like Neville's when you want to do nothing. Enjoy it. Use your laziness to your advantage. Are there parts of your work you can outsource, stop doing, or find software to do for you?"

**4. How can I double down on activities that move me toward my goals?**
> "Whichever activities you love or help you with your goals, put those on repeat. My motto: The more things that are on repeat, the better. . . . If the important tasks are automatically added, you free up your brain to focus on the more complex issues that give you energy and move you toward your goals." (Ch 9, PDF p. 140)

The (P)review email is organized under Work / Health / Personal / Travel with status notes per item (`done`, `made great progress on this`, `marketing advisor moved forward, cfo this week`) (Ch 9, PDF pp. 141–142).

## 6. Accountability Buddy — "Number One Productivity Hack"

> "We make better choices and work harder when someone else is observing our behavior. Researchers call it the Hawthorne effect. I call it my number one productivity hack." (Ch 9, PDF p. 141)

> "Every Sunday for the past ten years I've sent my friend Adam Gilbert my **Sunday (P)review** email outlining everything I said I'd do last week, how much of it I accomplished, and everything I want to do in the week ahead." (Ch 9, PDF p. 141)

Adam's reply shows what a real buddy does — calls out what's missing:

> "Nice work on Black Friday, can't wait to see how that turns out. How much time are you spending on your push-ups? I don't see them listed and you said it was an important goal for you this year." (Ch 9, PDF p. 142)

Selection rule:

> "This person must reply and hold you accountable. If they never reply or don't call you out when you don't follow through, you need to find a better person." (Ch 9, PDF p. 142)

Provenance:

> "Find one person to send your weekly goals to. For the past ten years, I've worked with Adam Gilbert from mybodytutor.com every week on my yearly goals. Accountability is a superpower." (Ch 9, PDF p. 142)

## 7. Goal-Display Surfaces

> "The best way to make sure you accomplish your goals is to see them often." (Ch 9, PDF p. 137)

Kagan's placements (Ch 9, PDF p. 137):

- On the lock screen of my phone
- On a sticky on my computer
- On a text file that I look at each week
- On the mirror of my bathroom
- On a daily note that I read every day

Goal-discipline rules right above the surfaces:

> "Don't worry about doing everything in your dream year. Really think about which ones would excite you. My rule of thumb is if you're hesitating on the dream, then it shouldn't make your goals list." (Ch 9, PDF p. 137)

> "This list works for you, not the other way around. If midyear you realize something doesn't matter, change it. I aim to review and update this list only twice a year." (Ch 9, PDF p. 137)

## 8. Three Networking Levers (Never Entrepreneur Alone)

> "Ninety percent of my net worth comes from meeting people." (Ch 9, PDF p. 141)

> "**Great entrepreneurs have great entrepreneurial communities. There's no such thing as *self-made*. Everyone is team-made.**" (Ch 9, PDF p. 141)

The three levers, by chapter heading:

1. **Get an Accountability Buddy** (Ch 9, PDF p. 141) — see §6.
2. **Target Prefluencers** (Ch 9, PDF p. 142)
3. **Build Your VIP Network with Referrals** (Ch 9, PDF p. 143)

### Lever 2 — Prefluencers (compliment-first, no ask)

> "I always make an effort to connect with ambitious people BEFORE they make it. It's so much easier to connect with them, help each other, and build actual relationships." (Ch 9, PDF p. 142)

Receipts: met Tim Ferriss in 2007 *before* *The 4-Hour Workweek*; met Ramit Sethi while iwillteachyoutoberich.com was making $0 (Ch 9, PDF p. 142).

Three-principle screen (Ch 9, PDF p. 143): 1) Who's doing work you're impressed by? 2) AND who doesn't have a ton of attention and is likely to reply? 3) AND what can you do to help this person?

**Verbatim Prefluencer script** (Ch 9, PDF p. 143):

```
Hey [first name],
LOVING what you're putting out. [Insert specifically what you liked
or how it impacted your life]
Keep going!
[Your Name]
```

> "Spam is sending a message asking for something, whereas connecting and building relationships like the above script is just sending a compliment without any expectations." (Ch 9, PDF p. 143)

### Lever 3 — VIP Network via Referrals (Andrew Chen)

a16z partner Andrew Chen moved to the Bay Area at 23 knowing only Kagan, and set this goal:

> "*Meet five new people per day for my first six months in the Bay Area.*" (Ch 9, PDF p. 143)

Within a year, associated with Marc Andreessen and Eric Yuan; became a general partner at Andreessen Horowitz (Ch 9, PDF p. 143).

> "His strategy amounted to persistently reaching out, following up, and asking for referrals." (Ch 9, PDF p. 143)

Andrew's thank-you-email structure (Ch 9, PDF p. 144): highlights from the chat / follow-ups and to-dos / request to meet more people.

## 9. Andrew Chen Thank-You / Referral-Ask Template (Verbatim)

After a first meeting (Ch 9, PDF p. 144):

```
Hi Noah,
Quick thanks for meeting with me. You = awesome.
Here are 3 epic lessons I took away from our chat:
- Look into haptics — great opportunity for biz growth
  (you mentioned it as the next billion-dollar industry . . . damn!)
- "To be successful, you need to start with things that DON'T scale"
  ← Great quote you mentioned
- Companies to watch for are Mutual Mobile, Onnit, and Backlinko

Super grateful.
I'm curious: Are there 1–2 other people you believe I should meet?
Thanks again,
Andrew
```

When he gets the referral, his intro email has three points (Ch 9, PDF p. 144): short blurb about himself / value he could provide / why he was excited to meet them.

Annotated intro (bracketed notes Kagan's, Ch 9, PDF p. 145):

```
SUBJECT: Steve Smith told me about you
  [your subject line needs to grab the reader with your strongest hook,
   in this case a mutual connection]

Hi Bob,
Hope you're doing awesome this Tuesday morning!
My good friend Steve Smith said you're his #1 pick of someone I should meet next.

Plus, I love your blog — especially the article on how to make it big in SF
  [be detailed to elevate the compliment].
I've been experimenting with Meetup.com events thanks to your inspiration.
  [must be a truthful statement]

Love to talk with you about how to do marketing for your business.
  [your "gift"]

How's next Tuesday at 10 am at Coupa cafe for you? Or whatever is most
convenient for you.  [The more specific your call to action, the better]

I'd also be happy to feature you in an upcoming blog post on my site,
which has about 2,000 monthly readers.  [more value for the person]

Thanks,
Andrew

P.S. About me: Just moved to SF, recently hung out with Marc Andreessen,
Mitch Kapor, and more.  [social proof]
```

> "Telling someone why you are interesting, how you can help, and why you want to meet works like gangbusters. If you fail to include these points for the person you're reaching out to, expect to be ignored." (Ch 9, PDF p. 145)

## 10. Referral Ask to a Friend (Verbatim)

Challenge: **Ask your friends for one referral.** (Ch 9, PDF p. 146)

Step 1: "Tell me the first person that comes to mind. Who's the most impressive friend you know?"

Step 2 — send (Ch 9, PDF p. 146):

```
Hey <friend>
You are the most impressive friend I know and I'd love your help in
expanding my network.
I love: VR, 3D printers, email marketing.
Who's one person you think I should connect with?
If no one comes to mind, no pressure.
```

Why *one* person, not several:

> "I like asking for just one person to make it easier to think about. And then say no pressure on the other person vs give them an assignment of having to introduce me to someone." (Ch 9, PDF p. 146)

Step 3: "After you've had a great meeting with that person, thank your original friend and ask the new one for a referral." (Ch 9, PDF p. 146) — this is how a single ask compounds into a network.

## 11. Harry Dry / Marketing Examples Case

Worked example of Prefluencer compounding (Ch 9, PDF pp. 142–143):

> "A few years ago, I contacted Harry Dry from Marketing Examples, a young kid from England. I loved what he was doing with his newsletter. . . . Connect with no expectations."

> "Today, Harry has 100,000 subscribers on his email list, 30,000 LinkedIn followers, and 140,000 on Twitter. He's doing epic! And we're friends! It would have been harder to connect now, but I got him as a Prefluencer, so it was simple."

Lesson: a cold compliment to someone *before* they're famous becomes a peer relationship by the time they are. Network status is a function of *when* you reached out.

## 12. Sustainable Goals > Heroics

The line that limits the whole goal-setting system:

> "Over the past ten years I've tried to set super-aggressive goals, but I've found that it's better to aim for more sustainable goals. It's more impressive to find and stick with something than burn out after being impressive for one year." (Ch 9, PDF p. 137)

Supporting:

> "Another key thing is consistency — it's a GOOD thing if you're continuing goals from previous years. Also, I prefer to have fewer things to accomplish but I'm very excited to do them." (Ch 9, PDF p. 135)

> "I don't always accomplish everything on my lists every year. And that's okay." (Ch 9, PDF p. 137)

This is the inverse of the "4 → 20 people in 6 months" hiring story at the top of the chapter — the lesson learned by undoing it.

## 14. Dream Year Worksheet — Vision → Weekly Schedule

The Ch 9 workflow: **Dream Year → Yearly Goals (4 categories) → Color-Coded Calendar → Sunday (P)review.** Each step is a Challenge in the book.

### Step A — Dream Year Checklist (Ch 9, PDF p. 135)

> "Imagine your best year ever. Close your eyes. Picture eating Chipotle with all the guacamole you want, you're making your Freedom Number, you spend half your day researching plants 'cause you love it, and you get to live in multiple places. Your dreams can become reality only if you think about what you really want."

Kagan's own list spans business / lifestyle / health / travel: "AppSumo is THE marketplace for software and easily grows to $30 million" / "Grow YouTube channel to 500,000 subscribers" / "Get an airplane pilot's license" / "Fly my parents to spend time with me in Europe" / "I do an RV/camper trip to the Grand Canyon . . ." (Ch 9, PDF pp. 135–136).

> "This is a DREAM YEAR. That means dreaming big and not worrying about the how." (Ch 9, PDF p. 136)

**Challenge:** Write out your Dream Year. (Ch 9, PDF p. 136)

### Step B — Turn into Goals across 4 categories

> "Categorize them into four sections: Work, Health, Personal, and Travel." (Ch 9, PDF p. 136)

Kagan's example (Ch 9, PDF pp. 136–137):

- **Work:** $30M for AppSumo / 500K YouTube subs / Finish *Million Dollar Weekend* book
- **Health:** Bike across America / 75,000 push-ups
- **Personal:** Pilot's license and fly to Albuquerque / Donate or spend with friends / Nice house in Austin
- **Travel:** One week solo / Visit a mountain biking city (Asheville, Sun Valley, Jackson Hole, Sedona) / Trip with parents and brother

**Challenge:** Yearly goals list — fill in **Work / Health / Personal / Travel.** (Ch 9, PDF p. 138)

### Step C — Color-Code the Calendar

Map the 4 categories to the 4 colors (Blue=Work, Green=Health, Purple=Personal, Yellow=Travel) and schedule weekly priorities by color. Front-load: most important goal earliest in the week.

### Step D — Sunday (P)review

15 min every Sunday, 4 questions (§5), email to your accountability buddy. This is what converts "yearly goals" into "52 weekly chances."

**Final challenge:**

> "**Find one person to send your yearly goals to.** This can be someone who invested $1 in you early on, a friend . . . anyone you trust to check in with you regularly and challenge you on your BS as they help you follow through on your promises." (Ch 9, PDF p. 137)

## How the Pieces Connect

- **Dream Year** is the only place "big" is allowed; everything downstream is sustainable consistency (§12).
- **Goals in 4 categories** mirror the **4 calendar colors** — one shared vocabulary from vision down to time block.
- **Color glance** is the diagnostic ("why is there so little blue?"); without color you can't audit alignment.
- **Sunday (P)review** is the weekly closed loop; the **accountability buddy** is the social pressure (Hawthorne effect) that makes it stick.
- **Prefluencers + Referrals** are the network-side analog: small weekly actions (a compliment, an intro ask) that compound over years (Harry Dry, Andrew Chen).
- **"52 chances"** is the permission-and-pressure frame for all of it — high frequency, low stakes per week, decisive over a year.
