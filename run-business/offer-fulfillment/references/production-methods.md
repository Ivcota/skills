# Production methods by deliverable type

Use the relevant sections to design a tailored playbook, not to generate the customer deliverable. These are practical starting frameworks except where a source is explicitly named. They do not substitute for domain expertise. Identify the method and its basis in each completed plan.

## Documents, audits, and personalized plans

Start with the decision or action the artifact must enable. Specify required evidence, how it is evaluated, and how findings become recommendations.

- **Audit:** define scope, criteria, evidence collection, comparison against criteria, severity or priority rules, and recommendation structure. Missing evidence means unknown, not automatic failure.
- **Roadmap:** connect each action to a diagnosed problem; sequence by dependencies and constraints; define owner, milestone, success measure, and review trigger.
- **Report:** separate observations, interpretations, and recommendations. Include source traceability and uncertainty where it affects decisions.

Quality: check whether a recipient can choose or act using the artifact, whether recommendations follow from evidence, and whether the plan fits available resources. A polished layout is insufficient.

## Reusable tools and resources

For templates, scripts, checklists, swipe files, and calculators, identify the moment of use and the decision or task being supported. Specify inputs, instructions, output, an example, and limits.

Plan a realistic user trial. Checklists need observable completion conditions; calculators need defined units, assumptions, and boundary cases; scripts need adaptation guidance. Define ownership and an update trigger so reusable assets do not become stale.

## Training and education — ADDIE adaptation

Use Analyze → Design → Develop → Implement → Evaluate as an iterative planning structure:

- Analyze the performance gap, learner prerequisites, and context. Determine whether lack of knowledge or skill is actually the obstacle.
- Design observable objectives, relevant practice, feedback, and assessments aligned to the desired performance.
- Plan development of learning materials and subject-matter review; specify formats and accessibility needs.
- Plan learner access, instructor readiness, delivery, and follow-up support.
- Plan evaluation of learning and application, with baseline evidence where needed. Satisfaction and completion do not prove learning or business impact.

Sources: [CDC, About Training Development](https://www.cdc.gov/training-development/php/about/index.html) and [CDC Quality Training Standards](https://www.cdc.gov/training-development/php/qts/index.html). These support training design; they do not validate the offer's specific subject matter. The skill remains at design and planning even when discussing ADDIE's development or implementation stages.

## Coaching and advice

Specify intake, progress evidence, session purpose, diagnosis prompts, how to select the next intervention, commitments, and follow-up. Distinguish advice, practice, and accountability rather than treating every session as an open conversation.

Define practitioner expertise, preparation time, session cadence, between-session access, and how stalled progress is reviewed. Quality includes the usefulness and completion of next actions, not simply attendance. Do not imply that this generic process is a validated specialist coaching methodology.

## Done-for-you work

Define the work order, prerequisites, production stages, review criteria, revision boundaries, customer acceptance, and handover. Specify the specialist method for the actual work: "build a website" and "run outreach" require different production procedures.

Separate customer preferences from objective defects. Plan what happens when requested changes alter scope. Do not prescribe a craft-specific procedure without enough domain context; identify the decisions and expertise still needed.

## Software and automation

Specify user workflows, acceptance scenarios, data inputs and outputs, integrations, access needs, failure behavior, and ownership. Plan a small usable increment followed by validation in the intended environment.

Include relevant reliability checks, monitoring, maintenance, and recovery procedures. For automation, define the trigger, preconditions, action, duplicate handling, and manual fallback where applicable. A successful demonstration alone does not establish production readiness. Stop at design, backlog, and validation planning.

## Ongoing services

Define the recurring cycle: observe → interpret → act → report → review. Specify frequency, thresholds for intervention, response expectations, exclusions, and escalation. Tie activity to the customer result; report volume is not evidence of improvement.

Model onboarding separately from recurring work and define renewal, offboarding, and knowledge transfer where relevant.

## Access, communities, and connections

Specify what access entitles the customer to, availability, onboarding, participation support, moderation, and offboarding. For introductions and partner resources, identify external dependencies and verify willingness and availability before treating access as secured.

Distinguish a promised opportunity to connect from a promised third-party response or commercial outcome. Design checks for whether customers can obtain the intended benefit, not merely log in.

## Physical products

Specify product requirements, materials or components, sourcing, production or assembly, inspection, packaging, dispatch, and returns. Plan a sample or prototype check before scaling purchases.

Identify supplier lead times, inventory dependencies, defect handling, and product-specific expertise or requirements that need verification. Model replenishment and fulfillment separately; an available supplier is not proof of available stock.

## Experiences and events

Define the intended participant result, agenda, venue or platform, staffing, attendee preparation, run-of-show, and follow-up. Map dependencies such as bookings, equipment, facilitators, and headcount.

Plan a walkthrough or rehearsal and relevant contingencies. Distinguish per-event costs and workload from per-attendee costs and workload. Evaluate both operational execution and the intended participant result.

## Mixed components

Example: a "90-Day Growth Accelerator" might contain an audit, roadmap, CRM setup, and coaching. Give each a suitable method, connect their inputs and outputs, and share intake and result tracking. Retain a single parent commitment so coverage and costs can be assessed without counting shared work twice.
