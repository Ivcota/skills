# Component production playbook

Adapt this structure to the component's complexity. Include enough detail for a competent operator to understand the work; leave specialist unknowns explicit.

## Identity and purpose

- Component ID and parent offer commitment.
- Customer benefit and contribution to the overall result.
- Deliverable type(s), method, and method source or adaptation label.
- Process status: proposed / piloted / established, with supporting evidence if any.
- Scope, exclusions, and unresolved decisions.

## Start conditions

- Trigger, prerequisites, input specification, and source of each input.
- Responsible role and necessary expertise.
- Missing-input response and effect on scheduling, preserving the offer's terms.

## Output specification

Define contents, format, required depth, customization, and customer use. Give acceptance criteria observable enough to distinguish an adequate output from a superficial one.

Example for a personalized roadmap: each recommendation must link to a documented finding, include a next action, identify its dependency and owner, and state a success measure. This is an illustrative specification, not a universal roadmap standard.

## Production procedure

| Step | Input / evidence | Action and decision rule | Responsible role | Output / next handoff | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Explain the consequential decisions. Replace "prioritize recommendations" with a relevant rule, such as resolving prerequisite failures before dependent work, then comparing expected effect, evidence strength, and feasible effort. Use rules suited to the actual domain.

## Delivery and follow-through

- Review and acceptance process, including revisions.
- Delivery channel, explanation, and customer next action.
- Customer adoption or result check, with timing and owner.
- Likely exceptions, response, and escalation.

## Operating requirements

- Tools, reusable assets, and supporting processes.
- One-time setup effort versus per-customer, per-cohort, or periodic effort.
- Active work versus elapsed time; estimated ranges and their basis.
- Relevant material or vendor costs and shared capacity dependencies.
- Maintenance owner and revision trigger.

## Pilot and refinement plan

Define a representative trial, what will be observed, and the criteria for revising the process. Include output quality, operator effort, customer effort, and outcome evidence appropriate to the component. State what the pilot cannot establish.

User acceptance of the design is not evidence of successful delivery. Keep proposed processes labeled accordingly until observations support an update.

## Implementation backlog entry

For each missing asset or capability, record what must be created or established, the relevant specification, dependencies, proposed owner, completion criteria, and whether it is needed before the first delivery. This is a build brief; do not create the asset during this planning workflow.
