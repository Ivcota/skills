# Running Lean

Ash Maurya's third edition follows Design, Validation, and Growth to move an idea toward a working business model. It favors continuous customer learning over building an assumed solution and hoping customers want it. [S1, Introduction, “How This Book Is Organized”; “What Determines Success Isn’t Differing Skill Sets But Differing Mindsets”]

## Core Principle

Treat the business model—not merely the solution—as the product. Love the problem, use traction as the goal, and prioritize the riskiest assumptions at the appropriate stage. [S1, Introduction, “Succeeding in the New World Requires New Mindsets”]

Before building an MVP, discover customer struggles, define a better outcome, demonstrate the solution, and test an offer through buying commitments. Keep talking to customers after launch; metrics reveal what is happening but do not explain why. [S1, Introduction, “Don’t Start with an MVP”]

**The foundation:** “Your business model is the product.” [S1, Introduction, “Succeeding in the New World Requires New Mindsets”]

## The Running Lean Framework

1. **Design** — Deconstruct the idea, stress-test desirability/viability/feasibility, then communicate the model. See [design.md](references/design.md). [S1, Introduction, “Part 1: Design”]
2. **Validation** — Use staged Model–Prioritize–Test cycles and Demo–Sell–Build to establish problem/solution fit. See [validation.md](references/validation.md). [S1, Introduction, “Part 2: Validation”]
3. **Growth** — Launch in stages, deliver customer progress, establish repeatability, then pursue growth. See [growth.md](references/growth.md). [S1, Introduction, “Part 3: Growth”]

## Running Lean Process

Follow the author's progression; loop back when evidence contradicts the model. The prompts below are operational adaptations of the linked chapters, not a separate framework.

1. **Locate the stage and existing evidence.** Distinguish a modeled assumption, an observed customer story, a buying commitment, and delivered value. Use [Design](references/design.md) and [Validation](references/validation.md).
2. **Model the business.** Sketch customer-specific Lean Canvas variants; keep the solution provisional. See [Design](references/design.md), chapter 1.
3. **Stress-test the model.** Examine switching motivation, minimum success economics, and a feasible traction/stage roadmap before treating a model as validated. See [Design](references/design.md), chapters 2–4.
4. **Plan the validation cycle.** Establish the traction objective, constraint, team/accountability, campaign, falsifiable hypotheses, and expected outcomes before testing. See [Validation](references/validation.md), chapters 6–7.
5. **Discover, design, and deliver an offer.** Reconstruct actual switching stories, define the smallest monetizable solution, demonstrate the promise, and request a meaningful commitment. See [Validation](references/validation.md), chapters 8–10.
6. **Review evidence and decide what follows.** Contrast expected and observed results; update the canvas and roadmap and explain the next action. See [Validation](references/validation.md), chapter 11.
7. **Deliver value before scaling.** Launch to manageable batches, learn from customer progress, improve the factory constraint, and validate the growth loop. See [Growth](references/growth.md), chapters 12–14.

Do not fabricate interview results or label assumptions as evidence. Keep suggested thresholds tied to their source context; use the [worksheet](references/checklist.md) to record what remains unknown. [S1, Introduction, “Part 2: Validation”; see cited experiment habits in [Validation](references/validation.md)]

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Start by shipping an arbitrary small solution | Small does not establish a worthwhile customer problem | Discover the problem and validate the offer first. [S1, Introduction, “Don’t Start with an MVP”] |
| Treat the canvas as fact | A model is an abstraction, not evidence | Test its assumptions. [S1, Introduction, “Part 2: Validation”] |
| Rely solely on analytics | Metrics cannot explain why customers struggle | Keep interviewing users. [S1, Introduction, “Don’t Start with an MVP”] |
| Scale immediately after launch | Repeatability has not been established | Use a stage-based launch before growth. [S1, Introduction, “Part 3: Growth”] |

## Quick Diagnostic

These are adapted audit prompts, one per part; supporting detail is in the linked reference.

| Question | If No | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Is the model coherent across customer switching, economics, and rollout? | Design assumptions are unresolved | Revise the canvas and stress tests. [Design](references/design.md) |
| Have observed customer behavior and offer commitments tested demand? | Interest may be mistaken for validation | Run discovery and offer experiments. [Validation](references/validation.md) |
| Do customers receive value repeatedly, with a validated growth mechanism? | Acquisition may mask a delivery or retention constraint | Improve value delivery before increasing growth. [Growth](references/growth.md) |
