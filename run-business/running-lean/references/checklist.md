# Running Lean application worksheet

This worksheet adapts the cited chapter guidance. It is not an author-provided scoring system; do not total checkmarks into a readiness score. Record evidence and uncertainties beside each item.

## I. Design

- [ ] State the business idea and separate customers, users, and early-adopter assumptions; create distinct canvas variants where the models differ. [Design, chapter 1](design.md#chapter-1--deconstruct-your-idea-on-a-lean-canvas)
- [ ] Record existing alternatives, switching triggers, desired outcomes, and why the promise would overcome inertia/friction. Distinguish assumptions from customer observations. [Design](design.md), chapter 2.
- [ ] Define minimum success independently of enthusiasm for the solution; estimate price, required customers, acquisition rate, and reachable leads with explicit assumptions. [Design](design.md), chapter 3.
- [ ] Chart the traction roadmap and Now–Next–Later rollout; identify the current stage rather than implementing every future capability. [Design](design.md), chapter 4.
- [ ] Prepare the pitch for its audience: customer value, business economics, or advisor feedback. [Design](design.md), chapter 5.

**Record:** current canvas variants; assumptions and evidence; switching hypothesis; economic inputs; roadmap; current riskiest issue. These are synthesized worksheet fields from [Design](design.md), chapters 1–5.

## II. Validation

- [ ] Declare the cycle's traction objective and current constraint; establish team ownership and external accountability. [Validation](validation.md#chapter-6--validate-your-idea-using-90-day-cycles)
- [ ] For each experiment, record expected measurable behavior, supporting reasoning, time box, and baseline/control before results arrive. [Validation: seven experiment habits](validation.md#seven-experiment-habits)
- [ ] Plan problem discovery → solution design → offer delivery, rather than defaulting to MVP construction. [Validation](validation.md#chapter-7--kick-off-your-first-90-day-cycle)
- [ ] Recruit people with actual experience of relevant alternatives; reconstruct past switching stories, capture customer forces, and compare clusters. Do not pitch during discovery. [Validation](validation.md#chapter-8--understand-your-customers-better-than-they-do)
- [ ] Describe Problem, Promise, Price, People, and Packaging. Validate a small monetizable outcome, not a feature bundle. [Validation](validation.md#chapter-9--design-your-solution-to-cause-a-switch)
- [ ] Assemble the customer story, demonstration, pricing rationale, and specific switching CTA; record qualification, objections, commitments, and actual actions. [Validation](validation.md#chapter-10--deliver-a-mafia-offer-your-customers-cannot-refuse)
- [ ] Track factory metrics and the current bottleneck; retain the real CTA and denominator when reporting conversion. Do not call accepted offers collected revenue or a generic benchmark proof of fit. [Validation](validation.md), chapter 10.
- [ ] Review what you thought/did/learned and what comes next; preserve prior models, update artifacts, and choose persevere/pivot/pause from evidence. [Validation](validation.md#chapter-11--run-a-90-day-cycle-review)

**Record:** prediction; action/outcome definition; source of target; baseline; observed result; interpretation; next decision. These are synthesized fields from [Validation](validation.md), chapters 6 and 11. Leave unobserved results explicitly unknown.

## III. Growth

- [ ] Keep acquisition running; launch manageable batches and test delivered value before broad publicity. [Growth](growth.md#chapter-12--get-ready-to-launch)
- [ ] Map lifecycle metrics to meaningful actions and compare cohorts rather than cumulative signups alone. [Growth](growth.md), chapter 12.
- [ ] Improve activation and retention; map customer progress, remove friction, prompt useful actions, and reinforce progress. Do not rely on lock-in to prevent switching. [Growth](growth.md#chapter-13--make-happy-customers)
- [ ] Separate temporary boosters from a sustainable growth loop. Identify revenue, engagement-derived assets, or referrals that can fuel acquisition. [Growth](growth.md#chapter-14--find-your-growth-rocket)
- [ ] Test the selected loop's feasibility and economics; optimize a primary engine only after evidence supports it. [Growth](growth.md), chapter 14.

**Record:** value-delivery event; retention evidence; delivery constraint; candidate loop/fuel; sustainability assumptions; test result; next review. These are synthesized worksheet fields from [Growth](growth.md), chapters 12–14.

## Optional: protect learning time

## Evidence check

Before presenting conclusions, distinguish what was modeled, what customers actually did, what they committed to, and what value was delivered. Explain contradictory results rather than selectively reporting favorable news. [Validation](validation.md), experiment habits and ethical boundary; [Growth](growth.md), happy customer loop.
