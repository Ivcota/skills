# Appendix A: Bonus Material

**Core concept:** Allocate time to the actions that currently create value; bootstrapping means efficient resource use rather than refusing to spend money. Trade money for speed when the return and cash availability justify it. [S1, Appendix A, “The True Value of Your Time”; “How to Determine ROI”]

**Why it works:** Maker work needs uninterrupted concentration while customer and team activities use a different schedule. Organizing both deliberately reduces waiting and context switching without dropping customer learning. [S1, Appendix A, “The Conflicting Pulls for Time”; “Scaling Flow”]

**Key insights:**
- Value your unpaid time rather than treating sweat equity as free. The author's illustrative risk-adjusted hourly valuation depends on assumptions about alternative earnings, success likelihood, and deferred payment; do not treat it as a universal wage or ROI guarantee. [S1, Appendix A, “How to Value Your Time”]
- Protect maker blocks, prefer the author's early-day maker/late-day manager arrangement where it fits, and leave room for unexpected customer support. These are Maurya's working practices, not universal scheduling rules. [S1, Appendix A, “Creating Daily Flow”; “Work Hack 1: Establish uninterruptible time blocks for maker work”; “Work Hack 3: Schedule manager activities as late in the day as possible”; “Work Hack 4: Always be ready for unplanned activities—especially customer support”]
- Group customer-development activities while retaining live conversations; asynchronous tools do not replace learning about unscripted problems. [S1, Appendix A, “Creating Weekly Flow”; “Work Hack 7: Balance face time with customers”]
- Let customer demand pull feature work, keep the current traction goal central, and organize building/testing/releasing as a flow rather than disconnected queues. [S1, Appendix A, “Eliminating Product Waste”; “Work Hack 8: Avoid overproduction by making customers pull for features”; “Work Hack 9: Razor-focus on your 90-day traction goal”; “Work Hack 10: Build your product to flow”]
- Scale in source order: allocation of valuable work; automation, outsourcing non-core work and delegating core work within the team; leadership that turns specialists into experimenters. [S1, Appendix A, “Stage 1: Maximizing Yourself—Allocation”; “Stage 2: Scaling Yourself—Automation, Delegation, and Outsourcing”; “Stage 3: Scaling Beyond Yourself—Leadership”]

**Product applications:**
