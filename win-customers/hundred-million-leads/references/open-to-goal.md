# Open To Goal — The One-Page Plan and the Seven Levels

Expansion of [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) Section 8. Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads* (Acquisition.com, 2023), Section V — "Get Started." Page numbers refer to the print edition unless noted.

This is the discipline chapter. Everything else in the book — Core Four, Lead Magnets, More-Better-New, Lead Getters — only matters if you actually run it. Open To Goal is how you run it.

---

## 1. The Flyer Parable — Volume Is Not Optional

June 2014. Hormozi prints **300 flyers**, distributes them, gets zero leads (and one person claiming his Mercedes was scratched). He calls his mentor (a tanning-salon operator):

> "Shoot, you only put out 300? Hard to know if anything works with such a small number. I test with 5,000. Then when we find a winner, we put out 5,000 per day, every day, for a month." (p. 364)

The mentor's benchmark: half a percent response is decent, one percent is a winner. With 300 flyers, half a percent is 1.5 people — there is not enough signal in the data to know whether it works.

Hormozi's takeaway lands as the rule that anchors the rest of the chapter:

> "The right action in the wrong amount still fails. Most people, myself included, stop too soon. We don't do enough." (p. 365)

> "I was doing 1/1500th the level of effort required to make a flyer campaign work — I just didn't know it." (p. 365)

The reframe he gives operators who report a failed channel:

> "'Alex, I reached out to 100 people over the last six weeks, I only got one customer, it doesn't work.' Response: 'You did 1/42 of the amount of work required. **It was 100 per day, not 100 over time.**'" (p. 365)

Neil Strauss, quoted in support:

> "Success comes down to doing the obvious thing for an uncommonly long period of time without convincing yourself you're smarter than you are." (p. 365)

## 2. Never Let Effort Be The Reason

The promise Hormozi makes himself after the flyer disaster:

> "I promised myself I would never let effort be the reason anything didn't work for me. It could be something else. The offer. The copy. The image. The targeting. The media. The platform. The position of the moon. But not. my. effort." (p. 364)

This is the diagnostic principle for every dead campaign. When something fails, the first variable to rule out is volume. Only after you have unambiguously over-supplied effort do the other variables — offer, copy, list, channel — become diagnosable. Under-volume contaminates every other reading.

## 3. Open To Goal — The Definition

> "A very successful gym chain allowed their sales managers to make their own schedules. But there was a catch — they had to sign up **five new members per day no matter what**. So if they did it by lunch, they could cut out early. But if it took 18 hours, so be it. They called this type of work schedule 'open-to-goal'." (p. 366)

Open To Goal = **Rule of 100 on steroids**. Rule of 100 commits to inputs (100/day for 100 days). Open To Goal commits to *outcomes* and refuses to stop until they're hit.

> "You don't just commit to doing something a specific number of times. You commit to the work until you hit a specific number of outcomes — no matter what. So it means you unlock a whole new level of effort you never even realized you had. It might mean only doing something fifty times to get the desired result. Or, like the flyers, five thousand times, every day, for years." (p. 366)

> "If you want to take your advertising to the next level — work until the job is done. Give up the idea of 'doing your best.' Instead, do what is required. And sometimes that means your best just needs to get better." (p. 366)

## 4. Hormozi's Habit Stack

His three highest-ROI habits, in order (p. 367):

1. **Wake up early (4–5 am).** "Pro tip, this actually means going to bed early."
2. **Get right to work.** "No rituals. No routines. I drink coffee and get to work."
3. **No meetings until noon.** "No interruptions. Nothing. Fully focused work time."

The math: 4–5 am to noon is 7–8 uninterrupted hours of the most productive work, on the highest-leverage activity (advertising), on top of a full night's sleep. Every day.

> "I don't think there's any magic to waking up early. But I do think there's magic in a long stretch of uninterrupted work immediately after a long stretch uninterrupted sleep. After all, it's the most productive hours in a row of the most productive work I can do, with nothing getting in my way. Every. Single. Day. How can you lose?" (p. 367)

> "Waking up early, getting right to work, and working 8 hours straight has been my highest ROI 'habit stack.' By a longshot. And for those of you thinking 'Wait! That's more than twelve hours of work a day!' — you're right. I'm playing to win. But if it overwhelms you at first, I get it. Just throttle it back a few hours and then work your way up." (p. 367)

The line he comes back to at low points:

> "Whenever I get to a low point where I think 'why do I even bother?' I just try to remind myself: this is where most people stop, and this is why they don't win." (p. 368)

> "Do more than they do, and you will have more than they have." (p. 368)

What goes in the block: advertising. Not email. Not meetings. Not "strategy."

> "Since my job is usually to 'get more customers' in most of my companies, advertising is what I focus on. This book, for example, was written exclusively in that open to goal time block. Why? Because it's an asset that can bring me more companies." (p. 368)

## 5. The One-Page Advertising Plan (5 Steps, pp. 369–371)

The simplest plan Hormozi can give. It lives inside the open-to-goal block.

1. **Pick the type of engaged lead** — Customers, Affiliates, Employees, or Agencies.
2. **Pick Rule of 100 or Open To Goal.** Commit to your daily advertising actions. The Rule-of-100 grid: Warm Outbound = reach outs; Cold Outbound = reach outs; Warm Inbound = minutes of content; Cold Inbound = dollars of paid ads. Bonus: do 100/day on more than one of the Core Four.
3. **Fill out the Advertising Daily Checklist** for that daily action (table below).
4. **Do this daily action until you have enough money to afford paying someone else to do it.**
5. **When you do, go back to step 1.** Make employees your new target lead type. Repeat steps 1–4 until you have the help you need. Then scale again.

> "Many skip planning, or worse, they write a one hundred page plan that never gets used. So skip the atrocious waste of time that is writing pages of baloney. Harness the power of laying out your action steps on a single page. It leaves little room for excuses, distractions, and delusions. You either did the stuff or you didn't. You can fill out your one page advertising checklist in about five minutes. And once the naked truth stares back at you, all you have left is to do it." (pp. 371–372)

## 6. The Daily Checklist (10 Rows)

| Field | Default value |
|---|---|
| **Who** | You |
| **What** | Your offer or lead magnet |
| **Where** | Platform |
| **To Whom** | Audience / list |
| **When** | First 8 hours |
| **Why** | Get X engaged leads or lead getters |
| **How** | Warm/Cold outreach, content, ads |
| **How Much** | 100/day or until you hit your goal |
| **How Many** | # of follow-ups / times retargeted |
| **How Long** | 100 days or until you hit your goal |

Five minutes to fill out. Single page. The point isn't elegance — it's that the page either gets done or it doesn't, and you can't pretend either way.

## 7. The Seven Levels of Advertisers (pp. 373–376)

The roadmap. Diagnose the level first, then execute the Primary Action for that level. Do not skip levels.

| Level | Headline | Primary Action |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Your friends know about the stuff you sell. | Warm outreach. |
| **2** | You consistently let everyone you know about the stuff you sell. | "Do as much warm outreach and post as much content as you can consistently." |
| **3** | You get employees to help you do more advertising. | "You hire people to advertise profitably on your behalf." |
| **4** | Your product is good enough to get consistent referrals. | Focus on product until referrals are consistent (~25%+ of customers), *then* scale advertising with a bigger team. |
| **5** | You advertise in more places in more ways with more people. | "Advertise profitably using at least two methods on multiple platforms." |
| **6** | You hire killers. | "Get battle-hardened executives and department heads to take over new advertising activities and channels." |
| **7** | (Unwritten — Hormozi has not yet crossed a billion.) | — |

**Level 1 — Friends know.** "To start getting engaged leads, you make one offer, to one avatar, on one platform. The moment you get engaged leads, is the moment you can start making money. For me, this started with reaching out to everyone I knew." (p. 373)

**Level 2 — Consistently telling everyone.** "You know the exact inputs to get an engaged lead with your chosen advertising method. And, by scaling those inputs, you get consistent customers with it. But the consistent customers come from maximizing your personal work capacity. For me, on top of warm reach outs, I maximized my personal work capacity with paid ads, using a case study as my lead magnet. **But looking back, I wish I would have started with posting free content. So I suggest that.**" (p. 374)

**Level 3 — Employees help.** "You've maxed your personal advertising inputs, but not the platform. And if you want more engaged leads that can only mean one thing. Doing more. For me, I hired a videographer and a media buyer to take most of the paid ads work off my plate." (p. 374)

**Level 4 — Consistent referrals.** "This is when I realized that my ads were shut off but I was still getting referrals every week. So, I doubled down on referrals. I built goodwill using customer feedback to update my product every two weeks. I also started a strong referral program with big incentives at the same time." (pp. 374–375)

> **The Level 4 Trap:** "Focus on your product until you get consistent referrals then go back to scaling your advertising with a bigger team. **This is where most people mess up. They let their product slip and never recover.**" (p. 375)

When referrals start coming in, the temptation is to push more leads through a product that is already showing strain. Don't. Fix the product first. Goodwill is the only compounding asset that doesn't cost you per unit, and it dies fastest when the product slips.

**Level 5 — More places, more ways, more people.** "First, you expand to new audiences on your best platform. Then, you make ads with all placements and media types the platform supports. And, after your team can get consistent results, you expand your team again to add: another platform, lead-getter, or core four activity. For me, I hit two birds with one stone. I expanded my paid ads to include potential affiliates. And this paved the way for my affiliate programs." (p. 375)

**Level 6 — Hire killers.** "Your executives grow departments specific to an advertising method or platform without you. And you're not looking for potential. You're looking for experienced leaders specializing in exactly what you want. We capped here." (p. 375)

> **Level 6 Pro Tip — Hire Experience, Not Potential:** "I tried cold reach outs twice before it worked the third time. The main difference: the person I hired to run it. Pick experience. They should know more than you do. **If you're not learning from them in the interview, you've got the wrong person.**" (p. 376)

**Level 7 — Intentionally blank.** "I'll come back and edit this chapter once I cross a billion. I promise, I'll send the lessons as soon as I have them. You have my word." (p. 376)

A note on the messiness:

> "I know this looks clean. But it never is. Real business is messy. It takes a lot to find what audiences, lead magnets, methods, and platforms work best. And you can only find out what works if you try. So you have to try a lot of different things, a lot of different ways, for a long enough time to know for sure." (p. 376)

## 8. A Decade In A Page — The Whole Book in Seven Bullets (pp. 380–381)

Hormozi's back-of-the-napkin recap of the entire book:

1. **How to define a lead.** Engaged leads, not just leads.
2. **How to turn leads into engaged leads** with an offer or lead magnet — and how to make them.
3. **The Core Four** — the only four ways to let people know about the stuff we sell:
   a. Warm outreach: ask people who know us if they know anybody.
   b. Post publicly: hook, retain, reward. Give until they ask.
   c. Cold outreach: lists, personalization, big fast value, volume.
   d. Paid ads: targeting, callouts, What-Who-When, CTAs, client-financed acquisition.
4. **Maximizing the Core Four — More Better New.** What keeps me from doing the current thing at 10x volume? Find the constraint, free it, scale until it constrains again.
5. **The Four Lead Getters** — Customers, Employees, Agencies, Affiliates. Get customers to refer; get employees to scale advertising without you; get an agency to teach you new skills; get affiliates launched and integrated.
6. **Open To Goal + Rule of 100** — the five-step one-page plan to get more leads today.
7. **The Seven Levels of Advertisers** and the *$100M Leads* machine in action.

## 9. Time Horizon — Five to Ten Years

> "How long does this take? For business owners who know what to do — anywhere from five to ten years. Building something great, even if you know exactly what to do, takes time. And so many like to trumpet 'overnight success,' but looking behind the curtain tells a different story. **It took my wife and I more than ten years of our best effort, to cross the first $100M in net worth.** So the bigger your goals, the longer your time horizons need to be. You want to play games where if you wait, you win." (p. 378)

> "Entrepreneurship isn't for the faint of heart. The load is heavy and the road is long." (p. 378, tweet inset)

## 10. The Many Sided Die — The Closing Parable (pp. 382–386)

Two players, two dice. One has 20 sides, one has 200. Each die has exactly one green side. Roll green: that die gains another green side, and you roll again. Roll red: nothing changes, but you roll again. Stop rolling: you lose.

The 20-sided player has a 1-in-20 shot per roll. The 200-sided player has a 1-in-200 shot. But every green roll widens the green for next time. The only losing move is to stop rolling.

Hormozi's two guarantees of the game:

> "1) The more times you roll, the better you get.
> 2) If you quit, you lose." (p. 386)

The application:

> "Learning to advertise is a lot like the game of the many sided die. You do not know if it will work until you try. And when you start advertising, you will probably hit red on your first rolls. But if you try enough times you will hit green. And when it works, you have a better chance of getting it to work again. The more you do it, the easier it gets. You begin to understand the game." (p. 385)

The closing line of the book proper:

> **"You cannot lose if you do not quit."** (p. 386, bold-underlined in source)

## 11. Common Mistakes — Consolidated From The Section

| Mistake | Source | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stopping too soon / under-volume | p. 365 | Rule of 100 or Open To Goal. Volume is the first variable. |
| 100 over time, not 100 per day | p. 365 | Daily concentration. The right action in the wrong amount still fails. |
| Blaming effort last instead of first | p. 364 | Never let effort be the reason. Rule it out before tweaking offer/copy/channel. |
| Writing a 100-page plan | p. 371 | One page. Five minutes. The plan is the checklist. |
| Reading instead of advertising | p. 371 | "Reading doesn't get people interested in the stuff you sell — advertising does." Close the book and run the plan. |
| Letting product slip after referrals start (Level 4 trap) | p. 375 | Pause ad scale at ~25% referral rate. Fix product first. |
| Hiring potential instead of experience for new channels | p. 376 | Hire someone who knows more than you. If you're not learning from them in the interview, wrong hire. |
| Quitting before the green roll | p. 386 | Stay in for three to six months minimum on a new channel. |
