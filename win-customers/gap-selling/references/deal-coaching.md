# Gap Deal Coaching and Pipeline Movement

Use this reference for stalled deals, pipeline coaching, opportunity audits, decision criteria, buying process, next yes, or qualification decisions.

Source refs: *Gap Selling*, Ch. 9, Ch. 12, Ch. 13, Ch. 18; see [`sources.md`](sources.md).

## Gap Value

Formula:

```text
Future State - Current State = The Gap
```

The gap is worth pursuing only when the desired outcome outweighs:

- Purchase price.
- Implementation cost.
- Time and effort.
- Risk and uncertainty.
- Political or emotional cost of change.
- Comfort with the status quo.

If the gap is too small, do not force the sale. Either:

1. **Widen the gap** through better discovery: uncover more impact, risk, root cause, or strategic consequence.
2. **Qualify out** if the offer cannot create enough value.
3. **Refer** if another solution better fits the buyer.

## Pipeline Movement Framework

To move a deal through the pipeline, know three things.

### 1. Decision criteria

Decision criteria are the factors the buyer will use to choose a solution.

Ask:

- “How are you going to decide what the best solution is?”
- “What will be the most important factors in making this decision?”

Audit:

- Do the criteria align with the future state?
- Are they solving the business problem or a side preference?
- Did a criterion appear that discovery did not uncover? If yes, go back.

Challenge pattern:

> “I’m confused. You said [desired outcome] was the priority, but [criterion] does not seem connected to that outcome. Did I misunderstand what you are trying to accomplish?”

Completion bar: every decision criterion is either aligned to the buyer's stated outcome or challenged.

### 2. Buying process

Buying process is the buyer's internal path to approval: steps, people, documents, committees, timing, legal/procurement, security, budget, and signatures.

Ask:

- “Can you tell me a little about your company's buying process?”
- “What has to happen between now and a decision?”
- “Who else needs to weigh in?”
- “What could slow this down?”

Completion bar: you know the sequence, stakeholders, timing, and required yeses.

### 3. Next yes

A sale moves by buyer commitments, not seller effort. Every step is a small sale.

A valid next yes is a buyer action that advances the sale:

- Introduction to another stakeholder.
- Agreement to a discovery call.
- Providing data, specs, current process, or documents.
- Agreement on decision criteria.
- Scheduling a demo after discovery.
- Confirming success criteria for a trial.
- Approval to meet legal, IT, finance, HR, or executive sponsor.

Invalid next yes:

- “I'll follow up.”
- “I'll check in.”
- “I'll send a proposal” without buyer commitment.
- Any seller-only activity.

Completion bar: the next yes names what the buyer will do, why they value doing it, and when it will happen.

## Deal Coaching Audit

Use this when a user asks “what should I do with this deal?” or provides deal notes.

| Check | Evidence needed | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Current state | Facts, problems, impact, root cause, emotion | Return to discovery |
| Future state | Quantified desired outcome and business effect | Ask outcome questions |
| Gap | Difference between current and future state | Quantify cost of inaction/value |
| Intrinsic motivation | Why this outcome matters now | Ask “what's driving this change?” |
| Decision criteria | How buyer will judge options | Ask and align criteria |
| Buying process | Steps, stakeholders, timing | Map internal approval path |
| Next yes | Buyer commitment | Sell the next small step |
| Evidence | Buyer words, data, CRM notes | Stop forecasting from hope |

Output format:

```text
Deal diagnosis:
- Primary risk:
- Missing information:
- Gap strength: strong / weak / unknown
- Buyer change readiness:
- Next yes:
- Recommended seller action:
- Qualify: advance / widen / hold / exit
```

## Dark or Stalled Deal Pattern

When a buyer goes dark or stalls, do not “just check in.” Return to their words, current state, and desired future state.

Pattern:

1. Start with “I’m confused. You said…”
2. Restate their problem and desired outcome.
3. Name the cost or consequence of delay.
4. Ask if something changed.
5. Ask for the next yes or a truthful no.

Example template:

> “I’m confused. You said [problem] was causing [impact], and that [future state] mattered because [intrinsic motivation]. I have not heard back, so I’m wondering if something changed. Were you able to solve [problem], or is [future state] no longer a priority?”

Completion bar: the message makes the buyer reconcile their own stated goals with their current inaction.

## Objection Audit

For any objection, ask:

- Does this objection affect the desired future state?
- If solved, would it materially improve the buyer's outcome?
- Is it a real business problem or a preference?
- Did discovery miss a stakeholder/current-state issue?

If the objection is aligned with the future state, solve or qualify out. If not, use the confusion pattern.

## Forecast Quality

A forecast is weak when based on:

- “They liked the demo.”
- “They said they are interested.”
- “I think it will close.”
- “They are getting back to me.”

A forecast is strong when based on:

- Confirmed current state and impact.
- Confirmed future state and intrinsic motivation.
- Known decision criteria and buying process.
- Clear next yes with date.
- Evidence in buyer words.

Completion bar: do not treat an opportunity as forecastable unless the buyer has taken or committed to a next yes.
