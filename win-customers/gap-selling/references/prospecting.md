# Gap Prospecting

Use this reference for outbound emails, calls, social messages, ICP targeting, cadence, or first-meeting asks.

Source refs: *Gap Selling*, Ch. 14-17; see [`sources.md`](sources.md).

## Prospecting Principle

Prospecting is the first yes. The buyer grants attention only when the message makes engagement feel in their best interest.

Do not ask for time to talk about your product. Ask for time to discuss a problem, impact, or insight that matters to the buyer.

## PIC to ICP

Use the Problem Identification Chart to identify who is likely suffering from problems you can solve.

Process:

1. List problems your product/service solves.
2. For each problem, list likely business impacts.
3. Identify root causes and evidence.
4. Ask which companies, departments, and roles are most likely to suffer.
5. Rank targets by problem count and pain level.

Target tiers:

| Tier | Condition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Many problems + high impact | Prioritize |
| **B** | Fewer problems + high impact | Prioritize after A |
| **C** | Many problems + low impact | Lower priority |
| **D** | Few problems + low impact | Ignore |

Completion bar: the ICP is based on likely pain, not company size or generic fit alone.

## Message Framework

Every prospecting message needs four elements:

1. **Intrigue** — a reason to stop and pay attention.
2. **Clear ask** — what you want the buyer to do.
3. **Valuable offer** — what the buyer gets for saying yes.
4. **Net benefit** — why the offer is worth more than the ask.

Formula:

```text
Offer - Ask = Value
```

If the ask costs 15 minutes, the offer must be worth more than 15 minutes to the buyer.

## Intrigue

Intrigue breaks the buyer's expected pattern. Create it through:

| Type | How it works | Example pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Surprise** | Unexpected observation or channel. | “Most teams think X is the bottleneck, but Y is usually where the loss starts.” |
| **Mystery** | Opens a loop the buyer wants closed. | “There are three places this process usually leaks revenue.” |
| **Knowledge gap** | Shows something the buyer may not know. | “Are you aware that [similar companies] often lose [impact] because of [problem]?” |

Use knowledge gaps ethically: base them on research, observable patterns, or a PIC hypothesis; do not fabricate fear.

## Message Template

```text
Subject: [intriguing problem/impact]

[Name],

I noticed / companies like yours often face [problem] when [context]. The impact is usually [business consequence].

If useful, I can share [specific useful offer] and help you determine whether [problem] is costing you [impact].

Would [small clear ask] be worth it to explore [buyer-valued outcome]?
```

Completion bar: the message starts with the buyer's world, not seller identity.

## Email Response Probability Scorecard

Score each 1-10:

| Element | Diagnostic |
|---|---|
| Subject line | Does it summarize the intrigue and make the buyer curious? |
| Intrigue | Does it create curiosity, challenge, surprise, or knowledge gap? |
| Offer | Is the offer valuable to the recipient? |
| Ask | Is the ask clear, fair, and proportionate? |
| Overall value | Is `Offer - Ask` positive for the buyer? |
| Length | Is it short enough to read quickly? |
| Relevance | Does it fit the recipient's objectives or anticipated issues? |
| Readability | Is it conversational, clear, and easy to engage? |

Completion bar: revise any message with weak intrigue, weak offer, or negative overall value before sending.

## Cadence Framework

Expect 8-12 touches. Use multiple channels and vary the message.

Rules:

- Use email, phone, video, LinkedIn, social, mail, or other channels based on buyer habits.
- Each touch should address a different impact, problem, insight, or offer.
- Do not repeat the same “checking in” message.
- Stay in the buyer's world; do not drift into product pitching.
- Never say no for the buyer; keep going until they respond or opt out.

Cadence skeleton:

```text
Touch 1: Problem + impact + small ask
Touch 2: Voicemail with same core problem
Touch 3: New impact or insight
Touch 4: Call or social touch
Touch 5: Different offer tied to same business issue
Touches 6-8: New evidence, research, question, or angle
Touches 9-12: Respectful challenge / opt-out prompt
```

Late-cadence challenge pattern:

> “I may be off, but I thought [problem/impact] could be important because [reason]. If this is not a priority, reply and I will stop reaching out. If it is, I can share [useful offer].”

Completion bar: cadence contains varied value across touches, not repeated seller asks.

## Prospecting Output Formats

### Single email

Return:

- Subject line.
- Email body.
- Scorecard self-score.
- Why the offer is net positive.

### Sequence

Return:

- ICP hypothesis.
- PIC assumptions.
- 8-12 touch cadence.
- Channel mix.
- Offer/ask per touch.
- Exit/opt-out language.

### Diagnosis

Return:

- What's seller-centric.
- Missing intrigue.
- Ask/offer imbalance.
- Revised message.
