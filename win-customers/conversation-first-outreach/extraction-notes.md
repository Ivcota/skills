# Extraction notes — Conversation-First Outreach v0

Source: user-provided framework notes, 2026-06-19.

## Core promise
Structure cold outreach conversations so the prospect feels safe saying no, recognizes their own problem, provides examples and impact, and accepts a low-pressure next step only after enough problem ownership exists. Key idea: **no means yes** — use no-oriented questions where “no” is the safe answer that grants permission or forward movement.

## Leading words / signature concepts
- Safety of no
- No-oriented questions / no means yes
- Negative frame
- Problem ownership
- OLQ / one last question
- Final challenge
- Gatekeeper
- Emotional peak

## Opener lines
Build an opener around candid unfamiliarity, relevance, and permission/help. Prefer no-oriented permission questions so the prospect does not feel cornered by a yes.

Examples:
1. “{Name}, we don't know each other. I'm calling as you're {title} of {company}, and I'd like to work with you. Can you help me understand if this is a good fit?”
2. “If I told you this visit/call was about {product/service/concept}, would a piece of you die inside?”
3. “Hey {name}, we don't know each other. If I told you this was a call about {problem}, you'd probably tell me {opposite}.”
4. “Would you be mad if I asked you something about {problem/context}?”

## Pitch framework
When speaking with other {title / ICP}, they mention facing problems like two or three pains with consequences:
1. {pain 1} + {consequence}
2. {pain 2} + {consequence}
3. {pain 3} + {consequence}

Then use a negative frame / safety of no:
“...but perhaps you don't have any of these issues and you're in {ridiculously positive opposite}.”

## Conversation core structure
1. Problem ownership — pitch different problems and have the prospect pick.
2. Example — “What does that look like?”
3. Impact — “What is the impact?”
4. Root cause — “Where does it come from?”
5. Summary — recap the conversation so far back to the prospect.
6. Closing.

## Objection handling
1. Acknowledge.
2. Ask permission to ask a question.
3. Challenge the objection and redirect to the problem.

## Qualifying questions
Use clarify questions. If the prospect asks the seller a question, answer by clarifying first.

Frameworks:
1. Clarify: “I'm confused. When you say {x}, what does that mean?”
2. Options: “When you say {x}, do you mean A, B, or C?”
3. The Why: “I'm guessing {x} is important to you for a reason?”

## Disqualifying questions
Goal: test if the person is a good fit without chasing.

Patterns:
- Negative frame: “You probably couldn't do {thing that would qualify them}?”
- Reduce problem: “You seem to be doing very well anyway.”
- Disqualify: “Even if we could fix that, it wouldn't be a priority, right?”

## OLQ / one last question
Use on the way out or to revive a dead conversation — the second wind.

Permission:
- “You okay if I ask one last question before I go?”
- “Can I ask one last question before I go then?”

Question options:
1. “If you could improve one thing, what would it be?”
2. “What would have to change to consider this?”
3. “Have you closed your mind to the idea that it could be done better?”
4. Tailor to the objection.

## Closing
Closing is like a marriage proposal: the close works because of the relationship and trust built before it. A bad close can undo the previous work. The goal is not to mess up.

Timing: close during the emotional peak. Signals:
1. They seem agitated about the problem.
2. They go into detail about the problem.

Minimum bar: they must have provided an example before trying to close. If possible, drill deeper on problems for more commitment.

Process:
1. Summary of the problem: “Let me see if I got this right...” Optional: reduce the problem slightly beforehand so they correct you.
2. Final challenge: “Do you mind if I ask a tough question? Have you accepted this is the way things are?”

Close examples:
- “Would it be a bad idea to have a sit down and see if I can help you?”
- “Is there any reason to not put some time aside to explore this further?”

Post-close:
1. Give one more objection: “You sure if I come back / come to the meeting you'll be like, ‘dang, I wish I never made that meeting’?” / “You sure you're not going to wake up that morning and be like, ‘dang, I don't have this issue’?”
2. Go back to the problems if needed before hangup.
3. Confirm invite.
4. Send agenda in separate invite.
5. Arrange follow-up confirmation call/text: “I'm going to give you a call/text before to confirm with you before the meeting.”

## Gatekeeper framework
Two branches: power through and get help.

### Power through
Gatekeeper: “Who is calling?”
Seller: “It's {surname}, thanks.”
Gatekeeper: “What company are you calling from?”
Seller: “{acronym}, thank you.”
Gatekeeper: “What is this call regarding?”
Seller: “{something technical enough that they won't know}.”
Gatekeeper: “Are they expecting your call?”
Seller: “I'd expect so.”

### Get help
Seller: “Hi, who am I speaking with?”
Gatekeeper: “Mark, who am I speaking to?”
Seller: “Hi Mark, my name is {x}, and I need to speak with {prospect name}. Can you put me through?”
Gatekeeper: “What is this regarding?”
Seller: “I help {job titles} fix {problems}; that's what {prospect} takes care of, right?”
Gatekeeper: “That's right.”
Seller: “Do you mind putting me through? Thanks.”
