# Fanatical Prospecting

Turn a sales target into a disciplined, multichannel prospecting operating system. Blount's central claim is that consistent daily prospecting fills the pipeline and that balanced execution, activity math, focused time, direct messaging, and mental toughness make the behavior sustainable.

## Core Principle

Treat prospecting as the controllable input to future pipeline. Keep adding qualified opportunities even while closing current deals; a seller who waits for need to appear will act desperate, while a seller who replaces opportunities continuously can stay objective.

Technique improves results, but execution comes first. Use the smallest relevant framework, produce a practical artifact, and attach measurable next actions rather than giving motivational advice alone.

## Prospecting Process

1. **Define the strike zone.** State account traits, buyer roles, buying-window signals, and disqualifiers.
2. **Set the outcome.** Name the pipeline or revenue result and its time horizon.
3. **Work backward through ratios.** Convert the outcome into sales, appointments, conversations, and touches using the seller's data.
4. **Choose a balanced mix.** Allocate phone, email, social, text, in-person, referral, networking, and inbound effort by context.
5. **Build the pyramid.** Segment the CRM by qualification, potential, probability, and buying window.
6. **Prepare objective-specific lists.** Choose appointment, information, sale, or familiarity for every block.
7. **Protect the block.** Move research and administration outside prime selling time; remove distractions.
8. **Craft the bridge and ask.** Connect a relevant reason to one specific next action.
9. **Execute and handle resistance.** Use the channel framework; apply Anchor-Disrupt-Ask to recurring RBOs.
10. **Log, review, and replace.** Record outcomes, compare efficiency with effectiveness, and replenish the pipe.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| One favorite channel | Concentrates risk and hides call reluctance | Rebalance by market and actual conversion data |
| Prospecting only when desperate | Activity pays later, after the pipe has already thinned | Protect a daily minimum |
| Random CRM calling | Wastes prime time on low-probability records | Work top-of-pyramid lists first |
| Research during the block | Perfectionism displaces conversations | Prepare in Platinum Hours |
| Feature-heavy messaging | Makes the interruption about the seller | Bridge to the prospect's reason |
| Weak or vague ask | Creates effort and ambiguity for the prospect | Offer one specific action and time |
| Arguing with pushback | Strengthens resistance | Anchor, disrupt, ask; then move on |
| Cold texting or bulk email | Feels intrusive and damages trust | Use one-to-one, context-aware outreach |

## Quick Diagnostic

| Question | If No | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Is the strike zone explicit? | Lists mix good and bad fits | Define qualification and disqualification rules |
| Do ratios connect touches to outcomes? | Goals are guesses | Build a personal conversion chain |
| Is prospecting daily? | The 30-Day Rule is being ignored | Schedule a protected minimum block |
| Are lists ranked by probability and potential? | Reps hunt through the CRM | Build the Prospecting Pyramid |
| Does each block have one objective? | Activity becomes unfocused | Choose appointment, information, sale, or familiarity |
| Does the message give a prospect-centered because? | The ask feels costly | Add a targeted or strategic bridge |
| Is the ask specific? | The prospect must design the next step | Offer a concrete action and time |
| Are common RBOs scripted and rehearsed? | Emotion controls the response | Prepare Anchor-Disrupt-Ask turns |
| Are touches and outcomes logged? | Coaching relies on feelings | Track efficiency and effectiveness |
| Are closed and lost opportunities replaced? | The pipeline rides a roller coaster | Apply replacement math every review |
