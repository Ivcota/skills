# Familiarity and Social Selling

## Core concept

Familiarity lowers the perceived risk of responding. Build it through persistent contact, trusted introductions, real-world relationships, company exposure, and a credible professional presence; use social channels to support those levers rather than replace active outbound. (Blount, chs. 12-13, pp. 96-131.)

## Five levers of familiarity

1. **Persistent, consistent prospecting:** Repeated names, messages, and touches create recognition. (Ch. 12, pp. 98-104.)
2. **Referrals and introductions:** Borrow credibility from a trusted customer, personal contact, or professional peer. (Ch. 12, pp. 98-103.)
3. **Networking:** Create genuine connections by asking, listening, following up, and logging commitments. (Ch. 12, pp. 100-103.)
4. **Company and brand familiarity:** Use marketing exposure where it exists; contribute to awareness when the company is unknown. (Ch. 12, pp. 103-104.)
5. **Personal branding:** Build recognition around relevant expertise and credibility. (Ch. 12, pp. 103-104.)

## Five Cs of social selling

- **Connecting:** Add people after real interactions and personalize requests. (Ch. 13, pp. 124-128.)
- **Content creation:** Publish useful material that demonstrates expertise and reveals interest. (Ch. 13, pp. 124-128.)
- **Content curation:** Share relevant third-party material with informed context and intent. (Ch. 13, pp. 124-128.)
- **Conversion:** Design social activity to create a conversation, lead, or next action. (Ch. 13, pp. 124-128.)
- **Consistency:** Time-box repeatable daily activity so the cumulative effect can build. (Ch. 13, pp. 124-128.)

## Strategic prospecting campaign

Use only for a small set of valuable conquest opportunities because it requires sustained attention. (Ch. 13, p. 121.)

1. Map decision makers and influencers.
2. Monitor trigger events and buying-window signals.
3. Share useful insight or education.
4. Layer social engagement with phone, voicemail, email, events, and introductions.
5. Track familiarity evidence and responses in the CRM.
6. Shift to a direct ask when a window or engagement signal appears.

Source: ch. 13, pp. 120-131.

## Social block

| Activity | Purpose | Evidence to log |
|---|---|---|
| Connection request | Anchor a real interaction | Accepted/ignored; relationship context |
| Relevant comment | Increase recognition | Reply, view, or later conversation |
| Curated insight | Add value | Engagement by target stakeholders |
| Trigger monitoring | Find timing | Role move, project, contract, growth, issue |
| Direct message | Ask or gather information | Response and next action |

## Copy patterns

- "Would you introduce me to [person/role]?" (Ch. 12, pp. 98-100.)
- "I enjoyed our conversation about [specific topic]." (Ch. 12, pp. 100-103.)
- "This [resource] made me think of [their stated issue]." (Ch. 13, pp. 120-131.)
- "Congratulations on [specific achievement]." (Ch. 13, pp. 115-120.)
- "I noticed [trigger]. How is that affecting [relevant area]?" (Ch. 13, pp. 121-123.)

## Familiarity plan

Build a plan only for prospects valuable enough to justify repeated attention:

| Touch | Channel | Familiarity purpose | Conversion signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Referral | Borrow trusted credibility | Prospect accepts contact |
| Connection | Social | Anchor a real interaction | Request accepted |
| Useful insight | Email/social | Demonstrate relevance | Reply, share, or question |
| Voicemail | Phone | Reinforce name and reason | Callback or recognition |
| Event contact | In person | Put a face to the name | Follow-up agreement |
| Direct ask | Best available | Convert familiarity into action | Meeting or information |

Source: ch. 12, pp. 96-104; ch. 13, pp. 120-131.

## Social workflow

1. **Choose the conquest list.** Avoid trying to build deep familiarity with the entire database. (Ch. 12, pp. 97-104.)
2. **Clean the professional presence.** Ensure profiles are complete, accurate, consistent, and credible. (Ch. 13, pp. 115-120.)
3. **Map people and signals.** Follow decision makers, influencers, groups, and relevant trigger sources. (Ch. 13, pp. 120-128.)
4. **Plan value.** Choose original or curated insight relevant to the target. (Ch. 13, pp. 120-128.)
5. **Time-box engagement.** Work social in a defined block without consuming Golden Hours. (Ch. 13, pp. 124-128.)
6. **Log evidence.** Record connections, interactions, triggers, and next actions in the CRM. (Chs. 11 and 13, pp. 92-95, 105-131.)
7. **Convert deliberately.** Make a direct channel-appropriate ask when context supports it. (Ch. 13, pp. 123-131.)

## Diagnostic questions

- Does the prospect recognize the seller, company, or source of introduction? (Ch. 12, pp. 96-104.)
- Has each social activity produced evidence, insight, or a next action? (Ch. 13, pp. 105-131.)
- Is the content useful to the target or merely promotional? (Ch. 13, pp. 114-128.)
- Is social activity supporting outbound or replacing it? (Ch. 13, pp. 129-131.)
- Is the level of effort proportional to the account's value? (Ch. 12, pp. 97-104.)

## Completion criteria

- The plan names a finite target list and objective. (Chs. 12-13, pp. 96-131.)
- Every touch adds familiarity, information, or movement. (Chs. 12-13, pp. 96-131.)
- Social work is time-boxed and connected to outbound. (Ch. 13, pp. 124-131.)
- A clear conversion trigger and direct ask are defined. (Ch. 13, pp. 120-131.)

## Guardrails

- Do not turn networking into an immediate pitch or implied quid pro quo. (Ch. 12, pp. 100-103.)
- Do not let familiarity work consume the active prospecting required for today's pipeline. (Ch. 12, p. 104.)
- Do not publish self-promotional spam or material likely to damage trust. (Ch. 13, pp. 114-128.)
- Review profile accuracy and professionalism because prospects use it as evidence. (Ch. 13, pp. 115-120.)
- Time-box social work outside Golden Hours when it is not a live sales conversation. (Ch. 13, pp. 124-128.)
