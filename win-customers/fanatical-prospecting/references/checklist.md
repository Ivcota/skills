# Prospecting System Checklist

Use this as a build worksheet or audit. Mark unknown items explicitly; never fill missing ratios, claims, or prospect facts with guesses.

## 1. Define the sales context

- [ ] Offer and average deal type are clear.
- [ ] Transactional versus complex sale is identified.
- [ ] Typical sales-cycle length is known or marked unknown.
- [ ] Territory, segment, and geography are stated.
- [ ] Seller tenure and database maturity are stated.
- [ ] Current pipeline gap and target horizon are stated.

Source: Blount, ch. 4, pp. 20-24; chs. 5-6, pp. 25-40.

## 2. Define the strike zone

- [ ] Ideal account traits are listed.
- [ ] Minimum qualification thresholds are listed.
- [ ] Disqualifiers are listed.
- [ ] Decision-maker, influencer, and coach roles are mapped.
- [ ] Contractual or budgetary buying windows are understood.
- [ ] Trigger events are listed.
- [ ] Required lead time before a buying window is estimated from experience.

Source: ch. 9, pp. 77-80.

## 5. Build the Prospecting Pyramid

- [ ] Buying-window qualified prospects are at the top.
- [ ] Hot inbound leads and referrals have an immediate follow-up path.
- [ ] Conquest accounts have named strategic campaigns.
- [ ] Qualified future-window prospects have nurture dates.
- [ ] Partially qualified records have an information objective.
- [ ] Unknown records have a data-verification objective.
- [ ] Saved CRM views can produce each list without hunting.

Source: ch. 10, pp. 84-91; ch. 11, pp. 92-95.

## 6. Protect execution time

- [ ] Golden Hours are identified for this buyer population.
- [ ] Prospecting blocks are calendar appointments.
- [ ] Every block has one objective and channel.
- [ ] Lists and research are prepared in advance.
- [ ] Email, alerts, social feeds, and unrelated tasks are closed.
- [ ] The activity or outcome target is visible.
- [ ] Platinum Hours hold research, preparation, proposals, and CRM cleanup.

Source: chs. 7-8, pp. 41-70.

## 7. Draft the message

- [ ] Identity and purpose are transparent.
- [ ] The message is quick and direct.
- [ ] A targeted or strategic bridge answers the prospect's WIIFM.
- [ ] Known facts and hypotheses are clearly separated.
- [ ] Every customer result or statistic is verified.
- [ ] The ask requests one specific action.
- [ ] A meeting ask includes a proposed time.
- [ ] The seller stops after the ask.

Source: ch. 14, pp. 132-153.

## 8. Prepare channel assets

### Phone

- [ ] Name, identity, reason, bridge, and ask are present.
- [ ] The opening can be delivered naturally without a distracting pause.
- [ ] The qualifying question or meeting time matches the objective.

Source: ch. 15, pp. 164-171.

### Voicemail

- [ ] Voicemail is reserved for a prospect worth the time.
- [ ] Identity, company, reason, callback value, and number are clear.
- [ ] The number is repeated slowly.

Source: ch. 15, pp. 172-174.

### Email

- [ ] AMMO defines Audience, Method, Message, and Outcome.
- [ ] Delivery risks are minimized.
- [ ] Subject and opening create a relevant hook.
- [ ] Body follows Hook-Relate-Bridge-Ask.
- [ ] Spelling, names, links, and claims are checked.

Source: ch. 19, pp. 214-234.

### Text

- [ ] Prior familiarity or a justified exception exists.
- [ ] Identity is included.
- [ ] Message is direct, brief, professional, and proofed.
- [ ] Links are transparent.
- [ ] Nonresponse has a channel-shift rule.
- [ ] The message will not be sent while driving.

Source: ch. 20, pp. 235-244.

### In-person

- [ ] Calls are clustered around scheduled appointments.
- [ ] The route minimizes drive time.
- [ ] Objective, questions, and close materials are prepared.
- [ ] CRM logging time is reserved.

Source: ch. 18, pp. 201-213.

- [ ] Scripts use the prospect base's real language.
- [ ] Responses are rehearsed until they sound natural.
- [ ] No response argues, shames, or tries to prove the prospect wrong.
- [ ] The second failed turnaround ends graciously.
- [ ] Gatekeeper language is respectful, transparent, and direct.

Source: chs. 16-17, pp. 178-200.

## 10. Run the daily review

- [ ] Touches, conversations, replies, appointments, information, opportunities, and wins are logged.
- [ ] Actual activity is compared with the block goal.
- [ ] Efficiency and effectiveness are reviewed separately.
- [ ] Closed, lost, and stale opportunities are replaced.
- [ ] One list, message, channel, or skill improvement is selected.
- [ ] Tomorrow's lists and blocks are prepared.
- [ ] The seller identifies the next controlled action after a setback.

Source: chs. 5-6, pp. 25-40; ch. 21, pp. 245-263.

## Audit result

Return:

1. **Primary constraint:** The single issue most responsible for the pipeline gap.
2. **Evidence:** Counts, ratios, behaviors, or missing information supporting the diagnosis.
3. **Immediate correction:** The action for the next prospecting block.
4. **System correction:** The process or asset to change this week.
5. **Measure:** The observable result that will confirm improvement.
6. **Review date:** When to reassess and rebalance.
