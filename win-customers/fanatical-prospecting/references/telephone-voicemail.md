# Telephone and Voicemail

## Core concept

Telephone prospecting should reach yes, no, or maybe quickly and professionally. A repeatable framework reduces seller anxiety, lowers resistance, and respects the prospect's time. Voicemail is selective: use it when a callback or familiarity with a high-value prospect justifies the time. (Blount, ch. 15, pp. 154-177.)

## Five-step telephone framework

1. **Get attention:** Use the prospect's name. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
2. **Identify yourself:** Give name and company without a deceptive preamble. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
3. **State the reason:** Name the objective immediately. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
4. **Bridge:** Give a relevant because tied to the prospect. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
5. **Ask and stop:** Request information, a conversation, or a specific meeting time; then remain silent. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)

Move through the opening without a gap that invites an automatic brush-off before the reason is clear. Do not use false familiarity or ask a ritual question merely to disguise the call. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)

## Call builder

```text
[Name], this is [seller] with [company].
The reason I'm calling is [objective].
[Bridge: relevant trigger, problem, proof, or useful reason].
[Specific information question or proposed meeting time].
```

Choose the ask by objective:

| Objective | Ask |
|---|---|
| Appointment | "How about [day] at [time]?" |
| Information | "Can you tell me [one qualifying question]?" |
| Immediate conversation | "What triggered your interest in [topic]?" |
| Referral | "Who handles [decision area]?" |

Source: ch. 15, pp. 164-171.

## Five-step voicemail framework

1. Identify yourself and company. (Ch. 15, pp. 173-174.)
2. State the callback number slowly and repeat it. (Ch. 15, pp. 173-174.)
3. Give the transparent reason for the call. (Ch. 15, pp. 173-174.)
4. Give a relevant reason to call back. (Ch. 15, pp. 173-174.)
5. Repeat name and callback number. (Ch. 15, pp. 173-174.)

Keep the message short enough to remain clear and easy to act on. The book proposes a brief fixed limit; treat that as a drafting constraint, not a reason to rush the phone number. (Ch. 15, pp. 173-174.)

## When to leave voicemail

Prefer voicemail when:

- The prospect is qualified, high-value, or a conquest account. (Ch. 15, pp. 172-174.)
- A buying-window signal makes contact timely. (Ch. 15, pp. 172-174.)
- The touch is part of a deliberate familiarity sequence. (Ch. 15, pp. 172-174.)
- The seller has a relevant reason likely to produce curiosity or response. (Ch. 15, pp. 172-174.)

Skip it when the record is unqualified and leaving a message would consume the block without a credible callback reason. (Ch. 15, pp. 172-174.)

## Phone block

- Book the block as a protected appointment. (Ch. 15, p. 163.)
- Prepare the target list and goal first. (Ch. 15, p. 163.)
- Remove email, mobile alerts, research, and task switching. (Ch. 15, p. 163.)
- Break a large target into psychologically smaller sets if useful. (Ch. 15, p. 163.)
- Track dials, connects, conversations, appointments, information, and outcomes. (Chs. 6 and 15, pp. 36-40, 163-177.)

## Quality check

- Identity and company appear in the first breath.
- The reason is explicit.
- The bridge is about the prospect.
- The ask matches one objective.
- The meeting ask contains a concrete time.
- The seller stops after the ask.
- Claims are verified and voice sounds natural.

## Rehearsal workflow

1. Write one opening for each primary call objective. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
2. Mark the name, identity, reason, bridge, and ask in the script. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
3. Read it aloud without pausing before the reason. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
4. Remove filler, ritual small talk, and seller-centered phrases. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
5. Practice the ask followed by deliberate silence. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
6. Record a practice attempt and check pace, confidence, and clarity. (Chs. 14-16, pp. 132-190.)
7. Run the script live, track results, and revise the bridge—not the entire structure—when evidence supports it. (Ch. 6, pp. 36-40; ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)

## Completion criteria

- The opening reaches the reason quickly. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
- The prospect hears a relevant because before the ask. (Ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
- The ask matches the list objective. (Ch. 9, pp. 71-83; ch. 15, pp. 164-171.)
- The seller has prepared RBO responses rather than extending the opening. (Chs. 15-16, pp. 164-190.)
- Voicemail is used selectively and contains an easy callback path. (Ch. 15, pp. 172-174.)
