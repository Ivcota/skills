# Daily Execution and Time Blocks

## Core concept

Prospecting fails when preparation, fear, and busy work displace the actual touch. Counter the Three Ps with the next action and protect concentrated prospecting during the hours when prospects are reachable. (Blount, chs. 7-8, pp. 41-70.)

## The Three Ps

| Pattern | How it appears | Countermove |
|---|---|---|
| Procrastination | Promising to make up today's activity later | Make a small daily commitment and begin now |
| Perfectionism | Researching or organizing far beyond the value of the call | Prepare in advance; accept useful imperfection |
| Paralysis from analysis | Rehearsing every possible "what if" | Make one call, then the next |

Source: ch. 7, pp. 41-48.

## Time architecture

- **CEO mindset:** Treat time, territory, database, and resources as assets for which the seller is accountable. (Ch. 8, p. 52.)
- **Golden Hours:** Reserve prime customer-access time for prospecting, qualifying, presenting, advancing, and closing. (Ch. 8, pp. 53-55.)
- **Platinum Hours:** Put research, planning, proposals, CRM updates, and administration before or after Golden Hours. (Ch. 8, p. 67.)
- **Time blocks:** Assign one activity and outcome to a short appointment on the calendar. (Ch. 8, pp. 57-62.)
- **Concentrated power:** Remove email, social feeds, research, and other task switching during the block. (Ch. 8, pp. 58-62.)

## Block design

Before:

1. Choose one objective and channel. (Ch. 8, pp. 57-62.)
2. Prepare the ordered list and necessary research. (Ch. 8, pp. 58-62.)
3. Set a time limit plus a touch or outcome goal. (Ch. 8, pp. 57-62.)
4. Close inboxes and alerts; signal that the block is protected. (Ch. 8, pp. 53-66.)

During:

1. Work the list without hunting for the next record. (Ch. 8, pp. 58-62.)
2. Record minimal notes and the live count. (Ch. 6, p. 40; ch. 8, pp. 58-62.)
3. Keep moving after rejection; do not turn the block into analysis. (Ch. 7, pp. 41-48.)

After:

1. Log full notes and next actions in the CRM. (Ch. 8, p. 67; ch. 11, pp. 92-95.)
2. Compare activity with the goal. (Ch. 6, pp. 36-40.)
3. Capture message or list changes for the next block. (Ch. 6, pp. 38-40.)

## Copy patterns

- "For the next [duration], the only job is [activity]." (Ch. 8, pp. 57-62.)
- "The list is ready; begin with the first record." (Ch. 8, pp. 58-62.)
- "Research belongs before or after this block." (Ch. 7, pp. 44-45; ch. 8, p. 67.)
- "Make one call. Then make the next." (Ch. 7, pp. 47-48.)
- "Is this task directly moving a qualified prospect?" (Ch. 8, pp. 53-55.)

## Guardrails

- Do not equate overwork with discipline; preserve the book's distinction between prime selling activity and support work. (Ch. 8, pp. 49-70.)
- Do not use time blocking to justify careless targeting. Lists and objectives must be prepared first. (Chs. 8-10, pp. 57-91.)
- Delegate with clear instructions, follow-up, and respect for support staff. (Ch. 8, p. 56.)

## Block rescue protocol

Use when a planned block has stalled:

1. **Stop preparing.** Save unfinished research for Platinum Hours. (Ch. 7, pp. 44-48; ch. 8, p. 67.)
2. **Shrink the commitment.** Focus on one call or a small set rather than the whole target. (Ch. 7, pp. 41-48; ch. 15, p. 163.)
3. **Restore the list.** If the seller is hunting for records, switch to a prepared saved view. (Ch. 8, pp. 58-62; ch. 10, pp. 84-91.)
4. **Close the noise.** Remove inboxes, alerts, and unrelated tabs. (Ch. 8, pp. 61-66.)
5. **Restart the clock.** Assign one activity to the remaining time. (Ch. 8, pp. 57-62.)
6. **Track visibly.** Mark each completed touch to prevent emotion from rewriting the count. (Ch. 6, p. 40.)
7. **Review later.** Do not analyze the entire process during prime selling time. (Chs. 7-8, pp. 41-70.)

## Diagnostic questions

- Was the block placed during actual prospect access time? (Ch. 8, pp. 49-55.)
- Was the list ready before the clock started? (Ch. 8, pp. 57-62.)
- Did the block have one objective and channel? (Ch. 8, pp. 57-62; ch. 9, pp. 71-83.)
- Which interruption was external, and which was chosen? (Ch. 8, pp. 53-66.)
- Did research quality materially justify its time cost? (Ch. 7, pp. 44-45.)
- Did actual activity match the felt level of effort? (Ch. 6, p. 40.)

## Completion criteria

- Golden and Platinum work are visibly separated. (Ch. 8, pp. 53-70.)
- Each block has a prepared list, objective, duration, and measure. (Ch. 8, pp. 57-62.)
- The calendar protects repeatable daily activity rather than a one-time sprint. (Chs. 5 and 8, pp. 25-35, 49-70.)
- The review improves the next block without interrupting the current one. (Chs. 6-8, pp. 36-70.)
