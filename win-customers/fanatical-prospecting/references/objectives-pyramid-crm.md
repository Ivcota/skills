# Objectives, Prospecting Pyramid, and CRM

## Core concept

Every prospecting touch needs a primary objective. Every block needs an ordered list. The CRM stores the evidence required to move prospects upward from unknown records to qualified, buying-window opportunities. (Blount, chs. 9-11, pp. 71-95.)

## Four objectives

1. **Set an appointment:** Obtain a mutual commitment to a specific date, time, and place. (Ch. 9, pp. 75-76.)
2. **Gather information and qualify:** Identify fit, decision roles, competitors, budgets, contracts, triggers, and buying windows. (Ch. 9, pp. 77-80.)
3. **Close the sale:** Use when the offer is transactional and the channel supports an immediate sales conversation. (Ch. 9, p. 81.)
4. **Build familiarity:** Accumulate recognition so later engagement feels less risky. (Ch. 9, pp. 82-83.)

Treat "call me sometime" as a brush-off, not an appointment. A valid appointment exists in both calendars and includes a specific time. (Ch. 9, pp. 75-76.)

## Define the strike zone

Capture:

- Ideal account traits and minimum thresholds. (Ch. 9, p. 80.)
- Decision-maker, influencer, and coach roles. (Ch. 9, pp. 77-80.)
- Contractual, budgetary, or operational buying windows. (Ch. 9, pp. 77-80.)
- Trigger events that open dissatisfaction or urgency. (Ch. 9, p. 80.)
- Disqualifiers and conditions that make the deal unattractive. (Ch. 9, pp. 77-80.)
- The lead time needed to engage before a window opens. (Ch. 9, p. 80.)

## Prospecting Pyramid

Work from top to bottom:

| Level | Evidence | Primary action |
|---|---|---|
| Buying-window qualified | Need, timing, fit, and stakeholders align | Ask for the next sales conversation |
| Hot inbound or referral | Recent hand raise or trusted introduction | Follow up immediately and qualify |
| Conquest account | High-value named target | Nurture, map stakeholders, monitor triggers |
| Qualified, future window | Fit confirmed; timing later | Maintain familiarity and schedule the next touch |
| Partially qualified | Some data; important gaps | Gather information |
| Unknown record | Little reliable information | Confirm data and begin qualification |

Source: ch. 10, pp. 84-91.

## List filters

Combine objective, channel, qualification level, potential, probability, geography, lead source, stakeholder role, vertical, seasonality, activity history, and buying-window timing. Start fresh blocks with the highest-probability, highest-value records appropriate to the objective. (Ch. 10, pp. 87-91.)

## CRM discipline

- Log every meaningful interaction and the next action. (Ch. 11, pp. 92-95.)
- Capture decision roles, buying windows, competitors, usage, budgets, and triggers as evidence emerges. (Ch. 11, p. 95.)
- Correct bad data instead of repeatedly working it. (Ch. 11, pp. 92-95.)
- Build saved views or filters so the next block begins immediately. (Ch. 10, pp. 90-91.)
- Treat the database as the seller's asset, not a manager's reporting chore. (Ch. 11, p. 94.)

## Copy patterns

- "Which objective does this touch serve?" (Ch. 9, pp. 71-83.)
- "Is this a real appointment in both calendars?" (Ch. 9, pp. 75-76.)
- "What evidence moves this record one level upward?" (Ch. 10, pp. 87-89.)
- "Is this the best prospect to contact next?" (Ch. 10, p. 91.)
- "What did we learn, and what exact follow-up belongs in the CRM?" (Ch. 11, pp. 92-95.)

## List-building workflow

1. **Select the objective.** Do not combine appointment setting, broad research, and nurturing indiscriminately in one list. (Ch. 9, pp. 71-83.)
2. **Choose the pyramid level.** Match the block to buying-window, conquest, qualified-future, partial, or unknown records. (Ch. 10, pp. 87-91.)
3. **Apply qualification filters.** Use strike-zone traits and known disqualifiers. (Ch. 9, pp. 77-80.)
4. **Rank potential and probability.** Place the best use of the next touch first. (Ch. 10, pp. 84-91.)
5. **Add channel constraints.** Include reachable contact data, geography, or message requirements. (Ch. 10, pp. 87-91.)
6. **Prepare the next action.** Every record needs a clear question, ask, or familiarity touch. (Ch. 9, pp. 71-83.)
7. **Save the view.** Make the list reusable without rebuilding it during Golden Hours. (Ch. 10, pp. 90-91; ch. 11, pp. 92-95.)

## CRM record minimum

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Account/contact identity | Enables accurate outreach |
| Decision role | Directs the objective and message |
| Qualification evidence | Places the record in the pyramid |
| Buying-window evidence | Sets priority and timing |
| Competitor/current state | Improves qualification and relevance |
| Last meaningful touch | Prevents random repetition |
| Next action and date | Turns information into execution |

Source: chs. 9-11, pp. 71-95.

## Completion criteria

- Every list can be named as `[objective] for [pyramid level] through [channel]`. (Chs. 9-10, pp. 71-91.)
- Top records have evidence supporting their rank. (Ch. 10, pp. 84-91.)
- Unknown fields become questions, not invented facts. (Chs. 9 and 11, pp. 77-80, 92-95.)
- A completed touch changes the record or schedules a next action. (Ch. 11, pp. 92-95.)
- The next block can start without hunting through the CRM. (Ch. 10, pp. 84-91.)
