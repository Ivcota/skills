# Mindset and Balanced Interruption

## Core concept

Prospecting requires interrupting people who were not expecting the contact. Stop debating whether a touch is cold, warm, or hot; choose the best channel for the situation and build the mindset to act despite uncertainty and rejection. (Blount, chs. 2-4, pp. 9-24.)

## Why it works

The seven mindsets support repeated action, while a balanced methodology avoids dependence on a single channel. The mix should reflect the industry, product, deal complexity, territory, buying behavior, available data, and seller tenure—not personal comfort alone. (Chs. 2 and 4, pp. 9-12, 20-24.)

## Seven mindsets

- **Optimistic and enthusiastic:** Begin each day expecting opportunity and carry enough energy into the next touch. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12.)
- **Competitive:** Treat attention and access as contests that reward preparation and effort. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12.)
- **Confident:** Manage fear and uncertainty so the prospect hears calm conviction. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12.)
- **Relentless:** Use rejection as a cue to continue rather than stop. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12.)
- **Thirsty for knowledge:** Seek feedback, coaching, and deliberate practice. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12.)
- **Systematic and efficient:** Protect time, prepare lists, and repeat a process. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12.)
- **Adaptive and flexible:** Adopt useful ideas, adapt them to context, and practice until adept. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12.)

## Build the channel mix

| Input | Question | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Market | Where do qualified buyers actually respond? | Weight proven channels without eliminating the rest |
| Product | Is the sale transactional, complex, contractual, or local? | Match channel cost to deal value and access needs |
| Territory | Is density high enough for efficient field calls? | Use in-person only where routing works |
| Tenure | Is the database mature and buying windows known? | New sellers qualify broadly; tenured sellers nurture precisely |
| Goal gap | Is near-term pipeline thin? | Increase active outbound while maintaining long-term familiarity |
| Data | Which channels are efficient and effective? | Reallocate based on measured ratios |

Source: ch. 4, pp. 20-24.

## Application prompts

- "Which channel am I avoiding because it produces rejection?" (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
- "Am I optimizing for comfort or pipeline performance?" (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
- "What do top performers in this exact segment and role do?" (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
- "What changed in the market, territory, or buying process?" (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
- "Which part of adopt-adapt-adept am I skipping?" (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12.)

## Output pattern

When asked for a plan, return:

1. The assumptions that determine channel choice.
2. A percentage or block-based channel allocation, clearly labeled as a starting hypothesis unless supported by seller data.
3. The behavior the seller is most likely to avoid.
4. A two-week measurement plan for touches, conversations, appointments, and opportunities.
5. A rule for reallocating effort after reviewing results.

## Channel selection workflow

1. **Inventory access.** List every channel the seller can legitimately use: phone, email, social, text, field, referral, networking, events, and inbound. (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
2. **Match economics.** Compare the time cost of each channel with account value, density, and deal complexity. (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
3. **Match readiness.** Separate unknown records, familiar contacts, active buying windows, and established customers. (Chs. 3-4, pp. 13-24.)
4. **Expose avoidance.** Ask which channel receives less effort because of rejection rather than poor results. (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
5. **Build the mix.** Assign blocks or shares of effort to the channels that fit each segment and objective. (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
6. **Measure outcomes.** Compare conversations, information, appointments, opportunities, and wins by channel. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12; ch. 6, pp. 36-40.)
7. **Adapt.** Preserve useful variety while shifting effort toward measured performance. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12; ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)

## Mindset audit

| Mindset | Observable evidence | Coaching action |
|---|---|---|
| Optimistic/enthusiastic | Energy returns after rejection | Create a short reset and start-of-block cue |
| Competitive | Activity and outcomes are visible | Set a personal block target |
| Confident | Ask is direct and delivery is calm | Rehearse the opening and ask |
| Relentless | Seller resumes after a no | Define the next-action trigger |
| Learning | Feedback changes later attempts | Review one call or message per block |
| Systematic | List, objective, and block are prepared | Standardize the pre-block checklist |
| Adaptive | Channel and message change with evidence | Schedule a weekly experiment review |

Source: ch. 2, pp. 9-12.

## Completion criteria

- The plan uses more than one appropriate prospecting channel. (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
- Each channel has a target segment and objective. (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24; ch. 9, pp. 71-83.)
- Allocation is justified by context or data, not preference. (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
- The avoided behavior is named without turning the plan into a character judgment. (Chs. 2-4, pp. 9-24.)
- A measurement period and adaptation rule are present. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12; ch. 6, pp. 36-40.)

## Fast diagnosis

- If activity is absent across channels, coach willingness to interrupt before refining technique. (Ch. 3, pp. 13-19.)
- If one channel dominates without evidence, inspect comfort-based avoidance. (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)
- If activity is high but inconsistent, strengthen the systematic mindset and daily blocks. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12.)
- If a proven channel has deteriorated, use adopt-adapt-adept to test a contextual change. (Ch. 2, pp. 9-12.)
- If a seller copies a veteran's mix, account for differences in tenure and database maturity. (Ch. 4, pp. 20-24.)

## Ethical boundary

Interruption is not permission to deceive. Be transparent about identity and purpose, respect explicit opt-outs, and use the fastest path to a relevant request. The book repeatedly contrasts professional directness with tricks and manipulation. (Ch. 3, pp. 13-19; ch. 17, pp. 193-200.)
