# Prospecting

Use prospecting as **sifting**, not alchemy. The job is to identify real buyers, gather intelligence, and transition only qualified prospects to the presentation.

## Four buyer archetypes

1. **Buyer in heat** — needs/wants the offer, can afford it, benefits from it, and is ready to act now.
2. **Buyer in power** — legitimate buyer but lower urgency; shopping, comparing, or waiting.
3. **Lookie-loo** — acts interested but lacks buying intent; consumes time.
4. **Mistake** — should not be in the funnel at all.

Only buyers in heat and buyers in power continue down the Straight Line. Lookie-loos and mistakes should be released quickly and respectfully.

## Prospecting goals

1. Remove lookie-loos and mistakes quickly.
2. Gather intelligence from buyers in heat and buyers in power.
3. Begin turning buyers in power toward heat by surfacing real pain and urgency.

## Ten rules of Straight Line prospecting

1. **Be a sifter, not an alchemist.** Do not try to turn non-buyers into buyers.
2. **Ask permission to ask questions.** Use a justifier: "so I don't waste your time," "so I can see if this is a fit."
3. **Always use a script.** Pre-map questions and order.
4. **Move from less invasive to more invasive.** Earn the right to ask sensitive questions.
5. **Use the right tonality for each question.** Pain questions need care; logic questions need clarity.
6. **Use correct body language while they respond.** Active listening maintains rapport.
7. **Follow a logical path.** The sequence should feel expert and intuitive.
8. **Make mental notes; don't resolve pain yet.** Understand and amplify before presenting.
9. **End with a powerful transition.** Qualify in or qualify out.
10. **Stay on the Straight Line.** Let brief tangents finish, acknowledge, then lead back.

## Intelligence fields

Gather only information relevant to fit and closing:

- Needs and secondary needs/problems.
- Beliefs about buying, trust, speed, or the category.
- Past experiences with similar products and salespeople.
- Values and value hierarchy.
- Ability to buy / budget / relevant financial or resource standards.
- Primary and secondary pain points.
- Current situation relevant to the purchase.

## Question path pattern

1. Permission: "Mind if I ask a couple quick questions so I can see if this is even worth your time?"
2. Current source/status: "What are you using now?" / "How are you handling this today?"
3. Likes/dislikes: "What do you like or dislike about that?"
4. Need/problem: "What made you start looking into this?"
5. Pain: "What's the biggest headache with this right now?"
6. Cost of inaction: "How long has that been going on?" "Do you see it getting better or worse?"
7. Ideal future: "If you could design the ideal solution, what would it do?"
8. Priority: "Of everything we discussed, what's most important to you?"
9. Ability/resources: ask only after rapport and need are established.
10. Completeness: "Have I missed anything important?"

## Qualify-in transition

Use only when fit is genuine:

> Based on what you told me, this looks like a strong fit. Let me show you why.

## Qualify-out transition

Use when not a fit:

> Based on what you told me, I don't think this is the right fit. I don't want to sell you something you won't be thrilled with.

## Lookie-loo signals

- Asks many questions they seem to know the answers to.
- Over-inspects details without decision intent.
- Gives exaggerated interest signals.
- Becomes vague or performative around budget/resources.
