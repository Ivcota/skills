# Looping

**Looping** handles objections by raising the missing certainty or threshold lever before asking again. A rebuttal alone only earns the right to speak more.

## Response categories after the ask

1. **Yes** — process the close.
2. **Firm no** — respectfully exit.
3. **Maybe / objection** — begin looping.

Common maybe objections: think about it, call me back, send information, no budget/not liquid, existing provider, bad timing, need to speak to someone else.

## First objection: deflection

Do not answer the first objection head-on. Deflect to diagnose product certainty.

Pattern:

```text
I hear what you're saying. Let me ask you a question: putting [money/timing/decision] aside for a second, does the idea make sense to you? Do you like it?
```

What this does:

1. Acknowledges the buyer so rapport does not break.
2. Sets the objection aside without arguing.
3. Tests the first Ten: product certainty.
4. Uses the enthusiasm of the answer as the gauge.

## Product loop

Use when product certainty is below an enthusiastic yes.

1. Estimate current certainty from words and tone.
2. Match slightly above the buyer's state; do not jump to 10 if they are at 5.
3. Pace, pace, lead into your strongest product proof/benefit.
4. Build logical certainty first, emotional certainty second.
5. Check: "You see what I'm saying? Do you like the idea?"
6. Continue only if the answer is an enthusiastic yes, roughly 8–9+ certainty.

If product certainty is below 3 after the presentation, usually exit or qualify out.

## Seller trust loop

Use after product certainty is high but the buyer still hesitates.

Goal: surface whether the real issue is lack of trust in the seller.

Pattern idea:

```text
If we'd been working together for years and I'd consistently delivered for you, you probably wouldn't be saying [objection]. You'd be saying [desired action]. Am I right?
```

Then run a Forrest Gump-style reintroduction:

```text
That I can understand. You don't know me yet, and I haven't earned a track record with you. So let me reintroduce myself...
```

Include truthful, relevant proof:

- role/title and experience,
- licenses/degrees/credentials,
- standards and ethics,
- customer-service philosophy,
- how you will be an asset over time.

Prepare primary, secondary, and tertiary versions to avoid repetition in later loops.

## Company trust loop

Transition directly from seller trust to company trust:

```text
And as far as my company goes...
```

Use truthful company proof:

- reputation,
- track record,
- leadership,
- market position,
- service/support standards,
- values/mission,
- third-party proof when available.

Then ask again, often with a smaller or safer first commitment if appropriate.

## Later loops

If the Three Tens are high and the buyer still hesitates, choose one lever:

1. Lower **action threshold**.
2. Raise/reconnect **pain threshold**.

Do not keep stacking loops blindly. Watch pressure signals.

## Action Threshold

Action threshold is how much certainty and safety the buyer needs before acting.

### Four ways to lower it

1. Money-back guarantee.
2. Cooling-off/rescission period.
3. Reassuring support phrases: "I'll walk you through every step," "we pride ourselves on long-term relationships," "our support is built for this."
4. Reverse the buyer's mental movies: shrink realistic downside and expand realistic upside.

### Mental movie pattern

```text
Let me ask an honest question: what's the worst that realistically happens if we start with [smaller/safe step] and it isn't right?
[Let buyer answer.]
Right. And if it works the way we both think it can, what changes for you?
[Let buyer answer.]
So this won't make or break you, but it can serve as a benchmark. Why don't we start with [smaller/safe ask]?
```

## Pain Threshold

Pain lowers action threshold by creating urgency. Use only buyer-disclosed pain.

### Pain loop pattern

1. Recall their pain: "Earlier you mentioned..."
2. Ask cost of inaction: "If nothing changes, where is this in 6–12 months?"
3. Empathize: "I get it. These things rarely fix themselves without action."
4. Future-pace the solved state.
5. Reconnect the offer to pain resolution.
6. Ask again with sincerity and low pressure.

## Pressure exit

If the buyer seems pressured:

```text
Please don't misconstrue my enthusiasm for pressure. I just believe this is a strong fit based on what you told me. If now isn't the right time, let's [send info / revisit / part ways] in a way that makes sense.
```

Then either repair rapport for one final low-pressure loop or exit.
