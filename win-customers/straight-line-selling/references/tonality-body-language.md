# Tonality and Body Language

Tonality and body language are delivery tools for emotional certainty and rapport. Use them to support truthful claims, not to hide weak claims.

## Ten core tonalities

1. **I care / I really want to know** — upbeat concern; useful for greetings and questions.
2. **Declarative as a question** — statement with questioning intonation; invites agreement/checking.
3. **Mystery / intrigue** — lowered voice; makes a point feel worth attention.
4. **Scarcity** — power whisper / reduced availability. Use only with real scarcity or legitimate urgency.
5. **Absolute certainty** — firm conviction; useful for true claims and strong recommendations.
6. **Utter sincerity** — calm, low-pressure honesty; useful near closes and pressure repair.
7. **Reasonable man** — makes request feel reasonable; useful for permission and soft closes.
8. **Money-aside** — hypothetical/non-pressured tone; useful for certainty checks.
9. **Obviousness** — implies a benefit or conclusion is self-evident; use carefully.
10. **I feel your pain** — empathy; useful for pain discovery and pain loops.

## Tonality rules

- Modulate; do not stay in one tone too long.
- Match the buyer's state before leading it upward.
- Use certainty only where you are genuinely certain.
- Use scarcity only when true.
- Use sincerity to lower pressure at the close.
- Use money-aside when asking diagnostic certainty questions.

## Body language principles

For in-person/video sales:

- Dress and grooming should be congruent with profession and trust context.
- Respect space; do not invade the buyer's space bubble.
- Use a cooperative handshake where culturally appropriate; no dominance games.
- Maintain engaged eye contact without staring down.
- Keep posture open and attentive.
- Use active listening: nods, facial expressions, short verbal cues.
- Match subtly; do not mirror creepily in real time.

## Active listening

Active listening communicates "I understand you" and "we are on the same page."

Use:

- nodding while they speak,
- narrowed eyes/compressed lips when discussing serious pain,
- small verbal cues on phone: "got it," "that makes sense," "mm-hmm,"
- leaning/attention shifts that fit the emotional weight of the question.

## Matching vs mirroring

- **Matching:** subtle delayed alignment with posture, pace, word choice, speech rate, or emotional state.
- **Mirroring:** obvious real-time copying. Avoid it.

## Pace, pace, lead

1. Enter the buyer's current world/state.
2. Pace it so they feel understood.
3. Pace again.
4. Lead toward certainty, calm, urgency, or action.

Use pace/lead in loops: if buyer sounds like a 5/10, do not answer at 10/10 intensity. Start slightly above their state and lead upward.
