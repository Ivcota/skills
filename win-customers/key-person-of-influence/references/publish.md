# Publish

**Core concept:** Publish is the second step of the KPI method — your ability to gain credibility through authoring content (book, articles, blogs, reports) on the micro-niche you claimed in Pitch. The word "authority" literally contains "author." A published book is the title deed to your micro-niche: it proves you've thought deeply enough about your topic to commit it to writing, and it converts strangers into people who treat you as a Key Person of Influence in the industry you love [S1, Ch 4, raw/kpi.txt line ~1341–1565].

**Why it works:** A published book answers two reader-trust questions at once — "Are you able to come up with credible insights?" and "Can you be trusted to get things done?" — long before the reader ever meets you [S1, Ch 4 intro, line 1361, "Able to come up with credible insights, and Trust your ability to get things done"]. The book also forces you to sharpen your thinking, spot trends, and generate intellectual-property assets you can later repurpose into chapters, articles, blog posts, talks, info products and productized services. Most books sell fewer than 500 copies a year and that is fine — "it is not authors who get great opportunities, it is KPIs. … The purpose of your book is to promote you, not the other way around" [S1, Ch 4 "Promoting your book", line 1538].

**Key insights:**
- Write 30,000–50,000 words on your topic — that is enough to publish, be Googled as an author, gift to prospects, and instantly chop into chapters, articles, blogs and reports [S1, Ch 4 "Commit to writing a book", line 1390, "I want to encourage you to write a book on your topic. It requires you to produce about 30,000 – 50,000 words"].
- A great book answers at least one significant question the reader is trying to answer; pick the core question first, then brainstorm sub-questions, then group into chapters [S1, Ch 4 "The message of the book", line 1464, "a great book must answer at least one significant question that the reader is trying to answer"].
- Plan heavily before writing — 1–2 hours mapping each chapter makes writing a breeze; planning is itself an intelligence amplifier [S1, Ch 4 "Planning the book", line 1480, "If you spend one to two hours really mapping out each chapter, writing the book is a breeze"].
- The title matters more than the content because more people hear the title than read the book — pick a brand-enhancing, micro-niche-clarifying title [S1, Ch 4 "Choosing the title of your book", line 1486, "Many more people will hear that you are the author of 'The Title You Chose,' than those who actually read the book"].
- The book is a credibility tool, not a revenue product — gift it, give multiple copies to clients, and let it do the introducing for you [S1, Ch 4 "Promoting your book", line 1538, "The purpose of your book is to promote you, not the other way around"].
- If a full book is wrong for you right now, write at least ten 1000–1500-word articles — they double as future book chapters, force clarity, and give you a reason to interview existing KPIs [S1, Ch 4 "Publishing articles", line 1547, "I would urge you to at least write a set of ten articles, each 1000 to 1500 words in length"].
- Environment dictates performance — join a writing group or get a coach; KPI Program participants regularly complete manuscripts in under 30 days [S1, Ch 4 "Environment dictates performance", line 1509, "many are shocked to see a completed manuscript in under thirty days"].

## Sub-models

### Five types of book you can write

Verbatim list from the chapter [S1, Ch 4 "Different types of books", line 1414].

| # | Type | One-line description | Choose when | Do NOT choose when |
|---|------|----------------------|-------------|--------------------|
| 1 | Thought leadership | Your story, ideas, and unique take on the niche — you are the star | You already have a clear pitch and want the book to broadcast you as the KPI in your micro-niche | You are not yet willing to put yourself at the center of the message [S1, line 1414] |
| 2 | Book of interviews | Feature existing KPIs in your niche; you ask the questions | You want to build relationships with the industry's inner circle and borrow their authority | You want the book to position you as the thought leader — in this format you are the interviewer, not the authority [S1, line 1414] |
| 3 | Book of tips | Quick-fire ideas (e.g. 10 categories × 10 tips = 100-tip book); easy to repurpose | You have a lot of accumulated practical knowledge and limited writing time | The topic requires depth or narrative — tips can feel shallow if used alone [S1, line 1414; "Book of tips" line 1442] |
| 4 | Picture book | For visual industries (photography, fashion, design); coffee-table friendly | The niche is inherently visual and before/after / portfolio imagery does the persuading | The topic is abstract or text-heavy; printing colour pages is expensive [S1, line 1414] |
| 5 | A creative piece | Parable or fiction that carries the idea | You have genuine storytelling skill and a parable form fits the message | You are not a skilled writer — this is the hardest genre to pull off and is "best left to skilled writers" [S1, line 1414] |

### Two reader-trust questions a book answers

A book is the artefact a stranger uses to decide whether to do business with you. It must answer both at once [S1, Ch 4 intro, line 1361, "Able to come up with credible insights, and Trust your ability to get things done"]:

1. **Are you able to come up with credible insights?** — Does the reader finish a chapter thinking "this person actually understands the niche"?
2. **Can you be trusted to get things done?** — Does the reader believe you can move from insight to delivery in the real world?

### The 10 × 10 = 100 tips scaffold

The Book-of-Tips scaffold from extraction-application, drawn from the chapter [S1, Ch 4 "Book of tips", line 1442]:

1. List the ten main categories of interest in your micro-niche.
2. Come up with ten tips for each category — now you have 100 tips.
3. Write three tips per day.
4. Result: a finished manuscript in under two months [S1, line 1442, "list off the ten main categories… ten tips for each category. Now you will have 100 tips… It will take you less than two months to write the book if you complete three tips per day"].

Example: JP DeVilliers, a fitness trainer, wrote *ReShape: 77 Ways To Reshape Your Body and Your Life* using this scaffold — "three short slots throughout his day to note down a tip and by the end of the month he had his book" [S1, Ch 4 "Book of tips", line 1440].

### Ten-article fallback

If a 30,000–50,000-word book is the wrong move right now, the authors prescribe a hard minimum [S1, Ch 4 "Publishing articles", line 1547–1548, "I would urge you to at least write a set of ten articles, each 1000 to 1500 words in length before you even begin to talk to publications"]:

- Write **at least 10 articles**.
- Each article is **1,000–1,500 words**.
- Write them **before** pitching publications.

Why it works:

- The articles **double as future book chapters** — you are pre-assembling the book without committing to it.
- The articles **force clarity** on your micro-niche.
- The articles **give you a reason to interview existing KPIs** and build inner-circle relationships [S1, Ch 4 "Publishing articles", line 1547].

## Planning a book (process)

Pulled directly from the "The message of the book" and "Planning the book" sections [S1, Ch 4, lines 1464–1485].

1. **Pick the core question your book answers.** "A great book must answer at least one significant question that the reader is trying to answer" [S1, line 1464].
2. **Brainstorm sub-questions.** Generate every related question your reader has under the core question.
3. **Group sub-questions into chapters.** Spend 1–2 hours planning each chapter before writing it [S1, line 1480, "If you spend one to two hours really mapping out each chapter, writing the book is a breeze"].
4. **Write the book.** With the plan in place, the writing itself is the easy part; environment (writing group, coach, deadline) carries you through to a finished manuscript — sometimes in under 30 days [S1, "Environment dictates performance", line 1509].

## Choosing the title

The authors' rule: the title matters more than the content because **more people hear the title than ever read the book** [S1, Ch 4 "Choosing the title of your book", line 1486, "Many more people will hear that you are the author of 'The Title You Chose,' than those who actually read the book"].

A title must do one job: signal, in a single phrase, what micro-niche you are a KPI in.

- **Cryptic title:** *The Race* — "people can't tell what you are a Key Person of Influence in" [S1, line 1490].
- **Niche-naming title:** *The Race Towards Green Energy* — "people instantly understand that you must be a KPI for green issues" [S1, line 1490].

## Ethical boundary

The Publish chapter has **no explicit ethical guardrail** in the source. The chapter is silent on what is or is not acceptable in authoring practice — there is no rule about ghost-writing, no rule about citing sources, no rule about claims made inside the book itself. Do not synthesise a generic publishing ethic that is not in the source.

The book's promotional ethics show up in the **adjacent** chapters and apply by cross-reference:

- **Product → back claims with real testimonials, not actor portrayals.** "An actual customer promo is ten times better than any actor portrayal. Real people offer real results" [S1, Ch 5 "Ten intangible qualities of a successful product", line 1793]. Any claim made in a book about a product or transformation must be backed the same way.
- **Profile → be careful, everything online is forever.** "Whatever you put online is potentially there for life. Getting it wrong can leave you with content that keeps coming back to haunt you" [S1, Ch 8 "The power of current best-practices", line 2323]. A book published under your name is part of that permanent record.

## Common Publish mistakes

Pulled from extraction-guardrails entries tagged Publish.

1. **Waiting until you're "ready" to write the book.**
   - *Why it fails:* People convince themselves they don't have enough content, or that their ideas aren't special. They get stuck half-way through several book ideas. "The book has no power until it's finished" [S1, Ch 4 "Environment dictates performance", line 1516].
   - *Fix:* Commit. Plan carefully, get a coach or writing group, set a deadline, target Amazon in under six months rather than thirty-six.

2. **Choosing a cryptic / vague book title.**
   - *Why it fails:* "If you introduce yourself by saying, 'I am the author of the book The Race,' people can't tell what you are a Key Person of Influence in" [S1, Ch 4 "Choosing the title of your book", line 1490]. The title is heard far more often than the book is read.
   - *Fix:* Pick a brand-enhancing, niche-naming title; test five candidates on real people first.

3. **Focusing on book sales instead of being an author.**
   - *Why it fails:* Most books sell under 500 copies a year. Energy spent chasing sales is energy not spent on the doors the book opens. "It is not authors who get great opportunities, it is KPIs" [S1, Ch 4 "Promoting your book", line 1538].
   - *Fix:* Treat the book as a credibility tool. Gift it. Give multiple copies to clients. Let the book do the introductions.

4. **Skipping Publish to leap to Partnership or Profile.**
   - *Why it fails:* "Almost having a book means you have zero books on Amazon" — and the five-step order matters because Profile and Partnership are built on the credibility Publish creates [S1, Ch 2 "The order is important / Then the results come", line 869].
   - *Fix:* Do the steps in order; finish the manuscript before amplifying yourself across the six must-use sites or chasing joint ventures.

5. **Picking the wrong format for the niche.**
   - *Why it fails:* A picture book in an abstract niche, a creative parable in the hands of a non-writer, or a tips book where depth is required all undercut the credibility a book is supposed to build. The chapter is explicit that a creative piece is "best left to skilled writers" [S1, Ch 4 "Different types of books", line 1414].
   - *Fix:* Use the Five Types table above; choose the format the niche actually calls for, not the one that flatters you.
