# Key Person of Influence (KPI) Method

Every industry has an inner circle of Key People of Influence — well-known, well-regarded, highly valued people who attract opportunities instead of chasing them — and an outer circle of everyone else fighting for scraps. The KPI Method is a five-step sequence — **Pitch → Publish → Product → Profile → Partnership** — designed to take you across that line in roughly twelve months.

## Core Principle

*"There are five things you need to have in place for you to demonstrate that you are a Key Person of Influence."* Do them in order, at a high standard, in twelve months, and you become one of the most highly valued and highly paid people in your field. Skip steps and you build a partnership on a poor pitch, a book on a poor pitch, a product on a poor pitch — nothing works. The underlying shift: in a digital, global, intangible economy, **vitality beats functionality**. A KPI is irreplaceable and aligned to results; a functional worker is replaceable and aligned to process.

**The foundation:** *"Until you are a Key Person of Influence in the industry you love, your full-time job is to become one."*

## Scoring

Rate each of the five P's from 1–10. A perfect 10 means people want to phone their most prized contacts to introduce you. Most people self-rate 7–8/10; the authors' realistic baseline is 1–2/10. Take the official KPI Scorecard at `keypersonofinfluence.com/scorecard`. **Goal: 10/10 on every P, in order.**

## The Five-Step KPI Framework

One line per step. Full mechanics, copy patterns, sub-models and ethical boundaries live in `references/`.

1. **Pitch** — the spoken-word answer to "What do you do?" that turns every interaction into a potential deal. See [references/pitch.md](references/pitch.md).
2. **Publish** — a 30–50k-word book (or 10 long-form articles) that proves you've thought deeply enough to commit it to writing. *Authority contains "author."* See [references/publish.md](references/publish.md).
3. **Product** — an ecosystem of free idea-spreaders and high-value implementation products so your value scales without your time. See [references/product.md](references/product.md).
4. **Profile** — a coherent web presence across six must-use sites so a Google search of your name proves on page one that you are a KPI. See [references/profile.md](references/profile.md).
5. **Partnership** — strategic relationships with people who already have the lists, distribution, brand or capital you need — networking for leverage, not clients. See [references/partnership.md](references/partnership.md).

## KPI Process

Apply in this order; do not skip:

1. **Stake a micro-niche** in an industry you love (gender / geography / specialty / shared interest / shared frustration). *Ch 1.*
2. **Define your Big Game** using the six game components: fun, rules, players, prize, way to win, way to lose. *Ch 3.*
3. **Craft your Pitch** — short social pitch + 7-step presentation pitch — and test for emotionally-charged (never polite) responses. *Ch 3.*
4. **Publish** — plan first (1–2 hrs per chapter), then write a 30–50k-word book or 10 × 1000–1500-word articles; get it on Amazon. *Ch 4.*
5. **Build a product ecosystem** — at minimum one free idea-spreader (podcast / info-kit / webinar) plus one high-value implementation product. Run each through Kevin's 10-question intangible-qualities checklist. *Ch 5.*
6. **Populate the six must-use sites** — YouTube, Slideshare, Facebook, LinkedIn, WordPress, Twitter — until a Google search of your name returns video, slides, blog, social and third-party media on page one. *Ch 6.*
7. **Initiate Partnerships** — list partners with leverage, make three calls per idea, meet using the six partnership rituals, start with one of the five deal types (affiliate / co-promotion / product-creation / packaging / free-bundle). *Ch 7.*
8. **Commit publicly with a hard deadline** — "Ready, Fire, Aim." Resourcefulness only shows up after the commitment. *Ch 8.*
9. **Get inside a peer environment** — mentors, coaches, current best-practice operators, a structured program. You cannot do this alone. *Ch 8.*
10. **Use momentum** — when the spark hits, act immediately; opportunities (PR, paid speaking, board seats, surprises) compound once all five P's are at high standard. *Ch 8.*

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| **Being a generalist** | The internet doesn't help generalists stand out — you can't be a KPI at "all sorts of things." | Claim a micro-niche; sniper rifle, not shotgun. |
| **Looking good, going nowhere** | Living out an 18-year-old's career decision in a world that no longer exists; competing on price. | Strip back to your raw essence; ask what you'd do if starting completely fresh. |
| **Chasing the next industry** | Jumping ship every time you see a KPI in a new field turns you back into a worker-bee. | Pick the industry you love and stay until you are inside the inner circle. |
| **Skipping steps** | "Almost having a book" means zero books on Amazon; you get no points for trying. | Complete all five P's in order, at a high standard. |
| **Settling for a polite response** | "Sounds interesting" is a meaningless non-response that keeps you looking good and going nowhere for years. | Aim for emotionally-charged responses — love it or hate it; a strong negative beats a polite yes. |
| **Trading time for money** | If you are your only product, you can only work a third of the day in one place; earnings are capped. | Build a product ecosystem; productise even services. Products don't sleep. |
| **Selling information instead of implementation** | Information is free on every phone; its value has fallen. | Give ideas away; charge premium to implement. |
| **Being invisible online** | "You are who Google says you are" — no Google profile, no big deals. | Populate the six must-use sites until videos, slides, blog and press fill page one. |
| **Networking for clients, not partnerships** | Hunting single sales is "trying to get to China on foot." | Hunt leverage — lists, distribution, brand, capital, products. |
| **Waiting until you're ready** | The ideal time never comes; people get stuck halfway through several book ideas; the book has no power until finished. | Ready, Fire, Aim. Commit publicly to a hard deadline before you feel ready. |
| **Language of requests** | "Can someone get me leads?" doesn't work except for children asking parents. | Use the language of opportunity: "Would you like 10% of the upside on these hot leads?" |
| **Going it alone** | Alone you become distracted, bored and uninspired; you're too close to your own material to see its value. | Get mentors, coaches and a peer environment with current best practice. |

## Quick Diagnostic

Audit yourself or anyone claiming to be a KPI. *Goal: every row Yes.*

| Question | If No | Action |
|----------|-------|--------|
| Can you answer "What do you do?" in under two minutes with such force the listener wants to refer you? | Polite responses, no follow-ups, no inbound. | Rebuild your Pitch (Ch 3). |
| Are you the author of a book that is on Amazon and Googleable for your micro-niche? | You read like an opinion, not an authority. | Plan + write a book or ten 1000-1500-word articles (Ch 4). |
| Do you have at least one free idea-spreader AND one high-value implementation product? | Your income tracks your hours; you are your only product. | Build a product ecosystem (Ch 5). |
| Does a Google search of your name return video, blog, slides, social and press on page one? | Buyers stall during the 7-hour deliberation; deals die. | Populate YouTube / Slideshare / Facebook / LinkedIn / WordPress / Twitter (Ch 6). |
| Do you have active partnerships where two parties build a "bakery" rather than fight over slicing a pie? | You're hunting transactions, working hard but not compounding. | Run the five-question partnership-scouting checklist on every new meeting (Ch 7). |

## About the Authors

**Daniel Priestley** founded the Key Person of Influence accelerator program; he built a global business turning over millions before age thirty and has tested the KPI Method across more than fifty industries in over a dozen countries with over a thousand entrepreneurs and leaders.

**Kevin Harrington** is a pioneer of the direct-response TV / "As Seen on TV" industry, a creator of the Entrepreneurs' Organization (EO), and a celebrated entrepreneur appearing on ABC's *Shark Tank*; he describes the KPI Method as "exactly how I did it" — and how, in his view, almost everyone in the inner circle of any industry does it.
