# Value Is Created in the Ecosystem

**Core concept:** Nothing works on its own. Sustainable value comes from a sophisticated ecosystem whose people, products, services, information, and implementation reinforce one another; the ecosystem as a whole creates the value, not any isolated product, service, system, or person. [Principle 6, “Value Is Created in the Ecosystem,” raw:2409-2432]

A product that looks uneconomic by itself may be doing essential work elsewhere in the system. Books may make restaurants popular, while sponsorship income may make sense alongside public-relations spending; pulling one element out and demanding that it justify itself alone misses how the parts work together to make money and have an impact. [Principle 6, “Value Is Created in the Ecosystem,” raw:2413-2429]

**Why it works:** The modern business leader’s job is not to make business simple, but to make a complex system seem effortless—to turn chaos into order with style. When people, products, and services work in harmony, the result is complex, paradoxical, difficult to mimic, and capable of keeping people involved and uplifted. [Principle 6, “Value Is Created in the Ecosystem,” raw:2456-2465]

The ecosystem also lets value rise in stages. Ideas attract and demonstrate thought leadership; components let customers build; supervision adds guidance; done-with-you work shares the effort; and done-for-you solutions assume responsibility for the result. The more responsibility the business takes for implementation—and the less energy the client must supply—the more value the offer creates. [Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” raw:2520-2558]

Low-risk first steps make that movement easier. A non-binding signal or a small initial engagement lets people move toward a larger decision without first having to make a commitment that is difficult to reverse. [Principle 6, “It’s Easier to Climb Small Stairs Than to Jump Big Walls,” raw:2582-2607]

The ecosystem should evolve without abandoning what made it work. Successful businesses innovate, but they preserve the winning formula at the core and make changes where change is actually needed. [Principle 6, “Innovate But Don’t Mess with a Winning Formula,” raw:2645-2670]

## Key insights

- **Measure the whole, not the pieces.** Books, appearances, sponsorships, restaurants, online activity, public relations, and charitable projects can look irrational in isolation while creating money and impact together. [Principle 6, “Value Is Created in the Ecosystem,” raw:2413-2429]

- **Build for return, not a single transaction.** The job is to create a remarkable ecosystem that delivers a positive experience and keeps people coming back for more. [Principle 6, “Value Is Created in the Ecosystem,” raw:2430-2432]

- **Make complexity feel effortless.** High-performing businesses coordinate people, products, and services in harmony; their complexity and paradox make the system hard for competitors to copy. [Principle 6, “Value Is Created in the Ecosystem,” raw:2456-2465]

- **Stop treating information as the scarce product.** Once free information became abundant, the value of information fell while the value of getting things done rose; the new model is to share information freely or cheaply and charge for implementation. [Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” raw:2492-2516]

- **Use ideas to create implementation demand.** Priestley says he shares ideas in books and blogs, after which readers approach his company with budgets to put those ideas into action. [Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” raw:2513-2516]

- **Match price to implementation responsibility.** Information and components leave most of the work with the client; supervision, done-with-you, and done-for-you offers progressively transfer responsibility to the business and create more value. [Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” raw:2520-2549]

- **Reduce the client’s required energy.** Across the value spectrum, the less energy the client must contribute to obtain the result, the more valuable the product becomes. [Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” raw:2547-2558]

- **Put several levels into one ecosystem.** A business should offer products across multiple value categories rather than relying on a single isolated offer. [Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” raw:2552-2561]

- **Create small stairs instead of big walls.** People resist large, hard-to-reverse commitments; a low-risk first step can reveal demand and later become a firm commitment. [Principle 6, “It’s Easier to Climb Small Stairs Than to Jump Big Walls,” raw:2582-2600]

- **Do not innovate away from market love.** Internal boredom is not evidence that the market is bored; keep the proven core and innovate at the edges. [Principle 6, “Innovate But Don’t Mess with a Winning Formula,” raw:2661-2678]

## Application sequence

1. **Map the ecosystem.** List the people, products, services, systems, content, experiences, and impact activities that work together around the customer. [Principle 6, “Value Is Created in the Ecosystem,” raw:2413-2432]

2. **Trace the reinforcement.** For each element, identify which other element it strengthens instead of evaluating only its isolated direct return. [Principle 6, “Value Is Created in the Ecosystem,” raw:2421-2429]

3. **Design the return journey.** Arrange the ecosystem to deliver a positive experience that gives people a reason to come back for more. [Principle 6, “Value Is Created in the Ecosystem,” raw:2430-2432]

4. **Separate ideas from implementation.** Decide which information can be shared freely or cheaply and where the business will charge to help make the ideas happen. [Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” raw:2508-2516]

5. **Build the value spectrum.** Connect information, components, supervision, done-with-you work, and done-for-you solutions so customers can enter at different levels of required effort. [Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” raw:2520-2558]

6. **Strengthen real implementation.** Where supervision is offered, add active involvement, accountability, and personalisation around the result. [Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” raw:2535-2537]

7. **Install a small first stair.** Before a large commitment, offer a low-risk and reversible way for an interested person to signal intent or begin working with the business. [Principle 6, “It’s Easier to Climb Small Stairs Than to Jump Big Walls,” raw:2582-2607]

8. **Name the winning formula.** Identify what the market already loves, keep that core intact, and direct innovation toward the edges. [Principle 6, “Innovate But Don’t Mess with a Winning Formula,” raw:2638-2670]

## Ethical boundary

Do not present lightly repackaged information as valuable implementation. Supervision only carries implementation value when the business takes an active role in the client’s continuing journey and provides real accountability or personalisation; without those elements, it falls back toward selling ideas. [Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” raw:2535-2537]

Do not use “innovation” as permission to discard a proven core merely because the business is bored with it. The source warns that the market may still love what insiders are tired of delivering. [Principle 6, “Innovate But Don’t Mess with a Winning Formula,” raw:2661-2678]
