# Nothing Beats Being Positively Remarkable

**Core concept:** The strongest route to oversubscription is to make the product, service, and experience so positively remarkable that customers talk about them voluntarily. Priestley’s blunt test is that positive word of mouth can reduce the marketing budget to zero; what matters most is having a product worth talking about. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Nothing Beats Being Positively Remarkable” and “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2704-2706; raw:2724-2726]`

**Why it works:** Buyers now carry smartphones, can ask their social networks for advice, and can inspect reviews, blogs, ratings, and other evidence before choosing. An advertisement may start the search, but the buyer can then discover and purchase from a more remarkable competitor; the advertiser pays to create interest in the category while recommendation directs the sale elsewhere. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2727-2749]`

A remarkable offer wins that redirected attention because it is best in a defined niche or micro-niche through genuine advantages, real benefits, superior experience, and authentic care. When existing customers receive the investment first, they can become the people who recommend the business and create the next wave of demand. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2750-2759; raw:2767-2777]`

## Key insights

- Positive conversation is the standard: “If people talk about you in a positive way, your marketing budget reduces to zero.” — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7 `[raw:2704-2706]`

- Traditional marketing and advertising matter less than they once did; the central asset is a product worth talking about. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2724-2726]`

- Buyers can immediately consult friends, reviews, blogs, star ratings, and other data, so seller-controlled promotion no longer controls the path to purchase. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2727-2729]`

- Advertising can market the competition: it may awaken demand, but search and recommendations can send the buyer to another seller. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2730-2749]`

- Being remarkable is not a matter of “stupid gimmicks or pointless stunts”; it means being the best in a niche or micro-niche through genuine advantages, real benefits, superior experience, and authentic care. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2750-2756]`

- Remarkable does not necessarily mean premium or high-end. A business can be remarkable for innovation, fair deals, low price as a true point of difference, or unusual effort on behalf of a customer. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2757-2759]`

- The practical test is unaided recommendation: when someone asks whom they should buy from, the business’s name should come up. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2760-2766]`

- Shift spending toward products and customer service and away from traditional advertising; oversubscribed businesses spend on existing customers before prospective ones so customers can perform the marketing. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2767-2777]`

- Priestley recommends transferring at least 50 percent of the traditional marketing budget into a “being remarkable” budget. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2773-2777]`

- A trusted personal brand can resolve conflicts, launch products, raise capital, attract leaders, and support social change, as Priestley illustrates through Richard Branson. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand” `[raw:2795-2805]`

- Companies become oversubscribed by building and leveraging the personal brands of the people inside, rather than existing only as faceless corporate symbols. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand” `[raw:2809-2811]`

- A personal brand is built when a person becomes better known, liked, and trusted through videos, blogs, articles, books, photos, quotes, live appearances, talks, publicity, and events. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand” `[raw:2812-2814]`

- Faces, voices, body language, and words from a person are easier for people to recognize, trust, connect with, discuss, and recommend than an abstract corporate symbol. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand” `[raw:2818-2820]`

- A personal brand lets a person’s distinctive point of view remain “in the room” when that person is absent, because others can discuss what that person would do. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand” `[raw:2821-2824]`

- Personal-brand value can survive a business sale or career change and provide a head start in the next endeavour. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand” `[raw:2825-2827]`

- Reddington operationalized personal branding by asking every employee to blog monthly under their own name, pitch the company vision, and build connections online. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand” `[raw:2828-2832]`

- Priestley attributes Reddington’s fast growth, large partnerships, ability to beat better-funded rivals, and attraction of high performers to building a company full of visible personalities. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand” `[raw:2833-2838]`

- The trade-off is direct: weigh the fear of visibility against the risk of remaining a faceless, generic business that is not oversubscribed. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand” `[raw:2839-2842]`

## Application sequence

1. **Test whether the offer is worth talking about.** Treat talk-worthiness, not advertising volume, as the central product test. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7 `[raw:2704-2706; raw:2724-2726]`

2. **Trace the buyer’s real decision path.** Account for search, reviews, blogs, ratings, and advice from friends after an advertisement creates initial interest. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7 `[raw:2727-2749]`

3. **Define substantive remarkability.** Compete through a niche-leading advantage, real benefit, superior experience, authentic care, or another genuine point of difference named by the source. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7 `[raw:2750-2759]`

4. **Fund the experience first.** Increase investment in products, customer service, and existing customers before adding more traditional advertising. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7 `[raw:2767-2777]`

5. **Make the people inside visible.** Build familiarity and trust through named people, their ideas, and the communication formats the source lists. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7 `[raw:2809-2820]`

6. **Distribute visibility across the team.** Encourage employees to publish under their own names, explain the company vision, and build connections, as in the Reddington example. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7 `[raw:2828-2838]`

## Ethical boundary

Do not label gimmicks or pointless stunts “remarkable.” Priestley’s boundary is substantive: genuine advantages, real benefits, superior experience, authentic care, and a class-of-one position in a defined niche; low price or fairness count only when they are real points of difference. — *Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget” `[raw:2750-2759]`
