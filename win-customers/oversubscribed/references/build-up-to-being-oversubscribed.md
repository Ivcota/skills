# Build Up to Being Oversubscribed

**Core concept:** Signal what will happen before it happens, explain the process and terms in advance, and ask the market to signal its intentions before it acts. A Campaign Driven Enterprise does not simply put a product up for sale and hope; it announces the future release, names the limited capacity, and gathers interest before making the offer available. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The Power of Signalling” [raw:3827-3838]

The build-up then turns that interested group from “maybe I would” to “I’d love to” through education or entertainment, relationship-building content, and products-for-prospects. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Educate and Entertain” and “Products-For-Prospects” [raw:4117-4138; raw:4273-4288]

**Why it works:** Asking for a sale creates a binary decision: people buy or they do not. Asking for a signal captures people who are interested but not yet ready, gives them a low-commitment next step, and reveals demand the business would otherwise never see. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Don’t Ask for the Sale Ask for the Signal” [raw:3923-3942]

Visible responses add energy because prospects can see how many other people want the announced capacity. Education and entertainment then build rapport, trust, understanding, and emotional connection until a significant purchase feels comfortable rather than forced. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Transparency,” “Educate and Entertain,” and “The 7-Hour Rule” [raw:3960-3972; raw:4120-4134; raw:4171-4198]

## Key insights

1. **Signal before you act.** Tell people what is going to happen, explain the process and terms, and invite them to indicate interest before the release. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The Power of Signalling” [raw:3827-3838]

2. **Name your terms openly.** State who you want to work with, how many people you can serve, the terms of participation, the registration and payment timing, and the grounds for acceptance or rejection. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Naming Your Terms” [raw:3857-3878]

3. **Ask for the signal, not the sale.** An email request, expression-of-interest form, like, click, or reply lets partially ready people identify themselves without immediately producing a credit card. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Don’t Ask for the Sale Ask for the Signal” [raw:3923-3942]

4. **Make genuine demand visible.** Show how many people received the signal and how many signalled back; in the source’s campaign, visible interest at five times capacity created energy and the released capacity was taken in a day. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Transparency” [raw:3960-3972]

5. **Create “one of me and lots of you.”** Transparency helps when prospects see one seller and many interested buyers, but works against the business when they see one undifferentiated seller among many with no line. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “There’s One of Me and Lots of You” [raw:4003-4028]

6. **Think mobile and media first.** Early interactions are likely to happen through content on a distracted prospect’s phone, so signalling should be concise, fast-loading, easy to act on, responsive, shareable, and delivered in frequent bite-sized updates. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Think Mobile and Media First” [raw:4060-4086]

7. **Choose education or entertainment as the dominant mode.** Education builds trust, understanding, and engagement; entertainment builds rapport, connection, and emotion. Use roughly 80% of one and a light amount of the other rather than trying to serve both equally. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Educate and Entertain” [raw:4117-4134]

8. **Allow relationship time for significant decisions.** The source proposes about seven hours for important, trust-dependent purchases and warns that raising business too soon can blow the deal. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The 7-Hour Rule” [raw:4171-4198]

9. **Build relationship time at scale.** Blogs, tweets, videos, podcasts, slides, photos, books, apps, questionnaires, reports, case studies, and events can contribute to the proposed seven hours or eleven touches of relationship-building content. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Brains Don’t Know it’s Digital” [raw:4220-4244]

10. **Create products-for-prospects.** Give away or sell low-cost products that educate or entertain, indicate interest, and ideally collect contact details or reveal needs before the core offering is released. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Products-For-Prospects” [raw:4266-4288]

## Application table

| Context | Application | Sourced example |
|---|---|---|
| **Music festival** | Require pre-registration, collect intended ticket quantity, communicate the rules, and announce the exact release time before tickets become available. | Glastonbury disclosed more than 350,000 pre-registrations for 120,000 tickets and reminded prospects that tickets usually sold in under 30 minutes. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The Power of Signalling” [raw:3795-3826] |
| **Interest campaign** | Ask a visible question or expression of interest before releasing capacity, then let respondents see the ratio of demand to availability. | A Facebook campaign drew five times more responses than delivery capacity within a day; when capacity was released, it was taken in a day. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Transparency” [raw:3960-3972] |
| **Trade show** | Create a visible reason to queue so the environment shows one sought-after seller rather than one booth among many. | After a main-stage talk, attendees queued at the booth to complete a five-question survey for a free book and samples, then carried the samples in brightly coloured branded bags. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “There’s One of Me and Lots of You” [raw:4026-4028] |
| **Mobile campaign** | Design the first signal for a distracted person holding a phone: concise copy, fast media, easy buttons, responsive pages, and a clear path to further content. | The source lists bite-sized communication, quick-loading images and video, mobile-friendly buttons, responsive sites, sharing tools, frequent updates, location relevance, and context. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Think Mobile and Media First” [raw:4060-4086] |
| **Workshop or event** | Make either education or entertainment the main experience and add the other lightly at the edges. | For an educational workshop, the source suggests humour, good food, drinks, and a classy location; for a sporting event, it suggests information kits, engineers available for natural conversations, and later follow-up. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Educate and Entertain” [raw:4128-4134] |
| **Trust-dependent sale** | Accumulate relationship time through useful content, conversation, and connection before asking for a significant commitment. | The source applies the proposed seven-hour pattern to buying a car, making a career move, engaging a consultant, and choosing a holiday destination. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The 7-Hour Rule” [raw:4171-4198] |
| **Automotive core offer** | Use lower-risk experiences and accessories as products-for-prospects that reveal interest before the car purchase. | BMW could promote a track day, exclusive event, weekend test drive, or accessories rather than only advertising cars. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Products-For-Prospects” [raw:4266-4279] |
| **Financial-services core offer** | Use information and tools to educate prospects before asking them to choose a mortgage. | Citibank could produce a report, book, series of talks, or useful software download as products-for-prospects. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Products-For-Prospects” [raw:4276-4279] |

## Build-up sequence

1. **Announce the intention.** Tell the market that a product or service will be released in the future rather than placing it on sale without warning. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The Power of Signalling” [raw:3830-3838]
2. **Declare the capacity and terms.** Explain who the offer is for, how much capacity exists, how people qualify, and when registration and payment occur. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Naming Your Terms” [raw:3857-3878]
3. **Invite a low-commitment signal.** Ask interested people to email, complete a form, click, like, or reply. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Don’t Ask for the Sale Ask for the Signal” [raw:3923-3927]
4. **Show the response when it helps.** Make real interest visible so prospects can see the relationship between demand and announced capacity. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Transparency” [raw:3960-3972]
5. **Design for mobile attention.** Use concise messages and easy mobile actions that lead to deeper content. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Think Mobile and Media First” [raw:4060-4086]
6. **Educate or entertain the interested group.** Choose one dominant mode and use the other lightly. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Educate and Entertain” [raw:4117-4134]
7. **Accumulate trust before the ask.** Provide enough content, ideas, conversation, and connection for an important decision. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The 7-Hour Rule” and “Brains Don’t Know it’s Digital” [raw:4171-4201; raw:4220-4244]
8. **Use scalable products-for-prospects.** Create low-cost or free experiences and media that educate, entertain, and reveal who may want the core offering. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Products-For-Prospects” [raw:4273-4288]

## Build-up audit

- **Has the future release been signalled before it goes on sale?** The source distinguishes a Campaign Driven Enterprise from a company that simply posts a product and hopes people buy. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The Power of Signalling” [raw:3830-3838]

- **Are capacity, fit, timing, payment, and selection terms explicit?** Naming these conditions in advance is part of the signal. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Naming Your Terms” [raw:3857-3878]

- **Can an interested person respond without making the full purchase?** The prescribed first response is an email, form, like, click, or reply rather than an immediate sale. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Don’t Ask for the Sale Ask for the Signal” [raw:3923-3927]

- **Does the campaign reveal real response volume where that visibility adds energy?** Transparency means showing how many people received the signal and how many signalled back. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Transparency” [raw:3960-3968]

- **Does the buying environment show concentrated demand rather than abundant sellers?** Transparency can help when a line is visible and hurt when the business appears to be one option among many with no one lining up. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “There’s One of Me and Lots of You” [raw:4003-4028]

- **Can a distracted mobile user understand and act on the signal?** The source calls for concise communication, fast media, easy buttons, responsive pages, and bite-sized updates. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Think Mobile and Media First” [raw:4060-4086]

- **Is education or entertainment clearly dominant?** The source warns that mixing them in the wrong doses can go badly and recommends an 80/20 balance. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Educate and Entertain” [raw:4128-4134]

- **Is enough relationship-building material available before a significant ask?** The source recommends seven hours or eleven touches made from articles, podcasts, videos, apps, reports, books, case studies, events, and related assets. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The 7-Hour Rule” and “Brains Don’t Know it’s Digital” [raw:4171-4198; raw:4236-4244]

- **Do products-for-prospects educate or entertain while revealing useful information?** An elegant product-for-prospects also gathers contact details or helps the business understand needs. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Products-For-Prospects” [raw:4273-4288]

## Copy patterns

- **Signal the future release:** “We intend to release [offer]. Capacity is limited to [number]. If you want more information or want to buy when it becomes available, [signal action].” — Generalized from *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The Power of Signalling” [raw:3830-3838]

- **Name the terms:** “If you want to work with us: [who qualifies], [capacity], [terms], [interest deadline], [payment date], and [acceptance or rejection grounds].” — Generalized from *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Naming Your Terms” [raw:3857-3878]

- **Ask for interest:** “There will be [offer] available soon. If you are interested in more information, please [email / complete the expression-of-interest form / click / reply].” — Generalized from *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Don’t Ask for the Sale Ask for the Signal” [raw:3923-3927]

- **Show demand against capacity:** “We sent the signal to [audience size]. [response count] people signalled back, and only [capacity] places or units are available.” — Generalized from *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Transparency” [raw:3960-3968]

- **Invite a public signal:** “I’m in” or “Let me know when I can get one, please.” — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “There’s One of Me and Lots of You” [raw:4032-4038]

## Ethical boundary

- **Terms are standards, not snobbery.** Naming terms is not about serving only rich people; the terms should define timing, performance standards, prerequisites, and genuine grounds for acceptance or rejection. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Naming Your Terms” [raw:3875-3878]

- **Relationship-building must be wanted.** Do not use the seven-hour idea in a “creepy, annoying or pestering way”; create content, events, and conversations that people voluntarily want to spend time with. — *Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The 7-Hour Rule” [raw:4196-4201]
