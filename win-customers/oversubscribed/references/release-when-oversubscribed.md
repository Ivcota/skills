# Release When Oversubscribed

**Core concept:** Do not release capacity merely because the campaign has reached launch day. Release only after measured interest is safely greater than the capacity you can deliver, then use that imbalance to select the right buyers, preserve campaign energy, complete disciplined sales follow-up, and hold the price and capacity you announced. (Daniel Priestley, *Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” and “Set Your Targets and Stay Firm” `[raw:4325-4356]`, `[raw:4703-4757]`)

**Why it works:** The strength and quantity of signals tell you whether demand is truly above supply before you expose the offer. Once that point is reached, selection directs scarce capacity toward people who already value the offer, controlled release mechanisms keep buyer energy high, rehearsed sales conversations convert warm interest, and firm targets protect trust and delivery quality. (Phase 3, “Measuring Interest,” “Selection Process,” “Managing Energy,” “Sales Conversations vs. Chit-Chats,” and “Set Your Targets and Stay Firm” `[raw:4375-4403]`, `[raw:4421-4431]`, `[raw:4449-4474]`, `[raw:4633-4684]`, `[raw:4703-4757]`)

## Key insights

1. **Release only when demand is safely above capacity.** Being one buyer over capacity technically creates oversubscription, but Priestley says that being only slightly oversubscribed is not enough; the campaign should cross one of his stronger release-readiness thresholds first. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” `[raw:4325-4351]`)

2. **Match the threshold to the strength of the signal.** The source gives three release-readiness tests: five times capacity in strong signals such as deposits, ten times capacity in educated or entertained prospects, or one hundred times capacity in soft interest such as downloads or competition entries. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” `[raw:4339-4351]`)

3. **Measure quantity and quality together.** Counts alone do not show readiness: a deposit, a detailed survey, an event attendance, an email opt-in, and a free download represent different levels of commitment. (Phase 3, “Measuring Interest” `[raw:4375-4400]`)

4. **Select the die-hard fans rather than arm-twisting reluctant buyers.** The build-up weeds people out and gives the business time to know them; when capacity is released, it can go to people who love the offer, value it, fit the business, and are likely to rave about the experience. (Phase 3, “Selection Process” `[raw:4421-4431]`)

5. **Preserve energy with mechanisms planned before the campaign.** Staged releases, special or limited editions, and price rises or time limits may maintain an imbalance between buyers and sellers, but adding them on a whim midway through a campaign looks tacky and opportunistic. (Phase 3, “Managing Energy” `[raw:4449-4474]`)

6. **Oversubscription does not remove the need to sell.** Pre-sold prospects may still need questions answered, options explored, or reassurance; scripted, rehearsed conversations with supporting materials and explicit asks convert warm interest more reliably than open-ended chit-chat. (Phase 3, “Sales Conversations vs. Chit-Chats” `[raw:4633-4684]`)

7. **Hold the announced deal when the rush arrives.** Raising the price unexpectedly or expanding capacity after demand appears damages trust, makes scarcity look like a gimmick, and can lower the standard delivered to every client. (Phase 3, “Set Your Targets and Stay Firm” `[raw:4703-4757]`)

## Release sequence

1. **Confirm the declared capacity.** Use the capacity already named and signalled to the market as the fixed denominator for the release-readiness calculation. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” `[raw:4325-4336]`)

2. **Classify each signal by commitment.** Treat pre-payments, deposits, or demanding pre-approval as strong; purchases of products-for-prospects, detailed surveys, substantial event attendance, or related training as moderate; and downloads, email captures, video views, competitions, or samples as soft. (Phase 3, “Measuring Interest” `[raw:4395-4400]`)

3. **Collate the signals on one scoreboard.** Combine the channels so the team can see both total volume and the judged commitment level while the campaign is running. (Phase 3, “Measuring Interest” `[raw:4387-4392]`)

4. **Test the relevant threshold.** Look for five times capacity in strong signals, ten times capacity in educated or entertained prospects, or one hundred times capacity in soft interest. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” `[raw:4339-4351]`)

5. **Continue the build-up if the test fails.** Run more signalling, education, entertainment, events, products-for-prospects, or download activity rather than releasing weak demand. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” `[raw:4354-4356]`)

6. **Select the buyers.** Give capacity to the people the business wants as clients, especially those whose enthusiasm, value perception, and fit were demonstrated during the build-up. (Phase 3, “Selection Process” `[raw:4421-4431]`)

7. **Use only preplanned release mechanics.** If staged releases, special editions, price rises, or time limits were built into the campaign plan, deploy them as planned; do not invent them during the rush. (Phase 3, “Managing Energy” `[raw:4453-4474]`)

8. **Run the sales follow-up.** Use rehearsed questions, brochures or case studies, a direct ask, and an order form or detailed CRM record; then promptly follow up warm leads and payment completion. (Phase 3, “Sales Conversations vs. Chit-Chats” `[raw:4650-4684]`)

9. **Stay firm on price and capacity.** Change price only when the campaign announced that mechanism in advance or after the campaign is completely finished; do not quietly admit extra buyers beyond the stated capacity. (Phase 3, “Set Your Targets and Stay Firm” `[raw:4703-4757]`)

## Signal-strength guide

| Signal class | Source examples | Release-readiness test | What the signal means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strong** | Pre-payment, refundable deposit, finance application, or proof of funds. | Five times the available capacity. | The prospect has made a meaningful commitment or demonstrated a real intention to complete the deal. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” and “Measuring Interest” `[raw:4339-4341]`, `[raw:4383-4385]`, `[raw:4395-4397]`)
| **Moderate** | Buying a product-for-prospects, completing a detailed survey, attending an event for several hours, or enrolling in related training. | Ten times the available capacity. | The prospect has invested enough time, attention, or money to be educated or entertained about the core offer. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” and “Measuring Interest” `[raw:4342-4344]`, `[raw:4395-4397]`)
| **Soft** | Downloading a document, entering an email address, watching online videos, entering a competition, or taking a free sample. | One hundred times the available capacity. | The prospect has shown interest with little commitment, so much more volume is required. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” and “Measuring Interest” `[raw:4345-4347]`, `[raw:4398-4400]`)

## Application table

| Context | Application | Source-backed example |
|---|---|---|
| **Campaign measurement** | Use declared delivery capacity as the denominator and compare it with signal volume before release. | For capacity of five products, the book's strong-signal example requires twenty-five people to have paid a small holding deposit. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” `[raw:4339-4341]`)
| **Events and education** | Treat substantial attendance by ideal prospects as moderate interest rather than the same as a deposit. | For capacity of five clients, the book's moderate-signal example uses fifty perfect clients attending an event that educated them about the result. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” `[raw:4342-4344]`)
| **Content and lead generation** | Require much greater volume when the action is easy and free. | For capacity of five clients, the book's soft-signal example uses five hundred people downloading a twenty-page report. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” `[raw:4345-4347]`)
| **Luxury or high-consideration products** | Use deposits or demanding qualification steps as evidence of strong intent, refunding the deposit when a person is not selected. | The source points to exclusive sports-car registration deposits and finance or proof-of-funds submissions as high-quality signals. (Phase 3, “Measuring Interest” `[raw:4395-4397]`)
| **Client selection** | Release capacity to high-fit enthusiasts instead of prospects who had to be persuaded. | Apple is described as initially releasing new products to die-hard fans who line up, write blogs, pose for media, and amplify demand. (Phase 3, “Selection Process” `[raw:4426-4431]`)
| **Events and entertainment** | Split planned capacity into successive releases so later availability can be announced against visible demand. | Monty Python first released one O2 Arena show, then announced nine more dates and another 45,000 tickets after the initial release sold out in seconds. (Phase 3, “Staged Release” `[raw:4492-4533]`)
| **Consumer products** | Divide an existing catalogue into special versions through added content, bundles, merchandise, packaging, or limited production. | Star Wars and Beatles releases are used to show how familiar material can become distinct special editions. (Phase 3, “Special Editions” `[raw:4551-4570]`)
| **Segmented services or offers** | Create a special edition for a defined demographic or profession when that segmentation is designed in advance. | The source proposes versions for under-30s, high-net-worth individuals, or people working in the medical industry. (Phase 3, “Special Editions” `[raw:4563-4570]`)
| **Retail and information products** | Use a large, genuine reduction only for the short period actually announced. | Amazon UK reduced *Entrepreneur Revolution* from £8.90 to £1.90 for one day; the book moved from about number 2,000 to the top 150 books on the site during that promotion. (Phase 3, “Price Rises and Time Limits” `[raw:4588-4599]`)
| **Sales operations** | Follow campaign demand with a predictable, rehearsed sales conversation rather than assuming warm prospects will complete unaided. | The source requires a script, materials such as brochures or case studies, an ask for the business, and an order form or detailed CRM notes. (Phase 3, “Sales Conversations vs. Chit-Chats” `[raw:4650-4675]`)
| **Capacity-constrained services** | Refuse extra clients when accepting them would divide the care and attention promised to the original group. | Priestley warns that hiring, buying equipment, or outsourcing after overselling still consumes time and risks letting down every client, not only the extras. (Phase 3, “Set Your Targets and Stay Firm” `[raw:4726-4743]`)

## Copy patterns

- **Readiness scoreboard:** “Capacity: [number]. Strong signals: [number]. Educated or entertained prospects: [number]. Soft signals: [number]. Release only after the relevant five-times, ten-times, or one-hundred-times threshold is exceeded.” This structure follows the source's three readiness tests and central-scoreboard requirement. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” and “Measuring Interest” `[raw:4339-4356]`, `[raw:4387-4392]`)

- **Continue-building decision:** “We have not yet reached [relevant threshold], so we will continue [signalling / events / products-for-prospects / education / entertainment / downloads] before releasing [capacity].” This generalizes the source's instruction to continue building interest until one criterion is met. (Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity” `[raw:4354-4356]`)

- **Selection decision:** “Select who you want as your clients, then release [capacity] so it goes to the people you choose.” This directly generalizes the source's own selection language. (Phase 3, “Selection Process” `[raw:4421-4425]`)

- **Staged-release announcement:** “Due to outstanding demand from [specific audience], [additional capacity] will be released on [date/time]. The previous release sold out in [source-backed time], so get in early.” This pattern follows the quoted Monty Python release announcement; include the sell-out time only when the campaign can substantiate it. (Phase 3, “Staged Release” `[raw:4511-4518]`)

- **Special-edition prompt:** “What version of [offer] could include [special features], serve [specific group], or be limited by [real production constraint]?” This follows Priestley's questions for turning an offer into a special edition. (Phase 3, “Special Editions” `[raw:4563-4570]`)

- **Sales-conversation structure:** “[Scripted questions] + [brochure or case studies] + [direct ask] + [order form or detailed CRM note].” This preserves the elements Priestley uses to distinguish a sales conversation from chit-chat. (Phase 3, “Sales Conversations vs. Chit-Chats” `[raw:4650-4675]`)

## Ethical boundary

- **Do not manufacture release mechanics mid-campaign.** Staged releases, special editions, and price or deadline mechanisms should be in the campaign plan from the outset; otherwise they appear tacky and opportunistic. (Phase 3, “Managing Energy” `[raw:4469-4474]`)

- **A price promotion must be genuine and actually brief.** The source rejects fake reference prices and “daily” deals that remain available, and says buyers quickly discover those tricks. (Phase 3, “Price Rises and Time Limits” `[raw:4595-4615]`)

- **Do not exploit the rush by changing the announced price or capacity.** Honor the price unless a change was declared in advance or the campaign has ended, and do not oversell capacity at the expense of client care. (Phase 3, “Set Your Targets and Stay Firm” `[raw:4703-4757]`)

- **Do not twist arms to fill capacity.** The purpose of oversubscription is to select people who love and value the offer; reluctant buyers are more likely to regret the decision and dispute price or value. (Phase 3, “Selection Process” `[raw:4426-4431]`)
