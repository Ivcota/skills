# Celebrate and Innovate

**Core concept:** Close every campaign before rushing into the next one. Publicly share the success, reward the people who made it possible, and look for ways to make the next campaign better by hunting down the stories, capturing the numbers, interrogating the data, and turning what you learn into reusable assets.

*Citation: Daniel Priestley, **Oversubscribed**, Phase 5, “Stories, Numbers and Insights” [raw:5042-5056].*

**Why it works:** A campaign produces more than immediate sales. It also produces stories that can create future demand, numbers that expose what actually worked, insights that prevent repeated mistakes, and a team that needs recognition and recovery. Capturing those assets after execution helps the next campaign strip out wastage, strengthen winning strategies, and begin with more confidence; celebration and downtime reduce the burnout risk created by intense campaign work.

*Citation: Daniel Priestley, **Oversubscribed**, Phase 5, “Stories, Numbers and Insights,” “The Truth Is in the Results,” “The Debrief,” and “Time to Party and Rest” [raw:5050-5056; raw:5211-5233; raw:5251-5268; raw:5286-5303].*

**Key insights:**

- Resist the addictive temptation to roll straight into another campaign. Skipping the close means repeating mistakes and failing to retain valuable assets.
  
  *Citation: Phase 5, “Stories, Numbers and Insights” [raw:5045-5056].*

- Finish with three assets: stories, numbers, and insights. Capture them, learn from them, share them, and recognize the people involved.
  
  *Citation: Phase 5, “Stories, Numbers and Insights” [raw:5053-5056].*

- A high-quality product is not enough to stand out when its stories remain hidden. Bremont combines product quality with stories about its founders, pilots, military connections, adventures, materials, and individual watches; many watches sell out in their release week.
  
  *Citation: Phase 5, “Tell Your Stories” [raw:5081-5118; raw:5135-5146].*

- Memorable stories do not need to be blockbusters. Authentic, human moments of triumph and real emotion can generate more interest than a large story that does not connect with the heart.
  
  *Citation: Phase 5, “So Many Great Hidden Stories” [raw:5164-5169].*

- Do not merely notice a story. Drill into its background, future impact, and finer details; capture it in writing, video, or audio; then make it available online.
  
  *Citation: Phase 5, “So Many Great Hidden Stories” [raw:5170-5179].*

- “The truth is in the results.” Measure what you can during the campaign, stay focused on executing the plan, and reserve the deeper investigation for the post-campaign review.
  
  *Citation: Phase 5, “The Truth Is in the Results” [raw:5211-5229].*

- Take more insights into the next campaign so you can remove wastage, put more energy behind what won, and operate with greater confidence.
  
  *Citation: Phase 5, “The Truth Is in the Results” [raw:5230-5233].*

- Debrief while events are fresh. Include everyone who participated, give each person a voice, and use the same five questions to score performance, preserve strengths, expose improvements, and identify magic moments.
  
  *Citation: Phase 5, “The Debrief” [raw:5251-5268].*

- Celebrate the effort regardless of the result, then schedule real downtime. Campaigns can be mentally, emotionally, and physically exhausting, and back-to-back intensity can damage morale and put the business at risk.
  
  *Citation: Phase 5, “Time to Party and Rest” [raw:5286-5303].*

**Application table:**

| Context | Application | Source-backed example | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Consumer products and retail** | Build the product’s stories into the campaign before release, rather than relying on generic category advertising. | Bremont tells stories about its founders, flying, military personnel, adventurers, materials, and individual watches before release; many watches sell out during release week. | *Phase 5, “Tell Your Stories” [raw:5081-5118].* |
| **Company and product history** | Audit whether customers and employees know the circumstances behind the company, its products, and its name. | Priestley asks how many customers know why the business started, how many products have a narrative about their uniqueness, and how many employees know why the company chose its name. | *Phase 5, “Tell Your Stories” [raw:5138-5146].* |
| **Hospitality** | Look for authentic human stories already unfolding around the experience and capture the moments that connect with the heart. | A restaurant may hold stories about dates, recovery from illness, and people reconnecting with loved ones on any given night. | *Phase 5, “So Many Great Hidden Stories” [raw:5164-5175].* |
| **Professional services** | Treat client progress and business milestones as story assets rather than leaving them hidden inside delivery. | An accounting practice may see clients take risks, build family businesses, hire their first employee, or raise life-changing investment. | *Phase 5, “So Many Great Hidden Stories” [raw:5173-5179].* |
| **Campaign analytics** | Attach identifiers to campaign assets, collect response data, and compare variants after execution. | Priestley’s newspaper campaign used booking codes and found differences between odd and even pages, colour treatments, and an upside-down advertisement that performed badly. | *Phase 5, “The Truth Is in the Results” [raw:5197-5210].* |
| **Marketing and sales operations** | Measure response and conversion variables during the campaign, then perform the deeper analysis after the campaign finishes. | The source tracks cost per lead, cost per sale, enquiries, unsubscribes, clicks, conversions, brochure variants, and sales-conversation length. | *Phase 5, “The Truth Is in the Results” [raw:5211-5229].* |
| **Events and campaign teams** | Debrief immediately with everyone who helped deliver the event or important campaign day. | Volunteers, staff, directors, guest speakers, and suppliers join the circle and answer the same five questions while the day is fresh. | *Phase 5, “The Debrief” [raw:5251-5268].* |
| **Team sustainability** | Close the campaign with recognition, a shared celebration, and scheduled time with no work contact. | After three successful but exhausting events were followed by three resignations, Priestley’s team learned to pair campaigns with a party and genuine downtime. | *Phase 5, “Time to Party and Rest” [raw:5286-5303].* |

**Copy patterns:**

- “How many of our customers know [the circumstances that caused us to start]? How many of our products have [a narrative about what makes them unique]? How many employees can tell [why the company chose its name]?”
  
  *Citation: generalized from the source’s company-story audit in Phase 5, “Tell Your Stories” [raw:5141-5146].*

- “Drill deep into [the story]. Find [the background], ask [what impact it has in the future], and examine [the finer details]. Capture it in [writing/video/audio], then make it available [online].”
  
  *Citation: generalized from the source’s story-capture instruction in Phase 5, “So Many Great Hidden Stories” [raw:5170-5172].*

- “The truth is in the results: even if we think [idea] is great, if it bombs on [the numbers], it was not such a great idea.”
  
  *Citation: generalized from Priestley’s office saying and explanation in Phase 5, “The Truth Is in the Results” [raw:5211-5219].*

- “Measure [cost per lead], [cost per sale], [enquiries], [unsubscribes], [clicks], and [conversion percentage]. Test [one asset] against [another], then investigate after the campaign.”
  
  *Citation: generalized from the campaign measurement list and analysis timing in Phase 5, “The Truth Is in the Results” [raw:5220-5229].*

- “Debrief: What would you score our team performance from 1–10, and why? What did we do well? What could we have done better? What magic moment did you notice? Anything else?”
  
  *Citation: closely generalized from the five-question debrief in Phase 5, “The Debrief” [raw:5251-5268].*

- “At the end of [campaign], regardless of the result, take time to [celebrate] and recognize [the effort]. Afterward, schedule [downtime] with no [email], no [phone calls], and no [work].”
  
  *Citation: generalized from the campaign-close instruction in Phase 5, “Time to Party and Rest” [raw:5290-5303].*
