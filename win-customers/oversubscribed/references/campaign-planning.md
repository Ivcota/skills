# Campaign Planning

**Core concept:** Know your capacity, who it is for, and when you can deliver it before promotion begins. Campaign planning starts by calculating how many people the business can genuinely leave delighted, defining the market that values the offer, can pay for it, and is enjoyable to serve, then designing the offer and rolling campaign schedule around that reality. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Campaign Planning” `[raw:3041-3049; raw:3053-3066; raw:3247-3258; raw:3517-3546]`]

**Why it works:** A real capacity number creates the line beyond which standards fall and demand becomes oversubscribed. A precise market and offer architecture concentrate effort on people who are likely to value the result and develop into ongoing clients. An annual schedule, campaign theme, and detailed timeline then give demand enough time to build while coordinating every communication, milestone, event, delivery date, and follow-up action. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3072-3081; raw:3326-3360; raw:3533-3546; raw:3589-3635; raw:3653-3678]`]

## Key insights

- **Begin with the honest limit.** “As many as people will buy” is not a capacity plan. Every business, including software, has limits, and it is almost impossible to become oversubscribed without knowing the real number of people it can properly serve. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3053-3066]`]

- **Begin value planning with the perfect client’s problem.** Perfect clients are enjoyable to work with, pay on time, return, and tell others; they love the business because it solves a high-value problem or unmet need in a remarkable way. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “It Begins with a Happy Customer” `[raw:3099-3114]`]

- **Test the offer against the four underlying drivers.** High-value products and services save or make money, save time or eliminate waste, increase emotional benefits, or ease pain and negative emotions; linking the offer to these drivers reveals why people buy and how the offer can be positioned as more valuable. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3115-3123; raw:3164-3206]`]

- **Measure capacity in delighted people, not shipped units.** The relevant limit includes every ingredient required to leave the buyer uplifted. Work backward from those ingredients to calculate how many people can receive that experience without standards dropping. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3207-3229]`]

- **Require all three market conditions.** The market must highly value the business, have the capacity to pay, and be a group the business wants to serve. Two out of three still produces a broken formula. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Who’s Your Market?” `[raw:3247-3258]`]

- **Design for both customers and clients.** A customer completes a transaction; a client develops an ongoing relationship. Use a low-risk product-for-prospects to create happy customers, then select and nurture suitable people into a full, remarkable core relationship. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Clients versus Customers” `[raw:3326-3360]`]

- **Turn the annual capacity into campaigns before execution.** Plan far enough ahead for momentum to build, choose a theme that can rise above product chatter, and place campaign dates, communications, milestones, events, shipping, and post-campaign work on one visible timeline. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Your Schedule for Becoming Oversubscribed,” “Create a Campaign Theme,” and “Create a Campaign Timeline” `[raw:3517-3571; raw:3589-3635; raw:3653-3678]`]

## Planning sequence

1. **Calculate delight capacity.** Ask how many clients can be served well, how many products can create an epic experience, and how many hours of truly great work can be delivered; state the resulting limit honestly. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3050-3081]`]

2. **Describe the perfect client and high-value problem.** Identify what that person was trying to achieve, what problem they faced, and which unmet need the business solves remarkably. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3099-3114]`]

3. **Map the offer to the four value drivers.** Record how the offer affects money, time or waste, positive emotion, and pain or negative emotion. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3115-3123; raw:3164-3199]`]

4. **Identify the ingredients of delight.** Include the details that protect the buyer’s emotional experience, then work backward from them to find the current capacity. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3207-3229]`]

5. **Define the market through three tests.** Name who can pay, what they would highly value, and why the business feels drawn to serving them. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3247-3258; raw:3274-3307]`]

6. **Create the offer architecture.** Specify the low-risk product-for-prospects and the core offering that can evolve into a sustained client relationship. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3326-3360]`]

7. **Check reality rather than defending the offer.** Observe whether customers are actually delighted, study negative feedback, and change the product or delivery until people leave uplifted. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Getting a Grip on Reality” `[raw:3379-3429; raw:3430-3463]`]

8. **Set the real annual number.** State how many people the business has the capacity to delight in the year ahead, even if the current honest answer is very small. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “The Real Number” `[raw:3474-3498]`]

9. **Break the number into intake targets.** Decide how many clients are wanted per quarter, per productive month, or per intake, then use those targets to shape the campaign calendar. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3533-3571]`]

10. **Choose the campaign theme.** Either use a seasonal event, promotion, launch, or special event, or pursue the bigger game by talking about the customer’s lifestyle, a philosophy, a major problem, or a desired transformation. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Create a Campaign Theme” `[raw:3589-3635]`]

11. **Build the campaign timeline.** Place the campaign boundaries, communications, milestones, supporting events, main events, shipping, and post-campaign actions on a shared visual plan. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Create a Campaign Timeline” `[raw:3646-3678]`]

## Campaign plan fields

- **Perfect client:** Who is the client the business enjoys serving, who pays on time, returns, and tells friends? [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3099-3114]`]

- **High-value problem:** What was that person trying to achieve, and what unmet need or problem does the business solve remarkably? [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3103-3114]`]

- **Delight ingredients:** What must happen for the customer to leave uplifted, and how many people can receive every one of those ingredients? [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3207-3229]`]

- **Market fit:** Who can pay, what do they highly value, and why does the business want to serve them? [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3247-3258; raw:3274-3307]`]

- **Offer ladder:** What low-risk product-for-prospects will create customers, and what core offering will sustain selected client relationships? [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3326-3360]`]

- **Annual capacity:** How many people can the business genuinely delight in the year ahead? [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3474-3498]`]

- **Campaign target:** How many campaigns will run, and how many clients must each campaign generate? [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3533-3571; raw:3731-3758]`]

- **Theme and timeline:** What bigger idea will the campaign communicate, and when will every communication, milestone, event, shipment, and follow-up happen? [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3589-3635; raw:3653-3678]`]

## Applications

| Context | Application | Source-backed example | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Professional services capacity** | Cap intake at the number the team can serve without risking quality, and make later prospects wait for the next opening. | Guy Rigby’s team limited new accounting clients each quarter; when full, he said the next referral had to wait three months. | *Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3067-3075]` |
| **Product positioning** | Examine a purchase through money, time, emotional benefit, and pain reduction rather than treating the product only as its literal function. | The Rolex example is unpacked as a status and performance signal, a shortcut in new encounters, a marker of achievement, and relief from feeling unrecognized. | *Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3125-3146]` |
| **Experience capacity** | Include the details that create delight when calculating how much can be served. | The source includes charging an iPhone before shipment, allowing restaurant guests time to linger, and limiting nightclub density so the experience remains enjoyable. | *Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3212-3229]` |
| **Luxury products** | Select a market that values the work, can afford it, and is enjoyable to serve rather than aiming at the average category buyer. | Boucher & Co. sells high-priced custom furniture to millionaires and billionaires who admire the work, can pay, and create a waiting list. | *Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3259-3267]` |
| **Property information and implementation** | Stop arguing that a weak product should delight people; change the value model until actual customer reactions improve. | One property-book seller faced low downloads and refund requests, while another business gave information away and charged to build, renovate, and rent property portfolios. | *Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3379-3429]` |
| **Consulting campaigns** | Convert a finite annual client target into a small number of timed campaign intakes. | A consultant seeking twelve $30,000 projects from franchise or multi-location owners plans three campaigns, each intended to sign four clients. | *Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3681-3686]` |
| **Technology beta launch** | Plan a defined first cohort, an observation period, and a later expansion campaign. | A startup seeking 250 beta users schedules a campaign in month one, monitors behavior for six months, and schedules expansion in month nine. | *Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3693-3695]` |

## Copy patterns

- **Capacity question:** “How many [clients] can you truly serve well? How many [products] can you really sell? How many [customers] can you genuinely make feel uplifted?” [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3076-3081]`]

- **Four-driver audit:** “How does [offer] save or make people more money? How does it save time or eliminate wastage? How does it deliver greater emotional benefits? How does it remove pain or negative emotions?” [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Activity 2” `[raw:3164-3199]`]

- **Market-fit audit:** “Who has the capacity to pay [us]? What would they highly value from [us]? What about these people makes [us] feel drawn to working with them?” [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Who’s Your Market?” activity `[raw:3274-3307]`]

- **Offer architecture:** “Our product-for-prospects is [low-risk product] aimed at generating happy customers. Our core offering is [full, remarkable client relationship] that evolves over time.” [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Clients versus Customers” `[raw:3352-3360]`]

- **Campaign arithmetic:** “[Business] wants [annual capacity] clients who pay [price]. The perfect client is [profile]. There are [number] good onboarding periods, so run [number] campaigns, each designed to sign [clients per campaign].” [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Create a Campaign Timeline” `[raw:3681-3686]`]

- **Timeline pattern:** “Campaign timeline: [start and finish], [communications schedule], [milestones], [supporting events], [main events], [shipping dates], [post-campaign stories, results, follow-up, and thanks].” [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Create a Campaign Timeline” `[raw:3653-3670]`]

## Ethical boundary

Do not pretend capacity is unlimited. State how many people can be properly served, and do not exceed the limit when doing so would lower standards for every client. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 `[raw:3053-3075]`]

Do not tell disappointed customers why they *should* be delighted. Observe whether they actually feel value, accept that fixing the offer is the business’s responsibility, and remain willing to change it until customers leave uplifted. [*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1 — “Getting a Grip on Reality” `[raw:3399-3429]`]
