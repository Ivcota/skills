# Oversubscribed Campaign Checklist

Use this checklist in the author’s sequence: establish the seven principles, then work through the five Campaign Driven Enterprise phases. The objective is an honest state in which many more qualified people want the offer than delivery capacity allows. (*Oversubscribed*, Introduction, [raw:848-850])

## Part I — Principles for Becoming Oversubscribed

### 1. Demand and Supply Set the Price

- [ ] Identify the smaller group that values the offer most rather than optimizing for the broad market. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 1, “The Story of the Two Bidders,” [raw:1047-1054])
- [ ] Answer: Who cannot live without this offer, what do they want, and where can they be found? (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 1, [raw:1062-1069])
- [ ] Check that willing buyers outnumber the capacity available to serve them. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 1, [raw:1036-1042], [raw:1144-1153])
- [ ] Accept that some qualified buyers must miss out if demand is to remain greater than supply. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 1, “Some People Miss Out,” [raw:1133-1149])
- [ ] Keep missed-out buyers interested while delivering remarkable value to accepted buyers. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 1, “Some People Miss Out,” [raw:1133-1149])
- [ ] Do not hard-sell uncertain prospects; let them opt out and serve people already waiting. (*Oversubscribed*, Introduction, [raw:877-881])

### 2. Separate Yourself from the Market

- [ ] Cultivate people loyal to the business, its products, its personality, and its philosophy. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 2, “How to Create Your Own Market,” [raw:1297-1302])
- [ ] Define a focused market that highly values the result, can afford the offer, and has unmet needs the business understands. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 2, “You Don’t Need Everyone,” [raw:1381-1392])
- [ ] Build recognition, liking, trust, and followership among a focused audience rather than seeking mass awareness. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 2, “Famous for a Few,” [raw:1416-1426])
- [ ] Reinforce the market with a consistent message, content, a commercial ecosystem, continuity, and collaboration. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 2, “Famous for a Few,” [raw:1452-1477])

### 3. The Four Drivers for a Market Imbalance

- [ ] Choose one primary market-imbalance driver: innovation, relationships, convenience, or price. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 3, [raw:1503-1528])
- [ ] If choosing innovation, determine whether the advantage comes from the product, delivery system, or brand. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 3, “Driver #1 Innovation,” [raw:1562-1591])
- [ ] If choosing relationships, determine whether the advantage comes from influence, greater familiarity, or lasting agreements. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 3, “Driver #2 Relationship,” [raw:1643-1658])
- [ ] If choosing convenience, reduce friction through distribution, market information, or automation. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 3, “Driver #3 Convenience,” [raw:1678-1713])
- [ ] If choosing price, establish a profitable cost advantage through investment, refinement, or systemization rather than sacrificing margin. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 3, “Driver #4 Price,” [raw:1733-1761])
- [ ] Do not combine positions whose operating requirements conflict. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 3, [raw:1521-1528])

### 4. Buying Environments Create Buyers

- [ ] Cluster genuine demand so prospects can see people moving toward the offer together. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 4, [raw:1783-1813])
- [ ] Make buyer interest visible without forcing the offer on anyone. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 4, [raw:1851-1873])
- [ ] Set boundaries and terms that protect the space needed to deliver something special. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 4, [raw:1862-1873])
- [ ] Invest in existing clients, celebrate their success, and showcase their stories. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 4, “Turn Your Clients Into Celebrities,” [raw:1905-1925])
- [ ] Ask people what they want, explain the offer in that context, and package what they need within it. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 4, “People Don’t Buy What They Need They Buy What They Want,” [raw:1971-1999])
- [ ] Remove constant discounts, shouting, or boundaryless service that signals desperation. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 4, “People Buy What Others Want to Buy,” [raw:1851-1869])

### 5. It’s OK to Be Different

- [ ] State the business’s strongly held beliefs, what it stands for, what it stands against, and the change it wants to create. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 5, “The Power of Philosophy,” [raw:2045-2092])
- [ ] Tell people honestly when the business is fully booked or constrained by a genuine operational bottleneck. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 5, “It’s OK to Fail,” [raw:2129-2139])
- [ ] Put measures in place that prevent overselling beyond delivery capacity. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 5, “It’s OK to Fail,” [raw:2164-2169])
- [ ] Define high standards for clients, hires, suppliers, investors, and commercial terms. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 5, “It’s OK to Say No,” [raw:2204-2233])
- [ ] Interview for fit and politely decline relationships that are wrong for the business. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 5, “It’s OK to Say No,” [raw:2224-2257])
- [ ] Let people wait rather than rushing, overcrowding, or lowering delivery standards. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 5, “It’s OK to Make People Wait,” [raw:2319-2339])
- [ ] Identify an expected industry norm, consider its opposite, and decide how to execute that opposite exceptionally well. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 5, “It’s OK to Buck the Trend,” [raw:2379-2389])

### 6. Value Is Created in the Ecosystem

- [ ] Map the people, products, services, information, and implementation work that create value together. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 6, [raw:2409-2432])
- [ ] Evaluate each component by how it supports the whole ecosystem, not only by its isolated direct revenue. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 6, [raw:2421-2432])
- [ ] Give information freely or cheaply and charge for implementation. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 6, “Give Away Ideas Charge for Implementation,” [raw:2492-2516])
- [ ] Build a value ladder from ideas and components through supervision, done-with-you work, and done-for-you outcomes. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 6, [raw:2520-2549])
- [ ] Ensure supervised offers include active involvement, accountability, and personalization. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 6, “Spectrum of Value,” [raw:2535-2537])
- [ ] Create a low-risk first step before asking for a large commitment. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 6, “It’s Easier to Climb Small Stairs,” [raw:2582-2607])
- [ ] Preserve the proven core formula and innovate at the edges. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 6, “Innovate But Don’t Mess with a Winning Formula,” [raw:2638-2670])

### 7. Nothing Beats Being Positively Remarkable

- [ ] Define the niche or micro-niche in which the business will deliver genuine advantages, real benefits, and a superior experience. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, [raw:2750-2759])
- [ ] Invest in products, service, and customer experience instead of relying on gimmicks or stunts. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, [raw:2730-2759])
- [ ] Spend on existing customers before spending on prospective customers so advocacy can generate demand. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, [raw:2767-2777])
- [ ] Make the people inside the company known, liked, and trusted in the market. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, “Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand,” [raw:2809-2827])
- [ ] Use people’s faces, voices, ideas, and appearances to make trust and recommendation easier. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 7, [raw:2818-2827])

## Part II — The Campaign Driven Enterprise Method

### Phase 1 — Campaign Planning

#### Capacity

- [ ] Calculate the annual number of people the business can properly serve and leave delighted. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, [raw:3053-3081], [raw:3207-3229])
- [ ] Base capacity on the ingredients required for an uplifting experience, then work backward to a number. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, [raw:3207-3229])
- [ ] Hold that honest capacity rather than increasing volume at the cost of standards. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, [raw:3061-3075])

#### Perfect Market and High-Value Problem

- [ ] Describe the perfect client and the high-value problem solved remarkably for that client. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, [raw:3099-3123])
- [ ] Confirm that the market highly values the offer. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Who’s Your Market?”, [raw:3247-3258])
- [ ] Confirm that the market has the capacity to pay. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Who’s Your Market?”, [raw:3247-3258])
- [ ] Confirm that the business wants to serve this market. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Who’s Your Market?”, [raw:3247-3258])
- [ ] Do not proceed with only two of those three market conditions. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Who’s Your Market?”, [raw:3247-3258])
- [ ] Test the offer against actual customer reactions instead of explaining why customers should be delighted. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Getting a Grip on Reality,” [raw:3399-3429])

#### Offer Architecture

- [ ] Define a product-for-prospects that can create many happy customers at low risk. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Clients versus Customers,” [raw:3326-3360])
- [ ] Define the core offering as a full, remarkable client relationship that can evolve over time. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Clients versus Customers,” [raw:3326-3360])
- [ ] Check how the offer makes or saves money, saves time or waste, creates emotional benefits, or removes pain and negative emotion. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Activity 2,” [raw:3164-3199])

#### Campaign Schedule, Theme, and Timeline

- [ ] Set a rolling 12-month campaign schedule from capacity and intake targets. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Your Schedule for Becoming Oversubscribed,” [raw:3517-3546])
- [ ] Allow enough preparation time for the campaign to build momentum. (*Oversubscribed*, Part II and Phase 1, [raw:2919-2934], [raw:3536-3543])
- [ ] Give the campaign a theme larger than the product itself. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, [raw:3589-3635])
- [ ] Put the campaign start and finish dates on the timeline. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Create a Campaign Timeline,” [raw:3653-3675])
- [ ] Put communications, milestones, supporting events, and main events on the timeline. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Create a Campaign Timeline,” [raw:3653-3675])
- [ ] Put shipping or delivery dates and post-campaign follow-up on the timeline. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 1, “Create a Campaign Timeline,” [raw:3653-3675])

### Phase 2 — Build Up to Being Oversubscribed

#### Signal Intent and Name Terms

- [ ] Announce what will be released before it is released. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The Power of Signalling,” [raw:3827-3838])
- [ ] State the genuine capacity, timing, and terms in advance. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, [raw:3827-3838], [raw:3857-3878])
- [ ] State who qualifies, how many people can be served, and the terms for working together. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Naming Your Terms,” [raw:3857-3878])
- [ ] State time limits, performance standards, prerequisites, payment timing, and acceptance or rejection grounds. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Naming Your Terms,” [raw:3857-3878])
- [ ] Use selectivity to protect fit and delivery, not as snobbery or exclusion based merely on wealth. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Naming Your Terms,” [raw:3875-3878])

#### Ask for and Display Signals

- [ ] Ask for a low-commitment expression of interest rather than an immediate sale. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Don’t Ask for the Sale Ask for the Signal,” [raw:3918-3942])
- [ ] Record how many people received the signal and how many signalled back. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Transparency,” [raw:3960-3972])
- [ ] Make genuine excess interest visible when it exists. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Transparency,” [raw:3960-3972])
- [ ] Avoid settings that visibly present the business as one of many sellers with nobody lining up. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “There’s One of Me and Lots of You,” [raw:4003-4028])

#### Educate or Entertain

- [ ] Choose education or entertainment as the dominant purpose of the prospect experience. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Educate and Entertain,” [raw:4120-4134])
- [ ] Use the source’s 80/20 mix: about 80% education with light entertainment, or about 80% entertainment with light education. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Educate and Entertain,” [raw:4120-4134])
- [ ] For significant trust-dependent purchases, assemble roughly seven hours or eleven touches of relationship-building material. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The 7-Hour Rule,” [raw:4171-4198], [raw:4236-4244])
- [ ] Build familiarity in ways people voluntarily want to engage with, without creepy, annoying, or pestering behavior. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “The 7-Hour Rule,” [raw:4196-4201])
- [ ] Create scalable products-for-prospects that educate or entertain and collect useful signals. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, “Products-For-Prospects,” [raw:4273-4288])

### Phase 3 — Release When Oversubscribed

#### Measure Release Readiness

- [ ] Maintain a central campaign scoreboard for signal quantity and signal quality. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Measuring Interest,” [raw:4375-4392])
- [ ] If using strongly signalled interest, reach five times capacity before release. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity,” [raw:4339-4351])
- [ ] If using educated or entertained prospects, reach ten times capacity before release. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity,” [raw:4339-4351])
- [ ] If using soft interest, reach one hundred times capacity before release. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Oversubscribe Your Capacity,” [raw:4339-4351])
- [ ] Continue building interest if none of the source’s release-readiness thresholds has been reached. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, [raw:4325-4356])
- [ ] Stop extending education and entertainment once sufficient measured demand exists rather than spending beyond the point required. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, [raw:4325-4356], [raw:4375-4385])

#### Select and Release

- [ ] Select high-fit, committed buyers rather than people who required arm-twisting. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Selection Process,” [raw:4421-4431])
- [ ] Use staged releases, special or limited editions, and price or time mechanisms only if they were planned from the outset. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Managing Energy,” [raw:4453-4474])
- [ ] Do not add scarcity mechanics opportunistically during a live campaign. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Managing Energy,” [raw:4469-4474])
- [ ] If using a discount or deadline, make the reduction genuine, brief, stated, and actually expiring. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Price Rises and Time Limits,” [raw:4595-4615])

#### Complete Sales Follow-Up and Hold Terms

- [ ] Rehearse a scripted sales conversation with predictable questions and a direct ask. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Sales Conversations vs. Chit-Chats,” [raw:4633-4684])
- [ ] Support the conversation with materials such as brochures or case studies. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Sales Conversations vs. Chit-Chats,” [raw:4650-4675])
- [ ] Record the outcome in a signed order form or detailed CRM notes. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Sales Conversations vs. Chit-Chats,” [raw:4650-4675])
- [ ] Follow up warm leads promptly; oversubscription does not remove the need to sell. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Sales Conversations vs. Chit-Chats,” [raw:4633-4668])
- [ ] Keep the announced price through the campaign. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Set Your Targets and Stay Firm,” [raw:4703-4708])
- [ ] Keep the announced capacity through the campaign. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Set Your Targets and Stay Firm,” [raw:4726-4757])
- [ ] Change price or capacity only when pre-announced or after the campaign ends. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 3, “Set Your Targets and Stay Firm,” [raw:4703-4757])

### Phase 4 — Remarkable Delivery

#### Remarkable Audit

- [ ] List every customer, team, and supplier touchpoint, including websites, brochures, people, products, premises, advertisements, staff materials, uniforms, and packaging. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 4, “The Remarkable Audit,” [raw:4847-4867])
- [ ] Score each touchpoint from 1–10 for whether it is positively remarkable. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 4, “The Remarkable Audit,” [raw:4847-4867])
- [ ] Select one or two weak touchpoints each month and invest in improving them toward 10/10. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 4, “The Remarkable Audit,” [raw:4847-4867])
- [ ] Audit the whole experience rather than only the headline product. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 4, [raw:4794-4829])

#### Create Up Energy

- [ ] Identify where delivery can create delight, spontaneous joy, pleasant surprise, magic, or unexpected care. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 4, “Energy Up, Down or Sideways,” [raw:4911-4932])
- [ ] Set accurate but restrained expectations so delivery can include genuine unexpected goodness. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 4, “Energy Up, Down or Sideways,” [raw:4928-4951])
- [ ] Follow the source’s guidance to sell only about 70% of what the business intends to deliver. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 4, “Energy Up, Down or Sideways,” [raw:4935-4951])
- [ ] Prevent sales or marketing from making preposterous claims to win business. (*Oversubscribed*, “Role 3: Head of Operations,” [raw:5934-5940], [raw:5974-5976])
- [ ] Use technology to track interactions, manage delivery, automate follow-up, collect data, and scale a consistent experience. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 4, “From Now On, You’re Also an IT Business,” [raw:4969-4980])
- [ ] Ask customers how likely they are to recommend the business and treat an average below 7/10 as incompatible with durable growth and profitability. (*Oversubscribed*, “Role 3: Head of Operations,” [raw:5920-5933])

### Phase 5 — Celebrate and Innovate

#### Capture Stories, Numbers, and Insights

- [ ] Hunt down authentic human stories created by the campaign. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, [raw:5042-5056], [raw:5164-5179])
- [ ] Capture useful stories in writing, audio, or video and make them available publicly. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “So Many Great Hidden Stories,” [raw:5164-5179])
- [ ] Collect campaign response, conversion, and outcome data. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “The Truth Is in the Results,” [raw:5197-5233])
- [ ] Perform the deeper investigation after campaign execution is complete. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “The Truth Is in the Results,” [raw:5211-5229])

#### Debrief

- [ ] Debrief while the events are fresh. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “The Debrief,” [raw:5251-5268])
- [ ] Ask: What score from 1–10 would you give the team’s performance, and why? (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “The Debrief,” [raw:5251-5268])
- [ ] Ask: What did the team do well? (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “The Debrief,” [raw:5251-5268])
- [ ] Ask: What could the team have done better? (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “The Debrief,” [raw:5251-5268])
- [ ] Ask: What magic moment did you notice? (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “The Debrief,” [raw:5251-5268])
- [ ] Ask: Anything else? (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “The Debrief,” [raw:5251-5268])
- [ ] Feed the captured stories, numbers, and insights into revisions for the next campaign. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, [raw:5042-5056], [raw:5251-5268])

#### Celebrate and Rest

- [ ] Celebrate the team’s effort at the end of the campaign regardless of the result. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “Time to Party and Rest,” [raw:5286-5303])
- [ ] Schedule genuine downtime after the campaign. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “Time to Party and Rest,” [raw:5286-5303])
- [ ] Keep the rest period free from email, calls, and work. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 5, “Time to Party and Rest,” [raw:5286-5303])

## Non-Negotiable Integrity Check

- [ ] Confirm the offering genuinely serves people and is not a gimmick, quick-sale device, or trick against unsuspecting buyers. (*Oversubscribed*, Introduction, [raw:895-900])
- [ ] Confirm all shortages and capacity limits are genuine and honestly explained. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 5, “It’s OK to Fail,” [raw:2129-2139])
- [ ] Confirm no pressure, manipulation, fake discount, invented deadline, or opportunistic scarcity was added. (*Oversubscribed*, Principle 4, [raw:1862-1873]; Phase 3, [raw:4469-4474], [raw:4595-4615]; “The CDE Team,” [raw:5618-5620])
- [ ] Confirm announced prices, capacity, prerequisites, and acceptance rules were honored. (*Oversubscribed*, Phase 2, [raw:3857-3878]; Phase 3, [raw:4703-4757])
- [ ] Confirm customers, staff, suppliers, and others touching the business were treated as partners with care and respect. (*Oversubscribed*, “The CDE Team — Creating Culture from the Beginning,” [raw:5627-5630])
- [ ] Confirm the campaign is intended to create value and positive impact, not pursued solely for money. (*Oversubscribed*, Part III, Conclusion, [raw:6055-6057], [raw:6105-6110])
