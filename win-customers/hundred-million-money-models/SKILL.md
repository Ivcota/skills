# $100M Money Models — The Four-Stage Offer Stack

A Money Model is a *sequence of offers* — Attraction, Upsell, Downsell, Continuity — designed so the gross profit from a customer in the first 30 days exceeds what it cost to acquire and serve them. Hormozi argues this is what separates businesses that can scale on credit-card float from businesses that need outside capital and "pray for profit." The thesis is one sentence: "make more money from customers than it costs to get them" (p. 18). When you do, "the cost of getting more customers will never be a problem again" (p. 19).

## Core Principle

A Money Model is a *sequence of offers*. "At their core, we find every opportunity to solve a customer's problem…and then offer to solve it" (p. 23). One offer is a transaction; a sequence is a system. Every offer creates the next problem; the next offer solves it.

Hormozi's foundational economic claim: cover the cost of acquiring a customer within **30 days**, because "any business can get interest-free money for 30 days in the form of a credit card" (p. 26). If 30-day gross profit per customer ≥ CAC + 30-day cost-to-serve, you can recycle the same dollar to acquire the next customer indefinitely. Cash stops being the constraint. This is what he calls a "$100M Money Model" — one that "makes more profit from one customer than it costs to get and service many customers in the first 30 days" (p. 181).

**The foundation:** "It costs many businesses more to get somebody to buy a thing than they make in profit off the thing. In other words, they lose money getting new customers — *that's a big problem*" (p. 25). A Money Model fixes this by stacking four offer types in sequence so the customer's *first* purchase already pays for the *next* customer's acquisition.

**The leverage math (p. 26):** If you double customer value × double volume × double speed of cash, "*your business grows 8x faster*. And if you triple them…*your business grows 27x faster*."

## Scoring — The CFA Breakdown

A Money Model passes or fails on one piece of arithmetic. Hormozi calls it 30-day cost recovery; the rest of the world calls it **Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA)**.

```
CFA Headroom  =  30-day Gross Profit per customer  −  (CAC + 30-day cost-to-serve)
```

| What it means | Action |
|---------------|--------|
| **CFA Headroom &lt; 0** | You lose money in the first 30 days. Cash drains every sale. The "death spiral" Hormozi describes on p. 25 |
| **CFA Headroom = 0** | Break-even on credit-card float. You can recycle the same dollar to acquire one new customer per cycle |
| **CFA Headroom &gt; 0** | You profit *and* fund the next acquisition. Each customer pays for themselves + a slice of the next |
| **CFA Multiple ≥ 1** (Headroom ≥ CAC + serve again) | One customer funds *another full acquisition*. This is the bare-minimum $100M Money Model bar (p. 181) |

**Worked example using Hormozi's gym numbers (p. 16):**

```
30-day GP per customer:  $680   ($600 program + $80 supplements)
CAC:                     $20    ($5/lead × 4 leads to close one)
30-day cost-to-serve:    ~$50   (estimated; not stated)
─────────────────────────────────
CFA Headroom:           +$610   per customer in the first 30 days
CFA Multiple:           ~8.7×   ($610 ÷ $70 = funds 8.7 more acquisitions)
```

This is why Hormozi could say "*$1 in → $34 out in 48 hours*" (p. 16) and why cash stopped being his constraint.

### The three-tier bar (p. 181)

- **Bare minimum:** CFA Headroom ≥ 0. "Makes more profit from a customer than it costs to get and service them in the first 30 days." Scale on credit-card float (the 30-day interest-free window, p. 26).
- **Good:** CFA Headroom &gt; 0 with margin to reinvest in scaling.
- **$100M Money Model:** CFA Multiple ≥ N where N customers can be acquired from one customer's 30-day GP. "Makes more profit from one customer than it costs to get and service *many* customers in the first 30 days."

### How each offer type moves the equation

| Offer type | Lever | What it changes |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| **Attraction** | Lowers effective CAC | Free/discount drives volume; built-in upsell hooks raise GP |
| **Upsell** | Raises 30-day GP | McDonald's-style 8–11× lift on the same customer (p. 80) |
| **Downsell** | Raises conversion rate (effectively lowering CAC) | Captures the 30–50% who would have said no |
| **Continuity** | Defers cash but lifts LTV | Doesn't help 30-day CFA directly — but compounds the customer pool that future upsells run on |

If your model fails the bare minimum, the rest of this skill is the playbook for fixing it. Add an Attraction Offer with built-in upsell triggers; add an Upsell at the moment of greatest need; add a Downsell to convert the no's; add Continuity to compound LTV.

## The Four Offer Types

Sequence matters: customers move through these in order. "I start with Attraction Offers, because if you're not getting customers, you need one of those first. Then, we cover Upsell Offers, followed by Downsell Offers. Then to finish the four types, I show you my favorite Continuity Offers" (p. 30). Permissive override: "Any Offer Can Be Used On Its Own, At Any Time, in Any Order" (p. 33).

### 1. Attraction Offers — turn strangers into customers

**Core concept:** "Attraction Offers generate leads *and* convert them into customers. They turn advertising into money by offering something free or at a discount" (p. 35). The role: cover CAC + delivery cost, ideally "*multiple times over*" (p. 83). Hormozi's five favorites: **Win Your Money Back, Giveaways, Decoy Offer, Buy X Get Y Free, Pay Less Now or Pay More Later**.

**Why it works:** Strangers can't yet judge value, but they can judge price. Free and discounts collapse risk to near zero. "Free and discount sit on a single continuum…they all discount a product to some level — even if you discount it by 100%" (p. 35). And "One free thing is awesome. Two free things are awesomer. And, maybe to get those two free things, *they have to buy one*. That's how we make money on free stuff" (p. 36).

**Key insights:**
- **Win Your Money Back** has "generated over $1 billion in sales industry-wide" and works on *Results*, *Actions*, or *Both* — "they bet on their own ability" (p. 41, 43).
- **Giveaways:** "make your Grand Prize *the thing you want everyone to buy*"; everyone else gets a "promotional offer" (the participation trophy) at the discount you assign relative to the Grand Prize anchor (p. 52).
- **Decoy Offer:** advertise a free or simpler version; when the lead engages, present a premium option side-by-side. "We got about eight out of ten people to take the $399 option" (p. 61).
- **Buy X Get Y Free** beats equivalent percent discounts because "more people know the value of *free* than the value of one shirt" (p. 71). Origin: Boot Factory's "Buy 1 Pair Get Two Pair Free" at $600 — three pairs of $200 boots reframed (p. 68).
- **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later:** $0 today + billed $X later (with conditional satisfaction guarantee) OR pay 20–50% less now + bonuses. "*combines the benefits of a delayed payment and a satisfaction guarantee*" (p. 75).
- **Discount over Free** for show-up sensitive businesses (doctors, lawyers, dentists) — fewer leads, but higher show rate (p. 64).

**Product applications:**

| Context | Application | Example (verbatim) |
|---------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Local service (gym/fitness)** | Win Your Money Back tied to a 6-week or 28-day blueprint | "$500 → train 8 weeks → hit goal → get $500 back as cash or credit; 13 referrals from one before/after" (p. 38–39) |
| **Physical product (DTC/retail)** | Buy X Get Y Free | "Buy 1 Pair Get Two Pair Free" at $600 — same as three pairs at $200 each (p. 68–69) |
| **Coaching / B2B** | Win Your Money Back: 5 Customers In 5 Days Free Challenge | "Send 100 messages/day, attend daily training, post homework, attend day-5 call" (p. 40) |
| **Digital product / education** | Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | "$0 + billed $297 if you don't double your reading speed in 3 hours, OR $97 today + recordings" (p. 74) |
| **High-ticket service** | Giveaway with $50,000 industry bundle as Grand Prize | Tickets-only Grand Prize flopped; replaced with "$50,000 bundle of equipment from a well-known industry supplier *plus* their core product for a year" — crushed (p. 55) |
| **Subscription / recurring** | Decoy Offer (free basic vs. $399 premium with 1:1 + guarantee) | Gym version: 1 session/week free vs. unlimited + coaching at $399, 80% chose premium (p. 61) |

**Copy patterns:**
- "If you do [X] within [Y] time within [Z] rules, you can get it free." (Win Your Money Back tagline, p. 38)
- "Many will enter…many will win." (Giveaways tagline, p. 43)
- "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" (Decoy permission question, p. 65)
- "Buy [X] Get [Y] Free" — keep more *free* stuff than *paid* (p. 70–71)
- "You can pay $0 now and get billed $[FULL] tomorrow if you don't [QUALIFY], OR pay $[DISCOUNTED] now and get [BONUS]." (Pay Less Now or Pay More Later, p. 74)

**Ethical boundary:** "If A Customer Asks For Their Money Back — *Give It Back*" (p. 32). "Hard Selling Is For Weak Products" (p. 32). "Be Transparent. State the facts… Don't lie. You'll short-change yourself long-term" (p. 32–33). Only run Win Your Money Back if your refund rate is already below 5% (p. 46). Sweepstakes are heavily regulated — consult counsel (p. 50).

### 2. Upsell Offers — get people to spend more cash

**Core concept:** "Upsells just mean *whatever we offer next*" (p. 78). The role: get 30-day profits "*well above*" CAC + delivery cost (p. 175). Hormozi's four favorites: **Classic Upsell, Menu Upsell, Anchor Upsell, Rollover Upsell**.

**Why it works:** "When an offer solves a problem, another appears. You *upsell* the solution to the problem your offer reveals" (p. 80). Current customers always have a higher chance of buying than strangers. McDonald's math: burger profit $0.25 → "Do you want fries?" → "Make it a meal?" → supersize → profit $3.00 — "*a 11.6x increase*" (p. 80). "*The thing you sell the most isn't always the thing you make the most profit on*" (p. 81).

**Key insights:**
- **Classic Upsell** = "You can't have X without Y!" Solve the *next* problem the moment it appears (helmet after bike, sealant after car wash, nutrition after exercise course) (p. 83–86).
- **Menu Upsell** combines four moves: **Unsell** (cross out what they don't need) → **Prescribe** (tell them what they do need + how to use it) → **A/B Upsell** ("chocolate or vanilla?") → **Card On File** ("just use the card on file?"). Hormozi sold 20 supplement customers in a row using this (p. 95–99).
- **Anchor Upsell:** show the premium thing first at 5–10× the price. Wait for "the gasp," then come to the rescue with the main offer at ~1/5 the price. Suit shop: $16,000 anchor → bought $2,200 (p. 107–113).
- **Rollover Upsell** is Hormozi's most-used: "credit some or all of a customer's previous purchases toward your next offer" (p. 118). Price the next offer 4× higher than the credit (= 25% effective discount). Works on lapsed customers, upset customers, *competitors'* upset customers, and current customers.
- **Magnetic Middle pricing:** to push customers up a tier, narrow the gap to the higher tier and widen the gap to the lower tier. (p. 92–93)
- **BAMFAM:** "Book A Meeting From A Meeting." "*A customer should know the next time they see you — and why — before they leave*" (p. 91).

**Product applications:**

| Context | Application | Example (verbatim) |
|---------|-------------|--------------------|
| **QSR / Retail** | Classic Upsell (bundle ladder) | McDonald's: burger $0.25 GP → meal $2.00 GP → supersize $3.00 GP — 11.6× lift (p. 80) |
| **Fitness / supplements** | Menu Upsell (Unsell → Prescribe → A/B → Card On File) | "Chocolate or vanilla protein? Kiwi or strawberry pre-workout? Use card on file?" — 20 closes in a row (p. 95–98) |
| **Service / consulting** | Anchor Upsell at 5–10× | $16,000 anchor → $2,200 main offer + $300 in accessories (p. 107–109) |
| **Lapsed customer winback** | Rollover Upsell | 200 personalized videos → $4,000 credit each → 20% take rate → ~$1.9M extra annual revenue (p. 121) |
| **Subscription** | Rollover into 12-month at $50/mo discount | "$600 first purchase rolled over = $50/mo discount × 12 months" (p. 119) |
| **Insurance / guarantees** | Add 5–50% to price for guarantee | Art studio replaced damaged frames free → started charging +10% → 30% take rate, "*pure profit*" (p. 93) |

**Copy patterns:**
- "You can't have [X] without [Y]." (Classic Upsell, p. 85)
- "You don't need that…you need this." (Menu Upsell — Unsell + Prescribe, p. 95)
- "You don't want anything else, do you?" (Say-no-to-say-yes question, p. 88)
- "[Premium]…or, this may be a better fit for you." (Anchor Upsell rescue, p. 107)
- "Since you already spent $[X], I'll just credit that towards [next offer]." (Rollover Upsell, p. 118)
- "What card do you wanna use?" / "Wanna just use the card on file?" (Card On File close, p. 99, 103)

**Ethical boundary:** "**Warning**: This section is brutally effective and must be used ethically" (p. 82). Anchor Upsells fail if you treat the anchor as fake — "you need to actually sell it" (p. 111). Only sell upsells that solve real next-problems; don't manufacture problems. "*The second worst thing that happens is they say no. The worst thing is if they would've said yes but you never asked*" (p. 92).

See: [references/upsell-offers.md](references/upsell-offers.md) for full scripts, magnetic-middle pricing tables, and the four-tactic Menu Upsell recipe.

### 3. Downsell Offers — get people to say yes when they would have said no

**Core concept:** "Downselling tweaks the original offer to find the highest value solution *for the customer's budget*. So any offer you make after someone says 'no' is a downsell" (p. 122). Hormozi's three sub-types: **Payment Plan Downsells, Trial With Penalty, Feature Downsells**.

**Why it works:** A "no" is "no for *this* offer, not all offers" (p. 123). Two axes of downselling: change *how they pay* (Payment Plan, Trial With Penalty) or *what they get* (Feature). The offer is "*never the same stuff for cheaper*" — that's discounting, and it kills trust. Sample close-rate impact: 100 calls → 25 buy at full price → after adding feature downsell → 35 buy main + 40 take downsell = 75% close rate (p. 152).

**Key insights:**
- **Payment Plan Downsell:** "*'It costs too much' really means 'this costs too much up front*'" (p. 128). Seven-step ladder: paid-in-full discount → 3rd-party financing → ½ now + ½ later → scale check → ⅓ × 3 → evenly spread → free trial.
- **Profitwell churn data (14,000 businesses, p. 134):** monthly billing → 10.7% monthly cancellation; quarterly → 5%; annual → 2%. "Start high (fewer bigger payments) and work your way down."
- **Trial With Penalty** = free if you complete the activation criteria; pay if you don't. Use *only* for recurring services where the customer must do work to get results (p. 147). "Just call it a Free Trial" externally — penalty is a backstop, not a marketing label (p. 147).
- **Feature Downsell:** remove features from highest to lowest value, drop price, ask "how about now?" Customers re-upsell themselves on the original when they see the value of what they'd lose (p. 152–153).
- **Naming the bottom rung "The Minimum"** lets you ask "*so nothing more than the minimum package then?*" — say no to say yes (p. 159).
- **"Deal?" or "Fair Enough?"** after each downsell — "*works astonishingly well*" (p. 159).
- **Barter discounts for advertising:** "$100 off if you: leave a review, video testimonial, public progress posts, two referrals." Advertising disguised as discount (p. 160).

**Product applications:**

| Context | Application | Example (verbatim) |
|---------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Coaching / fitness** | Payment Plan Downsell ladder | "Half now + half on payday" → ⅓ × 3 → evenly spread × 16 weeks (Gym Launch) (p. 129–131) |
| **SaaS onboarding** | Trial With Penalty | "$500 onboarding waived if you attend three Zoom calls + activate profile by call 3, then $99/mo" (p. 140) |
| **Legal / financial / pro services** | Feature Downsell — guarantee | "Drop money-back guarantee → save $400" → 25% close rate → 75% close rate (35 main + 40 downsell) (p. 151–152) |
| **Local service (chiropractor, painter)** | DFY → DWY → DIY downsell | "Instead of adjustments, here's tools you can use at home" / "Lease the spray machine instead of hiring us" (p. 156) |
| **Subscription with high churn** | Payment cadence shift | Bill weekly instead of monthly: 13 four-week cycles vs 12 months = +8.3% revenue at same conversion (p. 160) |

**Copy patterns:**
- "When's the next time you get paid? Wanna just put half down today and the rest down when you get paid?" (Payment Plan, p. 130)
- "Got it. So money is tight. Real quick — on a scale from 1–10 how bad do you wanna do this?" (Scale check, p. 130)
- "If you do [X, Y, Z], I'll let you start for free." (Trial With Penalty, p. 138)
- "If you're okay without [feature], I can knock off $[amount]." (Feature Downsell, p. 152)
- "Deal?" / "Fair enough?" (Close after every downsell tier, p. 159)

**Ethical boundary:** "Customers Talk About Price" — never charge two customers different prices for the same thing without a clear reason (p. 124). Trial With Penalty: "I can't ethically promise you that" — frame the trial around habits, not unrealistic results (p. 143). On the penalty itself: "You make money by getting people results and turning them into customers, *not* nickeling and diming people with fees" (p. 148). Customers must initial fee clauses separately (p. 144).

See: [references/downsell-offers.md](references/downsell-offers.md) for the seven-step Payment Plan ladder, the five-step Trial With Penalty sales script, and the Feature Downsell decision tree.

### 4. Continuity Offers — keep people buying

**Core concept:** Continuity Offers "*provide ongoing value that customers make ongoing payments for — until they cancel*" (p. 142). Three sub-types: **Continuity Bonus Offers, Continuity Discount Offers, Waived Fee Offer**.

**Why it works:** *"You can shear a sheep for a lifetime, but you can only skin it once*" (p. 142, John). Continuity multiplies LTV and gives you something to *keep* selling. The economic case (p. 142–143): $1,000 one-time at 10% conversion → $10,000 from 100 leads. Reframe as $50/mo continuity at 40% conversion held 20 months → "*you still make $1,000 from each customer*" but get **4× the customer pool** to upsell. "$10,000 now and $0 over time" → "$2,000 now and $40,000 over time."

**Key insights:**
- **Sequence rule:** make Continuity *last*. "I start with profitable Attraction Offers. Then make my Upsell and Downsell Offers. *Then,* I offer Continuity" (p. 175). Standalone continuity ads almost never work — "*Nobody wants to make a recurring commitment to something they haven't tried*" (p. 147).
- **Continuity Bonus Offers:** anchor an awesome bonus, then sell continuity behind it. Pricing table for one-time vs membership take rates (p. 152): standalone 1.33× monthly = 50% take continuity; 2× = 70%; 2.66× = 90%.
- **Continuity Discount Offers:** four ways to apply the discount (p. 158–160) — Up Front / At The End / Spread Over Time / After 1–2 Payments. Spreading is safer if churn history is unknown.
- **The "billing cycles" trick (p. 160):** bill every 4 weeks instead of monthly. 13 four-week cycles per year vs. 12 months = **+8.3% annual revenue at the same conversion**. On a 20%-margin business, that's +41% to profit.
- **Waived Fee Offer (p. 169):** charge a 3–5× monthly setup fee, waive it for a 12-month commitment, charge it on early cancellation. "Customers will stay longer if leaving costs more than staying" (p. 169).
- **3% processing fee** waivable with second form of payment / ACH = +30% to a 10%-margin business (p. 161).
- **Rule of thumb:** if more than 5% want to cancel early, the product is broken — "*pricing can't (and shouldn't) overcome a terrible product*" (p. 170).

**Product applications:**

| Context | Application | Example (verbatim) |
|---------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Pet / DTC subscription** | Continuity Bonus Offer | "Free $800 of dog toys when you sign up for $59/mo dog food + new toy each month" (p. 147) |
| **Coaching membership** | Bonus + bulk-prepay upsell | "Free $1,000 short-term accelerator + $100/mo VIP. Wanna save more? Prepay 6 months at discount" (p. 147–148) |
| **B2B services (trash, landscaping, SEO)** | Continuity Discount: 1 year free / 5 years paid | Trash hauler: free year for a 5-year contract → sold business at exit (p. 157) |
| **Digital newsletter / community** | Bonus + lifetime discount lock | "$15,880 of past newsletters free + $399/mo, OR pay today and lock $299/mo for life" (p. 149) |
| **High-ticket coaching / agency** | Waived Fee Offer | "Month-to-month: $5,000 setup + $1,000/mo. OR $1,000/mo with 12-month commit, fee charged on early cancel" (p. 169) |
| **Anything recurring** | 4-week billing instead of monthly | +8.3% annual revenue, +41% profit at 20% margins (p. 160) |

**Copy patterns:**
- "Become a member today and you'll get [bonus] as a free gift. Or, you can just buy it for $[X] — which would you prefer?" (Bonus anchor, p. 151)
- "If you sign up today, you get [X] time free." (Continuity Discount, p. 156)
- "You can sign up month-to-month with a setup fee, or I'll waive it if you commit to a year." (Waived Fee, p. 167)
- "We charge a 3% processing fee. Wanna save it? Just give us a second form of payment." (p. 161)
- "What cause do you absolutely hate? *If you cancel early, I'll donate your setup fee to them.*" (Cancellation-fee-for-a-cause, p. 171)

**Ethical boundary:** "**Make Sure Customers Know How To Cancel.** *Small businesses don't get rich by making stuff hard for their customers*" (p. 163). Hormozi's preferred cancellation policy: "make the cancellation fee *equal to the discount they agreed to get*" — equitable, not punitive (p. 162). Drop the fee after the customer fulfills the commitment (p. 170). On Waived Fees: "*nudge them, not handcuff people into paying for something they hate*" (p. 170). If &gt;5% are trying to cancel early, fix the product, not the contract (p. 170).

See: [references/continuity-offers.md](references/continuity-offers.md) for the standalone-vs-continuity pricing table, the four discount-application methods, and the Waived Fee math.

## The $100M Money Model Process

Build the model **one stage at a time** (p. 175–178). "Patience is still the fastest way to get to your goal" (p. 176).

1. **Start with an Attraction Offer.** Cover CAC. Pick one of the five (Win Your Money Back, Giveaways, Decoy, Buy X Get Y Free, Pay Less Now or Pay More Later). Advertise. Iterate until it reliably produces customers — "may take up to a year" (p. 175).
2. **Add an Upsell Offer.** Pick the type that matches the next problem your Attraction Offer reveals (Classic for immediate next-problem, Menu when you have multiple offers, Anchor when budgets vary, Rollover for lapsed/upset/cross-customers). Make the offer at the moment of greatest need (p. 175).
3. **Add a Downsell Offer.** For nos who can pay something — change *how* they pay (Payment Plan or Trial With Penalty). For nos who want different scope — change *what* they get (Feature Downsell). "Alternate between them in the same sale" (p. 175).
4. **Add a Continuity Offer.** Sell continuity *last*. Pick Bonus (rewards), Discount (rewards over time), or Waived Fee (committal/punishment). Then upsell a bulk prepay at a discount; the customer rolls into month-to-month after the bulk runs out (p. 175).

Assembly rules (p. 176–178):
- **Perfect one offer at a time.** Don't implement the whole Money Model at once — "It will break your business" (p. 178).
- **Measure in quarters, not weeks** (p. 176).
- **Raise prices in stages.** Start cheap to harvest yeses + feedback. Raise price until you start making *less* money (p. 176).
- **Simple Scales. Fancy Fails.** "100 ways to offer your product" beats "100 products to offer" (p. 177).
- **Use affiliate products to fill gaps** without taking on new delivery (p. 177).
- **Convert Attraction Offers into Continuity** with auto-renewal — e.g., "Buy 6 Months Get 6 Months Free" rolls into month-to-month at month 12 (p. 177).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| **Implementing all four offer types at once** | Each layer needs to be reliable before the next is added; stacking unreliable layers compounds failure | Perfect one stage at a time, then advance (p. 176) |
| **Selling "the same thing for cheaper" as a downsell** | It's a discount, not a downsell; trains customers to negotiate; erodes trust ("Customers Talk About Price") | Change *how they pay* or *what they get* — never just price (p. 122–124) |
| **Leading with continuity in ads** | "Nobody wants to make a recurring commitment to something they haven't tried" — kills 30-day GP, makes ads unprofitable | Make continuity the *last* offer; lead with Attraction (p. 147, 175) |
| **Up-front continuity discount in a high-churn business** | You give months free before you've earned trust; customers leave before paying | Spread discount over the term, or apply after 1–2 payments (p. 158) |
| **No premium / Anchor Offer** | "*The only thing worse than making a $1,000 offer to a person with a $100 budget is making a $100 offer to someone with a $1,000 budget*" — you lose $900 (p. 110) | Always have a 5–10× anchor, even if most don't buy |
| **Fake anchor in Anchor Upsell** | If you treat the anchor as a fake, the customer will too | Build a premium offer you'd be happy to deliver (p. 111) |
| **Trial With Penalty without sales touchpoints** | No upsell path; trial converts at a flat rate | Build sales meetings into the trial criteria (the customer must attend them to qualify) (p. 144) |
| **Hard cancellation policy** | "If customers have nowhere to complain inside your business, they will definitely complain outside" — 1-star reviews compound | Cancellation fee = the discount they got; obvious cancellation path; offer exit interview (p. 162–164) |
| **Buying Win Your Money Back when refund rate is &gt;5%** | Every refund is a loss; product is the bottleneck, not the offer | Fix the product first; refund rate must be &lt;5% before deploying (p. 46, 48) |
| **Standalone continuity offer in ads** | Won't convert — "Continuity Offers are tough to advertise profitably" | Front-end with an Attraction Offer; bolt continuity on as bonus or upsell (p. 147) |
| **Starting more businesses to make more offers** | Splits focus, dilutes leverage | "100 ways to offer your product" — same product, new packages (p. 177) |
| **Forcing continuity inside the first 30 days** | Sometimes the right timing is *after* day 30 | "Better to make the offer at the right time than to force it at the wrong time" (p. 176) |

## Quick Diagnostic

Audit an existing money model by walking these eight questions in order:

| Question | If No | Action |
|----------|-------|--------|
| Does my Attraction Offer cover CAC + delivery cost in 30 days? | Cash will run out before reinvestment cycles | Test a stronger Attraction Offer from the five (start with Win Your Money Back or Buy X Get Y Free) |
| Have I named the *next problem* my Attraction Offer creates? | I have no upsell hook | Map the customer journey post-purchase and pick the Classic Upsell ("X without Y!") |
| Do I have an Upsell that 30%+ of customers take? | Not maximizing 30-day GP | Add Magnetic Middle pricing or Menu Upsell (Unsell → Prescribe → A/B → Card On File) |
| Do I offer something to people who say no? | I'm leaving 30–50% of revenue uncaptured | Add Payment Plan (cost-too-much-up-front), Trial With Penalty (recurring service), or Feature Downsell (one-time) |
| Do I have a 5–10× premium offer in the room? | I'm losing $900 every time a $1,000-budget customer takes a $100 thing (p. 110) | Build an Anchor Upsell with primary features identical to the main offer |
| Do I have a Continuity Offer? | LTV stays flat; customer pool can't compound | Pick Bonus (reward-led), Discount (committal-reward), or Waived Fee (committal-fee) |
| Is the continuity offered *last*, after the customer has experienced the product? | Standalone continuity ads burn cash | Move continuity to post-purchase upsell |
| Is my refund/cancellation experience &lt;5% friction-driven? | Bad reviews compound; products with &gt;5% cancel-early need fixing, not contracts | Make cancellation obvious; offer exit interview; fix the product |
