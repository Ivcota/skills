# Downsell Offers — Deep Dive

> "Downselling tweaks the original offer to find the highest value solution *for the customer's budget*." — Hormozi (p. 122)

A downsell is anything you offer *after* a no. Hormozi's three sub-types and the ethics rule that separates a downsell from a discount.

## The Rule That Separates Downsell from Discount (p. 123)

> "The offer is *never the same stuff for cheaper*. We just keep tweaking the offer until we make it *the best deal for them*." (p. 152)

Cautionary tale: a car salesman dropped insurance from $5,000 → $400 for the **same product**. Customer lost trust in him *and* the car deal too. "You can offer something different for less. You just can't offer the *same thing* for less." (p. 123)

## Six Rules of Downselling (p. 123–124)

1. **No means no for *this* offer, not all offers.**
2. **Downsells are trades.** "*If you're gonna give something, get something.*"
3. **Personalize, don't pressure.**
4. **Offer the same things in new ways.** "Think of downselling more like a hundred ways to offer the stuff you already have."
5. **Don't drop your price just to get somebody to buy.**
6. **Customers talk about price.** Charging less "*just because*" creates an ethical problem.

## Two Axes of Downselling (p. 122)

- **How they pay** → Payment Plan Downsell, Trial With Penalty
- **What they get** → Feature Downsell

## 1. Payment Plan Downsell (p. 127–135)

> "*A huge percentage of the time 'it costs too much' really means 'this costs too much **up front**.'*" (p. 128)

People say they want a discount, but really want to pay less *in the moment*. Payment plans get more buyers AND preserve full price over time.

### The 7-step ladder (p. 129)

Apply in order — escalate only if the prior step fails.

1. **Reward paying in full** rather than punish paying over time. Quote price *with interest included*, then offer prepay discount: "*It's $15…but it's $10 if you prepay it. You save $5…that's what most people do.*"
2. **Offer 3rd-party financing** / credit card / layaway. Frame: "*Would you rather I decide your payment terms or you decide?*"
3. **Half now, half later.** "*When's the next time you get paid?*" → "*Wanna just put half down today and the rest down when you get paid?*" If not: "*What's the most you can put down today?*"
4. **Scale check.** "*Real quick — on a scale from 1–10 how bad do you wanna do this?*" If 8+: "*Awesome, we're gonna figure out a way.*" If 7 or below: "*Why not a 10?*" → move to Feature Downsells.
5. **One-third now × 3.** Either ⅓ + ⅓ on next two paychecks, or ⅓ + ⅓ over next two months.
6. **Evenly spread.** Across the service term (Gym Launch ran 16 weeks, charged each week).
7. **Free Trial.** Move to Trial With Penalty.

### "Seesaw" Downselling (p. 132)

> "*Would you rather have giant monthly payments or tiny ones?*"
>
> Tiny → "*It normally costs XXX. If you prepay today, you get a huge discount and zero monthly payments. That work?*"
>
> Can't afford up front → "*We'll simply adjust the down payment until you get a monthly rate you like.*"

### Profitwell churn data — 14,000 businesses (p. 134)

| Billing cadence | Monthly cancellation rate |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Monthly | **10.7%** |
| Quarterly | 5% |
| Annual | 2% |

> Rule: "*Start high (fewer bigger payments) and work your way down.*"

### Hidden upsell built in (p. 132)

Periodically offer the original prepay discount to people on a payment plan. They love it.

### Recover declined payments (p. 133)

Align payment schedules with paycheck schedules. Re-run declined cards multiple times **the same day**. "*I often recoup a third of my declined payments.*"

### Watch the dilution (p. 134)

After implementing, close rate goes up. But watch the paid-in-full count: "*You want to close more appointments overall but with the same percentage of appointments paying in full.*"

## 2. Trial With Penalty (p. 137–148)

> "*Here's my thing, you get it for free **so long as you do this stuff**…which makes you a perfect fit for my next offer. And if you don't, **then** you have to pay for it.*" (p. 138)

### Definition (p. 138)

"Customers can try your product or service for free *so long as they meet your terms*. Win Your Money Back gives money back *if they meet the terms*. Trial With Penalty: customers only **pay** *if they don't meet the terms*."

### Origin: Leila and HR software (p. 137–138)

Leila bought complicated HR software because they made onboarding free *only if* she completed training. She completed it, became locked in, kept paying.

### Use ONLY when (p. 147)

- Recurring product or service
- Customer must do work to get results
- Use **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later** instead for one-time / physical products

### Five-step sales script (p. 141–144)

1. **Use Free Trial as a downsell** (don't lead with it):
   > "*Hmmm…that sure is a pickle. I'll tell ya what — how about we just get ya started for free, would you be okay with that? We can just help you out, and if you like it, you can stay. Let me get your ID and we can get the process started — fair enough?*"

2. **Always get a credit card.** "*What card do you wanna use?*" If they balk: "*That's just how we've always done it.*" If they still refuse, show them out.

3. **Set the expectation of staying & paying.** "*If this program got you the result, will you stay long-term?*" If no: no trial. If they hesitate:
   > "*I don't want you to try it. I want you to get results. Out of integrity, I want to set realistic goals. You are not going to hit your long-term goals during this trial. But you will establish habits. We're gonna help you do that for free. But to get to your long-term results, you're gonna have to stay on after. I just wanna make sure you're not looking for a quick fix — because I can't ethically promise you that.*"

4. **Explain how fees motivate** (*after* getting their card):
   > "*We will do our part **so long as you do yours**. You bet on yourself — if you miss or skip stuff, your results will suffer. We charge to keep you on track. If you miss, no big deal. You'll get dinged a little fee. If you follow through, you get all this for free. So this is the best way we can get you amazing results and keep it free for you. Best of both worlds.*"

5. **Make sales meetings part of the trial criteria.** "*You agree to attend each of the three check-ins. We charge if you miss these because it's the only way we can get you results.*"

### Worked examples (p. 140–141)

| Industry | Free if you… | Penalty | Built-in upsell |
|----------|--------------|---------|-----------------|
| B2C — 28-Day Habit Blueprint | Attend all consulting calls + post weekly + journal daily + attend feedback session | Pay $X | Transformation session |
| B2B — 5-Day Get First 5 Customers Challenge | 100 outbound msgs/day + report stats + attend daily training + post in group + attend graduation | Pay $X | Graduation call upsell |
| SaaS — $500 HR Onboarding | Attend 3 × 60-min Zoom onboarding + homework + activate profile + employee setup | $500 fee | $99/mo subscription |

### Naming rule (p. 147)

> "*Just call it a Free Trial.*" Penalty is a backstop, not a marketing label. Deflectors: "*This is just how we've always done it*" / "*People get the best results this way.*"

### Initial each fee clause (p. 144)

Customers must initial fee clauses separately. "*Forces my sales guys to explain them.*"

### Upselling from a trial (p. 146)

Three outcomes from any trial:

- **Loved it** → upsell to full program
- **Hated it** → "*Turn that frown upside down.*" Ask what they'd have liked different. Tell them they're right. Be angry at *yourself*. Offer the higher-level fix → **about half buy.**
- **Didn't use it** → reach out multiple times before billing. Offer to waive the fee if they meet. *"I don't like billing non-starters. A small fee isn't worth a 1-star review."*

### Doubling math (p. 138)

Close 3 of 10 on Attraction → downsell another 4 onto Trial With Penalty → upsell 3 of those after the trial → **3 sales become 6 sales.**

### Ethical guardrails

- "*I can't ethically promise you that*" — frame trial around habits, not unrealistic results (p. 143)
- "*You make money by getting people results and turning them into customers, not nickeling and diming people with fees.*" (p. 148)
- Customers initial fee clauses separately (p. 144)
- "*If they say no [to staying long-term], there's no point in giving them a trial.*" (p. 142)

## 3. Feature Downsell (p. 151–162)

> "Lower prices by changing what customers get. Lesser quantity, lower quality, lower price alternatives, or cutting optional components." (p. 152)

### The mechanism: re-upsell via removal (p. 152–153)

"All features have a price and a value. Remove something → price drops. *Value drops too.* People weigh how much money they save against how much value they lose. So clever Feature Downselling gets customers to **re-upsell themselves on the more expensive offer**."

> Rule: **Remove features from highest to lowest value.**

### Worked example: tripled close rate (p. 151–152)

Friend's funnel: 100 calls → 25 buy at full price.

Added Feature Downsell: drop money-back guarantee = $400 off.
- **Script:** "*If you don't want the option to get your money back, you can pay less. Or, you can keep your money-back guarantee — which would you prefer?*"
- **Result:** 100 calls → **35 buy main + 40 take downsell** = **75% close rate** (up from 25%).
- Why: customers only saw the value of the guarantee *after* it was offered as a removable feature.

### Recipe (p. 153)

"Take something away, lower the price, and in so many words ask **'how about now?'**"

### Sequence (p. 158)

1. **First**, cut something valuable, lower price *a little*. Goal: drive re-upsell on the original.
2. If that fails, keep removing features and lowering prices until they buy.
3. "*I'd rather people get something rather than nothing.*"

### Feature Downsell categories with scripts (p. 154–156)

| Dimension | Script |
|-----------|--------|
| Quantity | "*Instead of three months supply, how about we start with just one?*" |
| Quantity (sessions) | "*Instead of four sessions per month, why don't we start at two?*" |
| Product quality | "*Instead of leather seats we can do vinyl, how does that sound?*" |
| Service speed | "*Instead of 5-minute response times, why don't we start at overnight? Save some money, still get answers.*" |
| Remove components | "*Instead of priority chat + email + calls, why don't we keep chat + email but drop calls to save you money?*" |
| DFY → DWY → DIY | "*Instead of chiropractic adjustments, here's tools you can use at home.*" / Painter: lease the spray machine. Hormozi: instead of acquiring your company, "*why don't you just attend a workshop?*" |

### Service-quality dimensions you can dial down (p. 154–155)

Time availability · location · cancellations · speed of response · speed of delivery · service ratio (1:1 vs many:1) · communication method · provider qualifications · live vs recorded · in-person vs remote · DIY/DWY/DFY · expirations · personalization · insurance/guarantee.

### Naming feature combinations (p. 158–159)

- Most expensive: "*The Whale Package*" / "*Total Transformation*" / "*High Roller.*"
- Airline frame: First / Business / Economy
- Cheapest: "*The Minimum*" — lets you ask "*so nothing more than the minimum package then?*" (Say no to say yes.)

### Temperature check after two downsells (p. 159)

> "*Got it. Real quick — on a scale from 1–10 how bad do you want this?*"

- **8+:** "*Awesome, we're gonna figure out a way.*"
- **7 or below:** "*What would a 10 look like?*" → recombine features.

### "Deal?" or "Fair Enough?" close (p. 159)

> "*Works astonishingly well.*"

### Free Orientation tactic (p. 159)

When DFY is rejected: "*Even though we're not gonna work together on X, I still want to help. How about you just come to a free orientation tomorrow?*" About half show. **Almost all of those buy supplements (DIY).**

### Feature-Downsell current customers (p. 159–160)

"Once you see a customer isn't using a feature, offer a lower price — only paying for the features they use." → "Customers we've downsold into a lower package *just for them* have the **second highest value** of all my customers."

### Barter With Reviews, Testimonials, Referrals (p. 160)

> "*I'll knock $100 off if you: 1) Leave me a review on all review sites 2) Leave me a video testimonial 3) Make a public social post at the beginning, middle, and end 4) Introduce me to two friends. Deal?*"

Discount in exchange for advertising.

### Pitfalls / decision rules (p. 162)

- Removing features customers *love* + small price drop → original wins (drives re-upsell)
- Removing features customers *hate* + big price drop → downsell wins
- If a prospect rejects multiple downsells, see if they still want your thing
- If they like a feature combo but not the price → start Payment Plan Downselling
- Feature-downsell current customers *before* they cancel

## Choosing between the three

| Customer says… | Use |
|---------------|-----|
| "Costs too much *right now*" | **Payment Plan** — change *how they pay* |
| "Can't afford it; recurring service; needs to do work" | **Trial With Penalty** — free if they perform |
| "Want different scope" / one-time service / re-upsell on original | **Feature Downsell** — change *what they get* |

> "*When you use both [payment plan and feature], you become very difficult to refuse.*" (p. 159)

## Closing tagline (p. 164)

> "*Everybody buys something.*"
