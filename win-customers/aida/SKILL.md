# AIDA Copywriting Coach

Use this skill to help the user build persuasive copy **one step at a time** using AIDA:

1. **Attention** — grab the right person's attention.
2. **Interest** — make them care enough to keep reading.
3. **Desire** — connect the idea to a concrete want, outcome, fear, status shift, or relief.
4. **Action** — tell them exactly what to do next.

## Operating rule

Ask **one question at a time**. Do not write the full piece upfront unless the user explicitly asks for a complete draft.

Default flow:

1. Ask what they are writing and for whom.
2. Ask for the goal / desired action.
3. Ask for the style: user may choose a style, describe a tone, or paste a sample.
4. Work through A → I → D → A in order.
5. At each step: explain the job of that section, ask for or propose 3 options, let the user choose, then move on.
6. After all four parts are chosen, assemble the draft and offer one tightening pass.

## AIDA prompts
### A — Attention
Goal: stop the right reader and make them think, “this is about me.”

Ask:
- Who needs to stop scrolling / reading?
- What specific image, weird observation, pain, desire, or contradiction would they recognize instantly?

Good attention patterns:
- “Have you ever noticed...?”
- “You know those people who...?”
- “Most people think X. The weird part is Y.”
- “If you're [specific person] trying to [specific outcome]...”
- Concrete scene > abstract claim.

### I — Interest
Goal: explain why the hook matters.

Ask:
- Why is this happening?
- What mechanism, story, example, or surprising reason keeps them reading?

Good interest patterns:
- “The reason is...”
- “Here's what's actually going on...”
- “The mistake is not X. It's Y.”
- “This matters because...”

### D — Desire
Goal: make them want the result.

Ask:
- What do they get if they believe / use / buy / try this?
- What pain disappears or what status, speed, money, confidence, relief, or capability appears?

Good desire patterns:
- Tie feature → outcome → emotion.
- Show before/after.
- Make the benefit specific and vivid.
- If practicing, fictional proof or exaggerated claims are allowed.

### A — Action
Goal: make the next step obvious and specific.

Ask:
- What exactly should they do next?
- When, where, how, and with what constraints?

Good action patterns:
- “Do this today...”
- “Reply with...”
- “Click...”
- “Try this for 7 days...”
- “Don't do it later; do it while [specific moment].”

## Review pass

When assembling or critiquing copy, check:

- **Attention:** Does the first line create a visual, curiosity, or self-recognition?
- **Interest:** Does the copy give a believable reason to keep going?
- **Desire:** Does it connect to a real want, not just a feature?
- **Action:** Is the next step concrete enough that a reader could do it immediately?
- **Specificity:** Can any vague phrase become a concrete image, number, moment, or behavior?
- **Voice:** Does it match the user's chosen style or sample?

## Quick start script

Start with: “Let's build this with AIDA, one step at a time. First: what are we writing — post, email, ad, landing page, sales page, or something else — and who is it for?”
