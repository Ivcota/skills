# UnderdogSales Cold Outreach

Live cold outreach works by giving the prospect safety of no: structure questions so a prospect can comfortably answer “no,” because in this method no often functions as consent or forward movement (“no means yes”). Then help them take problem ownership, drill into example-impact-root cause, and close only after enough problem energy exists.

Source: user-provided UnderdogSales framework notes captured in `sources.md`. Use exact templates from [`references/templates.md`](references/templates.md) when the user wants wording.

## Run sequence

1. **Choose the branch.** Classify the request as script/call plan, diagnosis, teaching, roleplay, objection handling, gatekeeper, or follow-up. Completion: the response format matches the branch instead of giving generic sales advice.
2. **Get the outreach basics.** Identify ICP/title, company/context, offer, top 2-3 pains with consequences, channel, and desired next step. Completion: if any missing piece blocks a useful script, ask for it; otherwise state assumptions.
3. **Open with safety of no.** Use candid unfamiliarity, relevance, and a no-oriented low-pressure question. Completion: the opener makes it easy for the prospect to answer “no” without feeling trapped, and treats that no as permission to continue when appropriate.
4. **Pitch problems, not the product.** Name 2-3 pains other similar prospects mention, each with a consequence, then negative-frame the opposite. Completion: the prospect is invited to pick a problem or say they have none.
5. **Create problem ownership.** Drill the chosen problem through `example -> impact -> root cause -> summary`. Completion: before any close, the prospect has given at least one concrete example and the agent has summarized it back.
6. **Handle prospect questions with the qualifying framework.** Treat prospect questions as chances to qualify. In most cases, respond with a qualifying question instead of answering directly. If a direct answer is necessary for basic clarity or trust, keep it to one sentence, then qualify with Clarify, Options, or The Why. Completion: the response turns the prospect's question into problem relevance, meaning, or priority instead of over-explaining the offer.
7. **Handle friction by returning to the problem.** For objections, acknowledge, ask permission to ask a question, then challenge the objection and redirect to the owned problem. Completion: the response does not debate the objection in isolation.
8. **Use OLQ for dead air or exits.** If the conversation stalls, ask permission for one last question, then revive with an improvement, change, closed-mind, or objection-tailored question. Completion: the OLQ seeks truth, not a forced yes.
9. **Close at the emotional peak.** Close only when the prospect is agitated, detailed, or self-persuading about the problem. Completion: the close asks for a low-pressure sit-down/exploration tied to their stated problem.
10. **Post-close against flake risk.** Give one more objection, reconfirm the problem, confirm the invite, send an agenda separately, and arrange a pre-meeting confirmation call/text. Completion: the next step has time, agenda, and confirmation plan.

## Branches

### Script or call plan

Return: opener, problem pitch, negative frame, drill questions, objection redirects, OLQ options, close, post-close confirmation. Completion: every line is speakable out loud.

### Diagnosis

Score the conversation against: safety of no, problem pitch, negative frame, problem ownership, example, impact, root cause, summary, close timing, post-close. Completion: name the weakest missing link and rewrite that section.

### Teaching

Explain the method as a live conversation arc, then show a tiny example. Completion: the user can see what to do next in their own outreach.

### Roleplay

Play the prospect or seller as requested. Keep replies realistic, resistant, and problem-centered. Completion: after roleplay, give a short debrief on what worked, what broke, and the next drill.

### Gatekeeper

Use either **power through** or **get help** from [`references/gatekeepers.md`](references/gatekeepers.md). Completion: the script asks for the prospect without over-explaining to the gatekeeper.

## Core patterns

- **Safety of no / no-oriented question:** Ask so “no” is the safe forward answer: “Would you be mad if I asked you something?” / “Would it be a bad idea to have a sit down?” / “Is there any reason to not put some time aside?”
- **Negative frame:** “Perhaps you don't have any of these issues and you're in {ridiculously positive opposite}.”
- **Clarify:** “I'm confused. When you say {x}, what does that mean?”
- **Options:** “When you say {x}, do you mean A, B, or C?”
- **The Why:** “I'm guessing {x} is important to you for a reason?”
- **Disqualify:** “Even if we could fix that, it wouldn't be a priority, right?”
- **OLQ:** “You okay if I ask one last question before I go?”
- **Final challenge:** “Do you mind if I ask a tough question? Have you accepted this is the way things are?”

## Guardrails

- Do not pitch features before the prospect owns a problem.
- Do not default to answering prospect questions directly; use qualifying questions first whenever possible, and only add a brief answer when clarity or trust requires it.
- Do not close before the prospect gives a concrete example.
- Do not chase yes; design no-oriented questions where a safe “no” creates consent, correction, or forward movement.
- Do not treat objections as separate debates; redirect them to the problem.
- Do not make cold email or DM the default; this skill is for live conversations.
