# Pre-flight Checklist — Breakthrough Advertising

A worksheet you fill in before the first word of copy.

> Schwartz: "These five or ten words will make up about 90% of the value of your ad. If you are right, they may start a new industry. If you are wrong, nothing you write after them will save your ad." (p. 67)

The full process "may take days, or weeks, or months to complete" (p. 66) — most of the work happens before any copy is written.

---

## Phase 1 — Diagnose the Market

### Mass Desire inventory (Ch. 1, pp. 5–8)

List every desire your product touches:

- [ ] Desire 1: ________________
- [ ] Desire 2: ________________
- [ ] Desire 3: ________________
- [ ] Desire 4: ________________

For each, score on three dimensions (1–10):

| Desire | Urgency | Staying power | Scope | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 |  |  |  |  |
| 2 |  |  |  |  |
| 3 |  |  |  |  |
| 4 |  |  |  |  |

Pick the highest-scoring one. **"Only one can predominate." (p. 8)**

> "Your choice among these alternate desires is the most important step you will take in writing your ad. If it is wrong, nothing else that you do in the ad will matter." (p. 8)

### Permanent forces vs Forces of change (p. 6)

- [ ] Is this desire a **Mass Instinct** (always there — virility, attractiveness, health, mass technological problems)? → your job is to differentiate, not create.
- [ ] Is this a **trend** (rising / fulfilled / reversing)? → read the wave; "be there first."

### Physical product vs Functional product (pp. 9–12)

Write down what your product *does*, not what it *is*:

- [ ] Functional benefit 1: ________________
- [ ] Functional benefit 2: ________________
- [ ] Functional benefit 3: ________________

> "No physical part of your product can ever become a headline." (p. 9)

- [ ] Confirm: no physical fact appears in the headline.
- [ ] Physical facts saved for body — to document price, quality, durability, mental picture, believability.

### Three components of the prospect's mind (Ch. 6, pp. 71–75)

- [ ] **Desires** identified — the physical/material/sensual wants. (p. 73)
- [ ] **Identifications** identified — the role / status / self-image the prospect wants to display. (p. 73)
- [ ] **Beliefs** identified — the filter your product-information must pass through. (p. 74)

## Phase 2 — Locate the Prospect

### State of Awareness (Ch. 2, pp. 14–35)

Which rung is the prospect on?

- [ ] **1. Most Aware** — knows you, wants you, hasn't bought. → Product name + price. (p. 16)
- [ ] **2. Knows the product, doesn't yet want it.** → Display product + state superiority. (p. 16)
- [ ] **3. How to Introduce New Products** — knows the desire, doesn't know your product exists. → Crystallize the amorphous desire in one statement. (p. 19)
- [ ] **4. How to Introduce Products That Solve Needs** — knows the problem, not the connection to your product. → Name the need / dramatize / present product as inevitable solution. (p. 21)
- [ ] **5. Completely Unaware** — doesn't know the desire, can't verbalize it, won't admit it. → Identification headline. (p. 23)

→ Write the headline at THIS rung. Not above. Not below.

> "Each of these stages is separated from the others by a psychological wall." (pp. 23–24)

### State of Sophistication (Ch. 3, pp. 37–50)

Audit competing ads. Which stage is the market on?

- [ ] **1. First in the market.** Be simple. Be direct. Name need or claim — nothing more. (p. 39)
- [ ] **2. Claim still working.** Enlarge it — bigger numbers, bolder promises. (p. 39)
- [ ] **3. Claims exhausted — introduce a new mechanism.** Headline = mechanism; HOW it works. (p. 41)
- [ ] **4. Mechanism known — elaborate it.** Easier, quicker, surer; remove old limitations. (p. 44)
- [ ] **5. Field said to be exhausted — identification only.** (p. 45)

---

## Phase 3 — Write the Headline (Ch. 4)

5–10 words. Check:

- [ ] Does it stop the prospect? (p. 14)
- [ ] Does it compel reading sentence 2? (p. 14)
- [ ] Does it match the awareness rung?
- [ ] Does it match the sophistication stage?
- [ ] Could you swap another brand-name in with no change? If yes — rewrite. (p. 215)
- [ ] Is it an Echo Ad (word-substitute on someone else's success)? If yes — rewrite. (p. 60)

> "Your headline does not need to sell at all… Your headline has only one job — to stop your prospect and compel him to read the second sentence of your ad." (pp. 14–15)

---

## Phase 4 — Body Copy: The Three Mechanisms

### Intensification — sharpen desire (Ch. 7, pp. 77–106)

Pick the relevant Ways. Aim 3–6 per ad:

- [ ] First presentation (sensory description) (p. 80)
- [ ] Put claims in action (motion verbs) (p. 83)
- [ ] Bring in the reader (second-person hypothetical) (p. 85)
- [ ] Show how to test the claims (p. 86)
- [ ] Stretch benefits in time (chronological gates) (p. 87)
- [ ] Bring in an audience (testimonial / case-history stack) (p. 89)
- [ ] Show experts approving (p. 90)
- [ ] Compare / contrast / prove superiority (p. 91)
- [ ] Picture the black side too (heaven-or-hell pivot) (p. 92)
- [ ] Show how easy (ease-refrain) (p. 94)
- [ ] Use metaphor / analogy / personification (p. 94)
- [ ] Summarize (catalog — rifle vs shotgun) (p. 95)
- [ ] Put guarantee to work (numbered, refundable) (p. 99)

> Cardinal rule: "You cannot repeat, but you can reinforce." (p. 79)

### Identification — sell who he becomes (Ch. 8, pp. 109–127)

- [ ] Primary image already on the product: ________________
- [ ] Character role you'll grow into it: ________________ (subconscious, never tested — virility, taste, etc.; p. 112)
- [ ] Achievement role you'll signal: ________________ (status / class / position; p. 113)
- [ ] Believability bridge from the prospect's side: ________________ (p. 125)

> "If you demand that your prospect jump across a believability-chasm, your ad will fail." (p. 125)

### Gradualization — build belief (Ch. 9, pp. 131–151)

- [ ] Inclusion Question (Yes-tag) at the top (p. 145)
- [ ] Detailed Identification — universal-symptom catalog (p. 145)
- [ ] Yes-cascade of universal symptoms
- [ ] Logical bridges ("Therefore…", "Here's why…", "And, most important of all…") (p. 147)
- [ ] Smuggle the new claim with paragraph parallelism (p. 142)
- [ ] Syllogistic close (major premise → minor → inevitable conclusion) (p. 149)

> "One fully-believed promise has ten times the sales power of ten partially-believed promises." (p. 144)

---

## Phase 5 — Handle Objections + Competition

### Redefinition (Ch. 10, pp. 153–164)

- [ ] **Too hard?** Simplify — redefine the loaded word; shrink the field with concrete numbers. (p. 155)
- [ ] **Not important?** Escalate — broaden role; cost-to-stake ratio. (p. 160)
- [ ] **Too costly?** Reduce price — switch comparison standard upward; "you can have" not "you can buy." (pp. 162–164)

### Mechanization (Ch. 11, pp. 165–172)

- [ ] **Name** the mechanism (familiar markets) (p. 167)
- [ ] **Describe** the mechanism (Promise → Reason-Why two-step) (p. 168)
- [ ] **Feature** the mechanism (mechanism = headline; sophisticated markets) (p. 170)
- [ ] Every claim has a mechanism
- [ ] Every price cut has a mechanism (p. 172)

### Concentration (Ch. 12, pp. 175–184) — only if needed

- [ ] **Bad/Good interleave** (clean one-for-one points) (p. 178)
- [ ] **Time-sequence** (Phase 1: what happens now; Phase 2: what happens when you switch) (p. 181)
- [ ] Word-image contrast pairs ("spark" vs "flame")
- [ ] Authority capstone after attack sequence
- [ ] **Never attack without supplying the solution.** (p. 177)

### Camouflage (Ch. 13, pp. 185–194)

- [ ] Adopt the host medium's format (p. 186)
- [ ] Adopt its phraseology (date-line, by-line, idiom) (p. 190)
- [ ] Consider **Understatement** (Volkswagen — few adjectives, self-kidding) to escape "advertising language" (p. 192)
- [ ] Consider **Deadly Sincerity** (lean over backward to flag flaws first) (p. 193)

---

## Phase 6 — Final Touches (Ch. 14, pp. 196–225)

- [ ] **Verification** — proof placed where the reader is *begging* for it; not in a "Here's Proof" box (p. 196)
- [ ] **Reinforcement** — claims paired so 1+1=10 (Avis: "We try harder" + "We're only second") (p. 200)
- [ ] **Interweaving** — every sentence carries promise + image + logic + emotion (Walter S. Campbell rule) (p. 203)
- [ ] **Sensitivity** — anticipate "I've read enough about this. Give me this instead." and shift direction (p. 205)
- [ ] **Momentum** — momentum-phrases AND incomplete-statement teasers (p. 215)
- [ ] **Mood** — mood-words (image-sharpeners + emotion-definers) invisible to the reader (p. 222)

### Mood audit (pp. 223–224)

- [ ] Read the ad aloud — does it scan with rhythm close to blank verse?
- [ ] Are emotion-definers ("blinded… explosion… spellbound") subtle enough to stay invisible?
- [ ] If *everybody* would notice the emotion-words → rewrite, fast.
- [ ] Tone matched to audience class (downshift as well as upshift)?

---

## Phase 7 — Architecture Diagnostic (p. 215)

After writing, before submitting:

- [ ] **Could another brand-name swap into this with no change?** If yes — rewrite. (p. 215)
- [ ] What kind of ad is this? Straight promise / interweaved / authority-led / promise-and-proof reinforcement?
- [ ] Have you left anything out? Could mechanism, believability, proof section, use photo, or fuller description sharpen impact? (p. 215)
- [ ] Is documentation buried in a "Here's Proof" box instead of placed at peak demand? (p. 199)
- [ ] If stuck: **throw away the headline, restart 180° from where you began.** (p. 215)

> "A new structure is a new ad. It brings out new ideas. Gives your promises new sharpness, new flavor, new believability and even begins to create whole new sections that you never dreamed were there in the product before." (p. 215)
