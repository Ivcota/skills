# Case Studies Reference — Breakthrough Advertising

> Source: Eugene M. Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (Boardroom 2004 reissue of 1966 original).
> All citations are to BOOK page numbers (printed in running headers), not PDF page numbers.
> Organized by industry for cross-chapter lookup. Each entry lists primary techniques demonstrated and key quotes.

---

## Automotive / Cars

### Chrysler — Fighting Mass Desire (pp. 3–4)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 1 (Mass Desire)
- **What happened:** Chrysler's 1948 short/squat car ran against "longer, lower, wider" demand with a multimillion-dollar campaign from a top-tier agency. Result: "catastrophic." (pp. 3–4)
- **Techniques violated:** Channeling vs. fighting mass desire.
- **Key quote:** "A multimillion-dollar campaign was prepared by one of the most creative agencies in America. But the results — against the tide of mass desire — were catastrophic." (pp. 3–4)

### Ford — Horsepower Wave vs. Safety (p. 4)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 1 (Mass Desire)
- **What happened:** 1954: Twin-H Hudson Hornet, twin-exhaust Cadillac, Chrysler 300 all rode the new horsepower desire and earned millions. Ford bet on safety, "hit a wall of disinterest," reversed course the next year and produced the highest-horsepower engines in their history. (p. 4)
- **Techniques:** Forces of Change — catching the rising tide; when to shift appeal.
- **Key quote:** Ford's safety campaign "hit a wall of disinterest." (p. 4)

### The Edsel (p. 4)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 1 (Mass Desire)
- **What happened:** A well-built car, massive advertising budget, killed by fighting the switch to compact cars.
- **Key quote:** "Here was a good car, backed by a deluge of fine advertising, that died trying to fight the overwhelming switch in demand to a cheap, simple, inexpensive-to-run compact car." (p. 4)

### Cadillac — One Dominant Personality Trait (pp. 63–64, 104)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 5 (Creative Planning), Ch. 7 (Intensification)
- **What happened:** Cadillac's defining trait is always quality — never just performance, prestige, or appearance alone. "Quality" is featured, and all other traits are interlaced with it. The dominant image lives in photography, typography, copy style, fashion accessories, and pen-and-ink drawings across every insertion.
- **Techniques:** Identification (product personality), Intensification (Central Campaign Idea with novelty inside continuity).
- **Key quote:** "One of these traits will always be the most effective in summarizing and expressing this personality. In the case of Cadillac, it has always been, and will always be, quality." (pp. 63–64)

### Chrysler 300 — Stage 2 Enlargement in Cars (p. 40)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 3 (Sophistication)
- **What happened:** Chrysler 300 was "pegged just at the limit of believability" as an example of Stage 2 enlargement — pushing the horsepower claim to the outer edge without breaking into disbelief.
- **Techniques:** Stage 2 Sophistication, claim enlargement.

### "Man-On-His-Way-Up" — Cadillac Progression (p. 114)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 8 (Identification)
- **What happened:** Illustrates achievement roles — Ford → Buick → Cadillac as visible status markers at successive career stages.
- **Key quote:** "'Man-On-His-Way-Up'… trades in his Ford for a Buick, and, when he becomes an 'Executive,' trades in his Buick for a Cadillac." (p. 114)

### Hypothetical Piston Ring Ad — Building Identification from a Bleak Product (pp. 121–123)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 8 (Identification)
- **What happened:** Schwartz constructs a hypothetical ad starting from the bleakest possible primary image (mechanical, unseen, replacement-only) and layers virility, precision, and achievement roles by casting the product as high-performance racing equipment.
- **Techniques:** Identification — building from primary image, visual implication over verbal naming.
- **Key quote:** "The mechanic is installing these new rings in this car as high-performance equipment, exactly as he would install a supercharger to increase its horsepower." (p. 123)

## Cigarettes / Tobacco

### Cigarettes — Full Five-Stage Walk-Through (pp. 46–49)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 3 (Sophistication)
- **What happened:** Schwartz calls cigarettes "the classic example of an industry which encountered all five Stages of Sophistication — and overcame them." (p. 46)
- **Stage 1 (taste/pleasure):** "I'D WALK A MILE FOR A CAMEL!"; "CHESTERFIELD — THEY SATISFY!" (p. 46)
- **Stage 2 (broader comparison):** "LIGHT UP A LUCKY, AND YOU WON'T MISS THE SWEETS THAT MAKE YOU FAT!" (p. 46)
- **Stage 3 (new mechanism):** "LUCKIES — THEY'RE TOASTED!"; "PALL MALL'S GREATER LENGTH FILTERS THE SMOKE FURTHER!"; "CAMELS — PROTECT YOUR T-ZONE!" (pp. 46–47)
- **Stage 4 (mechanism elaboration):** "PHILIP MORRIS — ALL THE HARSHNESS BAKED OUT!"; "NINE OUT OF TEN DOCTORS PREFER LUCKIES!" (p. 47)
- **Stage 5 (identification):** Government banned health claims; Motivation Research surfaced virility as the key identification; Marlboro Virile Men ran without mechanisms, claims, or even conventional headlines (p. 47)
- **Key quote:** "And so it goes. In industry after industry. The same life cycle for each market. The same deadly challenges. The same willingness to adapt rather than perish." (p. 50)

### Filter Cigarettes — Re-Tracing All Five Stages in Fifty Years (pp. 48–49)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 3 (Sophistication)
- **Stage 1:** "KENT'S MICRONITE FILTER TRAPS TARS BEFORE THEY REACH YOUR LIPS!"
- **Stage 2:** "20,000 FILTER TRAPS IN VICEROY!"
- **Stage 3:** "PARLIAMENT — THE MOST IMPORTANT ¼ INCH IN SMOKING TODAY — NO FILTER FEEDBACK!" [sic?]
- **Stage 4:** "TAREYTON — DUAL FILTER FOR DOUBLE THE PLEASURE!"
- **Stage 5:** Full-cycle return to flavor — "WINSTON TASTES GOOD LIKE A CIGARETTE SHOULD!"; "L & M HAS FOUND THE SECRET THAT UNLOCKS THE FLAVOR!" (pp. 48–49)

### Marlboro Tattoo Ad — Projecting a Hidden Desire (pp. 29–30, 118, 125)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 2 (Awareness), Ch. 3 (Sophistication), Ch. 8 (Identification)
- **What happened:** Research showed smoking gives men a feeling of virility, but any verbal expression of this would be "instantly rejected as absurd and offensive." Solution: virile men (cowboys, racing car drivers, sky divers) plus a tattoo — "one of the most primitive symbols of virility known to history." No claims, no mechanisms, barely a headline. (pp. 29–30)
- **Three symbolizations of a single dominant emotion:** (1) virile men, (2) in virile occupations, (3) with a tattoo. Later layers: tuxedo for success, boats/planes/horseback for adventure, a young girl for romance. (p. 121)
- **Techniques:** Stage 5 identification (Ch. 3), fifth-stage awareness hidden-desire tactic (Ch. 2), visual implication over verbal naming.
- **Key quote:** "A single dominant emotion — virility — symbolized three ways. The impact of repetition reinforced through variation." (p. 118)

### Chesterfield "Blow Some My Way" (1926) — Women's Market (pp. 48, 119–120)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 3 (Sophistication), Ch. 8 (Identification)
- **What happened:** Cigarettes were a man's product with a "man-smoking" primary image that couldn't be erased. The copy subdued the man's figure, darkened him into the background, redirected attention to the woman beside him. The arrangement of hands and face "direct the attention of the viewer past the man himself and into the focal point of the picture, the woman." (p. 120) A vast new market opened with four words.
- **Techniques:** Identification — softening an unremovable primary image, using it as a bridge into a broader appeal; Stage 5 identification.
- **Key quote:** "Nothing more needed to be said. A vast new market — opened up with four words." (p. 48)

### Postum / "WHY MEN CRACK…" — Fifth Stage Revival (pp. 27–28, 45)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 2 (Awareness), Ch. 3 (Sophistication)
- **What happened:** Postum had exhausted health/pep/taste mechanisms. The hidden-fear tactic surfaced the dread of burning out and cracking under strain — making Postum the escape, not just the beverage. The brand revived.
- **Techniques:** Exploiting a hidden fear (fifth-stage awareness), Stage 5 sophistication identification.
- **Key quotes:** "WHY MEN CRACK…" / "You know them. Strong men, vigorous men, robust men — men who have never had a sick day in their lives. They drive. They drive. They drive themselves to the limit. They lash themselves over the limit with stimulants. They crack. Often, they crash." / "Borrowed Energy Must Be Repaid!" (p. 28)

## Health / Weight Loss / Reducing

### Reducing Field — Full Five-Stage Demonstration (pp. 39–44)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 3 (Sophistication) — used as the running example through all stages.
- **Stage 1:** "NOW! LOSE UGLY FAT!" (p. 39)
- **Stage 2:** "LOSE UP TO 47 POUNDS IN 4 WEEKS — OR RECEIVE $40 BACK!"; "I AM 61 POUNDS LIGHTER… NEVER A HUNGRY MINUTE." (p. 39)
- **Stage 3 (mechanism in headline):** "FLOATS FAT RIGHT OUT OF YOUR BODY!"; "FIRST WONDER DRUG FOR REDUCING!" — then the original claim is restated in lead/body: "Released for the first time! The amazing scientific discovery that melts up to 37 POUNDS off men and women — without starvation diets, without a single hungry moment — without even giving up the foods you love!" (pp. 42–43)
- **Stage 4:** "FIRST NO-DIET REDUCING WONDER DRUG!" (p. 44)

### Lecithin Reducing Pill — Concentration (Time-Sequence Structure) (pp. 181–183)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 12 (Concentration)
- **What happened:** Market fully occupied by passive hunger-appeasing products. Schwartz demonstrates the time-sequence concentration structure: first, exhaustively define all existing options as "passive — depending strictly on your own will power" — then pivot to Lecithin as the active fat-burning agent discovered by a Nobel prize winner.
- **Techniques:** Concentration, Redefinition (passive vs. active), Mechanization, cause-and-effect logic, case-history return to promise.
- **Key negative phase quote:** "So what happened? If you were overweight, you struggled to do the job of reducing BY YOURSELF!… And finally, if you were lucky, you carved off 5, 10, or even 12 precious pounds. And then your will power snapped!… And the fat flowed back — heavier and uglier and more deadly than ever before!" (p. 182)
- **Hero phase:** "These doctors had the answer in a tiny grey pill… LECITHIN — brand-new — whose amazing fat-dissolving properties had been discovered by a Nobel prize winner — the co-discoverer of insulin… they never experienced a single hungry moment… the ugly excess fat around their bodies melted away! While they were eating three delicious meals a day, they were shedding as much as 5 pounds a week." (p. 183)

### Reducing-Splinter Market (Health vs. Appearance) (p. 65)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 5 (Creative Planning)
- **What happened:** A small advertiser avoids the crowded appearance-motivation segment and targets women who *must* lose weight for health reasons — different mechanism (safety, permanence), different media (health/geriatric magazines), different distribution (doctors rather than drug stores).
- **Techniques:** Splinter market selection, market sociology vs. geography.
- **Key quote:** "This deliberate focussing of the appeal would alter every aspect of the campaign, from the headline, through identification copy, through mechanism and substantiation — right down to the selection of media and channels of distribution." (p. 65)

### Memory Course ("Give Me One Evening") — Test + Catalog (pp. 87, 97–98)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification)
- **What happened:** The course offers readers a self-administered test — read 8 pages, then write 20 facts you could never memorize before, and amaze friends and family the next morning. Later the ad uses a vertical catalog of "over 50 MEMORY INTENSIFIERS" to stack application after application on the same dominant desire.
- **Techniques:** Show Him How to Test (Way 4), Vertical Catalog (Way 12), Bring In an Audience.

## Garden / Horticulture

### Rose Plant Ad — First Presentation + Expert Approval (pp. 80–81, 90–91)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification)
- **What happened:** Opens with quantified bloom counts (4,076 roses from a single plant across four months). The expert-approval passage stages a college horticulturist test; a famous rose expert exclaims, "Why, it's like having a complete rose garden of 20 plants — all on a single bush!" and "If we ever released these flowering figures to the public, they'd never believe it." (pp. 90–91)
- **Techniques:** Way 1 (First Presentation), Way 7 (Show Experts Approving).
- **Stage in market:** Stage 2 enlargement example with "WHO EVER HEARD OF 17,000 BLOOMS FROM A SINGLE PLANT?" (p. 40)

### Garden Field (Floribunda Rose / Cushion Mum) — Stage 2 Enlargement (p. 40)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 3 (Sophistication)
- **What happened:** Starting headline "PICK 25–50–100 ROSES FROM THIS ONE MAGNIFICENT PLANT!" escalated to "SIX HUNDRED MUMS FROM A SINGLE BUSH!" then to "WHO EVER HEARD OF 17,000 BLOOMS FROM A SINGLE PLANT?" as competitors outbid each other.

### English Plant-Food Pellets — Stretched Benefits in Time (pp. 87–88, 100)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification)
- **What happened:** Copy walks through three sequential stages — first month burst, second-stage nutrient "within one or two short weeks," then a third ingredient releasing at full height — climaxing in "AND YOU WILL BE BLINDED BY THE EXPLOSION OF COLOR THAT GREETS YOU IN YOUR GARDEN!" The guarantee stacks: USE THEM TO CREATE SUPER-FLOWERS!… USE THEM TO CREATE SUPER-VEGETABLES!… YES! USE THEM IN YOUR ENTIRE GARDEN! — each with a money-back clause.
- **Techniques:** Way 5 (Stretch Out Benefits in Time), Way 13 (Guarantee as Climax).

## Household Products / Cleaning

### Lifebuoy / "B.O." — Concept-Judo / Redefinition (pp. 38, 154)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 3 (Sophistication — "new medium/new performance"), Ch. 10 (Redefinition)
- **What happened:** Lifebuoy had a "horrible medicinal odor" that couldn't be removed without killing its cleaning power. Redefinition flipped the liability: the prospect's *own* body odor became the enemy; the medicinal smell became the proof of odor-destroying power. Also cited as an example of "discovering a previously unnoticed performance" that made Lifebuoy "first" in a new market category.
- **Techniques:** Concept-judo (liability-into-asset), Redefinition, Stage 1 Sophistication (first in new market).
- **Key quote:** "This is the simplest, and often the most effective kind of redefinition. A simple concept-judo. A complete reversal. Turning a liability into an asset, with a single idea." (p. 154)

### Rinso (1926) — Promise → Reason Why (p. 169)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 11 (Mechanization)
- **What happened:** The classic "Who else wants a whiter wash — with no hard work?" headline followed by mechanism paragraphs explaining why: Rinso's mild granulated soap gives lasting suds, and "dirt floats off — stains go." A 1926 example that Schwartz notes would need "far more mechanism" in a 1960s market.
- **Techniques:** Stage 3 awareness (crystallizing amorphous desire), Mechanization — Describe the Mechanism.
- **Key quote:** "Today, of course, in our much more sophisticated and exploited market, she would no longer do so. Today you would need far more mechanism." (p. 170)

### Deodorant Soap That Flopped — Identification Limit (p. 124)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 8 (Identification)
- **What happened:** A deodorant-soap campaign used elite/society imagery to sell a mass market. The men and women who composed the market couldn't make the jump. "Disbelief spread to the performance claims themselves and killed the sales." (p. 124)
- **Techniques:** Identification failure — asking the market to jump across a believability-chasm.

## Personal Care / Cosmetics / Beauty

### Odorono / "Within the Curve of a Woman's Arm" (pp. 28–29)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 2 (Awareness — Stage 5 fifth-stage tactic)
- **What happened:** A frank discussion of women's underarm odor would offend both publishers and prospects if stated directly. The headline opened with a poetic image; body copy moved through the acceptable to the problem. Copy opened: "A woman's arm! Poets have sung of its grace; artists have painted its beauty. It should be the daintiest, sweetest thing in the world. And yet, unfortunately, it isn't, always." (p. 29)
- **Techniques:** Leading into an unacceptable problem by starting with a universally accepted image (fifth-stage awareness tactic).

### Listerine / "Often a Bridesmaid But Never a Bride" (pp. 33–34)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 2 (Awareness — Stage 5)
- **What happened:** Dramatized the social consequence of bad breath (never getting married) so the reader self-identifies via the result (romantic failure) rather than the cause (halitosis). "Edna's case was really a pathetic one. Like every woman, her primary ambition was to marry… marriage seemed farther from her life than ever… She was often a bridesmaid but never a bride." (p. 33)
- **Techniques:** Projecting the result of a problem so people identify with the consequence, not the unflattering cause; fifth-stage awareness.

### Micrin Mouthwash — Package as Image Symbol (p. 127)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 8 (Identification)
- **What happened:** A Johnson & Johnson mouthwash whose container was shaped like surgical-glass containers found only in doctors' offices. Combined with an ice-blue liquid, "this says instantly, 'This is doctor's-liquid… This is medicine. This works.'" The container itself was the best image-illustration.
- **Techniques:** Identification from the physical product — appearance as authority signal.
- **Key quote:** "The container itself becomes the finest image-illustration that the agency could use in creating their ad." (p. 127)

### Clairol — Fixed Headline, Rotating Illustrations (p. 104)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification)
- **What happened:** "DOES SHE OR DOESN'T SHE? Hair-coloring so natural only her hairdresser knows for sure" — fixed headline with a series of glamorized everyday-life main illustrations that kept the central identification fresh.
- **Techniques:** Central Campaign Idea — novelty within continuity.

### Toni — Fixed Headline + Rotating Photos (p. 104)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification)
- **What happened:** "WHICH TWIN HAS THE TONI — and which has the $15 permanent?" was the fixed anchor. A continuously-changing stream of pretty-girl photos provided novelty around the stable comparison challenge.
- **Techniques:** Central Campaign Idea, Way 18 (Challenge the Reader).

### Helena Rubinstein Face Cream — Physical Fact Documenting Functional Claim (p. 10)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 1 (Mass Desire)
- **What happened:** Physical fact — "contains the placenta of living animals" — used to make the skin-impurity-removal claim believable via biological credibility.
- **Techniques:** Physical product as documentation for the functional claim.

## Mail-Order Education / Self-Improvement

### "They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano" — U.S. School of Music (pp. 32–33, 89)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 2 (Awareness — Stage 5), Ch. 7 (Intensification — Bring In an Audience)
- **What happened:** Opens with social humiliation ("Arthur had just played 'The Rosary.' The room rang with applause…"). The protagonist is dismissed by friends, then plays brilliantly. Wider audience than direct "learn piano" copy could reach.
- **Techniques:** Projecting an ultimate triumph the prospect identifies with (fifth-stage tactic); Bring In an Audience — average-person narration/case history.
- **Key quote:** Cited as "the canonical narration example" for Bring-In-an-Audience. (p. 89)

### "The University of the Night" — International Correspondence Schools (pp. 26–27)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 2 (Awareness — Stage 5)
- **What happened:** Giving Words to a Hidden Dream. Expanded the market for home correspondence courses beyond "Earn more money / Gain more skill" by naming the dream (self-education against the odds) rather than the product.
- **Techniques:** Giving words to a hidden dream (fifth-stage tactic).
- **Key quote:** "The young Lincoln, poring over borrowed school-books far into the night… here is an example which has inspired the man who strives against the odds of circumstances to make his place in the world." (p. 27)

### Television Repair Manual (1951) — Full Gradualization Anatomy (pp. 135–141)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 9 (Gradualization), Ch. 10 (Redefinition), Ch. 13 (Camouflage — Concentration)
- **What happened:** The obvious headline "Save up to $100 a Year on Your TV Repairs!" failed — too big a believability leap. The breakthrough: "WHY HAVEN'T TV OWNERS BEEN TOLD THESE FACTS?" — no claim, only an implication of withheld facts, then a chain of yes-generating questions, then the 90% unnecessary-breakdowns revelation, then the syllogistic close. Three steps of Redefinition simultaneously: "repairs" → "adjustments," "breakdowns" → "warning signals," and the 5% residual is the only category that needs a serviceman.
- **Techniques:** Gradualization (Inclusion Question, Yes-train, chain of acceptances, syllogistic close), Redefinition (Simplification), fifth-stage awareness (common resentment against TV service contracts).
- **Key quote:** "It's as simple as that. You pay a repairman — not for his work — but for his knowledge. If you had that knowledge yourself — then you would not have to pay him at all." (p. 143)

### "Here's an Extra $50, Grace" — I.C.S. (p. 34)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 2 (Awareness — Stage 5)
- **What happened:** Avoided showing the effort of study; directed mind away from work to reward — overheard-dialog format ("I've been promoted with an increase of $50 a month… Just a little reward for urging me to study at home…").
- **Techniques:** Projecting the result of an accomplishment to attract people frightened by the implied work.

### Sherwin Cody English Course — Multiple Techniques (pp. 94–95, 159–160)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification — Way 11 Metaphor), Ch. 10 (Redefinition — Simplification)
- **Metaphor / personification:** Course manual personified as a silent mentor whispering at your elbow — "That is wrong, it should be thus and so" — branded as a "100% Self-Correcting Device." (pp. 94–95)
- **Redefinition:** Simplified the field using hard numbers: 69 words make up more than half of all speech and letter writing; only one dozen fundamental punctuation principles; twenty-five typical errors are nine-tenths of all everyday grammar mistakes. "Thus the complicated becomes simple — the hard becomes easy." (pp. 159–160)
- **Techniques:** Way 11 (Metaphor/Analogy), Redefinition — Simplification, Language of Logic.

### "Word Power Machine" — English Course Escalation (pp. 160–161)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 10 (Redefinition — Escalation)
- **What happened:** A second-generation English-course ad. "Good English must cease to be an end in itself." Dominant benefit shifts to vocabulary as a persuasion tool: "the blazing new self-confidence you need to make anybody like you — to win people over irresistibly to your point of view." (p. 161)
- **Techniques:** Escalation — redefining the role of the product to a more desirable benefit area.

### *The Art of Selfishness* — Inclusion Question Opener (p. 145)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 9 (Gradualization)
- **What happened:** Nine-question Inclusion-Question stack, each designed so 95%+ of readers answer Yes and place themselves inside the product's target group. Sets up the Yes-train before any claim is made.
- **Techniques:** Inclusion Question (Gradualization opening device).

### "How to Make Anybody Like You!" (Parker) — Four-Element Ad Architecture (p. 206)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 14 (Final Touches — Sensitivity / Sample Ads)
- **Architecture pattern:** Promise → Paradox-Redefinition ("Can popularity be purchased?") → Documentation block (General Motors, DuPont, Sears Roebuck, Ford, Coca-Cola…) → Detailed Promise → Echo of Documentation + Price Comparison → Guarantee.
- **Also:** Same copy re-set in *Wall Street Journal* format ran 19× monthly with no drop-off. (p. 190)
- **Techniques:** Redefinition of product role, Camouflage (adopting WSJ format and phraseology), Verification (documentation placement at peak emotional readiness).

## Automotive Aftermarket

### SA-FIRE Injectors — Price Reduction via Redefinition + Test (pp. 86, 162–164, 178–180)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification), Ch. 10 (Redefinition), Ch. 12 (Concentration)
- **Price reduction:** $1.49 plug (1.5× field standard) made to feel cheap through 10 stacked bargain-phrases in two paragraphs: "practically made by hand… would have to sell for as high as $5 each… 30 or 40 dollars… get the price down so low… these injectors would pay for themselves 12 times in one year of driving… astonishing proposition… prepared to do… You can have… only $1.49 each." (pp. 163–164)
- **Concentration:** "Bad — Good; Bad — Good" interleave contrasting ordinary spark plug's air-gap and carbon buildup against fire injector's surface-firing and no-electrode design. Authority capstone: U.S. Air Force pays premium prices for surface-supported injectors in their aircraft. (pp. 178–180)
- **Test demonstration (Way 4):** Readers walk through before/after measurement of crawl-speed, RPM, and low-gear speed. (p. 86)
- **Key quote on price:** "Most of it — perhaps all of it — is never consciously noticed by the prospect. He simply realizes that he feels that a bargain is being offered to him." (p. 163)

### Power Flash Spark Plugs — Picture the Black Side + Numbered Guarantee (pp. 92–93, 99–100)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification — Ways 9 and 13)
- **Black side:** "What you'll see on the bottom of that plug is FILTH!… CORRODED — PITTED — SCARRED — AND WORN!" followed by pivot to "AT LAST! A PLUG SO SMART THAT IT THINKS!" (pp. 92–93)
- **Guarantee as climax:** Three numbered performance-bound clauses, each independently refundable, plus a two-year free-replacement offer. "Guaranteed For Two Full Years!" (pp. 99–100)
- **Syllogistic chain:** Larger spark → more powerful explosion → more power from gasoline; used in Gradualization chapter as the textbook proof sequence. (p. 149)

### Power Booster — Bring In the Reader (pp. 85–86)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification — Way 3)
- **What happened:** Second-person future-perfect script — "Picture this to yourself! Next weekend you go down to your car — the same tired car that you've been driving for years…" — ends with the reader outpacing another car at a stoplight and seeing "the startled look of amazement on the other driver's face" in the rear-view mirror.
- **Techniques:** Way 3 (Bring In the Reader).

### Fuel Reclaimer — Compare/Contrast (pp. 91–92)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification — Way 8)
- **What happened:** Before-after parallel lists — "polluted oil, polluted engine, polluted air" vs. "young-car power, young-car economy, young-car resistance to repair bills." (p. 92)
- **Techniques:** Way 8 (Compare, Contrast, Prove Superiority).

### Engine Additive "30 Seconds Work" — Ease Refrain (p. 94)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification — Way 10)
- **What happened:** "With just 30 seconds work" repeated as a refrain at the head of each benefit paragraph, so ease itself intensifies every claim.
- **Techniques:** Way 10 (Show How Easy), ease-refrain.

## Books / Publications

### Dollar Book Club Radio Spot — Price-Contrast Triplet (p. 91)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification — Way 8)
- **What happened:** "While your friends were paying $3.95 for their copies of Frank Yerby's run-away best seller, The Golden Hawk, Dollar Book Club members were getting the same exact book for only 99¢." (p. 91)
- **Techniques:** Compare/Contrast, price anchoring via side-by-side.

### Robert Collier Madras Shirt Letter — Mechanism for Bargain (p. 172)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 11 (Mechanization)
- **What happened:** Price cut justified by a chained mechanism: unseasonable weather → mill overstocked → S.O.S. calls → mill needed money → surplus yardage offered below any prior price. Body leads with the mechanism for six paragraphs before touching bargain claims — gamble paid off in 10× believability.
- **Key quote:** "Here the copy writer not only emphasizes bargain over and over again, but brings in quality as a counter-desire time after time. He thus uses a mechanism within a mechanism." (p. 172)

## Food & Beverage

- **Chapters:** Ch. 1 (Mass Desire), Ch. 2 (Awareness — Stage 2)

### Coca-Cola — "Thirst Knows No Season" (p. 18)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 2 (Awareness — Stage 2)
- **What happened:** Stage 2 task (c) — extend image of where/when the product satisfies the desire.
- **Pattern:** `[Desire] knows no [limiting condition] — [Brand].`

### Dole — "Tastes Like You Just Picked It" (p. 18)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 2 (Awareness — Stage 2)
- **What happened:** Stage 2 task (a, sensory sharpening) — sharpen the sensory image.

### Ovaltine — Repositioning (p. 38)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 3 (Sophistication)
- **What happened:** Repositioned from an insomnia aid to a body-builder for skinny children in the Twenties — example of "applying an existing product to a new market" and being first in that new market without a product change.
- **Techniques:** Stage 1 Sophistication via market repositioning.

## National Brand Campaigns

### Colgate Gardol — Rule and Example (p. 103)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification — Campaign-level)
- **What happened:** Central verbal rule stated and ritualistically repeated — "Gardol — the Invisible Shield that Protects Your Teeth" — with only the 10-second TV demo varying each week (a baseball thrown at the announcer, bouncing off the shield). The closing line recited verbatim every time.
- **Techniques:** Central Campaign Idea — Rule-and-Example structure, novelty within verbal continuity.

### Volkswagen — Product as Focal Point / Creative Gamble (pp. 105–106, 192–193)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification — Campaign-level), Ch. 13 (Camouflage — Understatement)
- **What happened:** Absolute reduction of ad to bare background + close-up product. No identification background, no trademark in headline, no performance claim stated outright. Headlines work by contradiction or unusual request that body copy must resolve:
  - "THINK SMALL" — compactness as asset
  - "Lemon" — one microscopic flaw disqualifying a unit from shipping
  - "Our new model" — engineering changes you can't see
  - "Why you should open the window before you close the door of a Volkswagen" — airtight cabin
  - "Our number one salesman" — the serviceman
- **Understatement copy:** Short sentences, barely any adjectives, sentences that fall rather than rise in tone. "You never run out of air. / You also won't have any worries about draining or flushing the radiator in spring. There is no radiator. / Or hoses. Or water pump. Or rust." (pp. 192–193)
- **Techniques:** Creative Gamble, Understatement (Camouflage), Central Campaign Idea — product as the only focal point.
- **Key quote:** "If he fails in his gamble, he has wasted his page. If he succeeds, he has doubled and tripled the effectiveness of his ad." (p. 106)

### Avis "We Try Harder" — Reinforcement (p. 200)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 14 (Final Touches — Reinforcement)
- **What happened:** Two emotional images that together produce far more impact than either alone: "We're only second" (honest limitation) + "Therefore we try harder" (the logical consequence). "One plus one can often equal ten." (p. 200)
- **Techniques:** Reinforcement — juxtaposition creating exponential emotional impact; Deadly Sincerity (admitting limitation as a believability amplifier).

### Pan American Plastic Model Jet — Sensory-Stack Description (pp. 83–84)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 7 (Intensification — Way 2)
- **What happened:** Static description packed with specifics ("tail section alone stands six full inches from the table," "JET SOUND SIMULATOR") then action — "Pick this magnificent plastic model up from the table, and hand it to your child… Before your astonished eyes, you will see one of the most thrilling sights of your entire life!" (pp. 83–84)
- **Techniques:** Way 1 (First Presentation via sensory specifics), Way 2 (Put the Claims in Action).

## Media Format

### "How to Make Anybody Like You!" — Wall Street Journal Adaptation (pp. 187–190)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 13 (Camouflage — Adopting Format)
- **What happened:** House-set version was "mildly successful." Re-set feature-for-feature in *Wall Street Journal* style (upper-and-lower-case headline, stacked double sub-heads, left-margin sub-heads, line drawings instead of photos) ran nineteen times monthly with no drop-off in pull. "A corny, old-fashioned, rather ugly advertisement has been developed for this specific medium — which has about twice the believability, twice the pulling power, and twice the staying power." (p. 190)
- **Techniques:** Camouflage — Adopting Format, borrowing the publication's stored believability reflex.

### Blackhead / Skin Specialist — News-Phraseology Opener (p. 191)

- **Chapters:** Ch. 13 (Camouflage — Adopting Phraseology)
- **What happened:** "SKIN SPECIALIST DEMONSTRATES / HOW TO RINSE AWAY YOUR BLACKHEADS / By Claire Hoffman / New York, N.Y. — A leading doctor today showed an audience…" — news-indicator opener (city + byline + today) set the ad's entire remainder as a factual report rather than a sales story.
- **Techniques:** Camouflage — Adopting Phraseology (news format).
