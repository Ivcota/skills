# Winning Markets

Market before offer. *(book)* **Starving crowd > Grand Slam Offer strength > persuasion skills.** A bad market stops the chain unless the user explicitly overrides after a hard warning.

Load [VOCABULARY.md](VOCABULARY.md). Use book pillar and market terms with the user; workflow labels stay in this file.

**Output is conversation-only.** No file unless the user asks to save a brief in the workspace.

## Pace and shape

- **Mini-interview per step** — 2–4 targeted questions; don't dump a single giant form.
- **Generation-heavy** — propose concrete avatar candidates and sub-niches; user kills / keeps / edits.
- **Each step ends with an explicit exit** carried forward.
- **Backtracking** — "go back to Step N" replays forward from the edit.
- **Tag moves** *(book)* vs *(workflow)*.

**Load `RUBRICS.md` once at Step 3** (pillar bands). **Load `LADDERS.md` once at Step 2** when generating niching depth or price-implied specificity.

## Chain position

```
winning-markets → market brief (stop)
                → value-equation — CREATE on Grand Slam Offer for this avatar
```

**Inbound:** User may arrive with a half-formed niche, a list of ideas, or nothing. Don't assume they've read the book.

## Intake (always, before Step 1)

Ask both (skip what's already clear; confirm in one line):

1. **Mode** — **Validate** an avatar they already lean toward, or **Discover** from their background and constraints?
2. **Constraints** — skills, access, geography, capital, and anything they refuse to sell *(workflow)*.

If they only want a quick gut-check on one niche, run Validate mode but still complete Steps 3–4 (pillars + tier). Compression is OK; skipping pillars is not.

## Step 1 — Primary market *(book)*

**Frame:** "We anchor in one of the three markets people always spend on."

| Market | Examples of sub-areas |
|--------|------------------------|
| **Health** | weight, fitness, mental health, longevity, pain |
| **Wealth** | business, career, income, investing, ops |
| **Relationships** | dating, marriage, parenting, leadership, social |

**Discover mode:** From constraints, propose **3–5 sub-markets** (not avatars yet) across the three primaries. User picks one to narrow.

**Validate mode:** Map their stated niche to Health / Wealth / Relationships. If it spans two, ask which primary market matches **purchasing power** when they're under stress *(book)*.

**Exit:** one line — "Primary market: [Health | Wealth | Relationships] → sub-market: [X]."

---

## Step 2 — Avatar (sub-niche) *(book)*

**Frame:** "Same product, 100× price difference — the avatar does the work. We go until it feels uncomfortably specific."

1. **Current breadth** — restate their audience in their words.
2. **Niching ladder** *(workflow)* — propose **three** avatars on one ladder (broad → mid → narrow). Use `LADDERS.md` patterns. Each step: role, situation, constraint, and **who has more pain + more purchasing power** *(book — e.g. seniors vs college students for relationship coaching)*.
3. **User picks one avatar** — edit until it passes the **two-sentence test** *(workflow)*: (a) a stranger in that avatar nods "that's me"; (b) a stranger outside it says "not for me."

**Anti-patterns to reject:**
- "Small business owners," "freelancers," "anyone who wants X"
- Audiences who **want** but don't **need** *(book — pain must be desperate, not nice-to-have)*

**Exit:** locked avatar — one sentence: role + situation + desired change. Example shape: *"Microgym owner, ~100 members, signed lease, at least one employee, wants clients to lose weight."*

---

## Step 3 — Four pillars *(book)*

**Frame:** "Pain · Purchasing Power · Easy to Target · Growing — the four things you look for in a market."

Load `RUBRICS.md`. For each pillar:

1. State **1–10** with the band label from RUBRICS.
2. Cite **one concrete fact** from the user or a labeled *(guess)* with what would confirm it.
3. One sentence on **implication for price and reach**.

| Pillar | Question |
|--------|----------|
| **Pain** | Do they *need* this solved now? Is the pain the pitch if you describe it accurately? |
| **Purchasing power** | Can they pay your **required** price (not charity pricing)? |
| **Easy to target** | Can you **find** them in a list — associations, platforms, job titles, venues? |
| **Growing** | Tailwind or headwind? *(book — avoid shrinking industries unless a starving-crowd spike)* |

**Hard flags** *(workflow)* — surface immediately; don't bury in averages:
- **Not growing** — Growing ≤3 without a temporary starving-crowd spike
- **No purchasing power** — Purchasing Power ≤3 for the price you need
- **Not easy to target** — Easy to Target ≤3; *needles in a haystack* *(book)*

If **two or more** pillars score ≤4, say the market is failing the four-pillar test before Step 4.

**Exit:** pillar table — four scores, four evidence lines, any hard flags named.

---

## Step 4 — Market tier *(book)*

**Frame:** "Great market forgives a bad offer. Normal market needs offer strength. Bad market — we pivot."

Combine pillars into a tier:

| Tier | Typical signal *(workflow)* | Implication |
|------|-----------------------------|-------------|
| **Great / starving crowd** | Strong pillars or obvious demand spike | Weak offer and persuasion can still win *(book)* — Grand Slam Offer still multiplies upside |
| **Normal market** | No hard flags; most pillars 5–7 | Need a **Grand Slam Offer** — run value-equation on all four drivers |
| **Bad market** | Shrinking market + low purchasing power, or two+ pillars ≤4 without spike | Pivot niche; don't build an offer yet |

**Starving crowd check** *(book)* — one question: "If you showed up mediocre — bad copy, high price, weak pitch — would this audience still buy because demand >> supply?" **Yes** nudges toward Great.

**Three-levers summary** *(book)* — starving crowd vs Grand Slam Offer vs persuasion: which must carry.

**Exit:** tier label + two-sentence rationale.

**Bad-tier gate:** Do not offer value-equation handoff unless the user **explicitly** accepts override after reading the pivot recommendation. Default exit = Market Brief + pivot options (Step 2 ladder alternates or new sub-market from Step 1).

---

## Step 5 — Pain statement *(book)*

**Frame:** "The pain is the pitch. If they feel understood, they buy."

1. Draft **2–3** pain paragraphs in **the avatar's words** (not marketer voice). Specific moments, costs, and failed attempts.
2. User edits until they say it would make a fit prospect feel **understood** *(book — persuasion goal)*.
3. Optional **headline hook** — one line they'd use above the fold.

**Exit:** locked pain statement (short paragraph + optional hook).

---

## Step 6 — Commitment *(book)*

**Frame:** "Pick, then commit. Niche slap is hopping before the offer had a fair shot."

1. **Commitment question** — "Will you keep this avatar through offer iteration (failed tests = fix the offer, not swap the niche) unless pillars materially change?"
2. If **no** — return to Step 2 with one new ladder branch; don't proceed to handoff.
3. If **yes** — record commit.

**TAM note** *(book — workflow)* — only if they ask about ceiling: niching is correct under ~$10M/yr; broadening comes after exhausting the niche. No TAM lecture unless requested.

**Exit:** commit yes/no; if yes, ready for handoff.

## Rules

- **Demand channeling, not creation** *(book)* — score existing pain and wallets; don't fantasize markets from scratch without evidence.
- **Don't proceed silently on Bad** — pivot or explicit override only.
- **B2B nudge** *(book/workflow)* — when Purchasing Power is borderline, ask if a **business buyer** exists (employer, owner) vs consumer wallet.
- **Dying industry** *(book)* — name the headwind (e.g. newspapers −25%/yr); contrast Lloyd pivot story when motivating change.
- **Niche until uncomfortable** — if avatar could fit "any course" in the space, send back to Step 2.
- **Conversation-only** unless user requests a saved brief.
