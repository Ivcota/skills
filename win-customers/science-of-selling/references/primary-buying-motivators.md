# Primary Buying Motivators

Use this to prepare discovery, listen during calls, and convert buyer language into value. The extraction notes cite Chapter 6: "The key to effective listening is not to listen more, but to know what you should be listening for." Hoffeld identifies three primary buying motivators: **buyer problems**, **dominant buying motives**, and **buying requirements**.

## Capture worksheet

| Motivator | What to capture | Questions | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Buyer problems** | The meaningful problem, its cause, scope, and cost. | "What prompted you to look at this?" "Where is this breaking down?" "What happens if it continues?" | Problem statement in buyer words. |
| **Dominant buying motives** | Emotional reasons to act: desire for gain and fear of loss. | "If this worked, what would it let you do?" "If you wait, what could go wrong?" | Gain/loss language the buyer personally owns. |
| **Buying requirements** | Decision process, decision makers, decision criteria. | "How is a decision like this made?" "Who else is involved?" "What criteria will you use?" | Qualification map and close path. |

## Discovery flow

1. **Open with context.** "What made this worth discussing now?"
2. **Find problems.** Name likely issues only as a prompt, not a leading verdict: "Many clients come to us because of [A] or [B]. Which is more relevant?"
3. **Layer questions.** Use first-level facts, second-level explanation, then third-level emotional impact.
4. **Capture buying requirements.** Do not leave without decision process, decision makers/influencers, and criteria.
5. **Confirm.** "Just so I understand, for this to be a possible solution, it needs to [criteria]. Is that correct?"

## Layered question bank

| Level | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **First-level** | Facts, situations, process. | "What is your current process?" "How do you decide on a vendor?" |
| **Second-level** | Explanation and assessment. | "Why is that important?" "How has that affected the team?" |
| **Third-level** | Emotional motive: gain/loss. | "If we solved that, what would it mean for you?" "If it stays unresolved, what happens?" |

The notes cite Chapter 5: first-level questions reveal "thoughts, facts, behaviors, and situations"; second-level questions guide buyers in "assessing and explaining"; third-level questions reveal dominant buying motives.

## Buyer-language rule

Every artifact you create should reuse the buyer's exact words. Replace seller language like "increase efficiency" with buyer language like "stop reps wasting two hours reconciling spreadsheets before Monday pipeline calls."

## Qualification red flags

- No clear problem → weak **Why Change**.
- No time pressure or consequence → weak **Why Now**.
- No decision owner or process → buying requirements missing.
- Criteria do not match your distinct value → risk of losing to a better-fit alternative.
- Buyer cannot state gain/loss → weak **Why Spend the Money**.
