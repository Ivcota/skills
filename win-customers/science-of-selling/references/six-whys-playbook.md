# Six Whys Playbook

Use this when building or auditing a pitch, call plan, demo, close, or sales message. Hoffeld's core equation is: **buying decision = commitments to the Six Whys + buyer emotional state**. The notes cite Chapter 3: "BD = f (SW, ES)" and "COMMITMENTS TO THE SIX WHYS® + POSITIVE EMOTIONAL STATE = POSITIVE BUYING DECISION (OR SALE)."

## Operating rule

Do not treat the Six Whys as a checklist you recite to the buyer. Treat them as **commitments the buyer must make** while moving through the sale. If one is missing, the sale stalls there and the objection usually points back to that rejected Why.

Add one explicit discovery checkpoint between early problem/urgency work and presenting: the **Buying Requirements Gate**. After the buyer at least tentatively accepts **Why Change** and **Why Now**, ask how they will judge, approve, time, and fund a solution. Do not present until criteria, stakeholders, process, timeline, and budget/ROI are confirmed in buyer words. This gate is not a seventh Why; it tells you how to answer Why Industry Solution, Why You/Company, Why Product/Service, and Why Spend the Money.

## The six commitments

| Why | Buyer must believe | Discovery questions | Proof / move | Commitment check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Why Change?** | The current state has a meaningful problem. | "What issues are you seeing with [current approach]?" "What is causing that?" "How far does that problem reach?" | Challenge status quo with insight; find problem, cause, scope, and pain. | "It sounds like this is worth changing, is that fair?" |
| **2. Why Now?** | Delaying has a cost, but the buyer still feels free. | "What happens if this stays unresolved for another quarter?" "Why is this coming up now?" | Show cost of delay; use autonomy language: "Of course, it's up to you." | "Knowing that, is there any reason to delay?" |
| **3. Why Your Industry Solution?** | This category beats doing nothing, DIY, internal workaround, or another category. | "What alternatives are you considering outside vendors like us?" "Have you thought about solving this internally?" | Compare outcomes and risks against non-consumption, DIY, and outside-industry options. | "Compared with [alternative], does this type of solution better fit what you're trying to do?" |
| **4. Why You / Your Company?** | The seller and company are credible, trustworthy, and capable. | "What would you need to know about us to feel confident?" | Relevant expertise, proof, confidence, references, implementation reliability. | "Do you feel comfortable with us as the team to solve this?" |
| **5. Why Your Product / Service?** | This specific offer has distinct value the buyer wants. | "Which capabilities matter most?" "What would a perfect solution need to include?" | Distinct value: unique + buyer-desired. Link feature to their stated motivator. | "Would [distinct value] meet the requirement you described?" |
| **6. Why Spend the Money?** | The gain or avoided loss justifies the investment. | "If you invested now, what would the main advantages be?" "If you waited, what could it cost you?" | Let buyer verbalize desire for gain and fear of loss; anchor price against ROI or avoided cost. | "For the reasons you mentioned, does investing now make sense?" |

## Why Change sequence

From the notes: Hoffeld gives "3 Steps for Answering 'Why Change?'": find problems, identify cause/scope, and ask deeper questions that help buyers feel painful outcomes.

Use this sequence:

1. **Find problem:** "Many teams like yours come to us because of [problem A] or [problem B]. Which is closer to what you're seeing?"
2. **Cause/scope:** "What's causing that?" "Where else does it show up?" "Who is affected?"
3. **Pain:** "If that keeps happening, what does it cost the team?" "What does it prevent you from doing?"
4. **Commit:** "So this is not just inconvenient; it is costing you [buyer words]. Is that right?"

## Why Now without pressure

Hoffeld warns that urgency can trigger reactance. Use urgency plus autonomy:

- "Waiting until next quarter means [cost/delay/risk]. Of course, it's up to you."
- "You can use [incentive/deadline] if it helps; you can just let me know if you want to use it."

## Why Spend the Money rule

Do not tell buyers their dominant buying motive. The guardrails cite: "never tell buyers their dominant buying motive. Instead, guide them into stating their reasons." Ask them to say the gain or loss, then build on their words.

## Audit pattern

For any sales asset, label every sentence by the Why it supports. Cut unsupported claims. If the buyer can say "yes" to all six and is in a positive/neutral emotional state, ask for the next commitment.
