# Strategic Commitments and Closing

Use this when planning closes, diagnosing stalls, or writing call scripts. Hoffeld reframes closing as a series of commitments, not a final trick. The guardrails cite Chapter 8: ignoring earlier commitments "forces the brain to take an unnatural mental leap that produces negative feelings of pressure and anxiety."

## Core rule

Ask for the sale only after the Sales Equation is fulfilled: the buyer has committed to the Six Whys and is in a positive/neutral emotional state. The primary-source notes for Chapter 8 say buyers are ready after "qualified buyers commit to each of the Six Whys® and adopt a positive emotional state."

## Commitment ladder

| Step | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Micro commitment** | Get agreement to continue discovery. | "Can I ask a few questions to see if this is relevant?" |
| **Involvement trial close** | Help buyer imagine ownership/use. | "If you moved forward, would you use A or B?" |
| **Value confirmation** | Confirm fit to requirements. | "Does this program meet the requirements your team laid out?" |
| **Commitment trial close** | Get verbal commitment to a Why. | "Based on what you've shared, this sounds like something you need to solve now, is that correct?" |
| **Final close** | Ask for purchase/next formal step. | "Would you like to move forward?" |

## Involvement trial closes

These imply use without forcing a decision. Use after value has been established.

Patterns from the application notes:

- "If you were to move forward and invest in [product], would you [option A] or [option B]?"
- "Does [value assertion] meet [buyer requirement]?"
- "Waiting to install this will cost [amount/time], won't it?"

## Commitment trial closes

Use these to turn comprehension into commitment. The guardrails cite Hoffeld: salespeople often mistake comprehension for commitment; "comprehension is not enough."

Patterns:

- Why Change: "This is worth solving, correct?"
- Why Now: "Knowing the delay cost, is there any reason to wait?"
- Why Industry Solution: "Does this category fit better than trying to handle it internally?"
- Why You/Company: "Do you feel comfortable with us as the partner?"
- Why Product/Service: "Does this meet the criteria you described?"
- Why Spend Money: "For the reasons you mentioned, does the investment make sense?"

## Handling noncommittal responses

The primary-source notes for Chapter 8 define a noncommittal response as "any reaction that is not a strong commitment." Treat it as useful diagnostic signal, not rejection.

### If they object

1. Diagnose the objection.
2. Resolve misunderstanding, present relevant information, or reframe.
3. Confirm buy-in.
4. Ask for the commitment again.

If unresolved, note it and revisit after new relevant information.

### If they give a weak response

Hoffeld says weak responses often show buyers are leaning your direction but not ready to say yes. Ask second-level questions that help them explain the response **in your favor**.

Pattern:

> Buyer: "I see your point, but I want to think about it more."
> Seller: "I understand. It sounds like you're interested in [feature/outcome]. If you did have [it], how would that benefit your organization?"
> Seller after answer: "That being said, is there any reason you would consider a solution without [it]?"

## Final close timing

Ask when:

- buyer is qualified,
- all Six Whys have commitments,
- no major objection is festering,
- emotional state is positive/neutral,
- next step is the natural next commitment.

Use either:

- **Closing statement:** "The next step would be..." / "To begin, I need..."
- **Closing question:** "Would you like to move forward?" / "Are you ready to give [company] a try?"

Do not use elaborate closing tricks after the buyer has already committed. Make the next step simple.
