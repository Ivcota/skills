# Sandler Selling System — framework extraction

Source: David H. Sandler, *You Can't Teach a Kid to Ride a Bike at a Seminar* (1996 PDF in `~/Downloads`). This is a paraphrased framework map, not a reproduction of the book.

## Core thesis

If the salesperson does not bring a system, they default to the prospect's system. The Sandler system is built to keep the salesperson in control without pressure: the prospect discovers pain, qualifies money and decision, and closes themselves.

Sandler treats sales mastery as reinforcement training, not a seminar event: technique + behavior + human dynamics + coaching + practice.

## 1. The prospect's default system

The buyer's system is defensive and information-seeking:

1. **Lie / withhold** — prospects protect themselves from sales pressure.
2. **Extract knowledge** — they try to turn the salesperson into an unpaid consultant.
3. **Mislead with hope** — they keep the salesperson working with vague next steps.
4. **Disappear** — they stop returning calls once they have what they need.

Counter-principle: get information before giving information.

## 2. Sandler's high-level selling rules

The book states the sales job as five core rules:

1. Qualify the prospect.
2. Extract the prospect's pain.
3. Verify money.
4. Verify decision authority/process.
5. Match the product/service to the pain.

A second operational five-point flow adds post-sell protection:

1. Uncover pain.
2. Put money issues on the table.
3. Discover the decision process.
4. Present only the pain-removing solution.
5. Post-sell to prevent buyer's remorse.

## 3. The Sandler Submarine / 7-step map

The submarine metaphor means each stage is a sealed compartment: finish it, close it, and do not let the prospect run backward.

1. **Bonding & Rapport** — make the prospect feel more okay than you.
2. **Up-front Contract** — agree on agenda, time, ground rules, and possible outcomes before moving.
3. **Pain** — uncover the emotional reason to change.
4. **Budget / Money** — establish whether money exists and how the investment will happen.
5. **Decision** — qualify who decides, when, how, and by what process.
6. **Fulfillment** — present only what fulfills the prior contract.
7. **Post-Sell** — revalidate the sale before leaving to reduce buyer's remorse.

## 4. I/R Model: Identity vs Role

Separate **who you are** from **what role you are performing**.

- Identity (`I`) is always a 10.
- Role performance (`R`) fluctuates.
- Sales failure is role feedback, not identity damage.
- Protecting identity enables risk, directness, and recovery after rejection.

This is the psychological foundation behind Sandler's behavior change work.

## 5. Failure processing

Sandler frames failure as a skill. His five-stage failure sequence:

1. Disbelief.
2. Fear.
3. Anger.
4. Acceptance.
5. Despair / reflection.

The point: do not make major decisions in early emotional stages; extract the lesson after acceptance.

## 6. Ten conditioning rules

Daily conditioning supports the system:

1. **Stay on the right side of the trouble line** — protect selling time; separate Pay Time from No Pay Time.
2. **Burn your bridges** — commit to sales as the professional path.
3. **Get mentally and emotionally tough** — seek yes/no, avoid vague “think it over.”
4. **Maintain self-esteem** — do not let prospects or gatekeepers damage identity.
5. **Cultivate a support group** — use reinforcement and coaching.
6. **Know when to use product knowledge** — sell first, educate later.
7. **Know your competition** — understand alternatives and weak points.
8. **Keep an Attitude/Behavior Journal** — track identity goals, role goals, activity, results, and review.
9. **Work a prospecting system** — move toward referrals/service-led prospecting, not endless cold calls.
10. **Use a selling system** — technique plus self-esteem/behavior work.

## 7. Dummy Up / Dummy Curve

Core rule: **get information; don't give it too early.**

The salesperson deliberately takes a curious, slightly unsure, Columbo-like stance. This lowers pressure, keeps the prospect talking, and prevents unpaid consulting.

Related principles:

- Sell today; educate tomorrow.
- Do not “paint seagulls” in the prospect's picture — let them discover missing pieces.
- Ask before answering.
- Use “I don't know, can I ask…” rather than rushing to prove expertise.

## 8. Reversing

Reversing means answering a prospect's question or statement with a question that uncovers the real issue.

Basic pattern:

1. Softening statement.
2. Repeat/acknowledge the question.
3. Ask why it matters or what it means.

Uses:

- Keep the prospect talking.
- Avoid getting boxed in.
- Clarify vague words.
- Let prospects resolve their own objections.
- Surface hidden motives.

Key variants:

- **Testing-the-water reverse** — “I get the feeling that…”
- **Page mismatch reverse** — slow the prospect down when they jump ahead.
- **Pressure reverse** — name pressure in the room.
- **Problem reverse** — state “we have a problem” and ask how they see it.
- **Never answer an unasked question** — convert statements into clarified questions.
- **Magic Wand / Druthers** — invite the prospect to define the ideal solution.
- **Off-the-record reverse** — reduce pressure around sensitive topics.
- **Stroke-Repeat-Reverse** — acknowledge, repeat the vague phrase, ask what it means.

Exception: if the prospect asks the exact same question twice, answer it.

## 9. Negative Reverse Selling

Negative Reverse Selling is the “give line to the fish” move: when the prospect shows interest, the salesperson moves slightly away instead of rushing toward the close.

Formula:

1. **Buildup** — name the attractive possibility.
2. **Take-away** — gently suggest it may not fit or may not be for them.

Purpose: create space for the prospect to sell themselves.

Clock model:

- 3 o'clock = sold/positive.
- 6 o'clock = neutral.
- 9 o'clock = negative.

Rule: do not get between the prospect's current position and the close. Work from behind the prospect's position, reduce pressure, and get them moving.

## 10. Bonding & Rapport

The short rule: make the prospect feel more okay than you.

Do not make the prospect feel stupid, exposed, embarrassed, or pressured. Rapport is the condition that lets pain, money, and decision conversations happen without defensiveness.

## 11. Up-front Contract

A contract sets ground rules before each move.

Formal components:

1. Lawful object.
2. Competency / authority.
3. Consideration.
4. Mutual consent.

Operational components:

- Time available.
- Agenda.
- Roles/questions from both sides.
- Permission for either side to say no.
- Permission to say yes if there is fit.
- No “think it over” drift.
- Clear next step.

Sandler's anti-fog rule: no mutual mystification. Before moving forward, ask the prospect to restate what happened and what happens next.

## 12. Pain

Sandler rejects feature/benefit selling as too intellectual. Buying is emotional; pain is the strongest driver.

Five emotional buying states:

1. Present pain.
2. Future pain / fear.
3. Present pleasure.
4. Future pleasure.
5. Interest / curiosity.

Sandler prioritizes pain.

Pain progression:

1. **Well** — no urgency.
2. **Hurt** — problem is noticed.
3. **Sick** — emotion increases.
4. **Critical** — prospect wants the problem fixed now.
5. **Miracle** — solution removes pain.

Pain discovery methods:

- Active listening.
- “I understand” / “that makes sense.”
- Parroting and paraphrasing.
- Feedback on emotional words.
- At least three probing questions before assuming real pain.

Guardrail: do not over-dig or humiliate the prospect. The goal is discovery, not amateur therapy.

## 13. Money / Budget

Money belongs early, after pain, not at the end.

Main budget question: whether a budget exists and how the prospect plans to make the investment.

Three responses:

1. **No budget** — ask how they plan to make the investment.
2. **Budget exists** — ask them to share it in round numbers.
3. **Budget exists but won't share** — use bracketing.

Budget techniques:

- **Bracketing** — offer ranges and ask which range to address.
- **That could be a problem** — surface budget mismatch without pressure.
- **Monkey's Paw** — sell a smaller first step that can pull through the larger sale.
- **Do not look in the prospect's pocket** — do not assume affordability based on your own wallet.
- **Do not casually discount** — protect your self-image and price integrity.

## 14. Decision

Qualify decision before fulfillment.

Four qualifiers:

1. **What** decision process do they use?
2. **When** will they move forward?
3. **How** will the purchase/payment happen?
4. **Who** besides them is involved?

If other people decide:

- Try to include them before presenting.
- If not possible, use the **Intermediate Decision-Making** technique: ask whether the current prospect would buy if it were solely their decision.
- Use the **Rehearsal Technique** to prepare them to present pain, money, and rationale to the committee.

Hard rule: yes is okay, no is okay, “think it over” is not okay unless explicitly contracted.

## 15. Fulfillment

Fulfillment is not a generic pitch. It is delivery against the prior contract.

Structure:

1. **Beginning** — review Pain, Money, and Decision agreements; get confirmation.
2. **Middle** — show only features/benefits tied to stated pain.
3. **End** — test whether the prospect is ready to buy.

Thermometer Close:

- Ask where they are on a 0–10 scale.
- If 10, stop presenting.
- If low, ask what is missing.
- If mid/high, ask what they need to see to reach 10.
- At 10, ask what they want you to do now.

## 16. Post-Sell

Buyer's remorse is expected. The post-sell gives the prospect a chance to back out while you can still handle it.

Process:

1. Close the sale mechanically: agreement, check, PO, delivery details.
2. Thank the customer.
3. Raise a prior compromise or possible concern.
4. Ask whether that issue could cause cancellation later.
5. Resolve any crack before leaving.

Final standard: the sale is not complete until the order is collected and clears.

## 17. Attitude–Behavior–Technique Success Triangle

Success depends on all three:

1. **Attitude** — self-image/outlook; includes You, Company, Market.
2. **Behavior** — goals, plan, action, tracking, fine-tuning.
3. **Technique** — tactics, strategy, delivery, prospect qualification, presentation, closing.

Sandler's behavior principle: it is not how you feel that determines how you act; it is how you act that determines how you feel.

## 18. Goal and motivation tools

Goal setting functions:

- Improve I and R.
- Expose strengths and weaknesses.
- Create direction and priorities.
- Separate reality from wishful thinking.
- Force responsibility.

Motivation definition: seeing a desired future in the present, accepting the price, and moving daily toward it.

Exercises:

- **Lifeline** — map birth, present, retirement, death, then inventory goals by life area.
- **Birdcage challenge** — decide the goal, build the cage/plan, trust the outcome to arrive.
- **Small-goal trap** — create small written commitments to build goal-achievement momentum.

## Potential skill shape

A Sandler skill should probably be model-invoked for sales conversations, discovery, qualification, objection handling, and deal coaching.

Likely branches:

1. Diagnose a live/stalled deal.
2. Build a sales-call plan.
3. Draft discovery/reversing questions.
4. Coach objection handling without pressure.
5. Design a post-sell / buyer's-remorse check.

The body should keep only the Submarine process and pointers to references for techniques, to avoid sprawl.
