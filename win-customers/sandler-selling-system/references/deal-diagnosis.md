# Deal Diagnosis

## Prospect's System

When a deal is unclear, check whether the seller has slipped into the prospect's system:

1. **Withholding / lying:** the prospect is protecting themselves from pressure.
2. **Unpaid consulting:** the seller gives expertise, demos, pricing, or proposals before qualification.
3. **Hopeful stalls:** “interesting,” “send info,” “circle back,” “committee,” “think it over.”
4. **Disappearance:** ghosting after the seller has already given value.

Diagnosis question: where did the seller give up control without a contract?

## Submarine Leak Audit

For every deal, mark each compartment:

| Compartment | Sealed | Leaky | Skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bonding | Prospect feels safe and candid. | Polite but guarded. | Seller rushed into agenda/pitch. |
| Contract | Time, agenda, outcomes, next step agreed. | Agenda vague. | No contract. |
| Pain | Emotional impact and urgency stated by prospect. | Intellectual need only. | Seller assumed pain. |
| Budget | Money source/range/process known. | Budget hinted. | Price saved for end. |
| Decision | What/when/how/who known. | One piece missing. | “I decide” accepted untested. |
| Fulfillment | Demo maps to pain. | Extra features shown. | Generic pitch. |
| Post-Sell | Doubts surfaced before leaving. | Light reassurance. | Seller ran after signature. |

## Stalled Deal Recovery

Common stall: “I need to think it over.”

Diagnosis:

- Pain may not be critical.
- Money may be unresolved.
- Decision process may be hidden.
- The upfront contract did not prohibit drift.
- The presentation created new doubts.

Recovery:

> “That's fair. Usually when someone says they need to think it over, it means I missed something. Is it the fit, the money, the timing, or someone else's decision?”

If the deal is likely dead:

> “It sounds like this may not be worth pursuing right now. Should we close the file?”

## Ghosting Recovery

Do not chase with value-free check-ins.

Use a clean reverse:

> “I haven't heard back, which usually means priorities changed or this is no longer worth discussing. Should I close the file?”

If reopening:

> “Before we restart, can we agree on what happens if there's a fit and what happens if there isn't?”

## Proposal Sent, No Response

Likely cause: proposal sent before pain, budget, or decision was sealed.

Recovery:

> “I may have sent that too early. Before either of us spends more time on it, can we revisit what problem is worth solving, what range makes sense, and who needs to weigh in?”

## Price Resistance

Diagnose before defending:

- Is price resistance real budget mismatch?
- Is pain too weak?
- Is prospect comparing against a different scope?
- Is there an unspoken decision maker?

Reverse:

> “You may be right. When you say it's too expensive, does that mean it exceeds the budget, the problem isn't costly enough, or you're comparing it to another option?”

## Qualify-In / Qualify-Out Rules

Qualify in when:

- Pain is emotional and current.
- Budget range can support a solution.
- Decision process is known.
- Prospect agrees to a clear next step.

Qualify out when:

- No pain or urgency.
- No plausible money path.
- Decision maker is unreachable and champion will not rehearse.
- Prospect refuses yes/no clarity.
- Seller is being used for unpaid consulting.

## Output Format for Deal Coaching

Return:

1. **Current compartment:** where the deal is now.
2. **Leak:** what is missing or fuzzy.
3. **Risk:** what happens if seller proceeds.
4. **Recovery move:** exact Sandler-style script.
5. **Next contract:** who does what by when.
6. **Verdict:** advance, repair, nurture, or qualify out.
