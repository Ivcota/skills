# Scripts and Objections

Use scripts as starting points. Keep tone soft, curious, and non-needy.

## Opening Up-front Contract

> “Thanks for making time. It probably makes sense to use this call to see whether [problem] is worth solving and whether we're a fit. I'll ask questions about what's going on, what it's costing, and how you'd decide. You should ask me anything you need. At the end, if it doesn't make sense, are you comfortable telling me no? If it does, we can decide the next step. Fair?”

## “Send Me Information”

> “Happy to send something. Before I do, can I ask a quick question? Sometimes when people ask for information this early, it means there isn't enough interest to meet and they're being polite. Is that the case here?”

If still sending:

> “I'll send [specific item]. If it lands, what would you want to happen next? And if it doesn't, are you okay telling me no?”

## “How Much Is It?”

Early call:

> “Good question. The honest answer is it depends on what you're trying to fix. Before I throw out a number that may be irrelevant, can I ask what prompted the question?”

After pain:

> “We should talk about money. Have you set aside a range to solve this?”

## “It's Too Expensive”

> “You may be right. When you say too expensive, do you mean it's outside the budget, it's more than the problem is worth, or it's more than another option?”

If budget mismatch:

> “That could be a problem. What were you planning to do if solving this required more than that?”

## “We Need to Think It Over”

> “That's fair. Usually when someone says that, it means I missed something. Is it the fit, the money, the timing, or the decision process?”

If still vague:

> “Would it be better to call it a no for now rather than have both of us chase something unclear?”

## “We Need to Talk Internally”

> “That makes sense. Who needs to be involved?”

Then:

> “If it were only your decision, would you move forward?”

If yes:

> “What questions or concerns will they raise, and how should we prepare for those?”

## “Your Competitor Is Cheaper”

> “They may be. When you compare us, are you comparing the same problem, same scope, and same outcome?”

If yes:

> “Then it may make sense to choose them. What would make it worth paying more?”

## “We're Happy With Our Current Vendor”

> “That's good. You probably shouldn't change unless there's a real reason. What made you take this conversation?”

## “Call Me Next Quarter”

> “Happy to. What do you expect will be different next quarter?”

If nothing changes:

> “Should we close this for now instead of pretending timing is the only issue?”

## “Just Give Me Your Best Price”

> “I can do that, but I don't want to guess. Best for what outcome? Lowest cost, lowest risk, fastest fix, or something else?”

## Post-Sell Check

> “Before I go, I want to check one thing. We agreed [decision/compromise]. If you woke up tomorrow with second thoughts, would it be because of that, or something else?”

## Follow-Up After Verbal Yes

> “You said yes to moving forward. Before we treat that as real, what could still stop this?”

## Clean Break Email

Subject: Close the file?

> “I haven't heard back, which usually means this is no longer a priority or something changed. Should I close the file, or is there still a reason to talk?”
