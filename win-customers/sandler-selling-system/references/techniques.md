# Sandler Techniques

## Dummy Up

Purpose: prevent unpaid consulting and keep the prospect's picture intact.

Rules:

- Get information before giving information.
- Let the prospect define the picture before you add details.
- Prefer “I don't know yet” to premature expertise.
- If asked a question, first find out why the answer matters.

Patterns:

- “Good question. What prompted that?”
- “I might be able to help, but can I ask a few questions first?”
- “I'm not sure yet. Help me understand what you're trying to solve.”
- “Suppose I could. What would happen next?”

Use when the user is about to explain, educate, demo, or defend before the prospect is qualified.

## Reversing

Reversing turns pressure into truth. It is not evasion; it is clarification.

Basic pattern:

1. Softener.
2. Repeat or acknowledge.
3. Clarifying question.

Examples:

- Prospect: “How much is it?”
  Seller: “Good question. Before I answer, what are you comparing it against?”

- Prospect: “Your price is high.”
  Seller: “That may be true. When you say high, compared to what?”

- Prospect: “Send me information.”
  Seller: “Happy to. Usually when people ask that early, it means there may not be enough interest to meet. Is that what's happening here?”

- Prospect: “We're close.”
  Seller: “I appreciate that. When you say close, what does that mean exactly?”

Reversing variants:

- **Testing water:** “I get the feeling that [hypothesis]. Is that fair?”
- **Page mismatch:** “You're on page seven and I'm still on page three. Can we back up?”
- **Pressure:** “I feel some pressure here, and I'm not sure why.”
- **Problem:** “We may have a problem. How do you think we should handle it?”
- **Meaning:** “When you say [word], what does that mean?”
- **Magic wand:** “If you could wave a magic wand, what would the ideal fix look like?”
- **Off the record:** “Off the record, what range were you hoping for?”

Exception: if the prospect asks the exact same question twice, answer it directly, then contract the next move.

## Negative Reverse Selling

Use when the prospect shows interest, is overly positive, is negative, or is stuck. Move slightly away from the close so the prospect moves toward it.

Formula:

1. Buildup: acknowledge the possible fit.
2. Take-away: suggest it may not be right or may need more thought.

Examples:

- “It sounds like this might help, but I'm not sure we've found enough pain to justify changing.”
- “You seem interested, but based on what you said earlier, I wouldn't have expected this to be a priority. What did I miss?”
- “Maybe this isn't worth solving right now. Should we stop here?”

Clock guide:

- **3 o'clock:** positive/sold. Do not jump in front of them; reverse lightly and let them confirm.
- **6 o'clock:** neutral. Move away enough to create motion.
- **9 o'clock:** negative. Get behind the negativity; ask whether it is over.

Use sparingly. Too much take-away becomes gimmicky or dismissive.

## Budget Bracketing

Use when the prospect will not reveal budget.

Pattern:

> “That makes sense. Work like this can be as light as [low range], more often [mid range], and sometimes [high range] depending on scope. Off the record, which range should we even be discussing?”

Then reverse the answer gently:

> “Interesting. I thought, based on the impact you described, you might have picked the higher range. What am I missing?”

Do not push until the prospect feels not-okay.

## Monkey's Paw

Use for big-ticket offers when full commitment is hard.

Create a smaller paid first step:

- assessment
- audit
- workshop
- pilot
- diagnostic
- strategy session
- implementation plan

Rules:

- It must create real value on its own.
- It should naturally pull through the larger engagement.
- It should reduce risk without discounting the core offer.

## Thermometer Close

Use during fulfillment to measure readiness.

> “On a 0–10 scale, where are you?”

Responses:

- **10:** stop and ask what they want you to do now.
- **6–9:** ask what they need to reach 10.
- **0–5:** ask what you missed; return to pain, money, or decision.

## Post-Sell

Use after agreement, before leaving.

Pattern:

1. Name the possible concern.
2. Make it safe to revisit.
3. Ask whether it could threaten the order.

Example:

> “One thing I want to check before I go: you originally wanted [X], and we agreed [Y] made more sense. I don't want you waking up tomorrow second-guessing that. Could that become an issue?”
