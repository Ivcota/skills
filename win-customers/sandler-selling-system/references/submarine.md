# Submarine Reference

## Submarine Map

Move forward one sealed compartment at a time. A compartment is **sealed** when the prospect confirms the truth in their words and agrees what happens next.

1. **Bonding & Rapport** — make the prospect feel safe and more okay than you. Do not embarrass, overpower, or outsmart them.
2. **Up-front Contract** — agree on purpose, time, roles, questions, possible yes/no, and next step.
3. **Pain** — uncover the emotional reason to change.
4. **Budget / Money** — confirm money exists and how the investment can happen.
5. **Decision** — qualify process, timing, payment mechanics, and people involved.
6. **Fulfillment** — show only what fulfills the prior pain/budget/decision contract.
7. **Post-Sell** — surface doubts before leaving so buyer's remorse does not cancel the sale.

## I/R Model

Use I/R to keep the seller non-needy.

- **I = Identity.** The person's human value; treat it as intact.
- **R = Role.** The selling performance; it can succeed, fail, improve, or need coaching.
- Rejection, stalls, and awkward money conversations are R feedback, not I damage.
- If the user is spiraling, separate the two before scripting tactics.

## Bonding & Rapport

Aim: lower defenses enough for truth.

Rules:

- Make the prospect feel okay, not cornered.
- Use softeners: “That makes sense,” “That's not unusual,” “Good question.”
- Match seriousness; do not celebrate when the prospect reveals pain.
- If the prospect feels not-okay, repair before proceeding.

## Up-front Contract

Use before every meaningful move.

Minimum contract:

1. Why we are meeting.
2. How much time we have.
3. What the seller will ask/do.
4. What the prospect can ask/do.
5. What outcomes are allowed: yes, no, or specific next step.
6. What should not happen: vague “think it over” drift.

Template:

> “It probably makes sense to spend [time] seeing whether [problem] is worth solving and whether we're a fit. I'll ask questions about [pain], [money], and [decision]. You should ask anything you need. At the end, if it doesn't fit, are you comfortable telling me no? If it does fit, we can decide the next step. Fair?”

Validation:

> “Just so I know we're in sync, what do you see happening at the end of this call?”

## Pain

Pain is the emotional reason to change. Do not settle for intellectual interest.

Progression:

1. **Well** — no urgency; likely unqualified.
2. **Hurt** — problem exists, still intellectual.
3. **Sick** — consequences and emotion appear.
4. **Critical** — prospect wants action now.
5. **Miracle** — your solution can remove the pain.

Pain path:

- “How long has that been going on?”
- “What have you tried?”
- “What happens if it stays this way?”
- “How is that affecting you/team/revenue/customers?”
- “Is this important enough to fix now?”

Stop when the prospect states the problem, impact, and urgency in emotional language. Do not over-dig into humiliation or therapy.

## Budget / Money

Discuss money after pain and before fulfillment.

Core question:

> “Have you set aside a budget to fix this?”

If no budget:

> “That's not unusual. How were you planning to make the investment if this made sense?”

If yes:

> “Would you be comfortable sharing that in round numbers?”

If they will not share:

> “That makes sense. Solutions like this can range from [low] to [mid], and more involved ones from [mid] to [high]. Off the record, which range should we even be talking about?”

Guardrails:

- Do not project your wallet into theirs.
- Do not discount to relieve your own discomfort.
- If full scope is too expensive, consider a Monkey's Paw: a paid smaller first step that can pull through the larger project.

## Decision

Qualify decision before fulfillment.

Four qualifiers:

1. **What:** “What process do you normally go through for a decision like this?”
2. **When:** “When would you want to move forward if it made sense?”
3. **How:** “How would the purchase actually happen — PO, card, contract, procurement?”
4. **Who:** “Who besides you would be involved?”

If they claim sole authority, verify gently:

> “So no committee, partner, finance, legal, or anyone else would need to weigh in?”

If they are not the final decision maker:

- Try to include the real decision makers.
- If impossible, ask: “If this were only your decision, would you move forward?”
- Rehearse how they will explain pain, money, and decision criteria to the others.

## Fulfillment and Post-Sell

Fulfillment sequence:

1. Review pain, budget, and decision agreements.
2. Present only the capabilities that remove stated pain.
3. Avoid new “seagulls” unless the prospect asks or the point maps to pain.
4. Use thermometer close.

Thermometer:

> “On a 0–10 scale, where 0 means no interest and 10 means you want to move forward, where are you?”

- If 10: stop presenting and ask, “What would you like me to do now?”
- If under 6: ask what you missed.
- If 6–9: ask what they need to see to reach 10.

Post-Sell:

1. Get the order/contract/check/details.
2. Thank them.
3. Raise one likely doubt or compromise.
4. Ask whether that could cause them to pause or cancel later.
5. Resolve it before leaving.

Template:

> “Before I go, one thing is still on my mind. Earlier we agreed [compromise/risk]. I don't want to leave and then have you feel uneasy tomorrow. Is there any chance that causes you to hold this up?”
